首闯大峡谷（卷 1 / 2）First through the Grand Canyon

约翰·韦斯利·鲍威尔 John Wesley Powell · 1869 · 中英逐段对照 · 第 1/2 卷

《首闯大峡谷》中英对照全文 · 卷 1 / 2 · 本卷起于「INTRODUCTION」

INTRODUCTION

科罗拉多河由格朗德河与绿河在美国犹他州东南部交汇而成。介绍这条大河沿途切开一系列深峡——许多地方谷深 4000 到 6000 英尺、近于垂直，这就是本书记述的主角。

The Colorado River of the West is formed in southeastern Utah by the junction of the Grand and Green rivers. For hundreds of miles it flows through a series of profound chasms, in many places from 4,000 to 6,000 feet deep, and rising nearly vertically for a considerable distance above the water. These cañons are from one to fifteen miles wide at the top. The most famous of them is the Marble-Grand cañon (really continuous, although it goes under two names, the Marble and the Grand). Through this vast gorge the Colorado drops 2,330 feet in 283 miles, the current sometimes attaining a velocity of twenty-five miles an hour. The river itself varies in width from seventy-five feet to a quarter of a mile. In the narrowest places it has at times a depth of over 100 feet.

直到 1869 年，人们几乎不了解科罗拉多河全程——它从源头到格兰德沃什谷口是什么样没人知道，只有峡谷顶上的瞭望点和少数下到谷底的人见过局部。这条河被视为神秘而可怕的存在，甚至被认为在地下流淌，没人活着穿过峡谷，连当地印第安人也认为那是异想天开。

Up to 1869 practically nothing was known of the Colorado River from its source to where it emerges into the valley of the Grand Wash, except what could be observed from look-out points at the tops of the cañons, or from the few places where descents had been made to the bottom. It was a river of mystery and of fear. For long distances it was supposed to flow underground. There was no evidence that any human being had ever passed through the cañons and come out alive. The Indians who lived in the neighborhood considered such a feat preposterous.

这时来了一位科学家兼硬汉——约翰·韦斯利·鲍威尔少校。他沿河多次仔细考察后，冷静决定冒着生命危险、乘船一路漂到沃什谷，揭开这条河的秘密。

Then came a scientist and a man of nerve, Major John Wesley Powell, who studied the river carefully at several points along its bank, and calmly decided to risk his life in clearing up the mystery by navigating the stream clear through to the Wash.

这件事尤为不易，因为鲍威尔只有一条胳膊——他的右臂在夏洛伊战役中失去了。他新婚一个月的妻子在他负伤时正在总部，主动提出当他的「义肢」，就地入伍，格兰特将军给她发了「永久通行证」跟随部队。靠着这份支持，鲍威尔一直服役到战争结束。

The undertaking was all the more remarkable from the fact that Powell had only one arm. He had lost his right arm in the battle of Shiloh. His plucky young wife, to whom he had been married but a month, was present at headquarters when he was wounded, and promptly offered herself as a substitute for the missing limb so that her husband could continue in service. She then and there enlisted, and General Grant gave her a “perpetual pass” to follow the army in the capacity she had chosen. With this help Major Powell continued in active service to the close of the war.

鲍威尔学生时代专攻当时所称的「博物学」。战后他在伊利诺伊卫斯理大学、后又任伊利诺伊师范大学地质学教授。1867 年夏，他开创带学生野外实习的做法，把全班带到科罗拉多山区做地质考察，正是在这次行程中萌生了探索科罗拉多河峡谷的想法。他筹得伊利诺伊公共机构的经费，1869 年 5 月 24 日从绿河城出发——科罗拉多河正源之上——开启探险史上最危险的一次冒险之一。8 月 29 日他带着出发时九人中的五人走出大峡谷，四人途中离队，其中三人被印第安人杀死。

In his student days Powell had made a specialty of what was then called “natural history.” When the war was over he accepted a professorship of geology in the Illinois Wesleyan University, and later held a similar chair in the Illinois Normal University. In the summer of 1867 he initiated the practice of student field work by taking his class to the mountains of Colorado for geological exploration. It was on this trip that he formed the idea of exploring the cañons of the Colorado River of the West. Having obtained funds from public institutions of Illinois to outfit his little expedition, he started from Green River City, above the head of the Colorado proper, May 24, 1869, on one of the most hazardous adventures in the history of exploration. He emerged from the Grand Cañon on August 29, with five of the nine men he had started with. Four had deserted on the way, and three of these were killed by Indians.

鲍威尔这次首探的报告 1875 年由史密森学会出版，连同科学数据构成一大册四开本，现已绝版；本书的叙述部分即据其不加删节重印。

Major Powell’s report on this first exploration of the Colorado River was published by the Smithsonian Institution in 1875. Together with the scientific data appended, it forms a large quarto volume, which is now out of print. The narrative part is here republished without abridgement.

1870 年国会设立科罗拉多河地形与地质调查局，交由鲍威尔负责。1871-72 年他第二次顺河而下，这次是为政府，再次平安穿过，证明他掌握了一种极难的航行技术——后来许多尝试者在那些激流中丧生。

In 1870, Congress established a Topographical and Geological Survey of the Colorado River of the West, and Powell was placed in charge of it. In 1871-1872 he made a second descent of the river, this time for the government. Again he came through unharmed, proving his mastery of a species of navigation so difficult that many who have tried it in later years have perished in those brawling waters.

鲍威尔一生大量精力投入美国民族学，政府成立民族学局时他出任局长；1881 年他接替克拉伦斯·金出任美国地质调查局局长。鲍威尔于 1902 年 9 月 23 日逝世。

Much of Powell’s attention was given to American ethnology, and when a Bureau of Ethnology was formed by the government, he was appointed its director. In 1881 he succeeded Clarence King as director of the U. S. Geological Survey. Major Powell died September 23, 1902.

FIRST THROUGH THE GRAND CANYON

CHAPTER I THE VALLEY OF THE COLORADO

第一章开篇即点题：科罗拉多河由格朗德河与绿河汇流而成——这是全书地理的出发点。

The Colorado River is formed by the junction of the Grand and Green.

格朗德河发源于落基山脉，朗氏峰以西五六英里处，约北纬 40°17′、西经 105°43′。它从雪山流下，先穿过高原再进入平原，河床渐深、坡降渐缓。

The Grand River has its source in the Rocky Mountains, five or six miles west of Long’s Peak, in latitude 40° 17′ and longitude 105° 43′ approximately. A group of little alpine lakes, that receive their waters directly from perpetual snow-banks, discharge into a common reservoir, known as Grand Lake, a beautiful sheet of water. Its quiet surface reflects towering cliffs and crags of granite on its eastern shore; and stately pines and firs stand on its western margin.

绿河发源于风河山脉弗里蒙特峰附近，约北纬 43°15′、西经 109°45′。与格朗德河一样，它也是一条被反复切割的河流，源头到汇口流程很长。

The Green River heads near Frémont’s Peak, in the Wind River Mountains, in latitude 43° 15′ and longitude 109° 45′, approximately. This river, like the last, has its sources in alpine lakes, fed by everlasting snows. Thousands of these little lakes, with deep, cold, emerald waters, are embosomed among the crags of the Rocky Mountains. These streams, born in the cold, gloomy solitudes of the upper mountain region, have a strange, eventful history as they pass down through gorges, tumbling in cascades and cataracts, until they reach the hot, arid plains of the Lower Colorado, where the waters that were so clear above empty as turbid floods into the Gulf of California.

科罗拉多河入海口位于北纬 31°53′、西经 115°——全河从高山雪原一直流向太平洋边的加利福尼亚湾。

The mouth of the Colorado is in latitude 31° 53′ and longitude 115°.

绿河比格朗德河大，是科罗拉多河的上游延续。算上绿河，整条河全长约两千英里，从落基山源头到入海，落差超过一万英尺。

The Green River is larger than the Grand, and is the upper continuation of the Colorado. Including this river, the whole length of the stream is about two thousand miles. The region of country drained by the Colorado and its tributaries is about eight hundred miles in length, and varies from three hundred to five hundred in width, containing about three hundred thousand square miles, an area larger than all the New England and Middle States, and Maryland and Virginia added, or as large as Minnesota, Wisconsin, Iowa, Illinois, and Missouri.

科罗拉多河流域分上下两段：下三分之一地势接近海平面，仅零星分布一些山岭；这段是沙漠与干谷。

There are two distinct portions of the basin of the Colorado. The lower third is but little above the level of the sea, though here and there ranges of mountains rise to an altitude of from two to six thousand feet. This part of the valley is bounded on the north by a line of cliffs, that present a bold, often vertical step, hundreds or thousands of feet to the table-lands above.

上三分之二的流域海拔在四千到八千英尺之间。这片高地东、北、西三面被高山环绕，河流在此积蓄力量，开始下切。

The upper two-thirds of the basin rises from four to eight thousand feet above the level of the sea. This high region, on the east, north, and west, is set with ranges of snow-clad mountains, attaining an altitude above the sea varying from eight to fourteen thousand feet. All winter long, on its mountain-crested rim, snow falls, filling the gorges, half burying the forests, and covering the crags and peaks with a mantle woven by the winds from the waves of the sea--a mantle of snow. When the summer-sun comes, this snow melts, and tumbles down the mountain-sides in millions of cascades. Ten million cascade brooks unite to form ten thousand torrent creeks; ten thousand torrent creeks unite to form a hundred rivers beset with cataracts; a hundred roaring rivers unite to form the Colorado, which rolls, a mad, turbid stream, into the Gulf of California.

设想一条支流的动作：源头是雪山融水，中段流过干旱平原。它不断下切河床，把河岸切成越来越深的峡谷——这是整章的地质核心论点：河流下切塑造峡谷。

Consider the action of one of these streams: its source in the mountains, where the snows fall; its course through the arid plains. Now, if at the river’s flood storms were falling on the plains, its channel would be cut but little faster than the adjacent country would be washed, and the general level would thus be preserved; but, under the conditions here mentioned, the river deepens its bed, as there is much through corrosion and but little lateral degradation.

于是所有支流都越切越深，直至两岸成为陡峭的岩壁。这些深而窄的峡谷，就是「cañon」。

So all the streams cut deeper and still deeper until their banks are towering cliffs of solid rock. These deep, narrow gorges are called cañons.

科罗拉多河沿程一千多英里都为自己切出了这样的峡谷，只有少数几条侧向支流汇入处稍宽。

For more than a thousand miles along its course, the Colorado has cut for itself such a cañon; but at some few points, where lateral streams join it, the cañon is broken, and narrow, transverse valleys divide it properly into a series of cañons.

西侧注入的支流有维尔京、卡纳布、帕里亚、埃斯卡兰特、恶鬼、圣拉斐尔、普赖斯、温塔等，东侧有格朗德、扬帕、圣胡安、科罗拉多奇基托等——它们都切出了各自的峡谷。

The Virgen, Kanab, Paria, Escalante, Dirty Devil, San Rafael, Price, and Uinta on the west, the Grand, Yampa, San Juan, and Colorado Chiquito on the east, have also cut for themselves such narrow, winding gorges, or deep cañons. Every river entering these has cut another cañon; every lateral creek has cut a cañon; every brook runs in a cañon; every rill born of a shower, and born again of a shower, and living only during these showers, has cut for itself a cañon; so that the whole upper portion of the basin of the Colorado is traversed by a labyrinth of these deep gorges.

由于地质条件千差万别，这些峡谷外观差异很大。例如维尔京河在长谷与摩门小镇之间的一段峡谷，岩性与别处迥异。

Owing to a great variety of geological conditions, these cañons differ much in general aspect. The Rio Virgen, between Long Valley and the Mormon town of Schunesburgh, runs through Pa-ru′-nu-weap Cañon, often not more than twenty or thirty feet in width, and from six hundred to one thousand five hundred feet deep.

北边，扬帕河注入绿河处有一段峡谷，1868 年秋我曾试图穿越，却日复一日受阻，直到……这体现了峡谷的险峻与多变。

Away to the north, the Yampa empties into the Green by a cañon that I essayed to cross in the fall of 1868, and was baffled from day to day until the fourth had nearly passed before I could find my way down to the river. But thirty miles above its mouth, this cañon ends, and a narrow valley, with a flood-plain, is found. Still farther up the stream, the river comes down through another cañon, and beyond that a narrow valley is found, and its upper course is now through a cañon and now a valley.

所有这些峡谷在地貌特征上都一样地多变、不可一概而论。

All these cañons are alike changeable in their topographic characteristics.

科罗拉多河上最长的峡谷，是科罗拉多奇基托河口到格兰德沃什之间的一段，长约两百多英里——这就是后来闻名的大峡谷主体。

The longest cañon through which the Colorado runs is that between the mouth of the Colorado Chiquito and the Grand Wash, a distance of two hundred and seventeen and a half miles. But this is separated from another above, sixty-five and a half miles in length, only by the narrow cañon-valley of the Colorado Chiquito.

这片峡谷区的一切景致都宏大、奇异而诡谲。河流在几乎不可到达的深处奔流，撞击着岩壁，发出轰鸣。

All the scenic features of this cañon land are on a giant scale, strange and weird. The streams run at depths almost inaccessible; lashing the rocks which beset their channels; rolling in rapids, and plunging in falls, and making a wild music which but adds to the gloom of the solitude.

沿河点缀的小河谷有柳树、槭树和小片三角叶杨林，是荒漠中的一线绿意。

The little valleys nestling along the streams are diversified by bordering willows, clumps of box-elder, and small groves of cottonwood.

低矮的方山（mesa）干燥无树，从谷缘向后延伸，常露出光滑的裸岩面。有些地方，台地表面平整如铺。

Low _mesas_, dry and treeless, stretch back from the brink of the cañon, often showing smooth surfaces of naked, solid rock. In some places, the country rock being composed of marls, the surface is a bed of loose, disintegrated material, and you walk through it as in a bed of ashes. Often these marls are richly colored and variegated. In other places, the country rock is a loose sandstone, the disintegration of which has left broad stretches of drifting sand, white, golden, and vermilion.

在砂岩砾岩处，地面留下一层彩色卵石拼成的「马赛克」，被流沙磨得发亮，在阳光下闪烁。

Where this sandstone is a conglomerate, a paving of pebbles has been left, a mosaic of many colors, polished by the drifting sands, and glistening in the sunlight.

除峡谷外，这一带最醒目的地貌是一道道长长的崖壁。它们是陡峭的悬崖（escarpment），往往高数百乃至数千英尺，层层如台阶。

After the cañons, the most remarkable features of the country are the long lines of cliffs. These are bold escarpments, often hundreds or thousands of feet in altitude, great geographic steps, scores or hundreds of miles in length, presenting steep faces of rock, often quite vertical.

爬上一级这样的台阶，又可沿缓坡——有时几乎察觉不到——降到下一级崖脚。如此呈现出一系列梯级地形。

Having climbed one of these steps, you may descend by a gentle, sometimes imperceptible, slope to the foot of another. They will thus present a series of terraces, the steps of which are well-defined escarpments of rock. The lateral extension of such a line of cliffs is usually very irregular; sharp salients are projected on the plains below, and deep recesses are cut into the terraces above.

间歇性的溪流从崖壁切出许多峡谷或谷道，旅行者可沿它们从下方平原登上台地。

Intermittent streams coming down the cliffs have cut many cañons or cañon valleys, by which the traveler may pass from the plain below to the terrace above. By these gigantic stairways, you may ascend to high plateaus, covered with forests of pine and fir.

这一带还点缀着短促的火山山岭。一个巨大的裂隙系统——深入岩层深处的裂缝——延伸开来，是近代火山活动的遗迹。

The region is further diversified by short ranges of eruptive mountains. A vast system of fissures--huge cracks in the rocks to the depths below--extends across the country. From these crevices, floods of lava have poured, covering _mesas_ and table-lands with sheets of black basalt. The expiring energies of these volcanic agencies have piled up huge cinder-cones, that stand along the fissures, red, brown, and black, naked of vegetation, and conspicuous landmarks, set, as they are, in contrast to the bright, variegated rocks of sedimentary origin.

这些峡谷、阻路的悬崖与荒漠，使外人长期无法深入此地。因此直到科罗拉多河探险之前，这里几乎不为外界所知，是真正的「空白之地」。

These cañon gorges, obstructing cliffs and desert wastes, have prevented the traveler from penetrating the country, so that, until the Colorado River Exploring Expedition was organized, it was almost unknown. Yet enough had been seen to foment rumor, and many wonderful stories have been told in the hunter’s cabin and prospector’s camp. Stories were related of parties entering the gorge in boats, and being carried down with fearful velocity into whirlpools, where all were overwhelmed in the abyss of waters; others, of underground passages for the great river, into which boats had passed never to be seen again. It was currently believed that the river was lost under the rocks for several hundred miles. There were other accounts of great falls, whose roaring music could be heard on the distant mountain-summits. There were many stories current of parties wandering on the brink of the cañon, vainly endeavoring to reach the waters below, and perishing with thirst at last in sight of the river which was roaring its mockery into dying ears.

印第安人也把峡谷的神秘织进了宗教神话：传说远古有位大智大勇的酋长，为族人的苦难而悲伤，于是……（下段接神话情节）。

The Indians, too, have woven the mysteries of the cañons into the myths of their religion. Long ago, there was a great and wise chief, who mourned the death of his wife, and would not be comforted until Ta-vwoats, one of the Indian gods, came to him, and told him she was in a happier land, and offered to take him there, that he might see for himself, if, upon his return, he would cease to mourn. The great chief promised. Then Ta-vwoats made a trail through the mountains that intervene between that beautiful land and this, the desert home of the poor Nu′-ma.

这条「路」就是科罗拉多峡谷。神明领他穿行其中；返回后，神明要酋长立誓，永不把这峡谷的秘密示人。

This trail was the cañon gorge of the Colorado. Through it he led him; and, when they had returned, the deity exacted from the chief a promise that he would tell no one of the joys of that land, lest, through discontent with the circumstances of this world, they should desire to go to heaven. Then he rolled a river into the gorge, a mad, raging stream, that should engulf any that might attempt to enter thereby.

我曾不止一次被印第安人警告不要进入峡谷。他们视之为对诸神的违抗、对其权威的轻慢。

More than once have I been warned by the Indians not to enter this cañon. They considered it disobedience to the gods and contempt for their authority, and believed that it would surely bring upon me their wrath.

探险之前两年，我一直在科罗拉多各峡谷上游做些地质研究，内心始终怀着顺流而下、探明这条河全程的渴望。

For two years previous to the exploration, I had been making some geological studies among the heads of the cañons leading to the Colorado, and a desire to explore the Grand Cañon itself grew upon me. Early in the spring of 1869, a small party was organized for this purpose. Boats were built in Chicago, and transported by rail to the point where the Union Pacific Railroad crosses the Green River. With these we were to descend the Green into the Colorado, and the Colorado down to the foot of the Grand Cañon.

CHAPTER II FROM GREEN RIVER CITY TO FLAMING GORGE

1869 年 5 月 24 日。绿河城的乡亲们出来送行。我们升起小旗，把船推离河岸，湍急的水流即刻把我们带向下游——探险正式启程。

May 24, 1869.--The good people of Green River City turn out to see us start. We raise our little flag, push the boats from shore, and the swift current carries us down.

我们的船共有四条：三条橡木造的，坚固牢实，双肋、双艏艉柱，还用舱壁加强，各分成三个隔舱。

Our boats are four in number. Three are built of oak; stanch and firm; double-ribbed, with double stem and stern posts, and further strengthened by bulkheads, dividing each into three compartments.

其中两条前后两端甲板封闭，形成水密舱；这样即使大浪打上船，也指望它们把船浮起来。小船长 21 英尺，卸下货物后四个人就能抬动。

Two of these, the fore and aft, are decked, forming water-tight cabins. It is expected these will buoy the boats should the waves roll over them in rough water. The little vessels are twenty-one feet long, and, taking out the cargoes, can be carried by four men.

第四条船用松木造，很轻，长 16 英尺，船头尖利，专为快划而设，也像其他船一样分舱。

The fourth boat is made of pine, very light, but sixteen feet in length, with a sharp cut-water, and every way built for fast rowing, and divided into compartments as the others.

我们带了足够十个月的干粮——因为预计冬天河水结冰时，要在某处上岸越冬，等春天再走；所以衣物也带得充足。还带了大量弹药和两三打捕兽夹；为盖小屋、修船等备了斧、锤、锯、钻和一批钉子螺丝；科学仪器有六分仪两台、天文钟四台、气压计、温度计、罗盘等一批。

We take with us rations deemed sufficient to last ten months; for we expect, when winter comes on and the river is filled with ice, to lie over at some point until spring arrives; so we take with us abundant supplies of clothing. We have also a large quantity of ammunition and two or three dozen traps. For the purpose of building cabins, repairing boats, and meeting other exigencies, we are supplied with axes, hammers, saws, augers, and other tools, and a quantity of nails and screws. For scientific work, we have two sextants, four chronometers, a number of barometers, thermometers, compasses, and other instruments.

面粉分三等份，肉和其他干粮同样分法。每条大船配斧、锤、锯、钻等工具，使各船载重均匀。这样分摊是为防万一某条船沉没，别的船上仍不缺关键物资。小船则装部分仪器、三支枪和三小捆衣物。我乘小船先行，探测航道。

The flour is divided into three equal parts; the meat and all other articles of our rations in the same way. Each of the larger boats has an ax, hammer, saw, auger, and other tools, so that all are loaded alike. We distribute the cargoes in this way, that we may not be entirely destitute of some important article should any one of the boats be lost. In the small boat, we pack a part of the scientific instruments, three guns, and three small bundles of clothing only. In this, I proceed in advance, to explore the channel.

J. C. 萨姆纳和威廉·H. 邓恩是我的「爱玛·迪恩」号船工；随后是「克莱德姐妹」号，由 W. H. 鲍威尔和 G. Y. 布拉德利驾驶；接着是「无名」号，载 O. G. 豪兰、塞内卡·豪兰和弗兰克·古德曼；最后是「峡谷少女」号，载 W. R. 霍金斯和安德鲁·霍尔。

J. C. Sumner and William H. Dunn are my boatmen in the _Emma Dean_;[1] then follows _Kitty Clyde’s Sister_, manned by W. H. Powell[2] and G. Y. Bradley; next, the _No Name_, with O. G. Howland, Seneca Howland, and Frank Goodman; and last comes the _Maid of the Cañon_, with W. R. Hawkins and Andrew Hall.

船都载得很重，在这条颠簸的河上要极其小心才能不进水。

Our boats are heavily loaded, and only with the utmost care is it possible to float in the rough river without shipping water.

出城一两英里，我们搁浅在沙洲上。大家跳进水里，减轻船的负载，让船漂过去，继续前进。为避开一块岩石，一条船折断了一支桨，船带着伤撞上去，被急流甩得歪歪斜斜冲进漩涡；混乱中又有两支桨落水，队员们一时手足无措，惹得其他人哈哈大笑。

A mile or two below town, we run on a sand-bar. The men jump into the stream, and thus lighten the vessels, so that they drift over; and on we go. In trying to avoid a rock, an oar is broken on one of the boats, and, thus crippled, she strikes. The current is swift, and she is sent reeling and rocking into the eddy. In the confusion, two others are lost overboard and the men seem quite discomfited, much to the amusement of the other members of the party.

捞回桨重新上路，船队再次顺流而下，直到在一小片三角叶杨林边靠岸，就地午餐。

Catching the oars and starting again, the boats are once more borne down the stream until we land at a small cottonwood grove on the bank, and camp for noon.

下午我们驶到一处河流贴着陡崖脚下流过的地方，在此扎营过夜。太阳还高，我便攀上崖壁，走进绿河荒芜之地那些形状奇特的岩石间。那是一片砂岩与页岩，灰、黄、红、棕、蓝、黑层层交替，几乎水平，几乎没有土壤和植被。岩质很脆，雨和溪流把它们刻成古怪的形状。眼前是荒凉的焦土，但自有一种美——那奇异的雕刻仿佛建筑形式，又像粗犷怪诞的雕像，配着岩石明丽的色彩，是生长在青山中的人难以体会的景象。

During the afternoon, we run down to a point where the river sweeps the foot of an overhanging cliff, and here we camp for the night. The sun is yet two hours high, so I climb the cliffs, and walk back among the strangely carved rocks of the Green River bad-lands. These are sandstones and shales, gray and buff, red and brown, blue and black strata in many alternations, lying nearly horizontal, and almost without soil and vegetation. They are very friable, and the rain and streams have carved them into quaint shapes. Barren desolation is stretched before me; and yet there is a beauty in the scene. The fantastic carving, imitating architectural forms, and suggesting rude but weird statuary, with the bright and varied colors of the rocks, conspire to make a scene such as the dweller in verdure-clad hills can scarcely appreciate.

站在高处，我可以向四面眺望广袤的大地，嶙峋的岩石和崖壁在夕阳下闪光。阴影正沉入谷底和沟壑，明暗的魔力让高处更高、深处更深。

Standing on a high point, I can look off in every direction over a vast landscape, with salient rocks and cliffs glittering in the evening sun. Dark shadows are settling in the valleys and gulches, and the heights are made higher and the depths deeper by the glamour and witchery of light and shade.

南边，温塔山脉横亘成一线，高峰刺入天空，雪原像熔化银子的湖泊闪闪发光；松林是沉郁的绿，玫瑰色的云绕着大片黑色山体的边缘嬉戏；高天、云霞、群山、雪原、森林、石地，融成一幅壮丽全景。现在太阳落山了，我回到营地。

Away to the south, the Uinta Mountains stretch in a long line; high peaks thrust into the sky, and snow-fields glittering like lakes of molten silver; and pine-forests in somber green; and rosy clouds playing around the borders of huge, black masses; and heights and clouds, and mountains and snow-fields, and forests and rock-lands, are blended into one grand view. Now the sun goes down, and I return to camp.

5 月 25 日。今天一早就出发，一路顺流，直到九点左右在一道砾石坝上搁浅。全体跳下去，硬把船抬过坝。随后下起雨来，河与云合伙把我们浇了个透。湿透、发冷、精疲力竭，我们在岸边一片三角叶杨林停下，生起大火，煮了杯咖啡，很快就恢复了精神，还颇为快活。等云「让出太阳」，我们又出发。往下几英里，见右边崖上一群盘羊。船悄悄靠岸，三四人去追猎，两三小时后回来——厨子成功打回一只肥羊羔，没打中的猎人讥笑他是捡到死羊，但很快它就收拾干净上了烤架，成了一顿丰盛的四点晚餐。

_May 25._--We start early this morning, and run along at a good rate until about nine o’clock, when we are brought up on a gravelly bar. All jump out, and help the boats over by main strength. Then a rain comes on, and river and clouds conspire to give us a thorough drenching. Wet, chilled, and tired to exhaustion, we stop at a cottonwood grove on the bank, build a huge fire, make a cup of coffee, and are soon refreshed and quite merry. When the clouds “get out of our sunshine,” we start again. A few miles farther down, a flock of mountain-sheep are seen on a cliff to the right. The boats are quietly tied up, and three or four men go after them. In the course of two or three hours, they return. The cook has been successful in bringing down a fat lamb. The unsuccessful hunters taunt him with finding it dead; but it is soon dressed, cooked, and eaten, making a fine four o’clock dinner.

“全员上船”，又顺流跑了十几英里。途中经过黑福克河口，一条浑黄的小河，似乎涨了水。就在它汇入口下方，我们上岸扎营。

“All aboard,” and down the river for another dozen miles. On the way, we pass the mouth of Black’s Fork, a dirty little stream that seems somewhat swollen. Just below its mouth, we land and camp.

5 月 26 日。今天我们经过几座形状奇特的土丘，立于河西岸与远处高崖之间。这些土丘是崖面露出岩层的外围残体：薄层状页岩和砂岩，颜色多样，上部是陡壁，下部是流水蚀成的坡积。有的比河面高出近千英尺。

_May 26._--To-day, we pass several curiously-shaped buttes, standing between the west bank of the river and the high bluffs beyond. These buttes are outliers of the same beds of rocks exposed on the faces of the bluffs; thinly laminated shales and sandstones of many colors, standing above in vertical cliffs, and buttressed below with a water-carved talus; some of them attain an altitude of nearly a thousand feet above the level of the river.

我们沿着平缓的河流静静滑行，驶过「恶地」那些被雕刻的崖壁，偶尔瞥见远山。时而有鹿从柳丛间的空地惊起；几只野雁在水里追了一阵后被射中。

We glide quietly down the placid stream past the carved cliffs of the _mauvaises terres_, now and then obtaining glimpses of distant mountains. Occasionally, deer are started from the glades among the willows; and several wild geese, after a chase through the water, are shot.

午饭后穿过一条短而窄的峡谷，进入一个开阔的河谷；两侧长长的侧谷向远处延伸到目光尽头。

After dinner, we pass through a short, narrow cañon into a broad valley; from this, long, lateral valleys stretch back on either side as far as the eye can reach.

再往下两三英里，亨利福克河从右侧注入。我们在汇口上方不远处靠岸——那里几个月前曾在崖脚一个洞穴里埋藏过一批仪器和干粮，离河有一段距离。那位置能躲风雨和野兽，却躲不过人。我们有些担心，因为听说一队印第安人在附近扎营好几周了。好在虚惊一场，东西原封未动：天文钟的齿轮没被拿去当发饰，气压计的管子没被当珠子，六分仪也没被当成「坏药」扔进河里——正如有人预言的那样。

Two or three miles below, Henry’s Fork enters from the right. We land a short distance above the junction, where a _cache_ of instruments and rations was made several months ago, in a cave at the foot of the cliff, a distance back from the river. Here it was safe from the elements and wild beasts, but not from man. Some anxiety is felt, as we have learned that a party of Indians have been camped near it for several weeks. Our fears are soon allayed, for we find it all right. Our chronometer wheels are not taken for hair ornaments; our barometer tubes, for beads; nor the sextant thrown into the river as “bad medicine,” as had been predicted.

取回储备，我们来到温塔山脚下，顶着寒冷的暴风雨扎营。

Taking up our _cache_, we pass down to the foot of the Uinta Mountains, and, in a cold storm, go into camp.

河水向南流，山脉却横亘东西，正好拦在河的去路上。可它依旧静静地滑过，仿佛觉得一条山脉算不上什么障碍。它从一道开阔、明艳的红色峡谷口进入山体，这道红口子从北面二十英里外就能望见。

The river is running to the south; the mountains have an easterly and westerly trend directly athwart its course, yet it glides on in a quiet way as if it thought a mountain range no formidable obstruction to its course. It enters the range by a flaring, brilliant, red gorge, that may be seen from the north a score of miles away.

切出这道峡谷的山脊主体是鲜朱红色的岩石，上面覆着宽宽的一道道斑驳的浅黄和灰色岩带，这些岩带从山脚一侧以缓和的弧线一直降到水边。

The great mass of the mountain-ridge through which the gorge is cut is composed of bright vermilion rocks; but they are surmounted by broad bands of mottled buff and gray, and these bands come down with a gentle curve to the water’s edge on the nearer slope of the mountain.

这就是我们即将探索的第一道峡谷的入口——是这条河切穿这片山岭的一系列峡谷中的第一道。我们给它取名「烈焰峡谷」（Flaming Gorge）。实测崖壁高约一千二百英尺。

This is the head of the first cañon we are about to explore--an introductory one to a series made by the river through this range. We name it Flaming Gorge. The cliffs or walls we find, on measurement, to be about one thousand two hundred feet high.

5 月 27 日。今天下雨，我们趁隙修一台从纽约来时摔坏的气压计：得换一根新管——把一根长玻璃管里灌进水银，每次四五英寸，逐段用酒精灯煮沸。这活儿稍不留神就会弄断玻璃，但我们成功了，又可以重新测量山峰高度了。

_May 27._--To-day it rains, and we employ the time in repairing one of our barometers, which was broken on the way from New York. A new tube has to be put in; that is, a long glass tube has to be filled with mercury four or five inches at a time, and each installment boiled over a spirit-lamp. It is a delicate task to do this without breaking the glass; but we have success, and are ready to measure the mountains once more.

5 月 28 日。今天登上左侧崖顶做高程观测，其余时间忙于地形和地质工作。

_May 28._--To-day we go to the summit of the cliff on the left and take observations for altitude, and are variously employed in topographic and geological work.

5 月 29 日。今晨我和布拉德利渡过河，爬升一千多英尺，到了一处能看见河水在下方峡谷中划出长长美弧的位置。转身向西，能看到亨利福克河的河谷，那条小河在谷中蜿蜒流过多英里，两岸散布着三角叶杨林，林间是草场。窄窄的山谷两侧是色彩明丽的光裸岩壁。谷南是温塔山，西边极远处隐约可见瓦萨奇山的峰影。北边是沙漠平原，散布着形状奇特的丘陵和土丘，一直延伸到视野尽头。

_May 29._--This morning, Bradley and I cross the river, and climb more than a thousand feet to a point where we can see the stream sweeping in a long, beautiful curve through the gorge below. Turning and looking to the west, we can see the valley of Henry’s Fork, through which, for many miles, the little river flows in a tortuous channel. Cottonwood groves are planted here and there along its course, and between them are stretches of grass land. The narrow mountain valley is inclosed on either side by sloping walls of naked rock of many bright colors. To the south of the valley are the Uintas, and the peaks of the Wasatch Mountains can be faintly seen in the far west. To the north, desert plains, dotted here and there with curiously carved hills and buttes, extend to the limit of vision.

许多年来，这条山谷是一群山民的家，他们原是以打猎、设阱为生、与印第安人同住的猎人。多数人有一两个印第安妻子。他们不再随游牧部落追逐鹿皮和海狸，而是攒下了成群的牛羊，自认相当殷实。有人盖了木屋，有人仍住帐篷。

For many years, this valley has been the home of a number of mountaineers, who were originally hunters and trappers, living with the Indians. Most of them have one or more Indian wives. They no longer roam with the nomadic tribes in pursuit of buckskin or beaver, but have accumulated herds of cattle and horses, and consider themselves quite well-to-do. Some of them have built cabins; others still live in lodges.

约翰·贝克是这些人中最出名的之一；从我们站的地方，能看见他的帐篷在河上游三四英里处。

John Baker is one of the most famous of these men; and, from our point of view, we can see his lodge three or four miles up the river.

从绿河城到烈焰峡谷共六十二英里。河水在两岸高崖间奔流，有的地方两岸几乎相贴，没有河漫滩——这种地方才算得上真正的峡谷。两侧的恶地间或点缀着一丛丛山艾。凡有河漫滩的地方，就能看见几棵三角叶杨。

The distance from Green River City to Flaming Gorge is sixty-two miles. The river runs between bluffs, in some places standing so close to each other that no flood-plain is seen. At such a point, the river might properly be said to run through a cañon. The bad-lands on either side are interrupted here and there by patches of _Artemesia_, or sage-brush. Where there is a flood-plain along either side of the river, a few cottonwoods may be seen.

（脚注1：爱玛·迪恩是鲍威尔夫人的闺名。）

[1] Mrs. Powell’s maiden name. (_Ed._)

（脚注2：W. H. 鲍威尔即少校最小的弟弟沃尔特·鲍威尔上尉。除两位鲍威尔外，萨姆纳、布拉德利和霍金斯都是退伍军人。）

[2] Capt. Walter Powell, the Major’s youngest brother. Besides the two Powells, Sumner, Bradley, and Hawkins were ex-soldiers. (_Ed._)

CHAPTER III FROM FLAMING GORGE TO THE GATE OF LODORE

本章开篇先给读者一个认识山系的方法：不要把它想成平原上的一列山峰，而应看成宽达许多英里的一座大台地，山是由水流从台地上雕刻出来的。还要想象这块台地被沟壑峡谷纵横切割，不同海拔散布着秀丽的河谷。我们要探的第一批峡谷，就是这条河穿过这样一条山系时切出的水道。峡谷先切进山系近一半深，然后转向东，沿山系中线（轴部）切行，渐渐越过轴线偏向南；这样走了五十多英里后，又陡然转向西南，斜穿山系南坡。

You must not think of a mountain-range as a line of peaks standing on a plain, but as a broad platform many miles wide, from which mountains have been carved by the waters. You must conceive, too, that this plateau is cut by gulches and cañons in many directions, and that beautiful valleys are scattered about at different altitudes. The first series of cañons we are about to explore constitutes a river channel through such a range of mountains. The cañon is cut nearly half-way through the range, then turns to the east, and is cut along the central line, or axis, gradually crossing it to the south. Keeping this direction for more than fifty miles, it then turns abruptly to a southwest course, and goes diagonally through the southern slope of the range.

这些我们进峡之前就大致知道——去年秋天我们曾对这一带做过部分勘查，爬上许多山峰，有几处到了峡壁边缘，从几百英尺高的悬崖俯瞰下面的河水。

This much we knew before entering, as we made a partial exploration of the region last fall, climbing many of its peaks, and in a few places reaching the brink of the cañon walls, and looking over the precipices, many hundreds of feet high, to the water below.

峡壁间或有侧向峡谷——小河注入大河的水道——打破。有三两条这样的侧谷，我们去年初冬曾顺它们下到绿河边，沿崖脚的枯水滩走了好几英里。在河呈东西走向的这一段，只有西半部切出了峡谷，东半部则形成一条宽阔河谷，为纪念一位老猎人而名「布朗公园」，长期以来是山民和印第安人喜爱的越冬地。

Here and there the walls are broken by lateral cañons, the channels of little streams entering the river; through two or three of these, we found our way down to the Green in early winter, and walked along the low water-beach at the foot of the cliffs for several miles. Where the river has this general easterly direction, the western part only has cut for itself a cañon, while the eastern has formed a broad valley, called, in honor of an old-time trapper, Brown’s Park, and long known as a favorite winter resort for mountain men and Indians.

5 月 30 日。今晨我们准备进入这神秘的峡谷，怀着几分不安启程。老山民说这峡谷跑不了船；印第安人则说「水会逮住人」，但大家都急着要试一试，出发了。

_May 30._--This morning we are ready to enter the mysterious cañon, and start with some anxiety. The old mountaineers tell us that it cannot be run; the Indians say, “Water heap catch ’em,” but all are eager for the trial, and off we go.

进入烈焰峡谷，我们借着湍急的水流很快穿了过去，来到一小片公园地。半英里下方，河猛转向左，我们拐进又一条切入山体的峡谷。进入狭窄水道，两边崖壁迅速升高。左边是悬垂的岩架和峭壁，高五百、一千、一千五百英尺。

Entering Flaming Gorge, we quickly run through it on a swift current, and emerge into a little park. Half a mile below, the river wheels sharply to the left, and we turn into another cañon cut into the mountain. We enter the narrow passage. On either side, the walls rapidly increase in altitude. On the left are overhanging ledges and cliffs five hundred--a thousand--fifteen hundred feet high.

右边岩石破碎嶙峋，水从这壁到那壁灌满河道。忽然河又绕一个岬角向右急转，水猛然冲下，落在巨大的岩石间——我们在这里尝到了峡谷激流的第一课。我站在船甲板上，在浪打礁石之间寻找通路。对这样的水我们全无经验，每一刻都充满紧张。船很快进入急流，这边划一两桨，那边划一两桨，我们以令人兴奋的速度穿过窄道，冲上高浪——浪花飞溅过顶——又扎进浪谷，直到到达下方平静的水面；这时才大大松了一口气。我们的第一段激流闯过了。又过一英里，重新进入河谷。

On the right, the rocks are broken and ragged, and the water fills the channel from cliff to cliff. Now the river turns abruptly around a point to the right, and the waters plunge swiftly down among great rocks; and here we have our first experience with cañon rapids. I stand up on the deck of my boat to seek a way among the wave beaten rocks. All untried as we are with such waters, the moments are filled with intense anxiety. Soon our boats reach the swift current; a stroke or two, now on this side, now on that, and we thread the narrow passage with exhilarating velocity, mounting the high waves, whose foaming crests dash over us, and plunging into the troughs, until we reach the quiet water below; and then comes a feeling of great relief. Our first rapid is run. Another mile, and we come into the valley again.

让我解释这道峡谷：河在上游向左转，直接向山里切，直抵山心，然后折返，从离入峡点仅半英里下游的地方流回它出发的河谷。所以峡谷呈拉长的 U 字形，尖端在山体中央。我们给它取名「马蹄峡谷」。

Let me explain this cañon. Where the river turns to the left above, it takes a course directly into the mountain, penetrating to its very heart, then wheels back upon itself, and runs out into the valley from which it started only half a mile below the point at which it entered; so the cañon is in the form of an elongated letter U, with the apex in the center of the mountain. We name it Horseshoe Cañon.

我们很快离开这河谷，进入另一条短峡谷，起初很窄，越往下越宽，峡壁也越高。在这里我们发现了美丽的小溪口，溪水从它窄窄的冲刷裂谷中流下。溪口处有一片两三百英亩的公园地，四周被几乎垂直的崖壁围住，崖高数百英尺，壁上开着三个「门」：一个朝上游，一个朝下游，第三个是小溪穿进来的通道。河水宽阔、深而平静，水面倒映着高耸的岩石。

Soon we leave the valley, and enter another short cañon, very narrow at first, but widening below as the cañon walls increase in height. Here we discover the mouth of a beautiful little creek, coming down through its narrow water worn cleft. Just at its entrance there is a park of two or three hundred acres, walled on every side by almost vertical cliffs, hundreds of feet in altitude, with three gateways through the walls--one up, another down the river, and a third passage through which the creek comes in. The river is broad, deep, and quiet, and its waters mirror towering rocks.

翠鸟在溪边嬉戏，于是我们给这溪、这公园、这峡谷都取名「翠鸟」。夜里，我们在这峡谷脚下扎营。

Kingfishers are playing about the streams, and so we adopt as names Kingfisher Creek, Kingfisher Park, and Kingfisher Cañon. At night, we camp at the foot of this cañon.

今天我们总的方向是南，但在这里河向东绕过一个圆丘状的岬角。岬角侧面被水流凿出许多小洞，壁上密密麻麻布满这种坑，几百只燕子在里面筑巢。它们在崖间飞掠，像成群的蜜蜂，让整个岬角看起来像一座老式的大蜂房。于是我们叫它「蜂房岬」。

Our general course this day has been south, but here the river turns to the east around a point which is rounded to the shape of a dome, and on its sides little cells have been carved by the action of the water; and in these pits, which cover the face of the dome, hundreds of swallows have built their nests. As they flit about the cliffs, they look like swarms of bees, giving to the whole the appearance of a colossal beehive of the old time form, and so we name it Beehive Point.

对面的崖壁是一座巨大的圆形剧场，一级级台阶层层升起，高达 1200 到 1500 英尺。每级台阶都是红砂岩，正面是光裸的红岩，斜面覆盖着绿植。所以这剧场看起来红绿相间，夕阳在岩石上投下玫瑰色的光，翠柏的枝条上闪着绿莹莹的光，跳荡的波浪上泛着虹彩。整片景观沐浴在阳光里。

The opposite wall is a vast amphitheater, rising in a succession of terraces to a height of 1,200 or 1,500 feet. Each step is built of red sandstone, with a face of naked, red rock, and a glacis clothed with verdure. So the amphitheater seems banded red and green, and the evening sun is playing with roseate flashes on the rocks, with shimmering green on the cedars’ spray, and iridescent gleams on the dancing waves. The landscape revels in the sunshine.

5 月 31 日。我们沿另一条峡谷下行，遇到被河中高礁石弄险的激流；于是靠岸，用绳索把船放下去。下午又遇更险的激流，停下来查看。我发现还得干同样的活儿，但我们在河的错误一侧，找不到立足点，得先过河——在这等激流里，下游又有礁石，过河可不容易。我们把先锋船「爱玛·迪恩」号先送过去，卸在岸上；它返回再装一批。来回几趟，很快把一半货物运过去；然后一条大船满载渡河，尽管拼命划，还是几乎被冲到礁石上。其余船跟上靠岸，我们扎营过夜。

_May 31._--We start down another cañon, and reach rapids made dangerous by high rocks lying in the channel; so we run ashore, and let our boats down with lines. In the afternoon we come to more dangerous rapids, and stop to examine them. I find we must do the same work again, but, being on the wrong side of the river to obtain a foothold, must first cross over--no very easy matter in such a current, with rapids and rocks below. We take the pioneer boat _Emma Dean_ over, and unload her on the bank; then she returns and takes another load. Running back and forth, she soon has half our cargo over; then one of the larger boats is manned and taken across, but carried down almost to the rocks in spite of hard rowing. The other boats follow and make the landing, and we go into camp for the night.

这边崖脚下有一条长长的松林坡，我们在树下铺床，日落后不久就躺下歇息。两侧崖壁都是红砂岩，向上耸入天空两千五百英尺。这边，长长的松林坡顶上是垂直的崖壁，崖顶也长着松树。对岸崖壁从水边起 2000 英尺都是光裸的岩石，再往后倾斜，让松树和柏树有了立足之地。

At the foot of the cliff on this side, there is a long slope covered with pines; under these we make our beds, and soon after sunset are seeking rest and sleep. The cliffs on either side are of red sandstone, and stretch up toward the heavens 2,500 feet. On this side, the long, pine clad slope is surmounted by perpendicular cliffs, with pines on their summits. The wall on the other side is bare rock from the water’s edge up 2,000 feet, then slopes back, giving footing to pines and cedars.

暮色渐深，岩石变得黑暗阴沉；河水那威胁性的轰鸣又响又不断。我躺着想明天和将要到来的峡谷，间或被风景的某些特征吸引。在这里我有了一个发现：正对着一座山看时，坡的陡峭程度被大大夸大，而到山顶的距离和真实高度则相应被缩小。我此前就发现，要正确判断山坡的坡度，得从侧面（轮廓）看它。今天下午顺河下来时，我观察了某段崖壁的坡并估了高度。吃晚饭时从正面看同一面崖，它显得更陡，却不到一半高。现在侧躺着看，真实比例显现了。这真是个奇观，我站起来看——还是晚饭时那面崖。再躺下，它又成了侧面轮廓的样子：长坡、远峰。我琢磨着这个，忘了「明天和将要到来的峡谷」。我找到一种方法，能像判断地平线距离那样估计高度和坡度。原因很简单——参考立体镜就想通了：双眼的间距构成了光学三角测量的基线。

As the twilight deepens, the rocks grow dark and somber; the threatening roar of the water is loud and constant, and I lie awake with thoughts of the morrow and the cañons to come, interrupted now and then by characteristics of the scenery that attract my attention. And here I make a discovery. On looking at the mountain directly in front, the steepness of the slope is greatly exaggerated, while the distance to its summit and its true altitude are correspondingly diminished. I have heretofore found that to properly judge of the slope of a mountain side, you must see it in profile. In coming down the river this afternoon, I observed the slope of a particular part of the wall, and made an estimate of its altitude. While at supper, I noticed the same cliff from a position facing it, and it seemed steeper, but not half as high. Now lying on my side and looking at it, the true proportions appear. This seems a wonder, and I rise up to take a view of it standing. It is the same cliff as at supper time. Lying down again, it is the cliff as seen in profile, with a long slope and distant summit. Musing on this, I forget “the morrow and the cañons to come.” I find a way to estimate the altitude and slope of an inclination as I can judge of distance along the horizon. The reason is simple. A reference to the stereoscope will suggest it. The distance between the eyes forms a base-line for optical triangulation.

6 月 1 日。今天我们有了一段刺激的旅程。河在峡谷里以惊人的速度奔流，没有礁石阻挡时，我们几乎是铁路速度。水流不时涌进窄峡，侧壁把水卷成大浪推向河心，船在这些浪上像活物一样腾跃。这让我想起在中公园见过的景象：受惊的鹿群在遍布倒木的林中奔跳。我跟几位猎人提起这种相似，他们觉得太像了，于是「看黑尾鹿跳木头」成了常说的话。有时浪打上船，得不停地舀水，只好不时停下。有一阵我们一小时跑十二英里，把停船时间都算进去了。

_June 1._--To-day we have an exciting ride. The river rolls down the cañon at a wonderful rate, and, with no rocks in the way, we make almost railroad speed. Here and there the water rushes into a narrow gorge; the rocks on the side roll it into the center in great waves, and the boats go leaping and bounding over these like things of life. They remind me of scenes witnessed in Middle Park; herds of startled deer bounding through forests beset with fallen timber. I mention the resemblance to some of the hunters, and so striking is it that it comes to be a common expression, “See the black-tails jumping the logs.” At times the waves break and roll over the boats, which necessitates much bailing, and obliges us to stop occasionally for that purpose. At one time, we run twelve miles an hour, stoppages included.

去年春天，我和一位叫帕里阿茨的老印第安人谈过话。他讲起他部族里有人试过闯这条峡谷：「石头，」他双手高举过头，两臂竖直，从两臂间望向天空，「石头高——高——高；水呼——呜——呜地冲；水马（船）使劲尥蹶子；水逮住它；再也看不见那个印第安人了！看不见他的女人了！看不见他的娃了！」

Last spring, I had a conversation with an old Indian named Pa′-ri-ats, who told me about one of his tribe attempting to run this cañon. “The rocks,” he said, holding his hands above his head, his arms vertical, and looking between them to the heavens, “the rocks h-e-a-p, h-e-a-p high; the water go h-oo-woogh, h-oo-woogh; water-pony (boat) h-e-a-p buck; water catch ’em; no see ’em Injun any more! no see ’em squaw any more! no see ’em pappoose any more!”

凡见过那些野性印第安矮马前后尥蹶子、俗称「bucking」的人，都能体会他的描述。

Those who have seen these wild Indian ponies rearing alternately before and behind, or “bucking,” as it is called in the vernacular, will appreciate his description.

终于我们到了平静的水面，远处传来威胁性的轰鸣。慢慢靠近声源，我们来到瀑布前，在瀑布上方左侧停船。这里必须抬船过陆（portage）；于是卸下货物，在小船船头和船尾各系一根长绳，把它牢牢泊在瀑布边缘。船头绳送到下游系牢；船尾绳由五六个人拽着，在激流中尽量多放一会儿；然后松开绳的一端，让它从环中滑过；船跃过瀑布，被下方绳索接住。

At last we come to calm water, and a threatening roar is heard in the distance. Slowly approaching the point whence the sound issues, we come near to falls, and tie up just above them on the left. Here we will be compelled to make a portage; so we unload the boats, and fasten a long line to the bow, and another to the stern, of the smaller one, and moor her close to the brink of the fall. Then the bow-line is taken below, and made fast; the stern-line is held by five or six men, and the boat let down as long as they can hold her against the rushing waters; then, letting go one end of the line, it runs through the ring; the boat leaps over the fall, and is caught by the lower rope.

现在我们歇夜。

Now we rest for the night.

6 月 2 日。今晨我们在岩石间开出一条小路，把货物运到瀑布下游，把剩下的船放下去，中午前就准备就绪。

_June 2._--This morning we make a trail among the rocks, transport the cargoes to a point below the falls, let the remaining boats over, and are ready to start before noon.

在小路经过的一块高岩上，我们发现刻字：「Ashley 18-5」。第三个数字模糊不清——有人读作 1835，有人读作 1855。

On a high rock by which the trail passes we find the inscription: “Ashley 18-5.” The third figure is obscure--some of the party reading it 1835, some 1855.[3]

老山民詹姆斯·贝克曾跟我讲过一队人顺河而下的事，其中提到阿什利。据说船在下方某条峡谷里翻了，一些人淹死。「阿什利」这名字是个警告，我们下定决心加倍小心。

James Baker, an old time mountaineer, once told me about a party of men starting down the river, and Ashley was named as one. The story runs that the boat was swamped, and some of the party drowned in one of the cañons below. The word “Ashley” is a warning to us, and we resolve on great caution.

这道瀑布，我们命名「阿什利瀑布」。

Ashley Falls is the name we give to the cataract.

河在这里很窄；右壁垂直两三百英尺，左壁高耸，崖脚与水面之间堆着大堆破碎岩石。一些从上方岩架崩落的岩石滚进河道，造成了这瀑布。一块三四十英尺高的巨大立方岩块立在河心，水分向两侧，下跌约十二英尺，又被小礁石撞成下方的一段急流。瀑布正下方，水占满整个河道，崖脚没有坡积物。

The river is very narrow; the right wall vertical for two or three hundred feet, the left towering to a great height, with a vast pile of broken rocks lying between the foot of the cliff and the water. Some of the rocks broken down from the ledge above have tumbled into the channel and caused this fall. One great cubical block, thirty or forty feet high, stands in the middle of the stream, and the waters, parting to either side, plunge down about twelve feet, and are broken again by the smaller rocks into a rapid below. Immediately below the falls, the water occupies the entire channel, there being no talus at the foot of the cliffs.

我们上船，往下跑了一段，找到一处午饭的靠岸点。

We embark, and run down a short distance, where we find a landing-place for dinner.

整个下午又在浪上。在这条我们取名「红峡谷」的峡谷下端，有一小片公园地，溪流从远处山巅下来，从两侧注入大河；我们在这里两棵挺拔的松树下扎营过夜。

On the waves again all the afternoon. Near the lower end of this cañon, to which we have given the name Red Cañon, is a little park, where streams come down from distant mountain summits, and enter the river on either side; and here we camp for the night under two stately pines.

6 月 3 日。今晨我们把干粮、衣物等摊在地上晒，几个人出去打猎。我走了五六英里，到一片松林公园地：草毯上缀着深红的天鹅绒般的花，成丛长在梨形仙人掌的茎上；这里一丛那里一簇的「画杯花」，红色苞片间探出黄花；蓝色的小花从草里探头；空气里满是白花绣线菊的芬芳。一条山溪穿园而过，下游被河狸坝拦成水塘。这是个远离峡谷喧嚣激流、安静隐退的好地方。

_June 3._--This morning we spread our rations, clothes, &c., on the ground to dry, and several of the party go out for a hunt. I take a walk of five or six miles up to a pine grove park, its grassy carpet bedecked with crimson, velvet flowers, set in groups on the stems of pear shaped cactus plants; patches of painted cups are seen here and there, with yellow blossoms protruding through scarlet bracts; little blue-eyed flowers are peeping through the grass; and the air is filled with fragrance from the white blossoms of a _Spiræa_. A mountain brook runs through the midst, ponded below by beaver dams. It is a quiet place for retirement from the raging waters of the cañon.

还记得从烈焰峡谷到蜂房岬这段，河是南向的，与温塔山脉垂直，切进山体直到距山脊线五英里处，然后转向东，沿一条与山脉走向不完全平行、略偏南东的路线缓缓斜穿轴线。因此在河与山轴之间有一块三角形地带，尖角朝东。我登上一座山俯瞰这片地区。东边山峰不高，积雪大多已融化，只在岩架背风处残留几小片。西边山峰渐高，雪原也更大。在峡谷边缘与这些山峰脚下之间有一道高台地。好几条溪流发源于南边的雪原，向北流进峡谷，在五六英里的距离内跌落三千到五千英尺。上游他们流过草场河谷；但接近红峡谷时，迅速没入地表之下，在下方峡谷中从自己深黑的峡谷里重新涌出。每条这样的短侧谷都以一连串瀑布和一片石、树、倒木、茂密灌丛的狂乱混杂为标志。

It will be remembered that the course of the river, from Flaming Gorge to Beehive Point, is in a southerly direction, and at right angles to the Uinta Mountains, and cuts into the range until it reaches a point within five miles of the crest, where it turns to the east, and pursues a course not quite parallel to the trend of the range, but crosses the axis slowly in a direction a little south of east. Thus there is a triangular tract between the river and the axis of the mountain, with its acute angle extending eastward. I climb a mountain overlooking this country. To the east, the peaks are not very high, and already most of the snow has melted; but little patches lie here and there under the lee of ledges of rock. To the west, the peaks grow higher and the snow fields larger. Between the brink of the cañon and the foot of these peaks, there is a high bench. A number of creeks have their sources in the snow banks to the south, and run north into the cañon, tumbling down from 3,000 to 5,000 feet in a distance of five or six miles. Along their upper courses, they run through grassy valleys; but, as they approach Red Cañon, they rapidly disappear under the general surface of the country, and emerge into the cañon below in deep, dark gorges of their own. Each of these short lateral cañons is marked by a succession of cascades and a wild confusion of rocks and trees and fallen timber and thick undergrowth.

上面的小河谷是美丽的公园地；公园之间是庄严的松林，半掩着红砂岩岩架。黑尾鹿和麋鹿很多；灰熊也很多；野猫、狼獾、山狮都在这里有家。林间的通道满是鸟鸣，公园里缀满花朵。喧闹的小溪蜿蜒穿过；可见长满苔藓的岩架；远处雪原闪烁，山巅隐入云中。

The little valleys above are beautiful parks; between the parks are stately pine forests, half hiding ledges of red sandstone. Mule-deer and elk abound; grizzly bears, too, are abundant; wild cats, wolverines, and mountain lions are here at home. The forest aisles are filled with the music of birds, and the parks are decked with flowers. Noisy brooks meander through them; ledges of moss-covered rocks are seen; and gleaming in the distance are the snow fields, and the mountain tops are away in the clouds.

6 月 4 日。我们一早出发，一路跑进布朗公园。山谷半道上，一座红山的支脉横跨大河，河从其中切出峡谷。这里的崖壁相对不高，但很陡。无数燕子在河两岸的崖面上筑了它们的泥屋。河水又深又静，燕子却又快又吵，时而沿弧形轨迹掠过天空，时而在岩石上啁啾。雏鸟把光秃秃的小脑袋从泥屋门口探出来，争着要吃的。它们是一群吵闹的家伙。

_June 4._--We start early and run through to Brown’s Park. Half way down the valley, a spur of a red mountain stretches across the river, which cuts a cañon through it. Here the walls are comparatively low, but vertical. A vast number of swallows have built their _adobe_ houses on the face of the cliffs, on either side of the river. The waters are deep and quiet, but the swallows are swift and noisy enough, sweeping by in their curved paths through the air, or chattering from the rocks. The young birds stretch their little heads on naked necks through the doorways of their mud houses, clamoring for food. They are a noisy people.

我们叫它「燕子峡谷」。

We call this Swallow Cañon.

仍顺河滑行，直到下午早早就到一处，在一棵大三角叶杨下扎营——它立在右岸，离水稍远。队伍成功打了一大堆野鸭，下午又捉了一顿鱼。

Still down the river we glide, until an early hour in the afternoon, when we go into camp under a giant cottonwood, standing on the right bank, a little way back from the stream. The party had succeeded in killing a fine lot of wild ducks, and during the afternoon a mess of fish is taken.

6 月 5 日。我和一个人爬右边的一座山。一条长长的支脊，带着破碎的岩架，直插到河边；我沿它的走向，也就是俗称的「猪背岭」往上爬。邓恩也往同一处爬，正从沟谷上来。两小时苦爬，我们到了山顶。这些山都披着绿装；松柏成林长在绿色的台地上；西边远处可见雪山；北边，上布林平原在我们面前展开，直到消失在蓝天里；只有半条被河切开的山系横亘其间，河水就在我们脚下。

_June 5._--With one of the men, I climb a mountain, off on the right. A long spur, with broken ledges of rocks, puts down to the river; and along its course, or up the “hog-back,” as it is called, I make the ascent. Dunn, who is climbing to the same point, is coming up the gulch. Two hours’ hard work has brought us to the summit. These mountains are all verdure clad; pine and cedar forests are set on green terraces; snow clad mountains are seen in the distance, to the west; the plains of the upper Breen stretch out before us, to the north, until they are lost in the blue heavens; but half of the river cleft range intervenes, and the river itself is at our feet.

河对岸这半条山系由长长的山脊组成，几乎与河谷平行。在北边更远的一道山脊上，四条小溪发源。它们切过中间的山脊——其中一条比它们发源的那道高得多——形成峡谷；然后以平缓的曲线穿过河谷，两岸长着柳树、槭树和三角叶杨林。

This half range, beyond the river, is composed of long ridges, nearly parallel with the valley. On the farther ridge, to the north, four creeks have their sources. These cut through the intervening ridges, one of which is much higher than that on which they head, by cañon gorges; then they run, with gentle curves, across the valley, their banks set with willows, box-elders, and cottonwood groves.

东边，我们望进朱红河谷，弗里蒙特当年就是穿过它走向科罗拉多的大公园的。

To the east, we look up the valley of the Vermilion, through which Frémont found his path on his way to the great parks of Colorado.

读完气压计，我们结伴下山，又累又饿地回到营地——但这丝毫不减我们的热情，我们讲着这一天的经历，讲着那壮丽的山景。

The reading of the barometer taken, we start down in company, and reach camp tired and hungry, which does not abate one bit our enthusiasm, as we tell of the day’s work, with its glory of landscape.

6 月 6 日。天刚破晓，我被一片鸟鸣唤醒。仿佛这一带所有的羽族歌手都聚到了那棵老树上：上面是几种莺、啄木鸟和扑翅鴷，草丛里有草地鹨，河里有野雁。我支着胳膊肘躺着，看近旁一只云雀，然后叫醒同伴，让他听我的「珍妮·林德」。对我来说这是一场晨间音乐会，可不是什么「日场演出」。

_June 6._--At daybreak, I am awakened by a chorus of birds. It seems as if all the feathered songsters of the region have come to the old tree. Several species of warblers, woodpeckers, and flickers above, meadowlarks in the grass, and wild geese in the river. I recline on my elbow, and watch a lark near by, and then awaken my bed fellow, to listen to my Jenny Lind. A morning concert for me; none of your “_matinées_.”

我们的厨子曾在大平原上的长队里赶牛，如今那种车队已见不到了。他还没忘老习惯：音乐进行到一半，他的嗓门插进来：「起来！起来！牛进圈了！把缺口拴上！起来！起来！起来！」这就是我们的早饭铃。

Our cook has been an ox-driver, or “bullwhacker,” on the plains, in one of those long trains now no longer seen, and he hasn’t forgotten his old ways. In the midst of the concert, his voice breaks in: “Roll out! roll out! bulls in the corral! chain up the gaps! Roll out! roll out! roll out!” And this is our breakfast bell.

今天穿过公园，在另一条峡谷的入口处扎营。

To-day we pass through the park, and camp at the head of another cañon.

6 月 7 日。今天，我们两三人爬上左侧崖顶，测得它比营地高 2086 英尺。岩石被裂缝劈开，又深又窄，有时深达一百英尺以上。高高的松树在充满松土和腐殖的裂缝里扎根。在一块岩石上，我们发现一汪清澈冰凉的水——是昨晚阵雨积的。痛快喝了一顿后，我们走到峡谷边缘，俯瞰下面的河水。我现在能做到这点了——但那是爬了好几年山才把神经练硬，如今才能坐在崖边，把脚悬在两千英尺深渊上空，平静地往下看。可我还是没法看着别人这样做：要么叫他走开，要么转过头去。

_June 7._--To-day, two or three of us climb to the summit of the cliff, on the left, and find its altitude, above camp, to be 2,086 feet. The rocks are split with fissures, deep and narrow, sometimes a hundred feet, or more, to the bottom. Lofty pines find root in the fissures that are filled with loose earth and decayed vegetation. On a rock we find a pool of clear, cold water, caught from yesterday evening’s shower. After a good drink, we walk out to the brink of the cañon, and look down to the water below. I can do this now, but it has taken several years of mountain climbing to cool my nerves, so that I can sit, with my feet over the edge, and calmly look down a precipice 2,000 feet. And yet I cannot look on and see another do the same. I must either bid him come away, or turn my head.

峡谷崖壁以宏大的规模层层支护，之间是深深的凹龛；柱状岩峰冠在崖顶，河水在下方奔流。

The cañon walls are buttressed on a grand scale, with deep alcoves intervening; columned crags crown the cliffs, and the river is rolling below.

中午回到营地时，阳光灿烂地照在朱红的崖壁上，长着地衣的地方渐成绿灰两色；水从这壁到那壁灌满河道；峡谷像一座美丽的门，通向一片荣耀之境。

When we return to camp, at noon, the sun shines in splendor on vermilion walls, shaded into green and gray, where the rocks are lichened over; the river fills the channel from wall to wall, and the cañon opens, like a beautiful portal, to a region of glory.

此刻我写着这些，太阳正在西沉，阴影在峡谷里蔓延。朱红的闪光和玫瑰色的霞光，与绿灰的色调交融，慢慢变成上方沉郁的棕色，黑色阴影从下方爬上来；如今它成了一座通向幽暗之地的黑暗之门——我们明天就要从这里进入，开始我们的探险之旅。我们会发现什么？

This evening, as I write, the sun is going down, and the shadows are settling in the cañon. The vermilion gleams and roseate hues, blending with the green and gray tints, are slowly changing to somber brown above, and black shadows are creeping over them below; and now it is a dark portal to a region of gloom--the gateway through which we are to enter on our voyage of exploration to-morrow. What shall we find?

从烈焰峡谷到蜂房岬共九又三分之二英里。除穿过峡谷本身，河还流经马蹄峡和翠鸟峡，由短河谷隔开。烈焰峡谷崖壁最高点高出河面 1300 英尺。马蹄峡谷顶点处的东壁高出水面约 1600 英尺，从这一点向峡谷头和尾两侧倾斜。

The distance from Flaming Gorge to Beehive Point is nine and two-thirds miles. Besides, passing through the gorge, the river runs through Horseshoe and Kingfisher Cañons, separated by short valleys. The highest point on the walls, at Flaming Gorge, is 1,300 feet above the river. The east wall, at the apex of Horseshoe Cañon, is about 1,600 feet above the water’s edge, and, from this point, the walls slope both to the head and foot of the cañon.

翠鸟峡从上游水边起，高度稳定增加，到下游末端达 1200 英尺。

Kingfisher Cañon, starting at the water’s edge above, steadily increases in altitude to 1,200 feet at the foot.

红峡谷长二十五又三分之二英里，最高崖壁约 2500 英尺。

Red Cañon is twenty-five and two-thirds miles long, and the highest walls are about 2,500 feet.

布朗公园是被两侧山脉夹住的河谷，其实是峡谷的扩宽处。河穿过公园段长三十五又半英里，途中穿过两条短峡谷——南边山系的支脊横拦在河道上。

Brown’s Park is a valley, bounded on either side by a mountain range, really an expansion of the cañon. The river, through the park, is thirty-five and a half miles long, but passes through two short cañons, on its way, where spurs, from the mountains on the south, are thrust across its course.

CHAPTER IV THE CANYON OF LODORE

6 月 8 日。我们进入峡谷，直到中午，遇到一连串激流，船都得设法过去。

June 8.--We enter the cañon, and, until noon, find a succession of rapids, over which our boats have to be taken.

这里我必须说明我们在这种地方的行进方法：先锋船「爱玛·迪恩」号在前，其余船按信号跟随。接近一段激流——换了别的河这常被称为瀑布——我就站到甲板上观察，桨手反向划水，让船尽量慢地漂。如果能在岩石间看到一条干净的水道，我们就冲下去；但如果河道被完全堵死，就向其他船发信号，靠岸，我沿岸走细看。若仍看不出安全水道，苦活就开始了：把船放到险处的正上游，用绳放下，或抬船过陆，常常连船带货从岩石上抬过去；若放船安全，就只抬货物。

Here I must explain our method of proceeding at such places. The _Emma Dean_ goes in advance; the other boats follow, in obedience to signals. When we approach a rapid, or what, on other rivers, would often be called a fall, I stand on deck to examine it, while the oarsmen back water, and we drift on as slowly as possible. If I can see a clear chute between the rocks, away we go; but if the channel is beset entirely across, we signal the other boats, pull to land, and I walk along the shore for closer examination. If this reveals no clear channel, hard work begins. We drop the boats to the very head of the dangerous place, and let them over by lines, or make a portage, frequently carrying both boats and cargoes over the rocks, or, perhaps, only the cargoes, if it is safe to let the boats down.

这类瀑布掀起的浪与海浪很不同。海浪只是水升降，只有波形在前进，波形追波形永不停歇；浮在海浪上的物体只是起伏，不借助风力等不会前进。但这里的浪，是水本身在前进，而波形保持在原处。水跌下十到二十英尺到瀑布脚，又腾起成巨浪，然后一降一升，成一连串浪涌，逐渐在下方较静的水面消失；但这些浪始终在那里，你站到上方能数它们。

The waves caused by such falls in a river differ much from the waves of the sea. The water of an ocean wave merely rises and falls; the form only passes on, and form chases form unceasingly. A body floating on such waves merely rises and sinks--does not progress unless impelled by wind or some other power. But here, the water of the wave passes on, while the form remains. The waters plunge down ten or twenty feet, to the foot of a fall; spring up again in a great wave; then down and up, in a series of billows, that gradually disappear in the more quiet waters below; but these waves are always there, and you can stand above and count them.

船骑在这样的浪上，高速地腾跃俯冲。当岩石不碍事时，驾驭这种瀑布的难点在瀑布脚的第一道浪。它有时会聚一会，越堆越高，直到向后翻折。如果船在它刚破开的瞬间撞上，就能切开穿过，狂暴的碎浪把水花浇上船，若不抓紧就会被冲下水。如果船过瀑布时被某股侧流带偏了方向，侧着撞上浪（broadside），而浪又恰好同时破开，船就会被掀翻。但即便如此我们也要死死抓住船——因为水密隔舱像浮筒一样，船沉不了；于是我们就这样被拖着穿过浪区，直到静水，再把船翻正爬上去。今天我们有好几次这样的经历。

A boat riding such, leaps and plunges along with great velocity. Now, the difficulty in riding over these falls, when the rocks are out of the way, is in the first wave at the foot. This will sometimes gather for a moment, heaping up higher and higher, until it breaks back. If the boat strikes it the instant after it breaks, she cuts through, and the mad breaker dashes its spray over the boat, and would wash us overboard did we not cling tight. If the boat, in going over the falls, chances to get caught in some side current, and is turned from its course, so as to strike the wave “broadside on,” and the wave breaks at the same instant, the boat is capsized. Still, we must cling to her, for, the water tight compartments acting as buoys, she cannot sink; and so we go, dragged through the waves, until still waters are reached. We then right the boat, and climb aboard. We have several such experiences to-day.

夜里我们在右岸一小块倾斜的岩石上扎营，夹在河与崖脚之间；夜幕降临时，这巨大的深峡里涌起了阴森。

At night, we camp on the right bank, on a little shelving rock, between the river and the foot of the cliff; and with night comes gloom into these great depths.

晚饭后，我们围坐在用岩石卡住的浮木生的营火旁，讲蛮荒生活的故事——这些人都在山里、平原上、南方的战场上见过世面。很晚我们才在海滩上铺开毯子。

After supper, we sit by our camp fire, made of drift wood caught by the rocks, and tell stories of wild life; for the men have seen such in the mountains, or on the plains, and on the battle fields of the South. It is late before we spread our blankets on the beach.

躺下来，我们仰望峡谷上方的天空，只见一小片蓝天挂在高处——一道蓝色的月牙，有两三个星座向下望着我们。

Lying down, we look up through the cañon, and see that only a little of the blue heaven appears overhead--a crescent of blue sky, with two or three constellations peering down upon us.

我一时睡不着，白天的兴奋还没消退。不久我看到一颗明亮的星，仿佛就歇在东方头顶崖壁的边缘。它缓缓像从岩石上的歇处飘过峡谷上空。起初它像镶在崖边的一颗宝石；当它移离岩石，我几乎担心它会掉下来。事实上它真像沿一道平缓的弧线下坠，仿佛缀满星星的明亮天空横铺在峡谷上，两端架在崖壁上，被自身重量压弯了。星星仿佛就在峡谷里。我很快认出那是明亮的织女星，于是想到给这段崖壁取名「竖琴崖」。

I do not sleep for some time, as the excitement of the day has not worn off. Soon I see a bright star, that appears to rest on the very verge of the cliff overhead to the east. Slowly it seems to float from its resting place on the rock over the cañon. At first, it appears like a jewel set on the brink of the cliff; but, as it moves out from the rock, I almost wonder that it does not fall. In fact, it does seem to descend in a gentle curve, as though the bright sky in which the stars are set was spread across the cañon, resting on either wall, and swayed down by its own weight. The stars appear to be in the cañon. I soon discover that it is the bright star Vega, so it occurs to me to designate this part of the wall as the “Cliff of the Harp.”

6 月 9 日。队里有人建议把这里叫「洛多尔峡谷」，大家采纳了。我们走得很慢，常常在岸边水边的岩石上爬几百码，先查看水道再闯。

_June 9._--One of the party suggests that we call this the Cañon of Lodore, and the name is adopted. Very slowly we make our way, often climbing on the rocks at the edge of the water for a few hundred yards, to examine the channel before running it.

下午，我们来到一处必须抬船过陆的地方。小船靠岸，其余船被信号召上来。

During the afternoon, we come to a place where it is necessary to make a portage. The little boat is landed, and the others are signaled to come up.

每当出现这种激流或破碎的瀑布，河道通常是被从崖上崩落或侧流冲来的岩石骤然收窄。在窄而多石的河道正上方，一侧或两侧常有一湾静水，我们可以从容靠岸。有时水从上方宽阔平静的水面，以半圆形的凹口，平滑无皱地落入下方狭窄狂暴的水道。千万要小心不能冲过这诱人的凹口边缘，但在它上方划船是安全的。我沿岸走查看地形，留下一个队员举旗给其他船指引靠岸点。我很快看到一条船安全靠岸，不再担心；但一分钟后就听见喊声，回头一看，一条船正顺凹口中心冲下去——是「无名」号，载着豪兰船长、他弟弟和古德曼。我感到它过凹口不可避免，跑去救第三条船。过了一分钟，那条船绕过岬角驶向岸边。然后我转身顺流，爬着去找已经冲下去的船。第一道落差不大，只有十到十二英尺，这种我们常闯；但下方，河水又在满是危险礁石的水道里翻跌四五十英尺，礁石把浪打碎成漩涡、拍成泡沫。我绕过一块大礁石，恰好看见船撞上一块岩石，反弹后倾斜，敞开的隔舱灌满了水。两个人丢了桨；船打横，快速侧向被冲下几码，又猛烈撞上另一块岩石的中段，断成两截，人被抛进河里；大半个船还浮着，他们很快抓住它，顺流漂下几百码，到第二段满是巨砾的激流，船再次撞上，被砸得粉碎，人和碎片很快漂出我的视线。我沿岸跑着，转过一个弯，看见一个人头露出水面，在一个大岩石下方的漩涡里打转。

When these rapids or broken falls occur, usually the channel is suddenly narrowed by rocks which have been tumbled from the cliffs or have been washed in by lateral streams. Immediately above the narrow, rocky channel, on one or both sides, there is often a bay of quiet water, in which we can land with ease. Sometimes the water descends with a smooth, unruffled surface, from the broad, quiet spread above, into the narrow, angry channel below, by a semicircular sag. Great care must be taken not to pass over the brink into this deceptive pit, but above it we can row with safety. I walk along the bank to examine the ground, leaving one of my men with a flag to guide the other boats to the landing-place. I soon see one of the boats make shore all right and feel no more concern; but a minute after, I hear a shout, and looking around, see one of the boats shooting down the center of the sag. It is the _No Name_, with Captain Howland, his brother, and Goodman. I feel that its going over is inevitable, and run to save the third boat. A minute more, and she turns the point and heads for the shore. Then I turn down stream again, and scramble along to look for the boat that has gone over. The first fall is not great, only ten or twelve feet, and we often run such; but below, the river tumbles down again for forty or fifty feet, in a channel filled with dangerous rocks that break the waves into whirlpools and beat them into foam. I pass around a great crag just in time to see the boat strike a rock, and, rebounding from the shock, careen and fill the open compartment with water. Two of the men lose their oars; she swings around, and is carried down at a rapid rate, broadside on, for a few yards, and strikes amidships on another rock with great force, is broken quite in two, and the men are thrown into the river; the larger part of the boat floating buoyantly, they soon seize it, and down the river they drift, past the rocks for a few hundred yards to a second rapid, filled with huge boulders, where the boat strikes again, and is dashed to pieces, and the men and fragments are soon carried beyond my sight. Running along, I turn a bend, and see a man’s head above the water, washed about in a whirlpool below a great rock.

那是弗兰克·古德曼，死死抱住岩石，命悬一线。来到近旁，我看见豪兰想从他被冲上的那个岛上去救他。很快他靠得够近，把一根竿子伸向弗兰克。后者放开岩石，抓住竿子，被拉上岸。塞内卡·豪兰被冲得离岛更远，被几块岩石挡住，虽有点擦伤，还是安全上了岸。说起来这仿佛很长时间，其实转瞬就完了。

It is Frank Goodman, clinging to it with a grip upon which life depends. Coming opposite, I see Howland trying to go to his aid from an island on which he has been washed. Soon, he comes near enough to reach Frank with a pole, which he extends toward him. The latter lets go the rock, grasps the pole, and is pulled ashore. Seneca Howland is washed farther down the island, and is caught by some rocks, and, though somewhat bruised, manages to get ashore in safety. This seems a long time, as I tell it, but it is quickly done.

如今三个人在岛上，两侧都是湍急危险的河水，下方是瀑布。「爱玛·迪恩」号很快放下水，萨姆纳从尽量远的上游出发，推出船去。他划桨非常熟练，几桨就把他送到岛上的恰当位置。然后他们都把船逆流尽量拉上去，直到水没到脖子。一人坐在岩石上扶住船，等其余人准备好一起拉，然后他推一把船，双手扒住船沿，在大家划向大陆时爬进船，安全靠岸。我们和他们握手时的高兴劲儿，就像他们刚环球航行归来、在遥远海岸遇难获救一样。

And now the three men are on an island, with a swift, dangerous river on either side, and a fall below. The _Emma Dean_ is soon brought down, and Sumner, starting above as far as possible, pushes out. Right skillfully he plies the oars, and a few strokes set him on the island at the proper point. Then they all pull the boat up stream, as far as they are able, until they stand in water up to their necks. One sits on a rock, and holds the boat until the others are ready to pull, then gives the boat a push, clings to it with his hands, and climbs in as they pull for mainland, which they reach in safety. We are as glad to shake hands with them as though they had been on a voyage around the world, and wrecked on a distant coast.

顺河下游半英里，我们发现失事船的后舱连同部分船底——破烂碎裂——漂到一块岩石旁搁浅了。舱里有贵重物品；但察看之后，我们认定不值得冒生命危险去救。当然，船上的干粮、仪器、衣物是没了。

Down the river half a mile we find that the after cabin of the wrecked boat, with a part of the bottom, ragged and splintered, has floated against a rock, and stranded. There are valuable articles in the cabin; but, on examination, we determine that life should not be risked to save them. Of course, the cargo of rations, instruments, and clothing is gone.

我们回到船边，扎营过夜。整个黑暗的夜晚我毫无睡意。干粮、仪器、衣物当初都分到各船，就是防备这样的意外；我们出发时每样要紧的东西都带了两份。但在分配中有一个例外——气压计全放在一条船上，现在全丢了。它们有可能就在卡在岩石旁的后舱里，因为平时就放在那里。可又怎么够得着呢！河水在涨。它们明天还在吗？我能去盐湖城、从纽约弄到新的气压计吗？

We return to the boats, and make camp for the night. No sleep comes to me in all those dark hours. The rations, instruments, and clothing have been divided among the boats, anticipating such an accident as this; and we started with duplicates of everything that was deemed necessary to success. But, in the distribution, there was one exception to this precaution, and the barometers were all placed in one boat, and they are lost. There is a possibility that they are in the cabin lodged against the rock, for that is where they were kept. But, then, how to reach them! The river is rising. Will they be there to-morrow? Can I go out to Salt Lake City, and obtain barometers from New York?

6 月 10 日。我下定决心，若气压计真在那里，就把它们从残骸里取回来。早饭后，队员们做搬运时，我又下去察看一下。那后舱还躺在那，只是又往下漂了五六十英尺。

_June 10._--I have determined to get the barometers from the wreck, if they are there. After breakfast, while the men make the portage, I go down again for another examination. There the cabin lies, only carried fifty or sixty feet farther on.

仔细查看地形后，我确信可以安全够到它，回去把我的结论告诉队员们。萨姆纳和邓恩自愿驾小船去试。他们出发、够到它，把气压计取了出来；小伙子们一阵欢呼，我也跟着叫好——他们和我一样为保住仪器而高兴。船靠回我们这边时，我发现从残骸里救出的只有气压计、一包温度计和一桶三加仑的威士忌——队员们欢呼的其实是那个。他们背着我把它搬上了船，现在我倒庆幸他们这么做了，因为他们认为那对他们有好处——他们每天都被落基山雪峰上流下来的融雪浇得透湿。

Carefully looking over the ground, I am satisfied that it can be reached with safety, and return to tell the men my conclusion. Sumner and Dunn volunteer to take the little boat and make the attempt. They start, reach it, and out come the barometers; and now the boys set up a shout, and I join them, pleased that they should be as glad to save the instruments as myself. When the boat lands on our side, I find that the only things saved from the wreck were the barometers, a package of thermometers, and a three gallon keg of whisky, which is what the men were shouting about. They had taken it aboard, unknown to me, and now I am glad they did, for they think it will do them good, as they are drenched every day by the melting snow, which runs down the summits of the Rocky Mountains.

现在我们回到搬运的活儿上。我们发现必须把干粮在岩石上搬近一英里，船则用绳放下，只有少数几处也得抬过去。

Now we come back to our work at the portage. We find that it is necessary to carry our rations over the rocks for nearly a mile, and let our boats down with lines, except at a few points, where they also must be carried.

在河与峡谷东壁之间有一片巨大的破碎岩石坡积。这些岩石从上方崖壁崩落，堆成一大堆巨大的棱角碎块。我们在其上修了一条四分之一英里长的路，通往一片覆着漂木的小沙滩；在漂木中清出几百码的路，再越过另一堆岩石继续前行，又往下近半英里，到一个小湾。一天的大部分时间都花在这活儿上。然后我们把货物搬到海滩，扎营过夜。

Between the river and the eastern wall of the cañon there is an immense talus of broken rocks. These have tumbled down from the cliffs above, and constitute a vast pile of huge angular fragments. On these we build a path for a quarter of a mile, to a small sand beach covered with drift-wood, through which we clear a way for several hundred yards, then continue the trail on over another pile of rocks, nearly half a mile farther down, to a little bay. The greater part of the day is spent in this work. Then we carry our cargoes down to the beach and camp for the night.

队员们在生营火时，我们发现一个铁烤炉、几个锡盘、一条船的一部分和许多碎片——这表明这里正是阿什利那队人失事的地方。

While the men are building the camp fire, we discover an iron bake oven, several tin plates, a part of a boat, and many other fragments, which denote that this is the place where Ashley’s party was wrecked.

6 月 11 日。这一天都在把干粮搬往那个海湾——扛着一袋袋面粉或咸肉翻越岩石可真不是小事。我们每约五百码停一站，等夜晚降临、最后一袋放到海滩上时，我们累得遍体瘀伤，只求一睡。

_June 11._--This day is spent in carrying our rations down to the bay--no small task to climb over the rocks with sacks of flour or bacon. We carry them by stages of about 500 yards each, and when night comes, and the last sack is on the beach, we are tired, bruised, and glad to sleep.

6 月 12 日。今天我们把船放下到海湾。干这活儿时，我们发现了失事船的三袋面粉，卡在岩石间。我们把它们搬到高水位线以上放着，因为剩下三条船的货已经够重了。我们还找到两三支桨，也一并放好。

_June 12._--To-day we take the boats down to the bay. While at this work, we discover three sacks of flour from the wrecked boat, that have lodged in the rocks. We carry them above high-water mark, and leave them, as our cargoes are already too heavy for the three remaining boats. We also find two or three oars, which we place with them.

因为阿什利那队人在这里失事，我们又在这同一处损失了一条船，我们把这充满危险与损失之地命名为「灾难瀑布」。

As Ashley and his party were wrecked here, and as we have lost one of our boats at the same place, we adopt the name Disaster Falls for the scene of so much peril and loss.

虽然他的几个同伴溺亡了，阿什利和另一个人却在失事中幸存，爬上峡谷崖壁，穿过瓦萨奇山脉一路走到盐湖城，主要靠浆果为食，穿过一片陌生而艰难的地区。到达盐湖时，他们几乎衣不蔽体、饿得半死。摩门教徒给了他们食物和衣服，雇他们在圣殿的地基上干活，直到挣够离开此地的盘缠。他们后来的遭遇我无从知晓。他们可能回到了出事地点——因为下方一条注入河流的小溪名叫「阿什利溪」，据说他在这条河上盖过小屋、过了一两个冬天设陷阱捕猎；但这也许发生在失事之前。

Though some of his companions were drowned, Ashley and one other survived the wreck, climbed the cañon wall, and found their way across the Wasatch Mountains to Salt Lake City, living chiefly on berries, as they wandered through an unknown and difficult country. When they arrived at Salt Lake, they were almost destitute of clothing, and nearly starved. The Mormon people gave them food and clothing, and employed them to work on the foundation of the Temple, until they had earned sufficient to enable them to leave the country. Of their subsequent history, I have no knowledge. It is possible they returned to the scene of the disaster, as a little creek entering the river below is known as Ashley’s Creek, and it is reported that he built a cabin and trapped on this river for one or two winters; but this may have been before the disaster.

6 月 13 日。还是岩石、激流和搬运。

_June 13._--Still rocks, rapids, and portages.

今晚我们在左壁脚下的一小片河漫滩扎营，长着茂密的槭树；早早停下，好把衣物和干粮摊开晾干——一切都湿透了，正在霉坏。

We camp to-night at the foot of the left wall on a little patch of flood-plain covered with a dense growth of box-elders, stopping early in order to spread the clothing and rations to dry. Everything is wet and spoiling.

6 月 14 日。我和豪兰爬上峡谷西壁，到两千英尺高处。站在上面向西望，我们发现一座大公园地，宽五六英里，长二三十英里。我们爬的这道崖壁成了峡谷与公园之间的墙，它西侧到谷地有八百英尺。一条小溪蜿蜒而下，比河面高一千二百英尺，穿过横亘的崖壁峡谷，化作破碎的瀑布，跌落一千多英尺，注入下方的河。

_June 14._--Howland and I climb the wall, on the west side of the cañon, to an altitude of 2,000 feet. Standing above, and looking to the west, we discover a large park, five or six miles wide and twenty or thirty long. The cliff we have climbed forms a wall between the cañon and the park, for it is 800 feet, down the western side, to the valley. A creek comes winding down, 1,200 feet above the river, and, entering the intervening wall by a cañon, it plunges down, more than a thousand feet, by a broken cascade, into the river below.

6 月 15 日。今天我们又在做一次搬运，东壁上的一座山峰由两个队员登上去，测得高出河面两千七百英尺。峡谷东侧被切出一座巨大的圆形剧场，有厚重的支壁和深黑的凹龛，龛里长着美丽的苔藓和纤秀的蕨类，泉水从深处涌出，化作银线蜿蜒流过砂岩地面。这里接连有三道瀑布：第一道，水被压缩进紧贴右壁的极窄水道，十码内跌落十五英尺；第二道，宽阔的水面越过一群黑头探出浪花的岩石，跌下二十英尺；第三道是破碎的瀑布——一段短促的陡急流，水在崩塌的巨岩间下跌二十多英尺。我们给这组命名「三连瀑」。

_June 15._--To-day, while we make another portage, a peak, standing on the east wall, is climbed by two of the men, and found to be 2,700 feet above the river. On the east side of the cañon, a vast amphitheater has been cut, with massive buttresses, and deep, dark alcoves, in which grow beautiful mosses and delicate ferns, while springs burst out from the further recesses, and wind, in silver threads, over floors of sand rock. Here we have three falls in close succession. At the first, the water is compressed into a very narrow channel, against the right-hand cliff, and falls fifteen feet in ten yards; at the second, we have a broad sheet of water, tumbling down twenty feet over a group of rocks that thrust their dark heads through the foaming waters. The third is a broken fall, or short, abrupt rapid, where the water makes a descent of more than twenty feet among huge, fallen fragments of the cliff. We name the group Triplet Falls.

第一道我们绕过去搬运，第二三道用绳放下。

We make a portage around the first; past the second and third we let down with lines.

下午，邓恩和豪兰爬山回来了。我们顺静水跑下四分之三英里，在又一道瀑布的顶端靠岸。查看后发现先是几英尺的陡跌，然后河在满是巨砾的水道里翻滚半英里，总落差一百英尺。这段河我们命名「地狱半英里」。

During the afternoon, Dunn and Howland, having returned from their climb, we run down, three-quarters of a mile, on quiet water, and land at the head of another fall. On examination, we find that there is an abrupt plunge of a few feet, and then the river tumbles, for half a mile, with a descent of a hundred feet, in a channel beset with great numbers of huge boulders. This stretch of the river is named Hell’s Half-Mile.

这一天剩下的时间，都在岩石间开一条通往急流下端的小路。

The remaining portion of the day is occupied in making a trail among the rocks to the foot of the rapid.

6 月 16 日。今晨第一件事是把货物搬到瀑布脚。然后开始放船。两条船安全放下，但费了极大的劲——因为这样巨大的水体沿斜坡滚下，被崖上突出的岩石和河道里的巨砾堆搅成漩涡和横流，要防止船撞上岩石或脱缰，需要极重的劳力和极细的小心。有时我们不得不把船抵住一块礁石、停在一条水道上方，等系在船尾的第二根绳送到下游某点，一切就绪后解开第一根绳，把船交给水流，它便冲下去，下游的人把它荡进某个漩涡。

_June 16._--Our first work this morning is to carry our cargoes to the foot of the falls. Then we commence letting down the boats. We take two of them down in safety, but not without great difficulty; for, where such a vast body of water, rolling down an inclined plane, is broken into eddies and cross currents by rocks projecting from the cliffs and piles of boulders in the channel, it requires excessive labor and much care to prevent their being dashed against the rocks or breaking away. Sometimes we are compelled to hold the boat against a rock, above a chute, until a second line, attached to the stem, is carried to some point below, and, when all is ready, the first line is detached, and the boat given to the current, when she shoots down, and the men below swing her into some eddy.

在这样的地方，我们正放下最后一条船。它一被松开，一个浪就把它打横冲向下游，系着绳的船尾离岸略高。他们拽绳想把它拉回岸，但斜着撞上它的水流力量太大，把它甩向河心。队员的手被擦过的绳子磨得烫伤；船挣断绳子，以极快的速度冲向下方。

At such a place, we are letting down the last boat, and, as she is set free, a wave turns her broadside down the stream, with the stem, to which the line is attached, from shore, and a little up. They haul on the line to bring the boat in, but the power of the current, striking obliquely against her, shoots her out into the middle of the river. The men have their hands burned with the friction of the passing line; the boat breaks away, and speeds, with great velocity, down the stream.

「峡谷少女」号似乎要没了；但它漂了一段，荡进一个漩涡，在那里打转，直到我们驾小船赶到，把它救回来。

The _Maid of the Cañon_ is lost, so it seems; but she drifts some distance, and swings into an eddy, in which she spins about, until we arrive with the small boat, and rescue her.

很快我们又上路了，在右边一条小溪口停下来吃迟到的午饭。这条小溪从远山沿深深的侧谷流下。我们去探它，但很快被一块高岩挡住去路——溪水从岩石上平铺滑下。岩石不完全垂直，水也不是跌成瀑布。

Soon we are on our way again, and stop at the mouth of a little brook, on the right, for a late dinner. This brook comes down from the distant mountains, in a deep side cañon. We set out to explore it, but are soon cut off from farther progress up the gorge by a high rock, over which the brook glides in a smooth sheet. The rock is not quite vertical, and the water does not plunge over in a fall.

然后我们向左爬了一小时，到了比河面高一千英尺、比小溪高六百英尺的地方。就在前方，峡谷分叉，右边下来一条小溪，左边一条，我们可沿任意一条峡谷向上望，穿过不断升高的视野，看到一英里后、头顶两千英尺处的崖壁、岩峰和塔岩。右边可见十几道闪亮的瀑布。松树和冷杉立在岩石上，白杨悬在小溪上方。下方的岩石红褐色，沉在深影里；上方却是浅黄和朱红，立在阳光中。上方的光被明亮的岩石衬得更亮，下方的影被褐色崖壁的沉郁色调衬得更暗，越发显得峡谷深不可测——向上回到阳光和开阔天空的世界似乎很远，向下沉入峡谷幽暗的底部也似乎很远。我从未如此强烈地感受过这些峡谷崖壁的巨大高度，即使在「竖琴崖」——那里苍穹仿佛就架在崖巅——也不曾有过。

Then we climb up to the left for an hour, and are a thousand feet above the river, and six hundred above the brook. Just before us, the cañon divides, a little stream coming down on the right, and another on the left, and we can look away up either of these cañons, through an ascending vista, to cliffs and crags and towers, a mile back, and two thousand feet overhead. To the right, a dozen gleaming cascades are seen. Pines and firs stand on the rocks and aspens overhang the brooks. The rocks below are red and brown, set in deep shadows, but above, they are buff and vermilion, and stand in the sunshine. The light above, made more brilliant by the bright-tinted rocks, and the shadows below more gloomy by the somber hues of the brown walls, increase the apparent depths of the cañons, and it seems a long way up to the world of sunshine and open sky, and a long way down to the bottom of the cañon glooms. Never before have I received such an impression of the vast heights of these cañon walls; not even at the Cliff of the Harp, where the very heavens seemed to rest on their summits.

我们坐在几块悬空的岩石上，享受了一会这景色，听着峡谷深处传来的落水音乐。我们给它取名「涟漪溪」。

We sit on some overhanging rocks, and enjoy the scene for a time, listening to the music of falling waters away up the cañons. We name this Rippling Brook.

傍晚我们短途跑下，来到另一条小溪口，它从左面下来，注入一个草木繁茂的凹龛。我们在此扎营，一边是一丛柏树，另一边是茂密的槭树和枯柳。

Late in the afternoon we make a short run to the mouth of another little creek, coming down from the left into an alcove filled with luxuriant vegetation. Here camp is made with a group of cedars on one side and a dense mass of box-elders and dead willows on the other.

我上去探查那个凹龛。离开时，一阵旋风刮来，把火吹散到枯柳和柏枝间，很快就酿成一场大火。队员们冲向船，丢下一切来不及抓起的东西，就这样还有人被烧了衣服、燎了头发，布拉德利被烫伤了耳朵。厨子抱了满满一怀炊具跳上船，绊了一跤摔倒了——我们的炊具全掉进了河里。盘子没了，勺子没了，刀叉也没了。「水逮住了它们；狠狠逮住了它们。」

I go up to explore the alcove. While away a whirlwind comes, scattering the fire among the dead willows and cedar-spray, and soon there is a conflagration. The men rush for the boats, leaving all they cannot readily seize at the moment, and even then they have their clothing burned and hair singed, and Bradley has his ears scorched. The cook fills his arms with the mess-kit, and, jumping into a boat, stumbles and falls, and away go our cooking utensils into the river. Our plates are gone; our spoons are gone; our knives and forks are gone. “Water catch ’em; h-e-a-p catch ’em.”

在船上时，队员们不得不砍断缆绳——火焰窜上垂柳，正燎烤着他们。一旦漂到河上，就只能往下走，因为水流太急，逆水划不动。正下方就是一段满是礁石的急流。他们冲下去，没探过水道，没有信号指引。就在这时我碰巧看见他们，但还没发现火情，队员们的奇怪举动让我满心惊讶。我爬下岩石，跑到岸边。等我到时，他们已经靠岸了。然后我们一起回到刚才的营地，看还有没有什么落下的能救出来。找到了几件从船上取出的衣物和铺盖，还有几只锡杯、脸盆和一个行军锅——这就是我们如今全部的行军餐具了。不过我们照样过得好好的。

When on the boats, the men are compelled to cut loose, as the flames, running out on the overhanging willows, are scorching them. Loose on the stream, they must go down, for the water is too swift to make headway against it. Just below is a rapid, filled with rocks. On they shoot, no channel explored, no signal to guide them. Just at this juncture I chance to see them, but have not yet discovered the fire, and the strange movements of the men fill me with astonishment. Down the rocks I clamber, and run to the bank. When I arrive, they have landed. Then we all go back to the late camp to see if anything left behind can be saved. Some of the clothing and bedding taken out of the boats is found, also a few tin cups, basins, and a camp kettle, and this is all the mess kit we now have. Yet we do just as well as ever.

6 月 17 日。我们跑到了扬帕河口。这真是一段充满灾难与辛劳的日子，尽管如此，洛多尔峡谷并非没有风光——甚至美得笔墨难尽。从进峡那刻起到在此靠岸，它的水声从未停歇，那段时间里没有一刻安宁。但它的崖壁和悬崖、它的山峰和岩峰、它的剧场和凹龛，讲着一个美丽而壮丽的故事，我至今仍听得见——而且会一直听下去。

_June 17._--We run down to the mouth of Yampa River. This has been a chapter of disasters and toils, notwithstanding which the Cañon of Lodore was not devoid of scenic interest, even beyond the power of pen to tell. The roar of its waters was heard unceasingly from the hour we entered it until we landed here. No quiet in all that time. But its walls and cliffs, its peaks and crags, its amphitheaters and alcoves, tell a story of beauty and grandeur that I hear yet--and shall hear.

洛多尔峡谷长二十又四分之三英里。它从我们称「洛多尔之门」的地方陡然开始，崖壁近两千英尺高，直到「凹龛溪」——距峡尾约三英里——之前从不低于此高度。崖壁极不规则：有些地方是垂直或悬垂的峭壁，有些地方呈台阶状，有些地方是陡坡退缩，还被许多侧向沟壑和峡谷打断。崖壁最高点在「邓恩崖」——靠近三连瀑——岩石达两千七百英尺，而稍远处的山峰还要再高近千英尺。黄松、坚果松、冷杉和柏树在温塔山成林，又从烈焰峡谷到回声公园一路贴着岩石、扎进石缝，下到水边。红砂岩上覆着地衣；湿润处生着纤巧的苔藓，蕨类垂饰着崖壁。

The Cañon of Lodore is twenty and three-quarter miles in length. It starts abruptly at what we have called the Gate of Lodore, with walls nearly two thousand feet high, and they are never lower than this until we reach Alcove Brook, about three miles above the foot. They are very irregular, standing in vertical or overhanging cliffs in places, terraced in others, or receding in steep slopes, and are broken by many side gulches and cañons. The highest point on the wall is at Dunn’s Cliff, near Triplet Falls, where the rocks reach an altitude of 2,700 feet, but the peaks a little way back rise nearly a thousand feet higher. Yellow pines, nut pines, firs, and cedars stand in extensive forests on the Uinta Mountains, and, clinging to the rocks and growing in the crevices, come down the walls to the water’s edge from Flaming Gorge to Echo Park. The red sandstones are lichened over; delicate mosses grow in the moist places, and ferns festoon the walls.

CHAPTER V FROM ECHO PARK TO THE MOUTH OF THE UINTA RIVER

扬帕河从东面注入绿河。在正对它河口的地方，绿河拐向南，沿一块约七百英尺高、一英里长的岩石脚下流过，然后猛绕过它向右转，又几乎与先前方向平行地向北流回近一英里，于是这条又长又窄的岩石的两侧都成了河的岸。这样形成的岩石舌是一片半岛状峭壁，东侧全程是墙一般的陡崖，西侧则有些地方塌落。

The Yampa enters the Green from the east. At a point opposite its mouth, the Green runs to the south, at the foot of a rock, about seven hundred feet high and a mile long, and then turns sharply around it to the right, and runs back in a northerly course, parallel to its former direction, for nearly another mile, thus having the opposite sides of a long, narrow rock for its bank. The tongue of rock so formed is a peninsular precipice, with a mural escarpment along its whole course on the east, but broken down at places on the west.

在河对岸、扬帕河口下游，有一小片公园地，恰好够做一个农场，四周围着高高的灰色均质砂岩墙。它有三个河上入口：一个从扬帕河下来，一个从下方沿绿河上来，还有一个从绿河下来；还有一个陆上入口，沿一条侧谷进来。其他地方都无法进入。这条陆上入口的小路是印第安猎人踩出来的，他们在某些季节下来猎盘羊。

On the east side of the river, opposite the rock, and below the Yampa, there is a little park, just large enough for a farm, already fenced with high walls of gray homogeneous sandstone. There are three river entrances to this park: one down the Yampa; one below, by coming up the Green; and another down the Green. There is also a land entrance down a lateral cañon. Elsewhere the park is inaccessible. Through this land-entrance by the side cañon there is a trail made by Indian hunters, who come down here in certain seasons to kill mountain sheep.

在绿河冲刷的岩石东侧，能看到巨大的空心穹顶；柳树沿河而立，槭树成丛，下端立着几棵三角叶杨。站在岩石对面，我们的话被异常清晰地重复回来，但音色柔和圆润，把我们的话变成了神奇的音乐。你几乎不敢相信那是你自己的回声。有些地方有两三道回声折回；有些地方回声不断自我重复，在这块岩石与东壁之间来回穿过河面。

Great hollow domes are seen in the eastern side of the rock, against which the Green sweeps; willows border the river; clumps of box-elder are seen; and a few cottonwoods stand at the lower end. Standing opposite the rock, our words are repeated with startling clearness, but in a soft, mellow tone, that transforms them into magical music. Scarcely can you believe it is the echo of your own voice. In some places two or three echoes come back; in other places they repeat themselves, passing back and forth across the river between this rock and the eastern wall.

要听清这些重复的回声，你得大声喊。队里有人说能听到十到十二次重复。在我听来，它们迅速减弱并因多次而融合，像旷野上绵延的电线杆。我以前在朗氏峰附近的崖壁见过同样的现象，很高兴又遇上了。

To hear these repeated echoes well you must shout. Some of the party aver that ten or twelve repetitions can be heard. To me, they seem to rapidly diminish and merge by multiplicity, like telegraph poles on an outstretched plain. I have observed the same phenomenon once before in the cliffs near Long’s Peak, and am pleased to meet with it again.

下午，我和布拉德利爬北边一些崖壁。盘羊在我们上方，站在岩石上专注地打量我们，仿佛一动不动。它们的颜色和脚下灰色砂岩很像，加上一动不动，看起来像雕刻的造型。忽然一头漂亮的公羊用前蹄敲击岩石，它们全体转身一起蹦跳而去，跃过岩石和深沟，爬上人类无法追随的崖壁，那种轻松优雅简直不可思议。夜里我们回到河边槭树下的营地。我们将在这里待两三天，做一系列测定经纬度的天文观测。

During the afternoon, Bradley and I climb some cliffs to the north. Mountain sheep are seen above us, and they stand out on the rocks, and eye us intently, not seeming to move. Their color is much like that of the gray sandstone beneath them, and, immovable as they are, they appear like carved forms. Now a fine ram beats the rock with his front foot, and, wheeling around, they all bound away together, leaping over rocks and chasms, and climbing walls where no man can follow, and this with an ease and gracefulness most wonderful. At night we return to our camp, under the box-elders, by the river side. Here we are to spend two or three days, making a series of astronomic observations for latitude and longitude.

6 月 18 日。我们给对岸那条长长的半岛状岩石取名「回声岩」。想爬上去，我和布拉德利驾小船尽量逆流而上——因为它正对面爬不了。我们在上端的岩石坡积处靠岸，找一处似乎可以攀爬的地方；但还得再往上游走。我们一路攀爬，直到一处河水贴着崖壁流过的地方，找到一条可通行的岩架，现在可以开始爬了。

_June 18._--We have named the long peninsular rock on the other side Echo Rock. Desiring to climb it, Bradley and I take the little boat and pull up stream as far as possible, for it cannot be climbed directly opposite. We land on a talus of rocks at the upper end, to reach a place where it seems practicable to make the ascent; but we must go still farther up the river. So we scramble along, until we reach a place where the river sweeps against the wall. Here we find a shelf, along which we can pass, and now are ready for the climb.

我们沿一条沟壑向上，然后向左转到崖壁的一道台地，再越过破碎的岩石往上，又到更多台地，沿台地走，再遇到破碎的岩石和裂缝，踩着它们继续上攀，直到爬升了六到八百英尺；然后迎面是一道笔直的绝壁。

We start up a gulch; then pass to the left, on a bench, along the wall; then up again, over broken rocks; then we reach more benches, along which we walk, until we find more broken rocks and crevices, by which we climb still up, until we have ascended six or eight hundred feet; then we are met by a sheer precipice.

环顾四周，我们找到一处似乎可以攀爬的地方。我在前；布拉德利把气压计递给我，跟在后面。就这样一段一段地，我们快到顶了。在这里，我奋力一纵，在一个小裂缝里找到了立足点，抓住头顶岩石的一个棱角。我发现自己再也上不去，也没法退回来——我不敢松手，脚下又够不到支撑。我喊布拉德利帮忙。他找到一条能上到我头顶岩石顶部的路，却够不到我。然后他四下找棍子或树枝，什么也没有。他提议不如把气压计的盒子递给我帮一把，但我怕自己抓不住。这一刻十分危险。我踮着脚尖站着，肌肉开始发抖。下面是六七十英尺的绝壁。若我失手，就会摔到底，也许还会滚过台地，再向崖下翻落。就在这当口，布拉德利想到脱掉他的衬裤——他真脱了，把裤腿甩下来给我。我紧贴岩石，松开手，抓住晃荡的裤腿，在他的帮助下爬上了顶。

Looking about, we find a place where it seems possible to climb. I go ahead; Bradley hands the barometer to me, and follows. So we proceed, stage by stage, until we are nearly to the summit. Here, by making a spring, I gain a foothold in a little crevice, and grasp an angle of the rock overhead. I find I can get up no farther, and cannot step back, for I dare not let go with my hand, and cannot reach foot-hold below without.[4] I call to Bradley for help. He finds a way by which he can get to the top of the rock over my head, but cannot reach me. Then he looks around for some stick or limb of a tree, but finds none. Then he suggests that he had better help me with the barometer case; but I fear I cannot hold on to it. The moment is critical. Standing on my toes, my muscles begin to tremble. It is sixty or eighty feet to the foot of the precipice. If I lose my hold I shall fall to the bottom, and then perhaps roll over the bench, and tumble still farther down the cliff. At this instant it occurs to Bradley to take off his drawers, which he does, and swings them down to me. I hug close to the rock, let go with my hand, seize the dangling legs, and, with his assistance, I am enabled to gain the top.

然后我们走到一块半岛状岩石上，做必要的观测，测定它高出营地的海拔，返回时找到一条容易的下山路。

Then we walk out on a peninsular rock, make the necessary observations for determining its altitude above camp, and return, finding an easy way down.

6 月 19 日。今天豪兰、布拉德利和我驾「爱玛·迪恩」号逆流上溯扬帕河。河水大涨，水流湍急，逆流只能缓缓前进。扬帕河这一段峡谷是浅灰色砂岩切的。河弯很多，急水通常在外侧，冲刷着常达千英尺的垂直崖壁。许多弯道中央，上方岩石悬垂在河上。对岸崖壁破碎、嶙峋、倾斜，间或有侧谷进来。划到相当疲惫时我们停下，借一处破碎的地方爬出峡谷。到了上面，能顺着扬帕河望出好几英里。

_June 19._--To-day, Howland, Bradley, and I take the _Emma Dean_, and start up the Yampa River. The stream is much swollen, the current swift, and we are able to make but slow progress against it. The cañon in this part of the course of the Yampa is cut through light gray sandstone. The river is very winding, and the swifter water is usually found on the outside of the curve, sweeping against vertical cliffs, often a thousand feet high. In the center of these curves, in many places, the rock above overhangs the river. On the opposite side, the walls are broken, craggy, and sloping, and occasionally side cañons enter. When we have rowed until we are quite tired we stop, and take advantage of one of these broken places to climb out of the cañon. When above, we can look up the Yampa for a distance of several miles.

从峡谷崖壁的顶部，岩石缓缓向后升起一两英里，形成一片谷地般的景象：不规则的圆形砂岩地面，而峡谷正是谷地中央的一道深沟。这条谷地北缘高出河面两千五百到三千英尺，南缘则没那么高。北缘上立着不少山峰，最高的一座已取名「道斯山」。

From the summit of the immediate walls of the cañon the rocks rise gently back for a distance of a mile or two, having the appearance of a valley, with an irregular, rounded sandstone floor, and in the center of the valley a deep gorge, which is the cañon. The rim of this valley on the north is from two thousand five hundred to three thousand feet above the river; on the south, it is not so high. A number of peaks stand on this northern rim, the highest of which has received the name Mount Dawes.

傍晚我们下到船边，顺急流滑回回声公园的营地，二十分钟滑下四五英里——这段路早上可是硬划了好几小时才上去的。

Late in the afternoon we descend to our boat, and return to camp in Echo Park, gliding down in twenty minutes on the rapid river a distance of four or five miles, which was only made up stream by several hours’ hard rowing in the morning.

6 月 20 日。今早两人把我沿扬帕河送了一段，我出去爬山。上到峡谷顶，我在大片光裸砂岩上行走，时而横穿沟壑，中午到达道斯山山顶。从这里北望，在朦胧的远处能看到甜水山和风河山，远在一百多英里外；西北是瓦萨奇山和温塔山的峰峦；东边能看到落基山的西坡，远在一百五十英里外。

_June 20._--This morning two of the men take me up the Yampa for a short distance, and I go out to climb. Having reached the top of the cañon, I walk over long stretches of naked sandstone, crossing gulches now and then, and by noon reach the summit of Mount Dawes. From this point I can look away to the north, and see in the dim distance the Sweetwater and Wind River Mountains, more than a hundred miles away. To the northwest, the Wasatch Mountains are in view and peaks of the Uinta. To the east, I can see the western slopes of the Rocky Mountains, more than a hundred and fifty miles distant.

今天的空气异常清澈；山峦和土丘轮廓分明，河谷在透视中伸展开去，我能俯瞰深深的峡谷沟壑，看见闪光的河水。

The air is singularly clear to-day; mountains and buttes stand in sharp outline, valleys stretch out in the perspective, and I can look down into the deep cañon gorges and see gleaming waters.

下山时，我翻过一道靠近洛多尔峡谷边缘的山脊，它的最高点几乎与前一座山一样高。

Descending, I cross a ridge near the brink of the Cañon of Lodore, the highest point of which is nearly as high as the last mentioned mountain.

傍晚我站在这高处，发现一座显然由人手筑成的纪念碑。岩石缝隙间长着几株植物，全都覆着程度不一的地衣——说明这石堆是很久以前垒的。这列山峰是温塔山脉的东延部分，取名「埃斯卡兰特山脉」，为纪念一位约一个世纪前在这一带游历的西班牙神父；也许这位可敬的神父正是垒这纪念碑的人。

Late in the afternoon I stand on this elevated point, and discover a monument that has evidently been built by human hands. A few plants are growing in the joints between the rocks, and all are lichened over to a greater or less extent, showing evidences that the pile was built a long time ago. This line of peaks, the eastern extension of the Uinta Mountains, has received the name of Sierra Escalante, in honor of a Spanish priest, who traveled in this region of country nearly a century ago; and, perchance, the reverend father built this monument.

现在我回到河边，朝天放一枪，作为让船来接我回营的信号。我们在公园这段时间，队员们抓到了不少鱼，供应很充足——这给我们的伙食添了不少彩。

Now I return to the river and discharge my gun, as a signal for the boat to come and take me down to camp. While we have been in the park, the men have succeeded in catching quite a number of fish, and we have an abundant supply. This is quite an addition to our _cuisine_.

6 月 21 日。我们绕过长岩石，进入又一条峡谷。崖壁高而垂直；峡谷很窄；河水灌满下方整个空间，崖脚没有一处可登岸。加入扬帕河后绿河大大增大了，我们现在面对一条大得多的河。所有这些水体被约束在窄水道里、以极高的速度奔涌，被突出的岩石和短弯搅成漩涡、打转，河水跳着华尔兹穿过峡谷，奏出它自己的汩汩、奔涌、咆哮的音乐。这峡谷比我们见过的任何一条都窄。我们费尽力气才控制住船。它们从一边转到另一边，我们不知道自己在往哪去，也根本没法让船头一直朝下游。起初这让我们很惊慌，但很快发现危险其实不大——河里总有一股向下游推进的主流，这旋转只是它的附带现象；这是河流欢乐的舞性，要跳着舞穿过这幽深的黑峡；我们也很高兴地加入了这场游戏。

_June 21._--We float around the long rock, and enter another cañon. The walls are high and vertical; the cañon is narrow; and the river fills the whole space below, so that there is no landing-place at the foot of the cliff. The Green is greatly increased by the Yampa, and we now have a much larger river. All this volume of water, confined, as it is, in a narrow channel, and rushing with great velocity, is set eddying and spinning in whirlpools by projecting rocks and short curves, and the waters waltz their way through the cañon, making their own rippling, rushing, roaring music. The cañon is much narrower than any we have seen. With difficulty we manage our boats. They spin about from side to side, and we know not where we are going, and find it impossible to keep them headed down the stream. At first, this causes us great alarm, but we soon find there is but little danger, and that there is a general movement of progression down the river, to which this whirling is but an adjunct; and it is the merry mood of the river to dance through this deep, dark gorge; and right gaily do we join in the sport.

很快我们的欢乐被一道瀑布打断；它咆哮的号令让我们把全部力气用在桨上，逆着旋转的水流拼命划。「爱玛·迪恩」号被顶到一块崖壁前，离瀑布边缘约五十英尺。靠着在靠墙一侧拼命划桨、仿佛要逆流而上，我们能把它抵在岩石上。后面的船被示意在能靠岸的地方靠岸。「峡谷少女」号被划到左壁，靠持续划桨，他们也能稳住它。「姐妹」号被驶进右侧一个凹龛，那里一个漩涡正跳着舞，它也加入了。如今我的小船只靠极限的用力才抵住崖壁，逆流前进是不可能的。查看后，我在岩石上找到一条水平裂缝，高出水面约十英尺，在我们下游一船长处，于是把它放下去到那个位置。一名队员爬进裂缝，他在里面只能勉强爬行；我们把绳扔给他，他把绳系牢在岩石上，我们的船拴住了。然后我跟着爬进裂缝，我们向上游爬了五十多英尺，找到一个破碎的地方，能再爬高五十英尺。这里我们站在一条岩架上，它顺着下游延伸到瀑布上方的一个点，那里塌落了，堆着一堆岩石，我们能踩着它们下到河边。

Soon our revel is interrupted by a cataract; its roaring command is heeded by all our power at the oars, and we pull against the whirling current. The _Emma Dean_ is brought up against a cliff, about fifty feet above the brink of the fall. By vigorously plying the oars on the side opposite the wall, as if to pull up stream, we can hold her against the rock. The boats behind are signaled to land where they can. The _Maid of the Cañon_ is pulled to the left wall, and, by constant rowing, they can hold her also. The _Sister_ is run into an alcove on the right, where an eddy is in a dance, and in this she joins. Now my little boat is held against the wall only by the utmost exertion, and it is impossible to make headway against the current. On examination, I find a horizontal crevice in the rock, about ten feet above the water, and a boat’s length below us, so we let her down to that point. One of the men clambers into the crevice, in which he can just crawl; we toss him the line, which he makes fast in the rocks, and now our boat is tied up. Then I follow into the crevice, and we crawl along a distance of fifty feet, or more, up stream, and find a broken place, where we can climb about fifty feet higher. Here we stand on a shelf, that passes along down stream to a point above the falls, where it is broken down, and a pile of rocks, over which we can descend to the river, is lying against the foot of the cliff.

前面提到有一条船在河对岸。我打信号让他们把船沿着崖壁拉上来，但办不到；又让他们过河。他们照做了，在我们这边、爱玛·迪恩号拴住处的上方靠上崖壁。

It has been mentioned that one of the boats is on the other side. I signal for the men to pull her up alongside of the wall, but it cannot be done; then to cross. This they do, gaining the wall on our side just above where the _Emma Dean_ is tied.

第三条船不见了，在一个凹处的漩涡里打转。我四下寻找，又找到一条水平裂缝，沿它爬到那船正上方的水面处，大声长喊，终于让船员们明白我要他们紧贴崖壁把船放下来。他们靠着利用崖面每一处裂缝和凸起，做到了这一点，三条船于是在瀑布上方几码处会合。现在把一根绳引到岩架上，船就能放到下方破碎的岩石处。我们照做了，做了一段短搬运，这里的麻烦就结束了。

The third boat is out of sight, whirling in the eddy of a recess. Looking about, I find another horizontal crevice, along which I crawl to a point just over the water, where this boat is lying, and, calling loud and long, I finally succeed in making the crew understand that I want them to bring the boat down, hugging the wall. This they accomplish, by taking advantage of every crevice and knob on the face of the cliff, so that we have the three boats together at a point a few yards above the falls. Now, by passing a line up on the shelf, the boats can be let down to the broken rocks below. This we do, and, making a short portage, our troubles here are over.

瀑布下方，峡谷变宽了，河与崖壁之间或多或少有了些空间；但河虽宽，水流却急，在岩石间以骇人的速度翻滚。我们极其小心地前进，大船完全靠信号行驶。

Below the falls, the cañon is wider, and there is more or less space between the river and the walls; but the stream, though wide, is rapid, and rolls at a fearful rate among the rocks. We proceed with great caution, and run the large boats altogether by signal.

夜里我们在一条小溪口扎营，溪里给我们供了一顿丰盛的鳟鱼晚餐。在营中，今晚我们讨论给这条峡谷取哪几个名字好。中午遇到的那道瀑布处，它的特征骤然改变：上方极窄、崖壁几乎垂直；下方峡谷宽得多、更展开，高高的侧壁上能看到岩峰、尖塔和塔岩。许多狂野的窄侧谷进入，崖壁也破碎得多。提了很多建议后，我们的选择落在两个名字上：「漩涡峡谷」和「嶙峋峡谷」，两者对上下两段都不完全贴切；但我们先搁下讨论，商定最好等看看峡谷下方是什么样再最后定名。

At night we camp at the mouth of a small creek, which affords us a good supper of trout. In camp, to-night, we discuss the propriety of several different names for this cañon. At the falls, encountered at noon, its characteristics change suddenly. Above, it is very narrow, and the walls are almost vertical; below, the cañon is much wider, and more flaring; and, high up on the sides, crags, pinnacles, and towers are seen. A number of wild, narrow side cañons enter, and the walls are much broken. After many suggestions, our choice rests between two names, Whirlpool Cañon and Craggy Cañon, neither of which is strictly appropriate for both parts of it; but we leave the discussion at this point, with the understanding that it is best, before finally deciding on a name, to wait until we see what the cañon is below.

6 月 22 日。仍在做短搬运、用绳放船。今天等午饭时，我爬上一处能看清下游两三英里河面的地方，觉得我们可以来一次长跑。午饭后出发；大船十五分钟后跟上，留意靠岸信号。我们划进河心，顺急流滑下，只偶尔划几桨调整方向。真是一段纵情狂奔！掠过礁石和岛屿！我很快充满了一种以前只有骑着快马驰骋在无垠草原上才体验过的兴奋。一英里、两英里、三英里、四英里，我们随着浪涛起伏奔跃，直到向右拐进一座美丽的公园，在一个岛上靠岸扎营。

_June 22._--Still making short portages and letting down with lines. While we are waiting for dinner to-day, I climb a point that gives me a good view of the river for two or three miles below, and I think we can make a long run. After dinner, we start; the large boats are to follow in fifteen minutes, and look out for the signal to land. Into the middle of the stream we row, and down the rapid river we glide, only making strokes enough with the oars to guide the boat. What a headlong ride it is! shooting past rocks and islands! I am soon filled with exhilaration only experienced before in riding a fleet horse over the outstretched prairie. One, two, three, four miles we go, rearing and plunging with the waves, until we wheel to the right into a beautiful park, and land on an island, where we go into camp.

日落前一两个小时，我渡到对岸，爬上一处能俯瞰这座公园及其四周的岩石。东边它被一道高峻的山脊围住；北、西、南三面是一圈光裸的丘陵。宽阔深沉的河蜿蜒穿过公园，被许多树木葱茏的岛屿打断；所以我命名「岛屿公园」，并决定把上面那条峡谷叫「漩涡峡谷」。

An hour or two before sunset, I cross to the mainland, and climb a point of rocks where I can overlook the park and its surroundings. On the east it is bounded by a high mountain ridge. A semicircle of naked hills bounds it on the north, west, and south. The broad, deep river meanders through the park, interrupted by many wooded islands; so I name it Island Park, and decide to call the cañon above Whirlpool Cañon.

6 月 23 日。今天留在营地修船——它们受了硬碰，正在漏水。两人带气压计去爬漩涡峡谷脚下的崖壁、测量崖高；另一人去山上打猎；布拉德利和我在岩石间待了一整天，研究一处有趣的地质褶皱并采集化石。傍晚猎人回来，带回一头又肥又好的鹿，于是我们用他的名字给那座山命名「霍金斯山」。天黑前我们把营迁到公园下端，顺河漂了约四英里。

_June 23._--We remain in camp to-day to repair our boats, which have had hard knocks, and are leaking. Two of the men go out with the barometer to climb the cliff at the foot of Whirlpool Cañon and measure the walls; another goes on the mountain to hunt; and Bradley and I spend the day among the rocks, studying an interesting geological fold and collecting fossils. Late in the afternoon, the hunter returns, and brings with him a fine, fat deer, so we give his name to the mountain--Mount Hawkins. Just before night we move camp to the lower end of the park, floating down the river about four miles.

6 月 24 日。我和布拉德利一早就去爬东边的山脊，测得山顶比营地高近三千英尺，爬上它颇费了些力气；但到了顶上——多么壮观的景色！温塔山脉伸出一道长长的支脊伸向南，河沿它纵向穿过。我们从洛多尔和漩涡峡谷一路下来，切穿了温塔山南坡；漩涡峡谷的下端伸进这道支脊，但河并没有把它整个劈开，而是在漩涡峡谷脚下向右一个大弧弯向西北，穿过岛屿公园。在公园下端，河又转向东南，切进山体直到山心，然后绕向西南，把这条山脊劈开六英里，几乎直到山脚，再向左转出山去。站在霍金斯山顶，这一切尽收眼底，于是我们把下方这道峡谷命名「劈山峡谷」。

_June 24._--Bradley and I start early to climb the mountain ridge to the east; find its summit to be nearly three thousand feet above camp, and it has required some labor to scale it; but on its top, what a view! There is a long spur running out from the Uinta Mountains toward the south, and the river runs lengthwise through it. Coming down Lodore and Whirlpool Cañons, we cut through the southern slope of the Uinta Mountains; and the lower end of this latter cañon runs into the spur, but, instead of splitting it the whole length, the river wheels to the right at the foot of Whirlpool Cañon, in a great curve to the northwest, through Island Park. At the lower end of the park, the river turns again to the southeast, and cuts into the mountain to its center, and then makes a detour to the southwest, splitting the mountain ridge for a distance of six miles nearly to its foot, and then turns out of it to the left. All this we can see where we stand on the summit of Mount Hawkins, and so we name the gorge below Split Mountain Cañon.

我们站在三千英尺高处俯瞰它的水——水被浪涛搅动，泛着白沫。峡谷崖壁布满岩峰、尖塔、扶壁般的塔岩和悬垂的穹顶。向右转，公园在我们下方，岛上的树丛倒映在深静的水中。肥沃的草地向两边伸展，直到一道从远山斜下的缓坡边缘。这些平原几乎全是光裸的岩石，与草地形成奇特的对照：蓝紫色、浅黄和粉色、朱红和棕色的岩石，所有颜色都明亮清晰。十几条一年大部分时间干涸的小溪，从半圆形裸露地层间流下，从岛屿中心向盆缘辐射。每条溪都有自己的支流系统，每条支流又有更细的分支，再继续分岔，于是这片倾斜的岩坡被精致地浮雕着。不同颜色地层形成的岩带在两侧平行排列。被起伏地貌改变的透视让岩带呈现波浪状外观，那些高饱和的色彩在正午阳光下闪着缎子般的光泽。我们真想把它叫「彩虹公园」。岩带之外是温塔山和瓦萨奇山，带着它们的松林、雪原和光裸的峰顶。现在我们转向右，仰望漩涡峡谷——一条深谷，河在谷底，一个阴森的裂隙，狂浪在其中咆哮；但在这个距离上，它显得一片寂静。

We are standing three thousand feet above its waters, which are troubled with billows, and white with foam. Its walls are set with crags and peaks, and buttressed towers, and overhanging domes. Turning to the right, the park is below us, with its island groves reflected by the deep, quiet waters. Rich meadows stretch out on either hand, to the verge of a sloping plain, that comes down from the distant mountains. These plains are of almost naked rock, in strange contrast to the meadows; blue and lilac colored rocks, buff and pink, vermilion and brown, and all these colors clear and bright. A dozen little creeks, dry the greater part of the year, run down through the half circle of exposed formations, radiating from the island-center to the rim of the basin. Each creek has its system of side streams, and each side stream has its system of laterals, and, again, these are divided, so that this outstretched slope of rock is elaborately embossed. Beds of different colored formations run in parallel bands on either side. The perspective, modified by the undulations, gives the bands a waved appearance, and the high colors gleam in the midday sun with the luster of satin. We are tempted to call this Rainbow Park. Away beyond these beds are the Uinta and Wasatch Mountains, with their pine forests and snow fields and naked peaks. Now we turn to the right, and look up Whirlpool Cañon, a deep gorge, with a river in the bottom--a gloomy chasm, where mad waves roar; but, at this distance and altitude, the river is but a rippling brook, and the chasm a narrow cleft. The top of the mountain on which we stand is a broad, grassy table, and a herd of deer is feeding in the distance. Walking over to the southeast, we look down into the valley of White River, and beyond that see the far distant Rocky Mountains, in mellow, perspective haze, through which snow fields shine.

6 月 25 日。今晨我们进入劈山峡谷，从一个宽阔、展开、明亮的门驶进去。仔细查看后我们闯了两三段激流。然后是一连串六七段，必须用绳放船过去。这占了一整天，夜里我们在一座大洞穴口扎营。

_June 25._--This morning, we enter Split Mountain Cañon, sailing in through a broad, flaring, brilliant gateway. We run two or three rapids after they have been carefully examined. Then we have a series of six or eight, over which we are compelled to pass by letting the boats down with lines. This occupies the entire day, and we camp at night at the mouth of a great cave.

洞穴在一段激流脚下，浪几乎打进洞的最深处。我们能沿旁边一条小岩架走到洞后部。燕子在洞顶筑巢，它们盘旋着飞进来，啁啾着责骂我们这些闯入者；但它们的喧闹几乎被水声淹没。从洞口望出去，能看到上游远处一列岩峰像哨兵一样立在河两侧，霍金斯山在更远处。

The cave is at the foot of one of these rapids, and the waves dash in nearly to its very end. We can pass along a little shelf at the side until we reach the back part. Swallows have built their nests in the ceiling, and they wheel in, chattering and scolding at our intrusion; but their clamor is almost drowned by the noise of the waters. Looking out of the cave, we can see, far up the river, a line of crags standing sentinel on either side, and Mount Hawkins in the distance.

6 月 26 日。整个上午都在把大船弄过激流。今天下午，我们发现三道紧密相连的瀑布。我们把干粮从岩石上搬过去，让船冲下瀑布，在下方漩涡里用绳止住、带到岸边。三点钟我们全部上船。急流以极快的速度载我们向下，不时靠恰到好处的一两桨绕过礁石。有一处，河从左右方向急转，与峡谷成直角，冲进一道长长的滑道，撞在右侧，水在那里堆成巨大的浪涛，翻涌成碎浪。我们滑进滑道后才看见危险，已经来不及停。右侧硬划两三桨，我们顿了一下，以为要被撞上岩石。船头高高跃上一道大浪；回弹的水把我们抛回，危险过去了。下一瞬间，其他船被匆忙示意在左岸靠岸。办成之后，队员们沿岸走，让船贴着岸边，随流绕过去。重新出发，我们很快驶进一座美丽的河谷，沿它滑行十英里，在一棵古老的巨大三角叶杨下扎营。这显然是印第安人常来的地方：地上散着帐篷杆，可见近日营火的余烬。左边平原上，羚羊在吃草。不时有一只羊抬起头……

_June 26._--The forenoon is spent in getting our large boats over the rapids. This afternoon, we find three falls in close succession. We carry our rations over the rocks, and let our boats shoot over the falls, checking and bringing them to land with lines in the eddies below. At three o’clock we are all aboard again. Down the river we are carried by the swift waters at great speed, sheering around a rock now and then with a timely stroke or two of the oars. At one point, the river turns from left to right, in a direction at right angles to the cañon, in a long chute, and strikes the right, where its waters are heaped up in great billows, that tumble back in breakers. We glide into the chute before we see the danger, and it is too late to stop. Two or three hard strokes are given on the right, and we pause for an instant, expecting to be dashed against the rock. The bow of the boat leaps high on a great wave; the rebounding waters hurl us back, and the peril is past. The next moment, the other boats are hurriedly signaled to land on the left. Accomplishing this, the men walk along the shore, holding the boats near the bank, and let them drift around. Starting again, we soon debouch into a beautiful valley, and glide down its length for ten miles, and camp under a grand old cottonwood. This is evidently a frequent resort for Indians. Tent poles are lying about, and the dead embers of late camp fires are seen. On the plains, to the left, antelope are feeding. Now and then a wolf is seen, and after dark they make the air resound with their howling.

6 月 27 日。现在我们的路是一条缓缓流动的河，密布着许多岛屿；两岸到处是树丛和天然草场，一群群羚羊在吃草。右方不时能看到远处山峦的景致。

_June 27._--Now our way is along a gently flowing river, beset with many islands; groves are seen on either side, and natural meadows, where herds of antelope are feeding. Here and there we have views of the distant mountains on the right.

下午我们向西绕了一个大弯，又回到距中午出发点不过半英里多一点的地方，在一道高崖下扎营过夜。

During the afternoon, we make a long detour to the west, and return again, to a point not more than half a mile from where we started at noon, and here we camp, for the night, under a high bluff.

6 月 28 日。今天河两岸的景色与昨日大致相同，只是我们在西边的河谷里发现了两三个湖。午饭后刚跑了几分钟，就发现了温塔河口——一条从西面流来的河。沿这条河的河谷上行约四十英里，就是尤特印第安人的保留地。我们打算去那里，看能不能补充我们的行军餐具，也许还能给朋友寄信。我们也想在这里建立一座天文观测站；因此这里将是我们的落脚点，要待上几天。

_June 28._--To-day, the scenery on either side of the river is much the same as that of yesterday, except that two or three lakes are discovered, lying in the valley to the west. After dinner, we run but a few minutes, when we discover the mouth of the Uinta, a river coming in from the west. Up the valley of this stream, about forty miles, the reservation of the Uinta Indians is situated. We propose to go there, and see if we can replenish our mess kit, and, perhaps, send letters to friends. We also desire to establish an astronomic station here; and hence this will be our stopping place for several days.

几年前，伯苏德上尉勘测了一条从盐湖城到丹佛的驿道，这里正是他渡过绿河的地方。他的队伍曾在这里扎营一段时间，造了一条渡船、修了一条路。

Some years ago, Captain Berthoud surveyed a stage route from Salt Lake City to Denver, and this is the place where he crossed the Green River. His party was encamped here for some time, constructing a ferry boat and opening a road.

温塔河口上方不远处，绿河西岸有一个几千英亩大的湖。我们把船搬过它与河之间的分水岭，在平静的湖水上划了一阵，还打了几只鸭子。

A little above the mouth of the Uinta, on the west side of the Green, there is a lake of several thousand acres. We carry our boat across the divide between this and the river, have a row on its quiet waters, and succeed in shooting several ducks.

6 月 29 日。从这里往下一又四分之三英里，是白河与绿河的汇合处。白河源头远在东部落基山。今晨我渡过绿河，走进白河的河谷，沿它蜿蜒的河道走了好几英里，最后望见一些形状奇特的岩石——休斯将军在他的日志里称之为「鬼怪城」。我们去年冬天的营地，在今天所达之点上游一百英里处。河道大部分在峡谷里，但有些地方有河谷。除了这些小河谷，这一带是一片大荒凉：干旱、几乎无树，有崖、丘陵、岩架和流沙。不过沿绿河，从劈山峡谷脚下到温塔河口下方一段，有许多三角叶杨林、天然草场和沃土。这片可耕地带东沿白河、西沿温塔河延伸一段，用不了多久移民就会进入这里安家。

_June 29._--A mile and three quarters from here is the junction of the White River with the Green. The White has its source far to the east, in the Rocky Mountains. This morning, I cross the Green, and go over into the valley of the White, and extend my walk several miles along its winding way, until, at last, I come in sight of some strangely carved rocks, named by General Hughes, in his journal, “Goblin City.” Our last winter’s camp was situated a hundred miles above the point reached to-day. The course of the river, for much of the distance, is through cañons; but, at some places, valleys are found. Excepting these little valleys, the region is one of great desolation: arid, almost treeless, bluffs, hills, ledges of rock, and drifting sands. Along the course of the Green, however, from the foot of Split Mountain Cañon to a point some distance below the mouth of the Uinta, there are many groves of cottonwood, natural meadows, and rich lands. This arable belt extends some distance up the White River, on the east, and the Uinta, on the west, and the time must soon come when settlers will penetrate this country, and make homes.

6 月 30 日。今天我们沿温塔河划了一趟，但逆着湍急的水流没能前进多少，于是断定只能全程步行到管理局。

_June 30._--We have a row up the Uinta to-day, but are not able to make much headway against the swift current, and hence conclude we must walk all the way to the agency.

7 月 1 日。这两天用来测定当地时间、做经纬度观测，并到邻近地区考察。今晨我带着两名队员出发去「管理局」（印第安保留地管理机构）。这是一段艰辛的步行，其中二十英里要穿过一片沙漠。不时还得蹚过河，来回横渡。傍晚我们渡过几条美丽的溪流——都是温塔河的支流——穿过松林和草场，黄昏时分到达保留地。管理员多兹上尉不在，去盐湖城了，但他的助手们很客气地接待了我们。重新看到一所房子、看到一些文明的迹象，是件挺愉快的事，即使它是在印第安保留地上、离白人最近的住家有好几天路程。

_July 1._--Two days have been employed in obtaining the local time, taking observations for latitude and longitude, and making excursions into the adjacent country. This morning, with two of the men, I start for the Agency. It is a toilsome walk, twenty miles of the distance being across a sand desert. Occasionally, we have to wade the river, crossing it back and forth. Toward evening, we cross several beautiful streams, which are tributaries of the Uinta, and we pass through pine groves and meadows, arriving just at dusk at the Reservation. Captain Dodds, the agent, is away, having gone to Salt Lake City, but his assistants received us very kindly. It is rather pleasant to see a house once more, and some evidences of civilization, even if it is on an Indian reservation, several days’ ride from the nearest home of the white man.

7 月 2 日。今早我去拜访老酋长 Tsau'-wi-at。这位老酋长只是一个人的残骸，再也没有影响力了。看着他，你几乎难以相信他是个人。他的皮肤干缩、布满皱纹，仿佛只包着一副骨架。据说他一百多岁了。我和他略作交谈，但他的言语语无伦次，只是似乎很自豪地给我看他的一些奖章——那一定是多年前颁发给他的。他有一支烟斗，说已经用了很久。我提出和他交换，他似乎乐于接受；于是我的烟斗收藏又添了一支。他的妻子——人称「主教」——是个非常健谈的老妇人；她很有影响力，备受尊敬。她是我所认识的唯一一个在议事圈里占有一席之地的印第安女人。她看起来比她丈夫年轻得多，虽然满脸皱纹、不好看，却依然精力充沛。她跟我谈了很多关于族人的境况，似乎很急切地希望他们学会种地、拥有农场、像白人一样生活。和这两位老人谈了两小时后，我去看农场。它们坐落在一片非常美丽的地方，许多清澈的溪流蜿蜒穿过冲积平原和草场。这些溪流落差不小，很容易把水从上游引出，漫灌土地。

_July 2._--I go, this morning, to visit _Tsau′-wi-at_. This old chief is but the wreck of a man, and no longer has influence. Looking at him, you can scarcely realize that he is a man. His skin is shrunken, wrinkled, and dry, and seems to cover no more than a form of bones. He is said to be more than a hundred years old. I talk a little with him, but his conversation is incoherent, though he seems to take pride in showing me some medals, that must have been given him many years ago. He has a pipe which, he says, he has used a long time. I offer to exchange with him, and he seems to be glad to accept; so I add another to my collection of pipes. His wife, “The Bishop,” as she is called, is a very garrulous old woman; she exerts a great influence, and is much revered. She is the only Indian woman I have known to occupy a place in the council ring. She seems very much younger than her husband, and, though wrinkled and ugly, is still vigorous. She has much to say to me concerning the condition of the people, and seems very anxious that they should learn to cultivate the soil, own farms, and live like white men. After talking a couple of hours with these old people, I go to see the farms. They are situated in a very beautiful district, where many fine streams of water meander across alluvial plains and meadows. These creeks have quite a fall, and it is very easy to take their waters out above, and, with them, overflow the lands.

应当记得，在这干燥的气候里，要种好庄稼必须灌溉。相当多的印第安人每人有一小块两三英亩的地，种着小麦、土豆、芜菁、南瓜、西瓜和其他蔬菜。大部分庄稼长得不错，他们带着那么自豪的神情让我们看他们能像白人一样种庄稼，真叫人吃惊。他们仍住在帐篷里，拒绝盖房子，理由是：谁死在帐篷里，那帐篷就会被废弃，常常连死者所有财物一起烧掉；给他们盖了房子后，也是同样处理。以他们不洁的习惯，固定住所确实不会是什么愉快的地方。这座美丽的河谷曾是一个比现在的尤特人文明程度更高的民族的家。证据相当多：昨天来这里的路上，我们沿途多处发现陶器碎片；今天在小小农场间漫步，我发现了古屋的基址和磨石——那不是游牧民族用的，因为对这样的部族来说太重没法搬，而且磨得很深。印第安人见我对此感兴趣，特意带我看了好几处留有这些遗迹的地方，并说他们自己也不知道过去住在这里的是什么人。他们还告诉我，峡谷深处岩石上刻满了图画。

It will be remembered that irrigation is necessary, in this dry climate, to successful farming. Quite a number of Indians have each a patch of ground, of two or three acres, on which they are raising wheat, potatoes, turnips, pumpkins, melons, and other vegetables. Most of the crops are looking well, and it is rather surprising with what pride they show us that they are able to cultivate crops like white men. They are still occupying lodges, and refuse to build houses, assigning as a reason that when any one dies in a lodge it is always abandoned, and very often burned with all the effects of the deceased, and when houses have been built for them they have been treated in the same way. With their unclean habits, a fixed residence would doubtless be no pleasant place. This beautiful valley has been the home of a people of a higher grade of civilization than the present Utes. Evidences of this are quite abundant; on our way here yesterday we discovered, in many places along the trail, fragments of pottery; and wandering about the little farms to-day, I find the foundations of ancient houses, and mealing stones that were not used by nomadic people, as they are too heavy to be transported by such tribes, and are deeply worn. The Indians, seeing that I am interested in these matters, take pains to show me several other places where these evidences remain, and tell me that they know nothing about the people who formerly dwelt here. They further tell me that up in the cañon the rocks are covered with pictures.

7 月 5 日。过去两天用来学习印第安语言，并收集能展示他们技艺状况的器物。

_July 5._--The last two days have been spent in studying the language of the Indians, and making collections of articles illustrating the state of arts among them.

今早弗兰克·古德曼告诉我，他决定不跟队伍继续走了，说他见够了危险。应当记得，「无名」号失事时，他正是船上的船员之一。我们的船载得相当重，所以他走我反而安心，虽然他一直是位忠实的伙伴。

Frank Goodman informs me, this morning, that he has concluded not to go on with the party, saying that he has seen danger enough. It will be remembered that he was one of the crew on the _No Name_ when she was wrecked. As our boats are rather heavily loaded, I am content that he should leave, although he has been a faithful man.

我们一早就启程返回船边，从保留地带了马同行，还有两个印第安人——他们负责把马牵回去。

We start early on our return to the boats, taking horses with us from the reservation, and two Indians, who are to bring the animals back.

漩涡峡谷长十四又四分之一英里，崖壁高从一千八百到两千四百英尺不等。河穿过岛屿公园九英里。劈山峡谷长八英里，崖上最高岩峰高出河面两千五百到两千七百英尺。在这些峡谷里，崖壁上只有柏树。

Whirlpool Cañon is fourteen and a quarter miles in length, the walls varying from one thousand eight hundred to two thousand four hundred feet in height. The course of the river through Island Park is nine miles. Split Mountain Cañon is eight miles long. The highest crags on its walls reach an altitude above the river of from two thousand five hundred to two thousand seven hundred feet. In these cañons, cedars only are found on the walls.

从劈山峡谷脚下到温塔河口，河程六十七英里。河穿过的河谷是许多羚羊的家园，我们采用了印第安名字 Won'sits Yu-av——「羚羊谷」。

The distance by river from the foot of Split Mountain Cañon to the mouth of the Uinta is sixty-seven miles. The valley through which it runs is the home of many antelope, and we have adopted the Indian name, _Won′sits Yu-av_--Antelope Valley.

CHAPTER VI FROM THE MOUTH OF THE UINTA RIVER TO THE JUNCTION OF THE

7 月 6 日。今早很早就出发。温塔河口下方不远处，我们来到一条长岛的上端。去年冬天，一个叫约翰逊的人——猎人和印第安商人——到白河谷我们的营地拜访过我们。这人有个印第安妻子，没有固定住处，通常跟着某个尤特人部族走。他告诉我，他打算春天在这岛上种些玉米、土豆和其他蔬菜，并且知道我们会经过，就邀请我们停下来随便拿，即使他不在也行；所以我们靠岸上岛。四下寻找，很快找到他的菜园，但状况很糟，种下后就没打理过。这个季节玉米还太早，但霍尔提议说土豆叶是很好的野菜，我们正想吃点咸肉以外的换换口味，就采了一大堆带上船。中午我们停下把野菜煮了当午餐；但很快队员们一个接一个病倒了——先是恶心，然后剧烈呕吐，我们在树下东倒西歪，痛得直哼哼，我有点担心，怕是中毒太深。愿意吃药的都用了催吐剂，大约下午三点半，我们全都摆脱了疼痛。杰克·萨姆纳在日记里写道：「7 月 6 日这天，土豆叶可不是好野菜。」（脚注5）

July 6.--Start early this morning. A short distance below the mouth of the Uinta, we come to the head of a long island. Last winter, a man named Johnson, a hunter and Indian trader, visited us at our camp in White River Valley. This man has an Indian wife, and, having no fixed home, usually travels with one of the Ute bands. He informed me it was his intention to plant some corn, potatoes, and other vegetables on this island in the spring, and, knowing that we would pass it, invited us to stop and help ourselves, even if he should not be there; so we land and go out on the island. Looking about, we soon discover his garden, but it is in a sad condition, having received no care since it was planted. It is yet too early in the season for corn, but Hall suggests that potato tops are good greens, and, anxious for some change from our salt meat fare, we gather a quantity and take them aboard. At noon we stop and cook our greens for dinner; but soon, one after another of the party is taken sick; nausea first, and then severe vomiting, and we tumble around under the trees, groaning with pain, and I feel a little alarmed, lest our poisoning be severe. Emetics are administered to those who are willing to take them, and about the middle of the afternoon we are all rid of the pain. Jack Sumner records in his diary that “Potato tops are not good greens on the sixth day of July.”[5]

傍晚我们进入又一条峡谷，几乎感觉不到，因为崖壁升得极缓。

This evening we enter another cañon, almost imperceptibly, as the walls rise very gently.

7 月 7 日。今天遇到静水，河以巨大而优美的弯道扫过，峡谷崖壁的高度持续上升。岩石切割面形成的陡崖常常垂直，有时呈台阶状，有些地方台阶的踏面还是倾斜的。在这些安静的弯道里形成了巨大的圆形剧场，有时刻在垂直的岩石里，有时刻在台阶上。

_July 7._--We find quiet water to-day, the river sweeping in great and beautiful curves, the cañon walls steadily increasing in altitude. The escarpment formed by the cut edges of the rock are often vertical, sometimes terraced, and in some places the treads of the terraces are sloping. In these quiet curves vast amphitheaters are formed, now in vertical rocks, now in steps.

弯道内突出的岩角通常碎成陡坡，我们偶尔在这种地方停下爬上去，从上往下看，能看见河以从容的大弯扫过对岸崖壁脚下，垂直或台阶状的壁从水边升起几百英尺。其中一座我们觉得非常对称，给它取名「萨姆纳剧场」。崖壁很少被侧谷的入口打破，我们绕过一道又一道弯，几乎是连续的壁面，绵延好几英里。

The salient point of rock within the curve is usually broken down in a steep slope, and we stop occasionally to climb up, at such a place, where, on looking down, we can see the river sweeping the foot of the opposite cliff, in a great, easy curve, with a perpendicular or terraced wall rising from the water’s edge many hundreds of feet. One of these we find very symmetrical, and name it Sumner’s Amphitheater. The cliffs are rarely broken by the entrance of side cañons, and we sweep around curve after curve, with almost continuous walls, for several miles.

傍晚，我们发现河水变得湍急得多，遇到一些激流，不算危险，但需要全神贯注。

Late in the afternoon, we find the river much rougher, and come upon rapids, not dangerous, but still demanding close attention.

夜里我们在右岸扎营，今天走了二十六英里。

We camp at night on the right bank, having made to-day twenty-six miles.

7 月 8 日。今早我和布拉德利出去爬山，升到河面上方两千多英尺，但仍未到达崖壁顶部。

_July 8._--This morning, Bradley and I go out to climb, and gain an altitude of more than two thousand feet above the river, but still do not reach the summit of the wall.

午饭后，我们穿过一片最狂野的荒凉之地。峡谷极其蜿蜒，河很急，两侧有许多侧谷进入。这些侧谷通常又有支谷，所以这片地区被切成一团灰褐色崖壁的荒野。好几处，这些侧谷之间只隔着窄壁——常达几百英尺高，但有些地方窄得厉害，下方遇到较软的岩石就崩塌了，在壁面上留下空洞，形成从一条峡谷通往另一条的通道。我们常把这些叫天然桥；但它们从来就不是为了跨过溪流。或许更该叫峡谷厅堂之间的「侧门」。

After dinner, we pass through a region of the wildest desolation. The cañon is very tortuous, the river very rapid, and many lateral cañons enter on either side. These usually have their branches, so that the region is cut into a wilderness of gray and brown cliffs. In several places, these lateral cañons are only separated from each other by narrow walls, often hundreds of feet high, but so narrow in places that where softer rocks are found below, they have crumbled away, and left holes in the wall, forming passages from one cañon into another. These we often call natural bridges; but they were never intended to span streams. They had better, perhaps, be called side doors between cañon chambers.

成堆的碎石倚在崖壁脚下；岩峰和塔形山峰随处可见；再往上是绵延的破碎崖壁，崖壁之上和之后是松林——我们透过岩石的缝隙偶尔瞥见它们。

Piles of broken rock lie against these walls; crags and tower shaped peaks are seen everywhere; and away above them, long lines of broken cliffs, and above and beyond the cliffs are pine forests, of which we obtain occasional glimpses, as we look up through a vista of rocks.

崖壁上几乎没有植被；偶尔能看到几丛矮灌木扒在岩石上，柏树从石缝里长出来——不像雨水滋润之地的柏树那样是缀满水珠的大锥体，而是丑陋的丛簇，像狼牙棒，长满尖刺。我们想把这叫「荒凉峡谷」。

The walls are almost without vegetation; a few dwarf bushes are seen here and there, clinging to the rocks, and cedars grow from the crevices--not like the cedars of a land refreshed with rains, great cones bedecked with spray, but ugly clumps, like war clubs, beset with spines. We are minded to call this the Cañon of Desolation.

今天风很烦人。因激流而翻涌的水，被迎面大风搅得更厉害。凡是大片朝南的岩面，受热的稀薄空气上升，风就从下方灌进来，沿峡谷向上或向下，或同时两个方向，形成局部的气流。

The wind annoys us much to-day. The water, rough by reason of the rapids, is made more so by head gales. Wherever a great face of rock has a southern exposure, the rarified air rises, and the wind rushes in below, either up or down the cañon, or both, causing local currents.

就在日落时分，我们闯过一段糟糕的激流，在它脚下扎营。

Just at sunset, we run a bad rapid, and camp at its foot.

7 月 9 日。今天我们穿行在一条峡谷里，崖壁参差破碎，两侧有许多侧沟或侧谷进入。河很湍急，偶尔过石滩时必须用绳。下午我们来到一条相当开阔的峡谷谷地，向西伸展，远端消失在群山间。从我们爬上的一个点，能清楚地看到它的走向，直到它带棱角的崖壁消失在视野里。

_July 9._--Our run to-day is through a cañon, with ragged, broken walls, many lateral gulches or cañons entering on either side. The river is rough, and occasionally it becomes necessary to use lines in passing rocky places. During the afternoon, we come to a rather open cañon valley, stretching up toward the west, its farther end lost in the mountains. From a point to which we climb, we obtain a good view of its course, until its angular walls are lost in the vista.

7 月 10 日。萨姆纳是个出色的机械师，正在学用六分仪测时。今天他留在营地练习。

_July 10._--Sumner, who is a fine mechanist, is learning to take observations for time with the sextant. To-day, he remains in camp to practice.

豪兰和我决定爬出去，沿一条侧谷上溯，带了一支气压计，用来测量我们经过的地层厚度。下方的气压计每半小时记录一次读数，我们的观测必须同步。要测量的岩层很厚时，我们得拼尽全力爬，好及时赶到它们的顶部；遇到较薄的岩层，就等到合适的时刻。就这样时紧时松地，我们上到峡谷崖壁顶，大约两点钟到达上方的高原。

Howland and myself determine to climb out, and start up a lateral cañon, taking a barometer with us, for the purpose of measuring the thickness of the strata over which we pass. The readings of a barometer below are recorded every half hour, and our observations must be simultaneous. Where the beds, which we desire to measure, are very thick, we must climb with the utmost speed, to reach their summits in time. Again, where there are thinner beds, we wait for the moment to arrive; and so, by hard and easy stages, we make our way to the top of the cañon wall, and reach the plateau above about two o’clock.

豪兰带着枪，看到一两英里外有鹿在吃草，就去打猎了。我去一座约半英里外、似乎是这一带最高的山峰，爬上去是为了描摹邻近地区的地形。从这个点能看到很美的景色。一条长高原横跨河流，东西走向，顶部覆着松林，夹着抬升的谷地和沟壑。高原本身被峡谷切成两半。其他侧谷从远离河流的地方起头，向下流入绿河。除此之外，还能看到深而陡的峡谷在高原上起头，向北流向温塔河和白河。还有更多峡谷从谷地起头，向南流去。高原海拔约八千英尺，把它带进了湿润地带——松林和草地的存在就是明证。高原似乎向西逐渐升高，直到融入瓦萨奇山。在这些高台地上，麋鹿和鹿很多；它们是尤特印第安人喜爱的猎场。

Howland, who has his gun with him, sees deer feeding a mile or two back, and goes off for a hunt. I go to a peak, which seems to be the highest one in this region, about half a mile distant, and climb, for the purpose of tracing the topography of the adjacent country. From this point, a fine view is obtained. A long plateau stretches across the river, in an easterly and westerly direction, the summit covered by pine forests, with intervening elevated valleys and gulches. The plateau itself is cut in two by the cañon. Other side cañons head away back from the river, and run down into the Green. Besides these, deep and abrupt cañons are seen to head back on the plateau, and run north toward the Uinta and White Rivers. Still other cañons head in the valleys, and run toward the south. The elevation of the plateau being about eight thousand feet above the level of the sea, brings it into a region of moisture, as is well attested by the forests and grassy valleys. The plateau seems to rise gradually to the west, until it merges into the Wasatch Mountains. On these high table lands, elk and deer abound; and they are favorite hunting grounds for the Ute Indians.

日落前不久，豪兰和我在侧谷口重新会合，向下走去。天晚了，我们必须非常赶，否则会被黑暗追上；所以一路能跑就跑，跳过岩架，看不清时互相把对方放下去、踩着松动的岩石走。黑暗降临时，我们离营地还有段距离，向着闪烁的营火做了一次漫长、缓慢、焦虑的下行。

A little before sunset, Howland and I meet again at the head of the side cañon, and down we start. It is late, and we must make great haste, or be caught by the darkness; so we go, running where we can; leaping over the ledges; letting each other down on the loose rocks, as long as we can see. When darkness comes, we are still some distance from camp, and a long, slow, anxious descent we make, towards the gleaming camp fire.

晚饭后做了纬度观测，天亮前只剩下两三个小时睡觉。

After supper, observations for latitude are taken, and only two or three hours for sleep remain, before daylight.

7 月 11 日。营地下方不远处我们闯了一段激流，闯的时候断了一支桨，又丢了一支——都是「爱玛·迪恩」号的。所以这艘先锋船只剩两支桨了。

_July 11._--A short distance below camp we run a rapid, and, in doing so, break an oar, and then lose another, both belonging to the _Emma Dean_. So the pioneer boat has but two oars.

我们找不到能做桨的材料，于是决定跑到某个似乎能爬出峡谷到高原松林的地方，在那里弄合适的木材做新桨。

We see nothing of which oars can be made, so we conclude to run on to some point, where it seems possible to climb out to the forests on the plateau, and there we will procure suitable timber from which to make new ones.

很快我们又接近一段激流。站在甲板上，我觉得它能闯，就继续前进。靠近后，我看见它脚下有个向左的短弯，水在那里堆向崖壁。我们想在这里靠岸，但很快发现，因为在上方急水中、又因丢了两支桨而残废，我们够不到岸边；于是船头转向下游。我们冲过一块大岩石；一道回波浪滚过我们的小船，把它灌满了。我看出这地方危险，急忙向其他船发信号，让它们找地方靠岸。这刚做完，又一道浪把小船掀翻，我被抛进水里一段距离。我很快发现游泳非常容易，我沉不下去。只要划动让头露出水面就行，虽然时不时一道碎浪盖过来，我闭上嘴，被它卷着穿过去。船在我前方二三十英尺漂着，等大浪过去，我追上它，看见萨姆纳和邓恩扒着船。一到静水，我们都游到一侧，把它翻正。翻船时邓恩失手沉了下去；他浮上来时被萨姆纳抓住，拉回船边。与此同时我们已漂了一段……

We soon approach another rapid. Standing on deck, I think it can be run, and on we go. Coming nearer, I see that at the foot it has a short turn to the left, where the waters pile up against the cliff. Here we try to land, but quickly discover that, being in swift water, above the fall, we cannot reach shore, crippled, as we are, by the loss of two oars; so the bow of the boat is turned down stream. We shoot by a big rock; a reflex wave rolls over our little boat and fills her. I see the place is dangerous, and quickly signal to the other boats to land where they can. This is scarcely completed when another wave rolls our boat over, and I am thrown some distance into the water. I soon find that swimming is very easy, and I cannot sink. It is only necessary to ply strokes sufficient to keep my head out of the water, though now and then, when a breaker rolls over me, I close my mouth, and am carried through it. The boat is drifting ahead of me twenty or thirty feet, and, when the great waves are passed, I overtake it, and find Sumner and Dunn clinging to her. As soon as we reach quiet water, we all swim to one side and turn her over. In doing this, Dunn loses his hold and goes under; when he comes up, he is caught by Sumner and pulled to the boat. In the meantime we have drifted down stream some distance, and see another rapid below. How bad it may be we cannot tell, so we swim toward shore, pulling our boat with us, with all the vigor possible, but are carried down much faster than distance toward shore is gained. At last we reach a huge pile of drift wood. Our rolls of blankets, two guns, and a barometer were in the open compartment of the boat, and, when it went over, these were thrown out. The guns and barometer are lost, but I succeeded in catching one of the rolls of blankets, as it drifted by, when we were swimming to shore; the other two are lost, and sometimes hereafter we may sleep cold.

岸上生起一大堆火，衣物摊开晾干，然后我们从漂木里挑了一根觉得能做桨的，把这一天剩下的时间都用来锯桨。

A huge fire is built on the bank, our clothing is spread to dry, and then from the drift logs we select one from which we think oars can be made, and the remainder of the day is spent in sawing them out.

7 月 12 日。今早新桨做完了，我们再次出发。闯过几段糟糕的激流，有一处做了段短搬运，中午前来到一道又长又坏的瀑布：水道左侧满是岩石，把水推向右侧，水从一块悬垂的岩石下流过。查看后我们决定闯它，在安全允许的范围内尽量贴近左侧岩石，以避开悬垂的崖壁。小船顺利闯过；另一条跟着，但队员们没能让它靠够左岸，它被一股急流冲进右侧的大浪里，被抛来抛去，布拉德利被撞到舷外，但他的脚卡在座位下，他就这样头朝下被拖在水里；他拼命挣扎，用左手抓住舷缘，时不时能把头抬出水面。在我们下方的人看来，船似乎不可能不撞到悬垂的崖壁；但鲍威尔那一刻顾不上布拉德利的险情，全力划了六七桨，危险过去了；然后他抓住布拉德利，把他拉进船里。

_July 12._--This morning, the new oars are finished, and we start once more. We pass several bad rapids, making a short portage at one, and before noon we come to a long, bad fall, where the channel is filled with rocks on the left, turning the waters to the right, where they pass under an overhanging rock. On examination, we determine to run it, keeping as close to the left hand rocks as safety will permit, in order to avoid the overhanging cliff. The little boat runs over all right; another follows, but the men are not able to keep her near enough to the left bank, and she is carried, by a swift chute, into great waves to the right, where she is tossed about, and Bradley is knocked over the side, but his foot catching under the seat, he is dragged along in the water, with his head down; making great exertion, he seizes the gunwale with his left hand, and can lift his head above water now and then. To us who are below, it seems impossible to keep the boat from going under the overhanging cliff; but Powell, for the moment, heedless of Bradley’s mishap, pulls with all his power for half a dozen strokes, when the danger is past; then he seizes Bradley, and pulls him in. The men in the boat above, seeing this, land, and she is let down by lines.

就在这里，我们驶出我们命名为「荒凉峡谷」的地方，进入一片较开阔的乡野，延续近一英里，然后进入另一条切过灰色砂岩的峡谷。

Just here we emerge from the Cañon of Desolation, as we have named it, into a more open country, which extends for a distance of nearly a mile, when we enter another cañon, cut through gray sandstone.

大约下午三点，我们遇到一个新难题。河灌满整个水道；两侧从水边起都是垂直的崖壁，一段糟糕的激流布满岩石。我们来到它的上端，在河中央一块岩石上靠岸；小船被放下去到下方另一块岩石，大船的人拉着绳；第二条船同样放下去，第三条船的绳被一起带过来。然后第三条船从上方的岩石驶出，因为我们有它的绳在下方，就拉绳收住它——它正从我们站立的岩石脚下扫过。第一条船再次顺流放下到绳的全长，第二条船被第一条放下去到它的绳长——由第一条船上的人拉住，所以它离起点有两绳长。然后第三条船被放下、经过第二条船，再往下几乎放到它绳的全长，所以它系在第二条船上，隔着另外两条船向下荡出三绳长。这样拉住，队员们能把它拽进左壁一个小湾里，在那里系牢。

About three o’clock in the afternoon we meet with a new difficulty. The river fills the entire channel; the walls are vertical on either side, from the water’s edge, and a bad rapid is beset with rocks. We come to the head of it, and land on a rock in the stream; the little boat is let down to another rock below, the men of the larger boat holding to the line; the second boat is let down in the same way, and the line of the third boat is brought with them. Now, the third boat pushes out from the upper rock, and, as we have her line below, we pull in and catch her, as she is sweeping by at the foot of the rock on which we stand. Again the first boat is let down stream the full length of her line, and the second boat is passed down by the first to the extent of her line, which is held by the men in the first boat; so she is two lines’ length from where she started. Then the third boat is let down past the second, and still down, nearly to the length of her line, so that she is fast to the second boat, and swinging down three lines’ lengths, with the other two boats intervening. Held in this way, the men are able to pull her into a cove, in the left wall, where she is made fast. But this leaves a man on the rock above, holding to the line of the little boat. When all is ready, he springs from the rock, clinging to the line with one hand, and swimming with the other, and we pull him in as he goes by. As the two boats, thus loosened, drift down, the men in the cove pull us all in, as we come opposite; then we pass around to a point of rock below the cove, close to the wall, land, and make a short portage over the worst places in the rapid, and start again.

夜里我们在一个沙海滩上扎营；风刮得像飓风；飞沙几乎迷了我们的眼；哪儿都找不到避风处。风整夜吹；沙透过毯子渗进来，堆在我们身上，直到我们像被雪堆埋住一样。天亮时我们很高兴。

At night we camp on a sand beach; the wind blows a hurricane; the drifting sand almost blinds us; and nowhere can we find shelter. The wind continues to blow all night; the sand sifts through our blankets, and piles over us, until we are covered as in a snow-drift. We are glad when morning comes.

7 月 13 日。今早我们有一段痛快的行程。河很急，有许多平滑的激流。我站在甲板上，仔细盯住前方，我们一英里又一英里地滑行，时而右桨时而左桨，刚好够把船带过岩石进入静水。中午我们从我们命名为「灰峡谷」的地方出来，在左岸一棵三角叶杨下扎营吃午饭。

_July 13._--This morning, we have an exhilarating ride. The river is swift, and there are many smooth rapids. I stand on deck, keeping careful watch ahead, and we glide along, mile after mile, plying strokes now on the right, and then on the left, just sufficient to guide our boats past the rocks into smooth water. At noon we emerge from Gray Cañon, as we have named it, and camp, for dinner, under a cottonwood tree, standing on the left bank.

广阔的沙原从河岸向两侧延伸，直到视野尽头。这些光裸流动的沙在七月的正午阳光下灿烂发光。耀眼表面反射的热量使大气产生奇特的运动：形成小小的气流，整片似乎都在颤抖、向许多方向移动——或者，看不出移动是在大气里的话，就给人一种土地不稳定的印象。平原、山丘、崖壁和远山仿佛都朦胧地漂浮在颤抖的、波浪起伏的海上，一片片景观似乎飘走、消失，又重现。

Extensive sand plains extend back from the immediate river valley, as far as we can see, on either side. These naked, drifting sands gleam brilliantly in the midday sun of July. The reflected heat from the glaring surface produces a curious motion of the atmosphere; little currents are generated, and the whole seems to be trembling and moving about in many directions, or, failing to see that the movement is in the atmosphere, it gives the impression of an unstable land. Plains, and hills, and cliffs, and distant mountains seem vaguely to be floating about in a trembling, wave rocked sea, and patches of landscape will seem to float away, and be lost, and then re-appear.

正对面有一些土台，是左侧崖壁的余脉。下方由浅蓝和板岩色的页岩、泥灰岩组成；上方是浅黄和灰色，然后是褐色的岩石。这些土台下方（天蓝色的岩层处）呈扶壁状，上方穿过灰褐岩层呈台阶状。一条长长的崖壁线或岩崖把灰峡谷切过的台地与其下的低平原隔开。肉眼能顺着河两侧追踪这些天蓝色岩层和崖壁，排成长长一线横过河道，直到在透视中淡去。这些崖壁绵延许多英里、高几百英尺；所有这些土台——巨大的岩石山体——都在舞动、淡去、重现，柔和地移动着，或者说透过变幻的大气看在眼里是这样。

Just opposite, there are buttes, that are outliers of cliffs to the left. Below, they are composed of shales and marls of light blue and slate colors; and above, the rocks are buff and gray, and then brown. The buttes are buttressed below, where the azure rocks are seen, and terraced above through the gray and brown beds. A long line of cliffs or rock escarpments separate the table lands, through which Gray Cañon is cut, from the lower plain. The eye can trace these azure beds and cliffs, on either side of the river, in a long line, extending across its course, until they fade away in the perspective. These cliffs are many miles in length, and hundreds of feet high; and all these buttes--great mountain-masses of rock--are dancing and fading away, and re-appearing, softly moving about, or so they seem to the eye, as seen through the shifting atmosphere.

下午我们的路穿过一条河谷，两侧是三角叶杨林。河又深、又宽、又静。

This afternoon, our way is through a valley, with cottonwood groves on either side. The river is deep, broad, and quiet.

距中午营地约两小时后，我们发现一个印第安渡口，几只用圆木粗粗扎起、用柳条绑在一起的木筏靠在岸边。靠岸后，我们看到有印第安人就在这几天渡河的迹象。这里正是可敬的甘尼森在 1853 年为勘测一条通往太平洋海岸的铁路路线渡河的地方。

About two hours from noon camp, we discover an Indian crossing, where a number of rafts, rudely constructed of logs and bound together by withes, are floating against the bank. On landing, we see evidences that a party of Indians have crossed within a very few days. This is the place where the lamented Gunnison crossed, in the year 1853, when making an exploration for a railroad route to the Pacific coast.

一小时后我们闯过一段长激流，在它脚下停船，查看一些由矿泉沉积的奇特岩石——这里从前一定有矿泉，但如今不再涌流了。

An hour later, we run a long rapid, and stop at its foot to examine some curious rocks, deposited by mineral springs that at one time must have existed here, but which are no longer flowing.

7 月 14 日。今早我们经过右侧一些奇特的黑色峭壁，然后是两三段短峡谷，接着发现了圣拉斐尔河口——一条从西边远山流下来的河。我们在这里停了一两个小时，沿河谷走了一小段，发现这是印第安人常来的地方。地上散落着箭头，许多非常漂亮。燧石片撒了一地，小路也磨得很深。

_July 14._--This morning, we pass some curious black bluffs on the right, then two or three short cañons, and then we discover the mouth of the San Rafael, a stream which comes down from the distant mountains in the west. Here we stop for an hour or two, and take a short walk up the valley, and find it is a frequent resort for Indians. Arrow heads are scattered about, many of them very beautiful. Flint chips are seen strewn over the ground in great profusion, and the trails are well worn.

午饭后出发，我们经过左侧一些美丽的土台，许多非常对称。它们主要由多种色调的石膏组成，从浅灰到板岩色；然后是粉色、紫色和褐色的岩层。

Starting after dinner, we pass some beautiful buttes on the left, many of which are very symmetrical. They are chiefly composed of gypsum of many hues, from light gray to slate color; then pink, purple, and brown beds.

现在我们进入又一条峡谷。随着前进，崖壁逐渐升高，峡谷顶部由同一层橙色砂岩组成。从边缘往回，高原的凹地填满了这些橙色岩层风化成的沙。沙是浓郁的奶油色、带栗色阴影，到处寸草不生，被吹积成长长的波浪状山脊。

Now, we enter another cañon. Gradually the walls rise higher and higher as we proceed, and the summit of the cañon is formed of the same beds of orange colored sandstone. Back from the brink, the hollows of the plateau are filled with sand disintegrated from these orange beds. They are of rich cream color, shaded into maroon, everywhere destitute of vegetation, and drifted into long, wave like ridges.

河的走向蜿蜒曲折，很多次几乎折回自身。水很静，要不断划桨才能前进。有时河与崖壁之间一侧或另一侧有一条窄河漫滩。在这些长而缓的弯道处，河冲刷着外壁的最底部。一道柳树镶边的草地长半岛伸进弯道内，崖脚的石堆通常覆着矮橡树。橙色砂岩结构非常均匀，崖壁通常垂直，虽然不太高。当河在一道崖壁下绕过弯道时，能看到巨大的空心穹顶，里面有许多洞穴和深凹龛，队员们经过时都大为赞叹。

The course of the river is tortuous, and it nearly doubles upon itself many times. The water is quiet, and constant rowing is necessary to make much headway. Sometimes, there is a narrow flood plain between the river and the wall, on one side or the other. Where these long, gentle curves are found, the river washes the very foot of the outer wall. A long peninsula of willow bordered meadow projects within the curve, and the talus, at the foot of the cliff, is usually covered with dwarf oaks. The orange colored sandstone is very homogeneous in structure, and the walls are usually vertical, though not very high. Where the river sweeps around a curve under a cliff, a vast hollow dome may be seen, with many caves and deep alcoves, that are greatly admired by the members of the party, as we go by.

夜里我们在左岸扎营。

We camp at night on the left bank.

7 月 15 日。我们的营地在峡谷的一个大弯里。弧线的周界朝西，我们在河东岸。正对面，一条小溪穿过一条窄侧谷流下来。我们渡过去，上去探它。就在它河口处，另一条侧谷在它与上方主峡谷之间的夹角进入。还有一条在下方峡谷与第一条提到的侧谷之间的夹角进入——所以三条侧谷在同一点进入。这些峡谷非常蜿蜒，几乎从视野里封闭起来，从河对岸看，它们像三个凹龛；我们把这命名「三龛弯」。

_July 15._--Our camp is in a great bend of the cañon. The perimeter of the curve is to the west, and we are on the east side of the river. Just opposite, a little stream comes down through a narrow side cañon. We cross, and go up to explore it. Just at its mouth, another lateral cañon enters, in the angle between the former and the main cañon above. Still another enters in the angle between the cañon below and the side cañon first mentioned, so that three side cañons enter at the same point. These cañons are very tortuous, almost closed in from view, and, seen from the opposite side of the river, they appear like three alcoves; and we name this Trin-Alcove Bend.

沿中央小湾里的小溪上行，我们从高高的砂岩壁之间穿过，在幽谷间蜿蜒。泉水从崖壁脚下喷涌；岩石间的窄通道被穿行，洞穴被进入，还看到许多侧谷。

Going up the little stream, in the central cove, we pass between high walls of sandstone, and wind about in glens. Springs gush from the rocks at the foot of the walls; narrow passages in the rocks are threaded, caves are entered, and many side cañons are observed.

右湾是一条窄而蜿蜒的峡谷，壁面悬垂，几乎挡住光线。

The right cove is a narrow, winding gorge, with overhanging walls, almost shutting out the light.

左湾是一个圆形剧场，螺旋向上，带着悬垂的岩架。上升途中，能看到一系列高低不同的盛水盆；下方右侧堆着巨岩，头顶是拱形的顶。探完这些凹龛，我们重新渡河，爬上弯角处浑圆的岩石。向每个方向望去，直到视线尽头，都是光裸的岩石。土台散布在景观上，有的浑圆成锥，有的呈扶壁状、柱状，雕出各种奇形怪状，带着深凹龛和下沉的凹陷。我们四周都是软砂岩里挖出的水盆；这些被近期的雨水灌满了。

The left is an amphitheater, turning spirally up, with overhanging shelves. A series of basins, filled with water, are seen at different altitudes, as we pass up; huge rocks are piled below on the right, and overhead there is an arched ceiling. After exploring these alcoves, we recross the river, and climb the rounded rocks on the point of the bend. In every direction, as far as we are able to see, naked rocks appear. Buttes are scattered on the landscape, here rounded into cones, there buttressed, columned, and carved in quaint shapes, with deep alcoves and sunken recesses. All about us are basins, excavated in the soft sandstones; and these have been filled by the late rains.

越过浑圆的岩石和水坑，我们看到一段美好的河景；更远处是光裸的岩石和美丽的土台，直到「天蓝崖」；天蓝崖之上和之后是「棕崖」，再往后是山峰；云朵堆在一切之上。

Over the rounded rocks and water pockets we look off on a fine stretch of river, and beyond are naked rocks and beautiful buttes to the Azure Cliffs, and beyond these, and above them, the Brown Cliffs, and still beyond, mountain peaks; and clouds piled over all.

午饭后继续前行，水很静，仍得划桨才有像样的速度。峡谷依然极其蜿蜒。

On we go, after dinner, with quiet water, still compelled to row, in order to make fair progress. The cañon is yet very tortuous.

距中午营地约六英里，我们绕过一个五英里长的大弯向右，回到距出发点四分之一英里内的地方。然后我们绕过另一个大弯向左，绕了九英里一圈，回到距弯道起点六百码处。这两圈几乎画出一个 8 字形。队员们叫它「河之领结」；所以我们命名「领结弯」。这条线的全长十四英里。

About six miles below noon camp, we go around a great bend to the right, five miles in length, and come back to a point within a quarter of a mile of where we started. Then we sweep around another great bend to the left, making a circuit of nine miles, and come back to the point within six hundred yards of the beginning of the bend. In the two circuits, we describe almost the figure 8. The men call it a bow-knot of river; so we name it Bow-Knot Bend. The line of the figure is fourteen miles in length.

今天顺这条美丽峡谷下行，有一种精致的魅力。每走一英里，峡谷就逐渐变深；崖壁对称地弯曲、庄严地拱起；颜色美丽，在许多地方倒映在静水中，几乎骗过眼睛，让观者以为自己正望进无底的深渊。我们都心情极好，觉得很欢快，队员们的打趣在壁间来回回响。我们不时吹口哨、喊叫或放一枪，听崖壁间的回荡。

There is an exquisite charm in our ride to-day down this beautiful cañon. It gradually grows deeper with every mile of travel; the walls are symmetrically curved, and grandly arched; of a beautiful color, and reflected in the quiet waters in many places, so as to almost deceive the eye, and suggest the thought, to the beholder, that he is looking into profound depths. We are all in fine spirits, feel very gay, and the badinage of the men is echoed from wall to wall. Now and then we whistle, or shout, or discharge a pistol, to listen to the reverberations among the cliffs.

夜里我们在巨大「领结」的南侧扎营，把晚餐摊在海滩上吃，给这峡谷命名「迷宫峡谷」。

At night we camp on the south side of the great Bow-Knot, and, as we eat our supper, which is spread on the beach, we name this Labyrinth Cañon.

7 月 16 日。我们仍沿着蜿蜒的路下行。经过塔形崖壁，然后发现河拓宽了好几英里，河与崖壁之间两侧都出现了草地。我们给河的这段扩展命名「塔公园」。

_July 16._--Still we go down, on our winding way. We pass tower cliffs, then we find the river widens out for several miles, and meadows are seen on either side, between the river and the walls. We name this expansion of the river Tower Park.

两点钟我们驶出迷宫峡谷，扎营。

At two o’clock we emerge from Labyrinth Cañon, and go into camp.

7 月 17 日。把迷宫峡谷与下方那条分隔开的只是一条线；今早我们立刻进入另一条峡谷。水灌满整个水道，哪里都没有靠岸的空间。崖壁不高但垂直，随着前进，它们逐渐升高。跑了两英里，河道向东方转了好几度。就在这里，一条小溪从右侧流入，壁面崩塌了；所以我们靠岸，出去看看四周的地貌。我们现在下到土台中间，处在一片地表是光裸整块岩石的地区——美丽的红砂岩，形成平滑、起伏的路面。印第安人把这叫 Toom'-pin Tu-weap'——「岩之地」，以及 Toom'-pin wu-near' Tu-weap'——「立石之地」。

_July 17._--The line which separates Labyrinth Cañon from the one below is but a line, and at once, this morning, we enter another cañon. The water fills the entire channel, so that nowhere is there room to land. The walls are low, but vertical, and, as we proceed, they gradually increase in altitude. Running a couple of miles, the river changes its course many degrees, toward the east. Just here, a little stream comes in on the right, and the wall is broken down; so we land, and go out to take a view of the surrounding country. We are now down among the buttes, and in a region the surface of which is naked, solid rock--a beautiful red sandstone, forming a smooth, undulating pavement. The Indians call this the “_Toom′-pin Tu-weap′_,” or “Rock Land,” and the “_Toom′-pin wu-near′ Tu-weap′_,” or “Land of Standing Rock.”

往南看，我们看到一座土台，形状像一个倒下的十字架。它虽在几英里外，在景观中却是不容忽视的形象，一定有好几百英尺高，很可能超过两千英尺。我们在图上记下它的位置，给它取名「十字土台」。

Off to the south we see a butte, in the form of a fallen cross. It is several miles away, still it presents no inconspicuous figure on the landscape, and must be many hundreds of feet high, probably more than two thousand. We note its position on our map, and name it “The Butte of the Cross.”

我们继续前行。许多地方，从水边升起的崖壁在两侧悬垂。水流依然安静，我们滑行着穿过一片奇异、神秘、壮丽的地区。远离河流的景观到处都是岩石——岩壁、岩桌、岩高原、岩台、岩峰——一万种奇形怪状。到处都是岩石，没有植被；没有土壤；没有沙。河以长而缓的弯道绕行于这些岩石之间。

We continue our journey. In many places the walls, which rise from the water’s edge, are overhanging on either side. The stream is still quiet, and we glide along, through a strange, weird, grand region. The landscape everywhere, away from the river, is of rock--cliffs of rock; tables of rock; plateaus of rock; terraces of rock; crags of rock--ten thousand strangely carved forms. Rocks everywhere, and no vegetation; no soil; no sand. In long, gentle curves, the river winds about these rocks.

说起这些岩石，我们不能想象成成堆的巨砾或碎石堆，而是一整片光裸的岩石大地，上面雕着巨大的形体：大教堂状的土台，高耸几百或几千英尺；无法攀登的崖壁；把河衬得渺小的峡谷壁，带着巨大的空心穹顶、高耸的尖塔，还有立在头顶边缘的石柱，全部色彩浓烈——浅黄、灰、红、棕和巧克力色；从不长地衣；从不覆苔藓；光裸，而且常常被磨光。

When speaking of these rocks, we must not conceive of piles of boulders, or heaps of fragments, but a whole land of naked rock, with giant forms carved on it: cathedral shaped buttes, towering hundreds or thousands of feet; cliffs that cannot be scaled, and cañon walls that shrink the river into insignificance, with vast, hollow domes, and tall pinnacles, and shafts set on the verge overhead, and all highly colored--buff, gray, red, brown, and chocolate; never lichened; never moss-covered; but bare, and often polished.

我们经过一处，河的两个弯道聚到一起，中间的岩石被磨穿，形成一条横穿的新水道。旧水道曾绕一个大圈向右，绕过一片曾呈圆形的半岛；然后成岛；再后来水完全离开旧水道，穿过新切口，旧河床干涸了。于是那块巨大的圆形岩石孤零零立着，四周都是陡壁，我们只找到一处可以爬上去。从它顶上看去，能看到长长一段河，紧贴着右侧悬垂的崖壁流过，但左侧与壁之间有一小片草地。弯道非常和缓、规则。我们命名「博尼塔弯」。

We pass a place where two bends of the river come together, an intervening rock having been worn away, and a new channel formed across. The old channel ran in a great circle around to the right, by what was once a circular peninsula; then an island; then the water left the old channel entirely, and passed through the cut, and the old bed of the river is dry. So the great circular rock stands by itself, with precipitous walls all about it, and we find but one place where it can be scaled. Looking from its summit, a long stretch of river is seen, sweeping close to the overhanging cliffs on the right, but having a little meadow between it and the wall on the left. The curve is very gentle and regular. We name this Bonita Bend.

就在这儿我们又爬出去，再一次对「十字土台」测方位。上到一处能俯瞰景观的高地，我们惊讶地发现，那座形状奇妙的土台其实是两座——一座立在另一座前面，从我们上一个视点看去，形成了十字的样子。

And just here we climb out once more, to take another bearing on The Butte of the Cross. Reaching an eminence, from which we can overlook the landscape, we are surprised to find that our butte, with its wonderful form, is indeed two buttes, one so standing in front of the other that, from our last point of view, it gave the appearance of a cross.

博尼塔弯下方几英里处，我们又沿岩石出去一两英里，走向「橙崖」，穿过铺着碧玉的台地。

Again, a few miles below Bonita Bend, we go out a mile or two along the rocks, toward the Orange Cliffs, passing over terraces paved with jasper.

崖壁不远，我们很快到了，在一些深的、上了彩色的凹龛里漫步——那是我们从河上就注意到的；然后回到船上。

The cliffs are not far away, and we soon reach them, and wander in some deep, painted alcoves, which attracted our attention from the river; then we return to our boats.

傍晚，水变急了，我们的船飞驰。这样疾驰一小时后，我们到了格兰德河与绿河的汇合处，即我们命名的「静水峡谷」的脚下。

Late in the afternoon, the water becomes swift, and our boats make great speed. An hour of this rapid running brings us to the junction of the Grand and Green, the foot of Stillwater Cañon, as we have named it.

这两条河在一千二百多英尺深的庄严深处汇合，远低于地表的普遍高度。静水峡谷下端的崖壁随河蜿蜒的路线弯曲得极美。格兰德河流下来的那条峡谷下端也很规则，但直得多；我们沿这条河向上望，望进远方的乡野，瞥见覆雪的山峰——一群名为「拉萨尔山脉」的山峦之巅。沿科罗拉多河往下，峡谷壁破碎得多。

These streams unite in solemn depths, more than one thousand two hundred feet below the general surface of the country. The walls of the lower end of Stillwater Cañon are very beautifully curved, as the river sweeps in its meandering course. The lower end of the cañon through which the Grand comes down, is also regular, but much more direct, and we look up this stream, and out into the country beyond, and obtain glimpses of snow clad peaks, the summits of a group of mountains known as the Sierra La Sal. Down the Colorado, the cañon walls are much broken.

我们划进格兰德河，在它西北岸扎营；我们打算在这里待几天，目的是测定经纬度和崖壁高度。大半夜都用来用六分仪做观测。

We row around into the Grand, and camp on its northwest bank; and here we propose to stay several days, for the purpose of determining the latitude and longitude, and the altitude of the walls. Much of the night is spent in making observations with the sextant.

从温塔河口到荒凉峡谷顶端的距离是二十又四分之三英里。荒凉峡谷长九十七英里；灰峡谷三十六英里。河穿过甘尼森谷地长二十七又四分之一英里；迷宫峡谷六十二又半英里。

The distance from the mouth of the Uinta to the head of the Cañon of Desolation is twenty and three-quarters miles. The Cañon of Desolation is ninety-seven miles long; Gray Cañon thirty-six. The course of the river through Gunnison’s Valley is twenty-seven and a quarter miles; Labyrinth Cañon, sixty-two and a half miles.

在荒凉峡谷里，紧贴河面最高的岩石约两千四百英尺——那是在「小木屋崖」。台地最高处在「棕崖」边缘附近。爬上峡谷的紧邻壁面，退回峡谷台地，再爬上去，我们发现高出河面的高度是 3,300 英尺。灰峡谷下端约 2,000 英尺；迷宫峡谷下端 1,300 英尺。

In the Cañon of Desolation, the highest rocks immediately over the river are about two thousand four hundred feet. This is at Log Cabin Cliff. The highest part of the terrace is near the brink of the Brown Cliffs. Climbing the immediate walls of the cañon, and passing back to the cañon terrace, and climbing that, we find the altitude, above the river, to be 3,300 feet. The lower end of Gray Cañon is about 2,000 feet; the lower end of Labyrinth Cañon, 1,300 feet.

静水峡谷长四十二又四分之三英里；最高崖壁 1,300 英尺。

Stillwater Cañon is forty-two and three-quarters miles long; the highest walls, 1,300 feet.

7 月 18 日。这一天用来测定时间、摊开我们的口粮——我们发现它们已严重受损。面粉被湿了又干、干了又湿许多次，全都发霉结块。我们用蚊帐布做了个筛子，把面粉筛了一遍，这一道工序就损失了两百多磅。自「无名」号失事以来的种种损失，加上今天扔掉的部分，我们的口粮只够两个多月了；要让它们撑这么久，我们只能祈求不再损失更多。

July 18.--The day is spent in obtaining the time, and spreading our rations, which, we find, are badly injured. The flour has been wet and dried so many times that it is all musty, and full of hard lumps. We make a sieve of mosquito netting, and run our flour through it, losing more than two hundred pounds by the process. Our losses, by the wrecking of the _No Name_, and by various mishaps since, together with the amount thrown away to-day, leave us little more than two months’ supplies, and, to make them last thus long, we must be fortunate enough to lose no more.

我们把船拖上岸、翻过来重新填缝抹沥青，萨姆纳在修气压计。我们要在这里休整一两天，打算把一切收拾到最好状态，好应付后面艰苦的行程。

We drag our boats on shore, and turn them over to recalk and pitch them, and Sumner is engaged in repairing barometers. While we are here, for a day or two, resting, we propose to put everything in the best shape for a vigorous campaign.

7 月 19 日。今早我和布拉德利出发去爬汇合处下方的左壁。我们选的路沿一条沟壑而上。在岩石间攀爬了一小时，我们发现自己进了一个巨大的圆形剧场，路被切断了。我们向左绕了半小时，发现那个方向上不去。又改试右边的岩石，发现一条窄岩架，近半英里长。有些地方它宽得能从容走过；有些地方又窄又斜，我们不得不趴下爬行。我们能从岩架边缘望下去八百英尺，看见河在岩石间翻滚奔涌；抬头向上五百英尺，崖边仿佛与天空融为一体。我们继续前行，直到崖壁再次塌落的地方。向上爬去。右边有一道窄窄的墙状岩角伸向河边，两三百英尺高、六到八百英尺长。我们绕回它与主壁相接处，发现它被一道巨大的裂隙与主壁隔开。我们钻进这道裂隙。现在，一条又长又窄的岩石横在我们与河之间。这岩石本身被纵向横向地劈裂；上方的雨水沿裂缝流下，在下方汇成水道，再流入河。裂缝通常上方窄，被水流侵蚀后下方宽，形成一张洞穴网；但每个洞穴都有一道窄窄的、蜿蜒的天窗，向上穿过岩石。

_July 19._--Bradley and I start this morning to climb the left wall below the junction. The way we have selected is up a gulch. Climbing for an hour over and among the rocks, we find ourselves in a vast amphitheater, and our way cut off. We clamber around to the left for half an hour, until we find that we cannot go up in that direction. Then we try the rocks around to the right, and discover a narrow shelf, nearly half a mile long. In some places, this is so wide that we pass along with ease; in others, it is so narrow and sloping that we are compelled to lie down and crawl. We can look over the edge of the shelf, down eight hundred feet, and see the river rolling and plunging among the rocks. Looking up five hundred feet, to the brink of the cliff, it seems to blend with the sky. We continue along, until we come to a point where the wall is again broken down. Up we climb. On the right, there is a narrow, mural point of rocks, extending toward the river, two or three hundred feet high, and six or eight hundred feet long. We come back to where this sets in, and find it cut off from the main wall by a great crevice. Into this we pass. And now, a long, narrow rock is between us and the river. The rock itself is split longitudinally and transversely; and the rains on the surface above have run down through the crevices, and gathered into channels below, and then run off into the river. The crevices are usually narrow above, and, by erosion of the streams, wider below, forming a network of caves; but each cave having a narrow, winding sky-light up through the rocks.

我们在这些廊道间游荡了一两个小时，找不到岩石塌落、可以爬上去的地方。最后我们决定试着从一条裂缝攀出去，选了一条觉得够宽、容得下身体穿过、又够窄、能手脚撑着两侧爬出去的裂缝。于是我们像爬井一样往上爬。布拉德利先爬；我把气压计递给他，然后爬过他头顶，他再把气压计递给我。就这样我们交替从彼此头顶爬过，直到钻出裂缝，来到岩石顶上。眼前展开的是一个多么壮丽的世界！下方是科罗拉多河穿行的峡谷。我们能沿它追踪好几英里，几处瞥见河水。西北方绿河从一条窄而蜿蜒的峡谷里来。东北方格兰德河从一道在我们脚下看起来深不见底的峡谷里来。西边是一道道崖壁和岩架——不是采石工人劈石料的那种岩架，而是诸神可以从中开采出整座山、滚落到下方平原上就能立成一条高峻山脉的岩架；也不是燕子筑巢的那种崖壁，而是连翱翔的鹰都还没飞到崖顶就消失在视野里的崖壁。

We wander among these corridors for an hour or two, but find no place where the rocks are broken down, so that we can climb up. At last, we determine to attempt a passage by a crevice, and select one which we think is wide enough to admit of the passage of our bodies, and yet narrow enough to climb out by pressing our hands and feet against the walls. So we climb as men would out of a well. Bradley climbs first; I hand him the barometer, then climb over his head, and he hands me the barometer. So we pass each other alternately, until we emerge from the fissure, out on the summit of the rock. And what a world of grandeur is spread before us! Below is the cañon, through which the Colorado runs. We can trace its course for miles, and at points catch glimpses of the river. From the northwest comes the Green, in a narrow, winding gorge. From the northeast comes the Grand, through a cañon that seems bottomless from where we stand. Away to the west are lines of cliffs and ledges of rock--not such ledges as you may have seen where the quarryman splits his blocks, but ledges from which the gods might quarry mountains, that, rolled out on the plain below, would stand a lofty range; and not such cliffs as you may have seen where the swallow builds its nest, but cliffs where the soaring eagle is lost to view ere he reaches the summit.

在我们与远方的崖壁之间，是 Toom'-pin wu-near' Tu-weap'——「立石之地」那些雕凿奇特、尖塔林立的岩石。峡谷对壁顶上有些我们看不懂的岩石形状。东边远处可见一群火山山脉——拉萨尔山脉。山坡覆着松林，深沟两侧是巨大的岩壁，峰顶附近可见雪原。所以群山披着统一的三色制服：绿、灰、银。无论朝哪儿看，都是一片岩石的荒野；深峡里河流消失在崖壁、塔岩和尖塔之下；四面八方有一万种奇形怪状；它们之外，群山与云朵交融。

Between us and the distant cliffs are the strangely carved and pinnacled rocks of the _Toom′-pin wu-near′ Tu-weap′_. On the summit of the opposite wall of the cañon are rock forms that we do not understand. Away to the east a group of eruptive mountains are seen--the Sierra La Sal. Their slopes are covered with pines, and deep gulches are flanked with great crags, and snow fields are seen near the summits. So the mountains are in uniform, green, gray, and silver. Wherever we look there is but a wilderness of rocks; deep gorges, where the rivers are lost below cliffs and towers and pinnacles; and ten thousand strangely carved forms in every direction; and beyond them, mountains blending with the clouds.

现在我们回营。吃晚饭时，我们很自然地谈起更好的伙食——毕竟大家都饿了。

Now we return to camp. While we are eating supper, we very naturally speak of better fare, as musty bread and spoiled bacon are not pleasant. Soon I see Hawkins down by the boat, taking up the sextant, rather a strange proceeding for him, and I question him concerning it. He replies that he is trying to find the latitude and longitude of the nearest pie.

7 月 20 日。今早鲍威尔上尉和我出去爬峡谷西壁，想查看昨天从对岸看到的那些奇怪岩石。两小时后我们到达顶部，位置在绿河与科罗拉多河之间，俯瞰两河汇合处。一条长长的岩石脖颈伸向格兰德河口。我们沿它走出去，跨过大量深裂缝。通常，光滑的岩石向两侧斜坡延伸到裂缝边。有时对我们来说，一个有趣的问题是：这坡是不是陡得站不住脚。有时一旦开始下坡，就不得不继续走，而我们并不总能确定裂缝是不是宽得跳不过去——当我们在上方用眼睛丈量时。

_July 20._--This morning, Captain Powell and I go out to climb the west wall of the cañon, for the purpose of examining the strange rocks seen yesterday from the other side. Two hours bring us to the top, at a point between the Green and Colorado, overlooking the junction of the rivers. A long neck of rock extends toward the mouth of the Grand. Out on this we walk, crossing a great number of deep crevices. Usually, the smooth rock slopes down to the fissure on either side. Sometimes it is an interesting question to us whether the slope is not so steep that we cannot stand on it. Sometimes, starting down, we are compelled to go on, and we are not always sure that the crevice is not too wide for a jump, when we measure it with our eye from above.

坡度本来也许不难走——要不是下端横着一条裂缝；而如果裂缝只有几英尺深，也不会让人恐惧。奇怪的是，当一步踏错就会把人送进深渊底部时，一个小障碍会变成多大的梗阻。爬崖面时，一个人会毫无犹豫地沿一条只有几英寸宽的石阶或岩架走——假如摔下去下方十英尺就是落点；但如果崖脚在一千英尺之下，他就会改为爬行。最后我们的路被一条又深又宽的裂缝切断，无法越过。于是我们转身走回腹地，走在那光滑光裸的、没有植被的砂岩上——除了偶尔有矮柏和矮松在巨大裂缝里找到立足之处。岩石里有巨大的水盆蓄着水；有些只有几加仑，另一些能装几百桶。

Probably the slopes would not be difficult if there was not a fissure at the lower end; nor would the fissures cause fear if they were but a few feet deep. It is curious how a little obstacle becomes a great obstruction, when a misstep would land a man in the bottom of a deep chasm. Climbing the face of a cliff, a man will walk along a step or shelf, but a few inches wide, without hesitancy, if the landing is but ten feet below, should he fall; but if the foot of the cliff is a thousand feet down, he will crawl. At last our way is cut off by a fissure so deep and wide that we cannot pass it. Then we turn and walk back into the country, over the smooth, naked sandstone, without vegetation, except that here and there dwarf cedars and piñon pines have found a footing in the huge cracks. There are great basins in the rock, holding water; some but a few gallons, others hundreds of barrels.

这一天我们就在这些奇异的景象中漫步。一条窄沟切进主峡谷的壁。沿它上行，就像爬一座山坡那样迅速上升，因为沟的源头离崖壁只有几百或几千码。但这沟又有自己的侧沟，越接近峰顶，会发现一组放射状的峡谷。这些小峡谷汇水的地带呈梯田状，多半呈圆形剧场形，有些是长方形，有些相当不规则。通常任意两条小侧谷的汇水地带之间隔着一条窄壁——一两百或三百英尺高，常常只有几英尺厚。有时这壁的上部破成一列金字塔，下部仍是整壁。现在，绿河主峡壁被许多这样的沟壑打破，每一条都有自己的侧谷和圆形剧场系统，被壁或尖塔线围住。

The day is spent in walking about through these strange scenes. A narrow gulch is cut into the wall of the main cañon. Follow this up, and you climb rapidly, as if going up a mountain side, for the gulch heads but a few hundred or a few thousand yards from the wall. But this gulch has its side gulches, and, as you come near to the summit, a group of radiating cañons is found. The spaces drained by these little cañons are terraced, and are, to a greater or less extent, of the form of amphitheaters, though some are oblong and some rather irregular. Usually, the spaces drained by any two of these little side cañons are separated by a narrow wall, one, two, or three hundred feet high, and often but a few feet in thickness. Sometimes the wall is broken into a line of pyramids above, and still remains a wall below. Now, there are a number of these gulches which break the wall of the main cañon of the Green, each one having its system of side cañons and amphitheaters, inclosed by walls, or lines of pinnacles.

在这一带，绿河的流向大致与科罗拉多河垂直，而在后者峡谷的边缘，我们发现了同样的阶梯状、围墙式的幽谷系统。这些壁、尖塔和塔岩都是砂岩，结构均匀，但颜色不均——呈现出红、浅黄、灰的宽条带。这种把岩石分段涂色的效果增加了它们的外表高度。有些地方，科罗拉多河沿岸的阶梯幽谷与绿河沿岸的连成了一片——也就是说，中间的壁被冲塌了。很少能看到松动的岩石。沙被冲走，幽谷的壁、台阶和坡面都是光滑的砂岩。

The course of the Green, at this point, is approximately at right angles to that of the Colorado, and on the brink of the latter cañon we find the same system of terraced and walled glens. The walls, and pinnacles, and towers are of sandstone, homogeneous in structure, but not in color, as they show broad bands of red, buff, and gray. This painting of the rocks, dividing them into sections, increases their apparent height. In some places, these terraced and walled glens, along the Colorado, have coalesced with those along the Green; that is, the intervening walls are broken down. It is very rarely that a loose rock is seen. The sand is washed off so that the walls, terraces, and slopes of the glens are all of smooth sandstone.

在崖壁本身里，雕出了奇异的洞穴和水道。有些地方有通上崖壁的小石阶；另一些地方，崖壁呈现所谓的「王冠拱门」；就这样我们从清晨一直游荡到傍晚，穿过幽谷、穿行在尖塔之间、攀爬崖壁。

In the walls themselves, curious caves and channels have been carved. In some places, there are little stairways up the walls; in others, the walls present what are known as royal arches; and so we wander through glens, and among pinnacles, and climb the walls from early morn until late in the afternoon.

7 月 21 日。今早我们踏上科罗拉多河。河很湍急，接连出现糟糕的激流。上午做了两次非常吃力的搬运。午饭后闯一段激流时，「爱玛·迪恩」号被灌满了，我们被抛进河里；我们扒住船，在下游第一片静水里把它扶正、舀干水；但这次事故丢了三支桨。大船在险段上方靠岸，我们做了一次搬运，占用了整个下午。夜里我们在左岸的岩石上扎营，几乎找不到躺下的地方。

_July 21._--We start this morning on the Colorado. The river is rough, and bad rapids, in close succession, are found. Two very hard portages are made during the forenoon. After dinner, in running a rapid, the _Emma Dean_ is swamped, and we are thrown into the river, we cling to her, and in the first quiet water below she is righted and bailed out; but three oars are lost in the mishap. The larger boats land above the dangerous place, and we make a portage, that occupies all the afternoon. We camp at night, on the rocks on the left bank, and can scarcely find room to lie down.

7 月 22 日。今早我们继续赶路，虽然桨不足。崖壁上够得到的范围内没有树，岸边也没有漂木，所以只能继续走，直到找到合适的材料。下游一又四分之三英里处，我们发现一大堆漂木，其中有几根三角叶杨圆木。我们从中挑了根认为最好的，队员们动手锯桨。我们的船又漏了——昨天糟糕的激流让它们受了力——所以午饭后把船翻过来，一些人去填缝。

_July 22._--This morning, we continue our journey, though short of oars. There is no timber growing on the walls within our reach, and no drift wood along the banks, so we are compelled to go on until something suitable can be found. A mile and three quarters below, we find a huge pile of drift wood, among which are some cottonwood logs. From these we select one which we think the best, and the men are set at work sawing oars. Our boats are leaking again, from the strains received in the bad rapids yesterday, so, after dinner, they are turned over, and some of the men are engaged in calking them.

鲍威尔上尉和我出去爬东边的壁，因为能看到上方有矮松，我们的目的是采集它们渗出的树脂，用来给船涂缝。我们带了一支气压计，发现崖壁越来越高了——现在测得高出河面近一千五百英尺。

Captain Powell and I go out to climb the wall to the east, for we can see dwarf pines above, and it is our purpose to collect the resin which oozes from them, to use in pitching our boats. We take a barometer with us, and find that the walls are becoming higher, for now they register an altitude, above the river, of nearly fifteen hundred feet.

7 月 23 日。一出发就遇到艰难的激流和瀑布，许多地方比我们走过的任何峡谷都更陡峭，我们决定把它命名为「瀑布峡谷」。

_July 23._--On starting, we come at once to difficult rapids and falls, that, in many places, are more abrupt than in any of the cañons through which we have passed, and we decide to name this Cataract Cañon.

从早晨到中午，河的流向朝西；景色壮丽，下方是激流和瀑布，上方崖壁布满岩峰和尖塔。刚过中午我们又折向南，扎营吃午饭。

From morning until noon, the course of the river is to the west; the scenery is grand, with rapids and falls below, and walls above, beset with crags and pinnacles. Just at noon we wheel again to the south, and go into camp for dinner.

趁厨子在做饭，布拉德利、鲍威尔上尉和我走进这个点进来的一条侧谷。我们从一条极窄的通道进去，不得不沿一条小溪涉水前行，直到一道瀑布挡住去路。然后我们向右爬上一百英尺，到一条小岩架，沿它小心走过——它很窄——直到绕过瀑布。这里峡谷豁然开朗，变成一座开阔的、以天空为穹顶的厅堂。远端是一片美丽的三角叶杨林，在我们与杨树林之间，小溪扩成三个清澈的小湖，湖底是光滑的岩石。杨树林之后，溪水化作一连串雪白闪亮的瀑布，从看似不可丈量的高处跌落。转过身，我们能透过来时的裂缝望出去，看见那条河，河那边是巍峨的崖壁。这是多么好的休憩之所！从整块岩石中凿出；天空作穹顶；瀑布泉作喷泉；林园当温室，清澈小湖可供爽身沐浴，还有一条……

While the cook is preparing it, Bradley, Captain Powell, and myself go up into a side cañon, that comes in at this point. We enter through a very narrow passage, having to wade along the course of a little stream until a cascade interrupts our progress. Then we climb to the right, for a hundred feet, until we reach a little shelf, along which we pass, walking with great care, for it is narrow, until we pass around the fall. Here the gorge widens into a spacious, sky roofed chamber. In the farther end is a beautiful grove of cottonwoods, and between us and the cottonwoods the little stream widens out into three clear lakelets, with bottoms of smooth rock. Beyond the cottonwoods, the brook tumbles, in a series of white, shining cascades, from heights that seem immeasurable. Turning around, we can look through the cleft through which we came, and see the river, with towering walls beyond. What a chamber for a resting place is this! hewn from the solid rock; the heavens for a ceiling; cascade fountains within; a grove in the conservatory, clear lakelets for a refreshing bath, and an outlook through the doorway on a raging river, with cliffs and mountains beyond.

午饭后我们的路穿过一道无法形容的壮丽峡谷。崖壁近乎垂直；河宽阔湍急，但礁石和瀑布不多。从水边到崖巅有一千六到一千八百英尺。在这巨大的深度里，河庄严地奔流。崖壁倒映在较静的河面上，我们仿佛置身地底，却又能望进倒映着无底深渊的水中。下午早些时候我们来到又一段激流和瀑布的上端，但已被前面的辛劳弄得疲惫，决定歇一歇，于是扎营，下午和晚上队员们都在讨论成功驶过下方河段的可能性。检查气压计记录，看离开格兰德河口后下降了多少，离开太平洋铁路后又下降了多少，以及到达这条大峡谷尽头前河还有多少落差。队员们得出的结论大致是：前面还有……

Our way, after dinner, is through a gorge, grand beyond description. The walls are nearly vertical; the river broad and swift, but free from rocks and falls. From the edge of the water to the brink of the cliffs it is one thousand six hundred to one thousand eight hundred feet. At this great depth, the river rolls in solemn majesty. The cliffs are reflected from the more quiet river, and we seem to be in the depths of the earth, and yet can look down into the waters that reflect a bottomless abyss. We arrive, early in the afternoon, at the head of more rapids and falls, but, wearied with past work, we determine to rest, so go into camp, and the afternoon and evening are spent by the men in discussing the probabilities of successfully navigating the river below. The barometric records are examined, to see what descent we have made since we left the mouth of the Grand, and what descent since we left the Pacific Railroad, and what fall there yet must be to the river, ere we reach the end of the great cañons. The conclusion to which the men arrive seems to be about this: that there are great descents yet to be made, but, if they are distributed in rapids and short falls, as they have been heretofore, we will be able to overcome them. But, may be, we shall come to a fall in these cañons which we cannot pass, where the walls rise from the water’s edge, so that we cannot land, and where the water is so swift that we cannot return. Such places have been found, except that the falls were not so great but that we could run them with safety. How will it be in the future! So they speculate over the serious probabilities in jesting mood, and I hear Sumner remark, “My idea is, we had better go slow, and learn to peddle.”

7 月 24 日。我们察看下方的激流。大量大岩石从崖壁崩落——巨大的棱角岩块顺着岩堆滚下，散在河道里。我们被迫连做三次搬运，全程不到四分之三英里，落差七十五英尺。在这些岩石之间，在急流槽、漩涡和大浪中，伴着奔涌的碎浪和泡沫，水为自己找出路，仍在往下翻跌。我们在距上个营地仅四分之三英里的地方停下来过夜。这天干了非常重的活，傍晚我坐在河边一块岩石上，看着水、听着它的咆哮。几小时前，太阳转到崖壁后面，深影已沉入峡谷。现在太阳无疑已经落山，因为我们看不到上方岩峰上有一点光。黑暗正在降临。浪在翻滚，泡沫的浪尖白得仿佛自带微光。近旁，一条水槽冲击着一块五十英尺高的巨大石灰岩脚下，水堆在它上面，然后……

_July 24._--We examine the rapids below. Large rocks have fallen from the walls--great, angular blocks, which have rolled down the talus, and are strewn along the channel. We are compelled to make three portages in succession, the distance being less than three-fourths of a mile, with a fall of seventy-five feet. Among these rocks, in chutes, whirlpools, and great waves, with rushing breakers and foam, the water finds its way, still tumbling down. We stop for the night, only three-fourths of a mile below the last camp. A very hard day’s work has been done, and at evening I sit on a rock by the edge of the river, to look at the water, and listen to its roar. Hours ago, deep shadows had settled into the cañon as the sun passed behind the cliffs. Now, doubtless, the sun has gone down, for we can see no glint of light on the crags above. Darkness is coming on. The waves are rolling, with crests of foam so white they seem almost to give a light of their own. Near by, a chute of water strikes the foot of a great block of limestone, fifty feet high, and the waters pile up against it, and roll back. Where there are sunken rocks, the water heaps up in mounds, or even in cones. At a point where rocks come very near the surface, the water forms a chute above, strikes, and is shot up ten or fifteen feet, and piles back in gentle curves, as in a fountain; and on the river tumbles and rolls.

7 月 25 日。今天仍是更多的激流和瀑布。其中一段，「爱玛·迪恩」号被漩涡抓住，开始打转；我们费了很大劲才脱身，还丢了一支桨。中午又做了一次搬运；继续前进，闯过一些激流、用绳放船过了另一些、还做了两段短搬运。我们在右岸扎营，又饿又累。

_July 25._--Still more rapids and falls to-day. In one, the _Emma Dean_ is caught in a whirlpool, and set spinning about; and it is with great difficulty we are able to get out of it, with the loss of an oar. At noon, another is made; and on we go, running some of the rapids, letting down with lines past others, and making two short portages. We camp on the right bank, hungry and tired.

7 月 26 日。今早我们只走了一小段就扎营，做桨、修船修气压计。峡谷崖壁到这里一直在稳步升高，现在已超过两千英尺。许多地方从水边起就是垂直的；另一些地方河与崖脚之间有一段岩堆，崖壁还常被侧谷打断。河现在很可能接近了它最低的水位。高水位线能高出目前水位四十、五十、六十甚至一百英尺。有时能看到原木和漂木卡在高处裂缝里——那是洪水把它们送到那里的。

_July 26._--We run a short distance this morning, and go into camp, to make oars and repair boats and barometers. The walls of the cañon have been steadily increasing in altitude to this point, and now they are more than two thousand feet high. In many places, they are vertical from the water’s edge; in others, there is a talus between the river and the foot of the cliffs, and they are often broken down by side cañons. It is probable that the river is nearly as low now as it is ever found. High water mark can be observed forty, fifty, sixty, or a hundred feet above its present stage. Sometimes logs and drift wood are seen wedged into the crevice overhead, where floods have carried them.

十点左右，鲍威尔、布拉德利、豪兰、霍尔和我沿一条侧谷向东走。很快来到几汪水前；然后是一条小溪，消失在下方沙地；沿溪上行，我们发现峡谷收窄、壁面合拢、常常悬垂，最后我们来到一个巨大的圆形剧场里，底部是一汪深、清、冷的水。起初路似乎断了；但很快我们发现一条小岩架，沿它爬过去，绕过水塘再走一百多码，向右转，又进到另一个穹顶状的圆形剧场。顶部有一条蜿蜒的裂缝通向外界，在近两千英尺的上方。浑圆的盆状底部直到壁脚都灌满了水。没有岩架能绕过去。如果我们游过去，迎面是一道几百英尺高的岩面，一道细流从上面滑落，不可能攀爬。所以这条峡谷我们没法再往上了。于是我们折回，仔细察看两侧的壁。

About ten o’clock, Powell, Bradley, Howland, Hall, and myself start up a side cañon to the east. We soon come to pools of water; then to a brook, which is lost in the sands below; and, passing up the brook, we find the cañon narrows, the walls close in, are often overhanging, and at last we find ourselves in a vast amphitheater, with a pool of deep, clear, cold water on the bottom. At first, our way seems cut off; but we soon discover a little shelf, along which we climb, and, passing beyond the pool, walk a hundred yards or more, turn to the right, and find ourselves in another dome-shaped amphitheater. There is a winding cleft at the top, reaching out to the country above, nearly two thousand feet overhead. The rounded, basin shaped bottom is filled with water to the foot of the walls. There is no shelf by which we can pass around the foot. If we swim across, we meet with a face of rock hundreds of feet high, over which a little rill glides, and it will be impossible to climb. So we can go no further up this cañon. Then we turn back, and examine the walls on either side carefully, to discover, if possible, some way of climbing out.

这次搜寻中，各人走各的路，我们都散开了。我几乎放弃出去的念头，正埋头找化石，忽然在北面发现一处崩塌的地方，也许能爬上去。路先是一段岩石滑坡；然后上一个不规则的圆形剧场，踩在像台阶、能抓手的地方；然后我到了一小段岩架，沿它走，发现一条垂直的裂缝，与壁面平行，通到更高的岩架。这条裂缝很窄，我试着爬上约四十英尺上方的岩架。我背上背着气压计，有点妨碍攀爬。裂缝两壁是光滑的石灰岩，既无脚点也无手点。于是我靠背抵一壁、膝盖抵另一壁支撑自己，就这样一点点把身体挪上去，一次几英寸，爬了大约二十五英尺时，裂缝变宽了一点，我没法再用膝盖抵住前面的岩石了。

In this search, every man takes his own course, and we are scattered. I almost abandon the idea of getting out, and am engaged in searching for fossils, when I discover, on the north, a broken place, up which it may be possible for me to climb. The way, for a distance, is up a slide of rocks; then up an irregular amphitheater, on points that form steps and give handhold, and then I reach a little shelf, along which I walk, and discover a vertical fissure, parallel to the face of the wall, and reaching to a higher shelf. This fissure is narrow, and I try to climb up to the bench, which is about forty feet overhead. I have a barometer on my back, which rather impedes my climbing. The walls of the fissure are of smooth limestone, offering neither foot nor hand hold. So I support myself by pressing my back against one wall and my knees against the other, and, in this way, lift my body, in a shuffling manner, a few inches at a time, until I have, perhaps, made twenty-five feet of the distance, when the crevice widens a little, and I cannot press my knees against the rocks in front with sufficient power to give me support in lifting my body, and I try to go back. This I cannot do without falling. So I struggle along sidewise, farther into the crevice, where it narrows. But by this time my muscles are exhausted, and I cannot climb longer; so I move still a little farther into the crevice, where it is so narrow and wedging that I can lie in it, and there I rest.

这样的放松休息了五到十分钟，我又继续往上，终于上到上面的岩架。在上面我能走四分之一英里，直到一处崖壁再次塌落、能继续往上爬的地方，一小时后我到了峰顶。我把气压计挂起来，让它稳定几分钟，然后专心采集矮松的树脂——这里矮松很多。这次爬山的几个主要目的之一就是取这种树脂，用来抹船；但我没有带下来的工具。天很热，外套留在营地了，没有衬里可撕。于是我想起可以剪下衬衣的一只袖子，扎住一头，用这个小口袋装了约一加仑的树脂。

Five or ten minutes of this relief, and up once more I go, and reach the bench above. On this I can walk for a quarter of a mile, till I come to a place where the wall is again broken down, so that I can climb up still farther, and in an hour I reach the summit. I hang up my barometer, to give it a few minutes’ time to settle, and occupy myself in collecting resin from the piñon pines, which are found in great abundance. One of the principal objects in making this climb was to get this resin, for the purpose of smearing our boats; but I have with me no means of carrying it down. The day is very hot, and my coat was left in camp, so I have no linings to tear out. Then it occurs to me to cut off the sleeve of my shirt, tie it up at one end, and in this little sack I collect about a gallon of pitch.

做完测高观测后，我在岩石上往回闲逛了一两个小时，忽然注意到一场暴风雨正从南面过来。我在岩石间找了一处遮蔽；但当风暴爆发时，水像从天上倾泻下来，不是先有稀疏雨滴、逐渐加大，而是仿佛猛然泼下。我浑身湿透，差点被冲走。它持续了不到半小时，云向北方扫去，又出太阳了。

After taking observations for altitude, I wander back on the rock, for an hour or two, when suddenly I notice that a storm is coming from the south. I seek a shelter in the rocks; but when the storm bursts, it comes down as a flood from the heavens, not with gentle drops at first, slowly increasing in quantity, but as if suddenly poured out. I am thoroughly drenched, and almost washed away. It lasts not more than half an hour, when the clouds sweep by to the north, and I have sunshine again.

趁这段时间，我找到一条更好的下山路，便以最快速度向营地赶去。下到侧谷谷底，我看见上千条溪流从四面崖壁泻下，夹带着红沙；它们在下方的峡谷里汇成一条巨大的红泥河。

In the meantime, I have discovered a better way of getting down, and I start for camp, making the greatest haste possible. On reaching the bottom of the side cañon, I find a thousand streams rolling down the cliffs on every side, carrying with them red sand; and these all unite in the cañon below, in one great stream of red mud.

我用尽全力奔跑，很快到了溪流下游——因为雨没有落到峡谷下端，水正流经一片干沙床；虽然水成浪涌来，有几英尺高、十五到二十英尺宽，但沙子把它吸干了，水消失了。但一波接一波，滚过去，被吞掉；洪水仍从上方不断涌来。我发现自己能比水流跑得快；于是我赶回营地，告诉队员们有一条河正沿峡谷下来。我们急忙把营具从岸边搬到自认为不会被水淹到的地方。然后我们站在一旁，看着这条河滚滚汇入科罗拉多河。峡谷底部发现大量石膏；所以我们给它命名「石膏峡谷」。

Traveling as fast as I can run, I soon reach the foot of the stream, for the rain did not reach the lower end of the cañon, and the water is running down a dry bed of sand; and, although it comes in waves, several feet high and fifteen or twenty feet in width, the sands soak it up, and it is lost. But wave follows wave, and rolls along, and is swallowed up; and still the floods come on from above. I find that I can travel faster than the stream; so I hasten to camp, and tell the men there is a river coming down the cañon. We carry our camp equipage hastily from the bank, to where we think it will be above the water. Then we stand by, and see the river roll on to join the Colorado. Great quantities of gypsum are found at the bottom of the gorge; so we name it Gypsum Cañon.

7 月 27 日。到中午前一直有激流和瀑布；然后我们来到峡谷一处窄地，几百英尺高的垂直壁，之上是陡峭台阶和斜坡岩石直到峰顶。河非常窄，我们紧贴左壁、仔细察看前方水路，极其小心、满怀焦虑地前行。

_July 27._--We have more rapids and falls until noon; then we come to a narrow place in the cañon, with vertical walls for several hundred feet, above which are steep steps and sloping rocks back to the summits. The river is very narrow, and we make our way with great care and much anxiety, hugging the wall on the left, and carefully examining the way before us.

傍晚，我们绕过一个下部有些崩塌的尖角向左，发现岩壁上一百多英尺高处有一群盘羊。我们迅速在一处隐蔽的小湾靠岸，所有带枪的猎人都悄悄爬上去——羊还没发现我们。很快我们听见枪声，留在船上的我们爬上高处看猎人们收获如何。已打死一只羊，还有两人在追。几分钟后我们又听见枪声，接着那群羊哗啦啦地从岩石上冲下来，离我们不到二十码。一个猎人抓起枪，又放倒一只，下一刻其余的羊消失在岩石后面。我们都去追；但在光裸岩石上根本没法循踪，再也看不见它们。它们是怎么从这岩壁围成的峡谷里逃出去的，是个谜——我们看不出任何出路。无疑，如果我们能腾出时间去搜寻，总能找到它们跑上去的某条沟壑。

Late in the afternoon, we pass to the left, around a sharp point, which is somewhat broken down near the foot, and discover a flock of mountain sheep on the rocks, more than a hundred feet above us. We quickly land in a cove, out of sight, and away go all the hunters with their guns, for the sheep have not discovered us. Soon, we hear firing, and those of us who have remained in the boats climb up to see what success the hunters have had. One sheep has been killed, and two of the men are still pursuing them. In a few minutes, we hear firing again, and the next moment down come the flock, clattering over the rocks, within twenty yards of us. One of the hunters seizes his gun, and brings a second sheep down, and the next minute the remainder of the flock is lost behind the rocks. We all give chase; but it is impossible to follow their tracks over the naked rock, and we see them no more. Where they went out of this rock walled cañon is a mystery, for we can see no way of escape. Doubtless, if we could spare the time for the search, we could find some gulch up which they ran.

我们把战利品绑在一条船的甲板上，继续走了一小段；但新鲜肉对我们太诱人了，我们早早停下大快朵颐。真是一场盛宴！两只肥美的小羊。今晚我们不在乎面包、豆子或干苹果；咖啡和羊肉就足够了。

We lash our prizes to the deck of one of the boats, and go on for a short distance; but fresh meat is too tempting for us, and we stop early to have a feast. And a feast it is! Two fine, young sheep. We care not for bread, or beans, or dried apples to-night; coffee and mutton is all we ask.

7 月 28 日。今早我们做了两次搬运，其中一次非常长。下午我们闯过一条水槽，半英里多长，又窄又急。这条水槽的底是大理石的；岩石朝我们行进的方向倾斜，水流的落差与岩层的倾角一致；所以我们漂在沿斜面滑行的水上。水槽脚下，河猛地向右转，水涌向一块从上方看似乎正横在它路上的岩石。我们靠近时，用尽全力向右划，但似乎不可避免地要被冲向崖壁；我们被高高托上浪尖——不是撞上岩石，因为回弹的水迎头撞上我们，把我们打回来，我们安全通过，只是被浇了个透湿。

_July 28._--We make two portages this morning, one of them very long. During the afternoon we run a chute, more than half a mile in length, narrow and rapid. This chute has a floor of marble; the rocks dip in the direction in which we are going, and the fall of the stream conforms to the inclination of the beds; so we float on water that is gliding down an inclined plane. At the foot of the chute, the river turns sharply to the right, and the water rolls up against a rock which, from above, seems to stand directly athwart its course. As we approach it, we pull with all our power to the right, but it seems impossible to avoid being carried headlong against the cliff, and we are carried up high on the waves--not against the rocks, for the rebounding water strikes us, and we are beaten back, and pass on with safety, except that we get a good drenching.

这之后，崖壁突然合拢，峡谷窄得我们从未见过。水从一壁灌到另一壁，崖脚下毫无靠岸之处；河极急，峡谷极蜿蜒，只能看见前方几百码；崖壁高耸在我们头顶，常常悬垂，几乎遮住光线。我站在甲板上，极度焦虑地盯着前方，生怕这会把我们带进什么危险；但我们一路滑行，没有障碍、没有瀑布、没有礁石，一英里半后从窄峡驶进峡谷更开阔破碎的一段。现在回想起来，穿过这种地方实在是一件很简单的事，但对前方可能有什么的恐惧给我们留下了深深的印象。

After this, the walls suddenly close in, so that the cañon is narrower than we have ever known it. The water fills it from wall to wall, giving us no landing place at the foot of the cliff; the river is very swift, the cañon is very tortuous, so that we can see but a few hundred yards ahead; the walls tower over us, often overhanging so as to almost shut out the light. I stand on deck, watching with intense anxiety, lest this may lead us into some danger; but we glide along, with no obstruction, no falls, no rocks, and, in a mile and a half, emerge from the narrow gorge into a more open and broken portion of the cañon. Now that it is past, it seems a very simple thing indeed to run through such a place, but the fear of what might be ahead made a deep impression on us.

三点钟我们到达瀑布峡谷的脚下。这里一条长长的峡谷谷地从东面下来，河沿侧谷的延伸方向急转向西。在右侧的弯道里，能看到大量岩峰、尖塔和塔形岩石。我们叫它「千岩弯」。

At three o’clock we arrive at the foot of Cataract Cañon. Here a long cañon valley comes down from the east, and the river turns sharply to the west in a continuation of the line of the lateral valley. In the bend on the right, vast numbers of crags, and pinnacles, and tower shaped rocks are seen. We call it Mille Crag Bend.

现在我们转入另一条峡谷，水很急，但无礁石阻挡。这条新峡谷非常窄、非常直，下方壁垂直，上方呈阶梯状。进入处崖边高出水面一千三百英尺，但岩石向西倾斜，而峡谷走向正朝那个方向，所以可见崖壁高度在逐渐降低。沿这条窄水道漂下，透过远处峡谷的裂缝望出去，能看见河又向左转去，在那点之外很远的地方，能看到一座大山。继续漂下，我们看到更多山——忽在右，忽在左——直到一整条大山系展现在眼前。我们命名「窄峡谷」，它终止于下方河的一个弯道处。

And now we wheel into another cañon, on swift water, unobstructed by rocks. This new cañon is very narrow and very straight, with walls vertical below and terraced above. The brink of the cliff is 1,300 feet above the water, where we enter it, but the rocks dip to the west, and, as the course of the cañon is in that direction, the walls are seen to slowly decrease in altitude. Floating down this narrow channel, and looking out through the cañon crevice away in the distance, the river is seen to turn again to the left, and beyond this point, away many miles, a great mountain is seen. Still floating down, we see other mountains, now to the right, now on the left, until a great mountain range is unfolded to view. We name this Narrow Cañon, and it terminates at the bend of the river below.

顺流而下到这个点时，我们发现一条从右面流来的河口。我们的小船驶了进去。后面船上一个人看见我们的举动，向邓恩喊话，问是不是条鳟鱼河。邓恩十分厌恶地回答，那是「一条脏恶魔河」，从此这条河就以这个名字为人所知。（脚注6）河水极其浑浊，还带着难闻的气味。

As we go down to this point, we discover the mouth of a stream, which enters from the right. Into this our little boat is turned. One of the men in the boat following, seeing what we have done, shouts to Dunn, asking if it is a trout-stream. Dunn replies, much disgusted, that it is “a dirty devil,” and by this name the river is to be known hereafter.[6] The water is exceedingly muddy, and has an unpleasant odor.

我们一些人出去走了半英里，爬上一座北面的土台。能沿「脏恶魔河」追踪好多英里。它从一条极窄的峡谷里流下，再往西南，有一条长长的崖壁线，崖壁与峡谷边缘之间是一条宽阔的台地或台阶，越过这些崖壁，就是我们沿窄峡谷下来时看到的那条山脉。

Some of us go out for half a mile, and climb a butte to the north. The course of the Dirty Devil River can be traced for many miles. It comes down through a very narrow cañon, and beyond it, to the southwest, there is a long line of cliffs, with a broad terrace, or bench, between it and the brink of the cañon, and beyond these cliffs is situated the range of mountains seen as we came down Narrow Cañon.

沿科罗拉多河往上看，能看到它穿行的深渊，但我们无法俯瞰它的水。整个地区都是一片光裸的多色岩石之地，四周是崖壁和土台，远处是巍峨的群山。

Looking up the Colorado, the chasm through which it runs can be seen, but we cannot look down on its waters. The whole country is a region of naked rock, of many colors, with cliffs and buttes about us, and towering mountains in the distance.

7 月 29 日。今天我们进入一条低矮红壁的峡谷。进入后不远，我们在左壁上发现一座古建筑的废墟。这里河与壁之间正好有一片窄平原，在这座两百英尺高的岩石边沿上立着这座老屋。它的墙是石头砌的，抹着灰泥，砌得非常整齐。它大概曾有三层楼高；底层几乎完好；二层已大量坍塌，三层几乎什么都不剩。近旁岩石上发现大量燧石片，还有许多箭头——有的完好，有的残破；陶器碎片散落一地。在建筑下方的崖面上，顺河往下两三百码，有许多刻绘。我们用两小时考察这些有趣的废墟，然后顺流而下十五英里，又发现了一组。主要建筑坐落在……

_July 29._--We enter a cañon to-day, with low, red walls. A short distance below its head we discover the ruins of an old building, on the left wall. There is a narrow plain between the river and the wall just here, and on the brink of a rock two hundred feet high stands this old house. Its walls are of stone, laid in mortar, with much regularity. It was probably built three stories high; the lower story is yet almost intact; the second is much broken down, and scarcely anything is left of the third. Great quantities of flint chips are found on the rocks near by, and many arrow heads, some perfect, others broken; and fragments of pottery are strewn about in great profusion. On the face of the cliff, under the building, and along down the river, for two or three hundred yards, there are many etchings. Two hours are given to the examination of these interesting ruins, then we run down fifteen miles farther, and discover another group. The principal building was situated on the summit of the hill. A part of the walls are standing, to the height of eight or ten feet, and the mortar yet remains, in some places. The house was in the shape of an L, with five rooms on the ground floor, one in the angle, and two in each extension. In the space in the angle, there is a deep excavation. From what we know of the people in the province of Tusayan, who are, doubtless, of the same race as the former inhabitants of these ruins, we conclude that this was a “kiva,” or underground chamber, in which their religious ceremonies were performed.

我们离开这些废墟，顺流而下两三英里，下午三点左右扎营。现在我去爬崖壁，到后方腹地走走。

We leave these ruins, and run down two or three miles, and go into camp about midafternoon. And now I climb the wall and go out into the back country for a walk.

切出这条峡谷的砂岩是红色的、质地均匀，与迷宫峡谷穿过的那种相同。光滑光裸的岩石向河两侧伸展许多英里，但到处散布着雕凿奇特的土丘和圆锥，还磨出了深洞。许多这样的水坑盛着水。其中一个二十英尺深的洞（或称井）里，我发现长着一棵树。这坑太窄，我可以从坑沿一步跨到树上的一根枝杈，顺着这架「生长的梯子」下到井底。这些坑许多是壶穴，出现在偶尔降雨时奔流的小溪或溪流的水道上；坑底往往能找到几块显然参与了挖掘的较硬岩石。另一些较浅的就不太好解释了。也许它们出现在砂岩中原先较软的地方——那些更易受风化侵蚀的点。

The sandstone, through which the cañon is cut, is red and homogeneous, being the same as that through which Labyrinth runs. The smooth, naked rock stretches out on either side of the river for many miles, but curiously carved mounds and cones are scattered everywhere, and deep holes are worn out. Many of these pockets are filled with water. In one of these holes, or wells, twenty feet deep, I find a tree growing. The excavation is so narrow that I can step from its brink to a limb on the tree, and descend to the bottom of the well down a growing ladder. Many of these pockets are pot-holes, being found in the courses of little rills, or brooks, that run during the rains which occasionally fall in this region; and often a few harder rocks, which evidently assisted in their excavation, can be found in their bottoms. Others, which are shallower, are not so easily explained. Perhaps they are found where softer spots existed in the sandstone, places that yielded more readily to atmospheric degradation, and where the loose sands were carried away by the winds.

就在日落前，我试着爬一座浑圆的丘陵，希望从上面能好好眺望四周的乡野。它由一个个光滑的土丘堆叠而成。我沿着它们蜿蜒攀爬，寻找可行的路，快到顶时它们陡得走不动了。我四处找了一会儿更易走的路，却惊讶地发现一段石阶——显然是人手在岩石里凿出来的。在一处十到十二英尺高的垂直壁前，我找到一架老旧摇晃的梯子。也许这是那个古老民族的一座瞭望塔——他们的家在废墟里被我们找到了。我曾在科罗拉多河的许多支流上考察过他们废弃的居所。那些显示是他们在这一带居住后期建造的，通常都建在最难攀登的崖壁上。有时洞穴口被砌墙封住，还有许多其他证据表明他们……

Just before sundown, I attempt to climb a rounded eminence, from which I hope to obtain a good outlook on the surrounding country. It is formed of smooth mounds, piled one above another. Up these I climb, winding here and there, to find a practicable way, until near the summit they become too steep for me to proceed. I search about, a few minutes, for a more easy way, when I am surprised at finding a stairway, evidently cut in the rock by hands. At one place, where there is a vertical wall of ten or twelve feet, I find an old, ricketty ladder. It may be that this was a watch-tower of that ancient people whose homes we have found in ruins. On many of the tributaries of the Colorado I have heretofore examined their deserted dwellings. Those that show evidences of being built during the latter part of their occupation of the country, are, usually, placed on the most inaccessible cliffs. Sometimes, the mouths of caves have been walled across, and there are many other evidences to show their anxiety to secure defensible positions. Probably the nomadic tribes were sweeping down upon them, and they resorted to these cliffs and cañons for safety. It is not unreasonable to suppose that this orange mound was used as a watch-tower. Here I stand, where these now lost people stood centuries ago, and look over this strange country. I gaze off to great mountains, in the northwest, which are slowly covered by the night until they are lost, and then I return to camp. It is no easy task to find my way down the wall in the darkness, and I clamber about until it is nearly midnight, before I arrive.

7 月 30 日。今天进展很好，因为水虽平稳却很快。有时峡谷壁垂直直到顶部；有时下方垂直、上方是覆着土丘的斜坡；还有别处，带土丘的斜坡一直伸到水边。

_July 30._--We make good progress to-day, as the water, though smooth, is swift. Sometimes, the cañon walls are vertical to the top; sometimes, they are vertical below, and have a mound covered slope above; in other places, the slope, with its mounds, comes down to the water’s edge.

继续前行，我们发现橙色砂岩被一组坚硬的钙质岩层切成两半，下层底下垫着软的含石膏页岩。有时上部均质的岩层是一面平滑的垂直壁，但通常被雕成带缓蜿蜒谷线的土丘状。下部岩层在下方较软页岩被河水掏蚀后，受重力作用破裂成棱角表面，常呈柱状外观。你几乎会想象这些崖壁是带着某种意图雕刻的，以表现巨大的建筑形式。

Still proceeding on our way, we find the orange sandstone is cut in two by a group of firm, calcareous strata, and the lower bed is underlaid by soft gypsiferous shales. Sometimes, the upper homogeneous bed is a smooth, vertical wall, but usually it is carved with mounds, with gently meandering valley lines. The lower bed, yielding to gravity, as the softer shales below work out into the river, breaks into angular surfaces, often having a columnar appearance. One could almost imagine that the walls had been carved with a purpose, to represent giant architectural forms.

在崖壁的深深凹处，我们发现泉水，湿润的砂岩上长着苔藓和蕨类。

In the deep recesses of the walls, we find springs, with mosses and ferns on the moistened sandstone.

7 月 31 日。今天穿过峡谷这一段，我们有一段凉爽愉快的行程。崖壁高度稳步增加，弯道和缓，常常河沿一段光滑完整的垂直壁弧扫过，然后是一个点缀着王冠形拱门、苔藓凹龛、深而美的幽谷和彩绘洞窟的弯道。

_July 31._--We have a cool, pleasant ride to-day, through this part of the cañon. The walls are steadily increasing in altitude, the curves are gentle, and often the river sweeps by an arc of vertical wall, smooth and unbroken, and then by a curve that is variegated by royal arches, mossy alcoves, deep, beautiful glens, and painted grottos.

午饭后不久，我们发现了圣胡安河口，在那里扎营。下午其余时间用来寻找能爬出峡谷的路；但以失败告终。

Soon after dinner, we discover the mouth of the San Juan, where we camp. The remainder of the afternoon is given to hunting some way by which we can climb out of the cañon; but it ends in failure.

8 月 1 日。今早我们下行两英里，再次扎营。东边崖壁沿线有一条低矮的、覆着柳树的地带。我们穿过它，去探查一条从河上看到的凹龛。进去后，我们找到一小片槭树和三角叶杨林；向右转，发现自己在一座从岩石中凿出的大厅里。上端有一汪清澈的深水池，水边草木葱茏。站在池边，能看到入口处的树林。这大厅高两百多英尺，长五百英尺，宽两百英尺。穿过穹顶、再向上穿过一千英尺的岩石，有一条窄窄的蜿蜒天窗；而这一切都是被一条小溪凿出来的——这条小溪只在干燥之地偶落的几场阵雨时流淌。峡谷后方光裸岩石上的水迅速汇成一条小水道，侵蚀出一条深侧谷，溪水从那里……

_August 1._--We drop down two miles this morning, and go into camp again. There is a low, willow covered strip of land along the walls on the east. Across this we walk, to explore an alcove which we see from the river. On entering, we find a little grove of box-elder and cottonwood trees; and, turning to the right, we find ourselves in a vast chamber, carved out of the rock. At the upper end there is a clear, deep pool of water, bordered with verdure. Standing by the side of this, we can see the grove at the entrance. The chamber is more than two hundred feet high, five hundred feet long, and two hundred feet wide. Through the ceiling, and on through the rocks for a thousand feet above, there is a narrow, winding skylight; and this is all carved out by a little stream, which only runs during the few showers that fall now and then in this arid country. The waters from the bare rocks back of the cañon, gathering rapidly into a small channel, have eroded a deep side cañon, through which they run, until they fall into the farther end of this chamber. The rock at the ceiling is hard, the rock below, very soft and friable; and, having cut through the upper harder portion down into the lower and softer, the stream has washed out these friable sandstones; and thus the chamber has been excavated.

我们把营地搬到这里。晚上当「老谢迪」为我们唱歌时，我们高兴地发现这块岩石中的空腔充满了甜美的声音。它无疑是由它的风暴造就的建筑师为音乐厅准备的；所以我们命名「音乐殿」。

Here we bring our camp. When “Old Shady” sings us a song at night, we are pleased to find that this hollow in the rock is filled with sweet sounds. It was doubtless made for an academy of music by its storm born architects; so we name it Music Temple.

8 月 2 日。今天我们仍留在音乐殿营地。

_August 2._--We still keep our camp in Music Temple to-day.

如果可能，我想看看邻近的乡野；所以一早队员们把我渡过河，我沿崖脚逆流走了半英里，先爬过破碎的岩架，再沿光滑的斜坡岩石上两三百码，然后走上一条窄山脊。但我发现自己还没达到能俯瞰峡谷外区域的高度；于是下到一条小沟，又爬到更高的山脊，一路都在光裸砂岩上，最后到了一处视野极佳的点。我能望出圣胡安河好几英里，科罗拉多河很长一段；西北能看见亨利山脉；东北是拉萨尔山脉；东南是未知的群山；西南是峡谷的蜿蜒。然后我回到河边。

I wish to obtain a view of the adjacent country, if possible; so, early in the morning, the men take me across the river, and I pass along by the foot of the cliff half a mile up stream, and then climb first up broken ledges, then two or three hundred yards up a smooth, sloping rock, and then pass out on a narrow ridge. Still, I find I have not attained an altitude from which I can overlook the region outside of the cañon; and so I descend into a little gulch, and climb again to a higher ridge, all the way along naked sandstone, and at last I reach a point of commanding view. I can look several miles up the San Juan, and a long distance up the Colorado; and away to the northwest I can see the Henry Mountains; to the northeast, the Sierra La Sal; to the southeast, unknown mountains; and to the southwest, the meandering of the cañon. Then I return to the bank of the river.

我们又在音乐殿睡了一夜。

We sleep again in Music Temple.

8 月 3 日。今早很早出发。这条峡谷的特征极其多样。时而仍是垂直的壁。这些通常立在巨大的弯道上方。河绕过这些弯时，在一些地方掏蚀崖壁底部。有时岩石悬垂；另一些弯道里有奇特的窄幽谷。我们沿粗糙的石阶爬进去，也许几百英尺，来到一处泉水从悬垂崖壁下涌出的地方，那里立着三角叶杨和柳树，而沿小溪的弯道，橡树生长，还可见其他茂盛的植被，与光裸岩石的总体外观形成鲜明对比。我们叫它们「橡树幽谷」。

_August 3._--Start early this morning. The features of this cañon are greatly diversified. Still vertical walls at times. These are usually found to stand above great curves. The river, sweeping around these bends, undermines the cliffs in places. Sometimes, the rocks are overhanging; in other curves, curious, narrow glens are found. Through these we climb, by a rough stairway, perhaps several hundred feet, to where a spring bursts out from under an overhanging cliff, and where cottonwoods and willows stand, while, along the curves of the brooklet, oaks grow, and other rich vegetation is seen, in marked contrast to the general appearance of naked rock. We call these Oak Glens.

其他绝妙的特征是我们要经过的许多侧谷或峡谷。有时我们停下来稍作探查。有些地方，它们的壁上方比下方更靠近，所以看起来有点像岩石中的洞穴或厅堂。通常沿这样的峡谷上行，我们会发现美丽的植被；但路常被深盆或所谓「壶穴」切断。

Other wonderful features are the many side cañons or gorges that we pass. Sometimes, we stop to explore these for a short distance. In some places, their walls are much nearer each other above than below, so that they look somewhat like caves or chambers in the rocks. Usually, in going up such a gorge, we find beautiful vegetation; but our way is often cut off by deep basins, or pot-holes, as they are called.

在崖壁上，以及深入乡野许多英里的地方，能看到大量纪念碑形的土台。所以我们有了一组奇异绝妙的特征——雕凿的壁、王冠拱门、幽谷、凹龛沟、土丘和纪念碑。该从哪个特征选名呢？我们决定叫它「幽谷峡谷」（Glen Cañon）。

On the walls, and back many miles into the country, numbers of monument shaped buttes are observed. So we have a curious _ensemble_ of wonderful features--carved walls, royal arches, glens, alcove gulches, mounds, and monuments. From which of these features shall we select a name? We decide to call it Glen Cañon.

掠过这些高耸的纪念碑，掠过这些橙色砂岩的土丘浪涛，掠过这些橡树点缀的幽谷，掠过这些蕨类装饰的凹龛，掠过这些墙形的弯道，我们一小时一小时地滑行，不时停下——因为某个新的奇观吸引住我们的目光——直到来到一个具有历史意义的点。

Past these towering monuments, past these mounded billows of orange sandstone, past these oak set glens, past these fern decked alcoves, past these mural curves, we glide hour after hour, stopping now and then, as our attention is arrested by some new wonder, until we reach a point which is historic.

1776 年，西班牙神父埃斯卡兰特从圣菲出发向西北远征，渡过格兰德河和绿河，然后沿瓦萨奇山脉和南部高原下行，直到抵达维尔京河。他的本意是穿越到蒙特雷传教区；但从印第安人那里得到的信息让他断定那条路不可行。他不想沿刚走过的绕远路回圣菲，便尝试走一条更直接的路，那条路在「神父渡口」（El vado de los Padres）一带渡过科罗拉多河。根据我们读到的描述，我们能够确定那个地点。一条小溪从西面穿过一条极窄的侧谷流下。他正是沿这条溪下来的，而我们的船正停在渡口所在的点。这里至今还能看到一条被踩得很深的印第安小路。崖壁与河之间有一小片草地。能看到许多营火的灰烬，还有……

In the year 1776, Father Escalante, a Spanish priest, made an expedition from Santa Fé to the northwest, crossing the Grand and Green, and then passing down along the Wasatch Mountains and the southern plateaus, until he reached the Rio Virgen. His intention was to cross to the Mission of Monterey; but, from information received from the Indians, he decided that the route was impracticable. Not wishing to return to Santa Fé over the circuitous route by which he had just traveled, he attempted to go by one more direct, and which led him across the Colorado, at a point known as _El vado de los Padres_. From the description which we have read, we are enabled to determine the place. A little stream comes down through a very narrow side cañon from the west. It was down this that he came, and our boats are lying at the point where the ford crosses. A well beaten Indian trail is seen here yet. Between the cliff and the river there is a little meadow. The ashes of many camp fires are seen, and the bones of numbers of cattle are bleaching on the grass. For several years the Navajos have raided on the Mormons that dwell in the valleys to the west, and they doubtless cross frequently at this ford with their stolen cattle.

8 月 4 日。今天崖壁更高，峡谷窄得多。两侧仍能看到纪念碑；美丽的幽谷、凹龛、峡谷和侧谷仍有发现。午饭后，我们发现河突然转向西北，峡谷的整个特征都变了。崖壁高出好几百英尺，岩石主要是色彩斑斓的杂色页岩——上方奶油橙，然后明亮的朱红，下方是紫和巧克力色的岩层，夹杂绿黄沙。我们沿此向西北偏西方向跑了四英里；又折向西，进入峡谷特征更像弯道上方的一段。夜里我们在一条从右面流来的小溪口停下，估计这就是帕里亚河——去年一位摩门教传教士向我描述过它。

_August 4._--To-day the walls grow higher, and the cañon much narrower. Monuments are still seen on either side; beautiful glens, and alcoves, and gorges, and side cañons are yet found. After dinner, we find the river making a sudden turn to the northwest, and the whole character of the cañon changed. The walls are many hundreds of feet higher, and the rocks are chiefly variegated shales of beautiful colors--creamy orange above, then bright vermilion, and below, purple and chocolate beds, with green and yellow sands. We run four miles through this, in a direction a little to the west of north; wheel again to the west, and pass into a portion of the cañon where the characteristics are more like those above the bend. At night we stop at the mouth of a creek coming in from the right, and suppose it to be the Paria, which was described to me last year by a Mormon missionary.

在这里峡谷在一道崖壁线处骤然终止，这道崖壁线从两侧横过河流。

Here the cañon terminates abruptly in a line of cliffs, which stretches from either side across the river.

8 月 5 日。带着几分焦虑，今早我们进入一条新峡谷。我们已学会密切观察岩石的质地。在较软的岩层里，河是平静的；在较硬的岩层里，我们会遇到激流和瀑布。我们下方是瀑布峡谷里见过的那种石灰岩和硬砂岩。这预示着一路辛劳和危险。除了岩石质地，还有另一个影响水道特征的条件——我们已凭经验发现。岩层水平时，河常常是平静的；但即使某些地方很急，也没有大障碍。岩石朝行进方向倾斜时，河通常以极快的速度奔流，但我们仍很少有激流和瀑布。但当岩石向河流上游方向倾斜、河斜切过翘起的地层——上方硬岩、下方软岩——我们就有了激流和瀑布。今早我们正是进入硬岩和向上游倾斜的岩层，开始一段漫长、多石的路程。

_August 5._--With some feeling of anxiety, we enter a new cañon this morning. We have learned to closely observe the texture of the rock. In softer strata, we have a quiet river; in harder, we find rapids and falls. Below us are the limestones and hard sandstones, which we found in Cataract Cañon. This bodes toil and danger. Besides the texture of the rocks, there is another condition which affects the character of the channel, as we have found by experience. Where the strata are horizontal, the river is often quiet; but, even though it may be very swift in places, no great obstacles are found. Where the rocks incline in the direction traveled, the river usually sweeps with great velocity, but still we have few rapids and falls. But where the rocks dip up stream, and the river cuts obliquely across the upturned formations, harder strata above, and softer below, we have rapids and falls. Into hard rocks, and into rocks dipping up stream, we pass this morning, and start on a long, rocky, mad rapid. On the left there is a vertical rock, and down by this cliff and around to the left we glide, just tossed enough by the waves to appreciate the rate at which we are traveling.

峡谷很窄，壁垂直，逐渐升高。遇到更多激流和瀑布。我们来到一处落差十六英尺的，绕它做了一次搬运，然后停下吃午饭。

The cañon is narrow, with vertical walls, which gradually grow higher. More rapids and falls are found. We come to one with a drop of sixteen feet, around which we make a portage, and then stop for dinner.

然后跑了两英里，又一次又长又难的搬运；然后我们在一片沙岸上扎营过夜。

Then a run of two miles, and another portage, long and difficult; then we camp for the night, on a bank of sand.

8 月 6 日。峡谷壁随着我们穿越地层而越来越高。崖脚有陡峭的岩堆，有些地方崖壁上段呈阶梯状。

_August 6._--Cañon walls, still higher and higher, as we go down through strata. There is a steep talus at the foot of the cliff, and, in some places, the upper parts of the walls are terraced.

十点左右，我们来到一处河占满整个水道、壁从水边垂直而上的地方。我们看到下方有瀑布，便把船划到崖壁边。头顶几英尺处有一条小岩架，或者说水平裂缝。一个人站在船甲板上，另一个人踩上他的肩膀爬进裂缝。然后我们把一根绳递给他，另外两三个人和我跟着爬进去；我们沿裂缝前行，直到它变成一条岩架——因为上部的顶塌掉了。我们沿它走了一小段，一路缓缓攀升，直到岩架断掉、再也走不过去的地方。然后我们回到船边，渡过河，从岩石间取了几根卡住的原木，拖回我们这边，沿裂缝和岩架传递，在断处搭了座桥。然后我们继续走到瀑布上方的一个点，但没有得到满意的……

About ten o’clock we come to a place where the river occupies the entire channel, and the walls are vertical from the water’s edge. We see a fall below, and row up against the cliff. There is a little shelf, or rather a horizontal crevice, a few feet over our heads. One man stands on the deck of the boat, another climbs on his shoulders, and then into the crevice. Then we pass him a line, and two or three others, with myself, follow; then we pass along the crevice until it becomes a shelf, as the upper part, or roof, is broken off. On this we walk for a short distance, slowly climbing all the way, until we reach a point where the shelf is broken off, and we can pass no farther. Then we go back to the boat, cross the stream, and get some logs that have lodged in the rocks, bring them to our side, pass them along the crevice and shelf, and bridge over the broken place. Then we go on to a point over the falls, but do not obtain a satisfactory view. Then we climb out to the top of the wall, and walk along to find a point below the fall, from which it can be seen. From this point it seems possible to let down our boats, with lines, to the head of the rapids, and then make a portage; so we return, row down by the side of the cliff, as far as we dare, and fasten one of the boats to a rock. Then we let down another boat to the end of its line beyond the first, and the third boat to the end of its line below the second, which brings it to the head of the fall, and under an overhanging rock. Then the upper boat, in obedience to a signal, lets go; we pull in the line, and catch the nearest boat as it comes, and then the last. Then we make a portage, and go on.

下午我们早早扎营，地点看起来似乎能爬出去，晚上用来「做测时观测」。

We go into camp early this afternoon, at a place where it seems possible to climb out, and the evening is spent in “making observations for time.”

8 月 7 日。历书告诉我们今天有日食，所以鲍威尔上尉和我一早带着仪器出发，想对日食做观测以测定我们的经度。辛苦攀爬四小时、上升了两千三百英尺到达峰顶后，我们匆匆搭起一座石台来放仪器，静静等待日食；但云上来了，下起雨，太阳和月亮都被遮住了。

_August 7._--The almanac tells us that we are to have an eclipse of the sun to-day, so Captain Powell and myself start early, taking our instruments with us, for the purpose of making observations on the eclipse, to determine our longitude. Arriving at the summit, after four hours’ hard climbing, to attain 2,300 feet in height, we hurriedly build a platform of rocks, on which to place our instruments, and quietly wait for the eclipse; but clouds come on, and rain falls, and sun and moon are obscured.

大失所望，我们开始回营，但天已晚，云把夜弄得漆黑。我们仍极其小心地在岩石间摸索下行，走了两三个小时，但进展确实缓慢。最后我们迷了路，不敢再往前走了。雨倾盆而下，我们找不到遮蔽。上不能上，下不能下，黑暗中也不敢乱动，只能坐着「熬过」这一夜。

Much disappointed, we start on our return to camp, but it is late, and the clouds make the night very dark. Still we feel our way down among the rocks with great care, for two or three hours, though making slow progress indeed. At last we lose our way, and dare proceed no farther. The rain comes down in torrents, and we can find no shelter. We can neither climb up nor go down, and in the darkness dare not move about, but sit and “weather out” the night.

8 月 8 日。天亮来临，经过一个漫长——哦，多么漫长——的夜晚，我们很快回到营地。

_August 8._--Daylight comes, after a long, oh! how long a night, and we soon reach camp.

早饭后我们再次出发，上午做了两次搬运。

After breakfast we start again, and make two portages during the forenoon.

这条峡谷的石灰岩常常被磨光，成为一种漂亮的大理石。有时岩石五彩斑斓——白、灰、粉、紫，带着藏红花的色调。我们以极大的辛劳推进，遇到许多障碍，闯激流、用绳一段段地放船，有时还要把船和货物搬过糟糕的地方。夜里，刚做了一次艰难的搬运，我们在悬垂的崖壁下扎营，庆幸有处避雨。我们找了好一阵，才找到几根够煮一杯咖啡的漂木。

The limestone of this cañon is often polished, and makes a beautiful marble. Sometimes the rocks are of many colors--white, gray, pink, and purple, with saffron tints. It is with very great labor that we make progress, meeting with many obstructions, running rapids, letting down our boats with lines, from rock to rock, and sometimes carrying boats and cargoes around bad places. We camp at night, just after a hard portage, under an overhanging wall, glad to find shelter from the rain. We have to search for some time to find a few sticks of driftwood, just sufficient to boil a cup of coffee.

水在这个河肘处急冲，在岩石下掏挖，挖出一个巨大的半圆形厅堂——若用作剧场，足可容纳五万人。有人可能会对它提出异议：涨水时，地板会被狂暴的洪水淹没。

The water sweeps rapidly in this elbow of river, and has cut its way under the rock, excavating a vast half circular chamber, which, if utilized for a theater, would give sitting to fifty thousand people. Objections might be raised against it, from the fact that, at high water, the floor is covered with a raging flood.

8 月 9 日。如今景色已是壮丽的规模。峡谷壁两千五百英尺高，由大理石构成，颜色多种美丽，下方常被波浪磨光，或高处崖壁被阵雨冲下的沙磨过。

_August 9._--And now, the scenery is on a grand scale. The walls of the cañon, 2,500 feet high, are of marble, of many beautiful colors, and often polished below by the waves, or far up the sides, where showers have washed the sands over the cliffs.

有一处我在一条大理石路面上走了一英里多，路面全被磨光，刻着奇异的花纹，凸起成千上万种奇幻的图案。阳光穿过壁上一道裂缝照在这路面上，闪着彩虹般的美丽光泽。

At one place I have a walk, for more than a mile, on a marble pavement, all polished and fretted with strange devices, and embossed in a thousand fantastic patterns. Through a cleft in the wall the sun shines on this pavement, which gleams in iridescent beauty.

我走进那道裂缝。它极窄，越往里，一连串水塘站在越高的位置上。塘里的水清澈凉爽，来自泉水。然后我回到路面上——它其实只是一道台地或台阶，洪水时河会漫过它，眼下却露着。沿路面许多地方有一洼洼清水，与河的红泥形成奇特的对照。最后我走到这条大理石台地的尽头，重新上船。

I pass up into the cleft. It is very narrow, with a succession of pools standing at higher levels as I go back. The water in these pools is clear and cool, coming down from springs. Then I return to the pavement, which is but a terrace or bench, over which the river runs at its flood, but left bare at present. Along the pavement, in many places, are basins of clear water, in strange contrast to the red mud of the river. At length I come to the end of this marble terrace, and take again to the boat.

向下漂了一小段，一幅美丽的景色呈现。河急转向东，仿佛被一道镶着百万颗明亮宝石的壁围住。这是什么意思？每双眼睛都被吸引，人人都在猜测。靠近后，我们发现泉水从高处岩石里喷出，阳光下的水雾形成了点缀崖壁的宝石。泉下方的岩石覆着苔藓、蕨类和许多美丽的花卉。我们命名「瓦西天堂」，以纪念去年与我们同行的植物学家。

Riding down a short distance, a beautiful view is presented. The river turns sharply to the east, and seems inclosed by a wall, set with a million brilliant gems. What can it mean? Every eye is engaged, every one wonders. On coming nearer, we find fountains bursting from the rock, high overhead, and the spray in the sunshine forms the gems which bedeck the wall. The rocks below the fountain are covered with mosses, and ferns, and many beautiful flowering plants. We name it Vasey’s Paradise, in honor of the botanist who traveled with us last year.

今天我们经过许多侧谷，它们是幽暗阴森的通道，伸进构成这条峡谷所切穿高原的岩石深处。

We pass many side cañons to-day, that are dark, gloomy passages, back into the heart of the rocks that form the plateau through which this cañon is cut.

下午又下雨了。头几滴雨刚落，细流就沿壁淌下。风暴渐强，细流不断增大，直到汇成巨大的水流。虽然峡谷壁主要是石灰岩，邻近乡野却是红砂岩；现在这些夹带红沙的水化作明亮红泥的河流下来，化成无数瀑布跃过崖壁。现在很清楚这些壁为什么许多地方被磨光了。

It rains again this afternoon. Scarcely do the first drops fall, when little rills run down the walls. As the storm comes on, the little rills increase in size, until great streams are formed. Although the walls of the cañon are chiefly limestone, the adjacent country is of red sandstone; and now the waters, loaded with these sands, come down in rivers of bright red mud, leaping over the walls in innumerable cascades. It is plain now how these walls are polished in many places.

最后风暴停了，我们继续前行。我们已切穿峡谷上部遇到的砂岩和石灰岩，以及一条一千英尺厚的大理石岩层。这层大理石里挖出大量洞穴，还看到一些让人联想到建筑形式的雕刻——虽然规模如此宏大，建筑术语反而贬低了它们。由于这巨大岩层是峡谷的一个显著特征，我们叫它「大理石峡谷」。

At last, the storm ceases, and we go on. We have cut through the sandstones and limestones met in the upper part of the cañon, and through one great bed of marble a thousand feet in thickness. In this, great numbers of caves are hollowed out, and carvings are seen, which suggest architectural forms, though on a scale so grand that architectural terms belittle them. As this great bed forms a distinctive feature of the cañon, we call it Marble Cañon.

这些壁的一个奇特之处是，许多突出的结构伸进河里，仿佛壁被加了扶壁支撑。壁本身半英里高，这些扶壁也是相应规模，向河中突入几十英尺。在这些突出之间的凹处，有安静的小湾——除非在一段激流脚下，那时它们变成旋舞的涡流或漩涡。有时这些凹龛后部有洞穴，让它们显得极深。然后上方又看到别的洞穴，形成巨大的穹顶状厅堂。壁、扶壁和厅堂全都是大理石。

It is a peculiar feature of these walls, that many projections are set out into the river, as if the wall was buttressed for support. The walls themselves are half a mile high, and these buttresses are on a corresponding scale, jutting into the river scores of feet. In the recesses between these projections there are quiet bays, except at the foot of a rapid, when they are dancing eddies or whirlpools. Sometimes these alcoves have caves at the back, giving them the appearance of great depth. Then other caves are seen above, forming vast, dome shaped chambers. The walls, and buttresses, and chambers are all of marble.

河现在平缓了；峡谷变宽。在上游，河水涨时水会壅高，高水位与低水位的差常达五十甚至七十英尺；但在这里，高水位线只比目前水位高二十英尺。有时水与壁之间有一条窄河漫滩。

The river is now quiet; the cañon wider. Above, when the river is at its flood, the waters gorge up, so that the difference between high and low water mark is often fifty or even seventy feet; but here, high-water mark is not more than twenty feet above the present stage of the river. Sometimes there is a narrow flood plain between the water and the wall.

在这里我们第一次发现牧豆树灌木，或小树——叶子细裂，结荚，有点像皂荚树。

Here we first discover _mesquite_ shrubs, or small trees, with finely divided leaves and pods, somewhat like the locust.

8 月 10 日。崖壁更高；水又湍急起来。我们经过右侧几条宽阔破碎的峡谷，透过它们瞥见数英里外西面一片森林覆盖的高原。

_August 10._--Walls still higher; water, swift again. We pass several broad, ragged cañons on our right, and up through these we catch glimpses of a forest clad plateau, miles away to the west.

两点钟我们到达小科罗拉多河口。这条河穿过一条峡谷流入，规模与科罗拉多河本身一样宏大。它是一条很小的河，极其浑浊和咸。下午我沿它上行三四英里，在容易涉水的地方来回横渡。然后我在一处爬升几百英尺，能沿这条河穿行的深渊望出好几英里。回来的路上我打死两条响尾蛇，回到营地时发现营边刚被打死了一条。

At two o’clock, we reach the mouth of the Colorado Chiquito. This stream enters through a cañon, on a scale quite as grand as that of the Colorado itself. It is a very small river, and exceedingly muddy and salt. I walk up the stream three or four miles, this afternoon, crossing and recrossing where I can easily wade it. Then I climb several hundred feet at one place, and can see up the chasm, through which the river runs, for several miles. On my way back, I kill two rattlesnakes, and find, on my arrival, that another has been killed just at camp.

8 月 11 日。今天我们留在这个点，目的是测定经纬度、测量崖壁高度、晾干口粮、修理船只。

_August 11._--We remain at this point to-day for the purpose of determining the latitude and longitude, measuring the height of the walls, drying our rations, and repairing our boats.

一早鲍威尔上尉带着气压计出去，爬两条河之间的一个点。

Captain Powell, early in the morning, takes a barometer, and goes out to climb a point between the two rivers.

我沿崖脚左边的峡谷往下走，爬上一道台地，发现一条在岩石里磨得很深的小路。在它横穿侧沟的地方，有些地方凿了台阶。我看不出它有多久没人走了。这无疑是比现在印第安人更早居住此地的那个民族用过的路——就是建那些公共住宅、前文提过的那个民族。

I walk down the gorge to the left at the foot of the cliff, climb to a bench, and discover a trail, deeply worn in the rock. Where it crosses the side gulches, in some places, steps have been cut. I can see no evidence of its having been traveled for a long time. It was doubtless a path used by the people who inhabited this country anterior to the present Indian races--the people who built the communal houses, of which mention has been made.

我大约三点回营，发现一些队员发现了废墟和许多陶器碎片；还有岩石上的刻绘和象形文字。

I return to camp about three o’clock, and find that some of the men have discovered ruins, and many fragments of pottery; also, etchings and hieroglyphics on the rocks.

今晚对照气压计读数后，我们发现崖壁约三千英尺高——超过半英里——这样的高度仅凭英尺数字很难体会。攀登不是靠爬普通山峰那种坡度，而是陡得多——常常几百英尺垂直——所以给我们的印象是身处在极深之处；我们抬头只见一小片天空。

We find, to-night, on comparing the readings of the barometers, that the walls are about three thousand feet high--more than half a mile--an altitude difficult to appreciate from a mere statement of feet. The ascent is made, not by a slope such as is usually found in climbing a mountain, but is much more abrupt--often vertical for many hundreds of feet--so that the impression is that we are at great depths; and we look up to see but a little patch of sky.

在小科罗拉多河上方、两条河之间，有些地方岩石破碎倾斜达六七英尺；然后是一道斜坡台地，只能找到某条沟壑爬上去；然后又是一道台地，再往后又是一道崖壁。崖顶高出河面三千英尺——我们的气压计为证。

Between the two streams, above the Colorado Chiquito, in some places the rocks are broken and shelving for six or seven hundred feet; then there is a sloping terrace, which can only be climbed by finding some way up a gulch; then, another terrace, and back, still another cliff. The summit of the cliff is three thousand feet above the river, as our barometers attest.

我们的营地在科罗拉多奇基托河下游、峡谷东侧。

Our camp is below the Colorado Chiquito, and on the eastern side of the cañon.

8 月 12 日。营地以上的岩石是铁锈色的砂岩和砾岩。有些很硬，有些相当软。它们几乎都接近水平，较软的材料层被冲蚀掉、留下较硬的，形成一系列岩架。能看到长长一线这样的岩架，厚度不一，从一两英尺到二三十英尺，间隔也同样多变。今早我花了两三个小时在这些岩架间攀爬，然后越过它们，沿一道长坡上到崖脚，想找到某种能到达崖顶的路；但我发现去路被一个圆形剧场切断。然后我绕向左，沿一条小沟和台地，不时攀爬，直到达到近两千英尺的高度，再也上不去了。从这个点能向西望进科罗拉多河的侧谷，还能看见……

_August 12._--The rocks above camp are rust colored sandstones and conglomerates. Some are very hard; others quite soft. These all lie nearly horizontal, and the beds of softer material have been washed out, and left the harder, thus forming a series of shelves. Long lines of these are seen, of varying thickness, from one or two to twenty or thirty feet, and the spaces between have the same variability. This morning, I spend two or three hours in climbing among these shelves, and then I pass above them, and go up a long slope, to the foot of the cliff, and try to discover some way by which I can reach the top of the wall; but I find my progress cut off by an amphitheater. Then, I wander away around to the left, up a little gulch, and along benches, and climb, from time to time, until I reach an altitude of nearly two thousand feet, and can get no higher. From this point, I can look off to the west, up side cañons of the Colorado, and see the edge of a great plateau, from which streams run down into the Colorado, and deep gulches, in the escarpment which faces us, continued by cañons, ragged and flaring, and set with cliffs and towering crags, down to the river. I can see far up Marble Cañon, to long lines of chocolate colored cliffs, and above these, the Vermilion Cliffs. I can see, also, up the Colorado Chiquito, through a very ragged and broken cañon, with sharp salients set out from the walls on either side, their points overlapping, so that a huge tooth of marble, on one side, seems to be set between two teeth on the opposite; and I can also get glimpses of walls, standing away back from the river, while over my head are mural escarpments, not possible to be scaled.

瀑布峡谷长四十一英里。进入处崖壁一千三百英尺高，逐渐增高到约一半处的两千七百英尺，然后降到脚部的一千三百英尺。窄峡谷长九英里半，进入处壁高一千三百英尺，到脚部降到水面。

Cataract Cañon is forty-one miles long. The walls are 1,300 feet high at its head, and they gradually increase in altitude to a point about half-way down, where they are 2,700 feet, and then decrease to 1,300 feet at the foot. Narrow Cañon is nine and a half miles long, with walls 1,300 feet in height at the head, and coming down to the water at the foot.

这条峡谷及邻近乡野几乎没什么植被。就在格兰德与绿河汇合处有几棵朴树；沿瀑布峡谷全程，高水位线都由同种树木的零星散布标示。能见到少量坚果松和柏树，偶尔有紫荆或犹大树；但峡谷及邻近乡野的总体观感是光裸的岩石。

There is very little vegetation in this cañon, or in the adjacent country. Just at the junction of the Grand and Green, there are a number of hackberry trees; and along the entire length of Cataract Cañon, the high-water line is marked by scattered trees of the same species. A few nut-pines and cedars are found, and occasionally a red-bud or judas tree; but the general aspect of the cañons, and of the adjacent country, is that of naked rock.

穿过幽谷峡谷的距离是一百四十九英里。它的壁从两三百英尺到一千六百英尺不等。大理石峡谷长六十五又二分之一英里。进入处深两百英尺，向脚部稳步加深，那里壁高三千五百英尺。

The distance through Glen Cañon is 149 miles. Its walls vary from two or three hundred to sixteen hundred feet. Marble Cañon is 65-1/2 miles long. At its head, it is 200 feet deep, and steadily increases in depth to its foot, where its walls are 3,500 feet high.
