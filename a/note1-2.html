CHAPTER VIII THE GRAND CANYON OF THE COLORADO

8 月 13 日。我们现在准备出发，驶向下游的「伟大的未知」。我们的船拴在同一根桩上，被躁动不安的河水抛来抛去，互相摩擦。它们浮得很高很轻——因为负载比我们希望的还轻。我们只剩一个月的口粮。面粉已用蚊帐筛重新筛过；坏了的腌肉已晾干，最糟的部分煮过；那几磅干苹果摊在太阳下晒过，又缩回正常大小；糖全化了，顺着河下去了；但我们有一大袋咖啡。给船减载有个好处：它们能更好地驾驭波浪，搬运时我们也没什么可扛的。

August 13.--We are now ready to start on our way down the Great Unknown. Our boats, tied to a common stake, are chafing each other, as they are tossed by the fretful river. They ride high and buoyant, for their loads are lighter than we could desire. We have but a month’s rations remaining. The flour has been resifted through the mosquito net sieve; the spoiled bacon has been dried, and the worst of it boiled; the few pounds of dried apples have been spread in the sun, and reshrunken to their normal bulk; the sugar has all melted, and gone on its way down the river; but we have a large sack of coffee. The lighting of the boats has this advantage: they will ride the waves better, and we shall have but little to carry when we make a portage.

我们身处大地深处四分之三英里，这条大河撞在崖壁和悬崖上——它们通向地面的世界——愤怒的浪花反而显得无足轻重；它们不过是微小的涟漪，我们也只是侏儒，在沙地上奔跑，或迷失在巨石之间。

We are three-quarters of a mile in the depths of the earth, and the great river shrinks into insignificance, as it dashes its angry waves against the walls and cliffs, that rise to the world above; they are but puny ripples, and we but pigmies, running up and down the sands, or lost among the boulders.

前方还有未知的距离要跑；还有未知的河要探索。有什么瀑布，我们不知道；有什么岩石阻路，我们不知道；有什么崖壁耸立在河上，我们也不知道。啊，好吧！我们可以猜测许多事。队员们像往常一样谈笑风生；今早玩笑开得肆无忌惮；但对我来说，那快活是阴郁的，那玩笑是可怕的。

We have an unknown distance yet to run; an unknown river yet to explore. What falls there are, we know not; what rocks beset the channel, we know not; what walls rise over the river, we know not. Ah, well! we may conjecture many things. The men talk as cheerfully as ever; jests are bandied about freely this morning; but to me the cheer is somber and the jests are ghastly.

带着几分急切、几分焦虑和几分疑虑，我们进入下方的峡谷，被湍急的水流带着，穿过从水边就立起的崖壁。它们的结构与昨天注意到的一样——下方是一层层不规则的岩架，之上是陡坡，直到大理石崖脚。我们用半个多小时跑了六英里，进入峡谷更开阔的一段，那里高丘和岩架横在河与远处的崖壁之间。就在这片开阔地的顶端，河横穿一条岩墙——也就是岩石中一条通向下方的裂缝被喷发物质填满，冷却后比裂缝所穿的岩石更硬；当周围岩石被冲蚀掉后，这较硬的火山物质就成了一道墙，而河在它上面切出一个几百英尺高、同样宽的「门」。它穿过岩墙的地方，下方有一道瀑布，还有一段糟糕的激流，满是暗色火成岩的巨砾；所以我们停下来做搬运。然后继续前行，滑过高丘和岩架，远处崖壁在望；掠过锐利的岩角；在几处停下察看激流——发现都能闯过去——直到又跑了五英里，我们上岸吃午饭。

With some eagerness, and some anxiety, and some misgiving, we enter the cañon below, and are carried along by the swift water through walls which rise from its very edge. They have the same structure as we noticed yesterday--tiers of irregular shelves below, and, above these, steep slopes to the foot of marble cliffs. We run six miles in a little more than half an hour, and emerge into a more open portion of the cañon, where high hills and ledges of rock intervene between the river and the distant walls. Just at the head of this open place the river runs across a dike; that is, a fissure in the rocks, open to depths below, has been filled with eruptive matter, and this, on cooling, was harder than the rocks through which the crevice was made, and, when these were washed away, the harder volcanic matter remained as a wall, and the river has cut a gate-way through it several hundred feet high, and as many wide. As it crosses the wall, there is a fall below, and a bad rapid, filled with boulders of trap; so we stop to make a portage. Then on we go, gliding by hills and ledges, with distant walls in view; sweeping past sharp angles of rock; stopping at a few points to examine rapids, which we find can be run, until we have made another five miles, when we land for dinner.

然后我们用绳放下一段长长的激流，再次出发。崖壁又一次合拢，我们发现自己在一个窄峡里，水又灌满整个水道，非常湍急。我们极其小心、时刻警惕地前进，下午跑了大约四英里，在一个洞穴里扎营。

Then we let down with lines, over a long rapid, and start again. Once more the walls close in, and we find ourselves in a narrow gorge, the water again filling the channel, and very swift. With great care, and constant watchfulness, we proceed, making about four miles this afternoon, and camp in a cave.

8 月 14 日。破晓时分我们沿河边一片小沙滩走下去，察看峡谷里一个新特征。此前，硬岩给我们糟糕的河；软岩给我们平稳的水；现在一片比我们经历过的任何岩石都硬的岩系开始了。河进入了花岗岩！（脚注7）

_August 14._--At daybreak we walk down the bank of the river, on a little sandy beach, to take a view of a new feature in the cañon. Heretofore, hard rocks have given us bad river; soft rocks, smooth water; and a series of rocks harder than any we have experienced sets in. The river enters the granite![7]

我们能看见花岗岩峡的里面一点，但它看起来很有威胁。

We can see but a little way into the granite gorge, but it looks threatening.

早饭后我们闯进波浪。一进去就让人敬畏。峡谷比我们见过的任何时候都窄；水更急；河道里破碎的岩石不多；但两侧崖壁布满尖塔和岩峰；锐利棱角的扶壁——布满风浪磨出的尖柱——远远伸进河里。

After breakfast we enter on the waves. At the very introduction, it inspires awe. The cañon is narrower than we have ever before seen it; the water is swifter; there are but few broken rocks in the channel; but the walls are set, on either side, with pinnacles and crags; and sharp, angular buttresses, bristling with wind and wave polished spires, extend far out into the river.

岩架伸进水流，顶部有时恰好在水面下，有时露出水面几英尺或许多英尺；岛状的岩架、岛状的尖塔和岛状的塔岩把湍急的水流劈成水槽、涡流和漩涡。我们很快来到一条从左面流来的小溪处，就在下游，水道被从这条侧谷冲下来的巨砾堵住，形成一道坝，坝上有三四十英尺的瀑布；但在巨砾上我们站得住脚，做了一次搬运。

Ledges of rocks jut into the stream, their tops sometimes just below the surface, sometimes rising few or many feet above; and island ledges, and island pinnacles, and island towers break the swift course of the stream into chutes, and eddies, and whirlpools. We soon reach a place where a creek comes in from the left, and just below, the channel is choked with boulders, which have washed down this lateral cañon and formed a dam, over which there is a fall of thirty or forty feet; but on the boulders we can get foothold, and we make a portage.

又发现三座这样的坝。我们绕过一座做了搬运；另外两座找到可以闯过去的水槽。

Three more such dams are found. Over one we make a portage; at the other two we find chutes, through which we can run.

继续前行，花岗岩升得更高，直到崖壁下段近一千英尺都由这种岩石构成。

As we proceed, the granite rises higher, until nearly a thousand feet of the lower part of the walls are composed of this rock.

大约十一点我们听见前方传来巨大的轰鸣，非常小心地靠近。随着我们前进，声音越来越大，最后我们来到一道长长的破碎瀑布上方，崖壁的岩架和尖塔阻挡着河道。三分之一英里内落差大约七十五到八十英尺，奔腾的水在岩石上撞成大浪，把自己抽打成疯狂的白色泡沫。我们正好能在上方靠岸，但两侧都没有落脚点可做搬运。到花岗岩顶近一千英尺，所以不可能把船搬过去——虽然我们能沿一条侧沟爬上峰顶，走一两英里，再下到河边。这是我们察看后发现的；但这样的搬运对我们来说行不通，我们必须闯过这段激流，否则就得放弃这条河。没有犹豫。我们跨进船，推开岸就出发了——先是在平稳却湍急的水上，然后撞上一道玻璃般的浪，骑到它顶上，又跌进浪谷，再爬上更高的浪，在越来越高的浪上起落，直到我们撞上一道正好往回卷的浪，一道碎浪从我们的小船上滚过。但我们仍向前飞驰，掠过突出的岩石，直到小船被……

About eleven o’clock we hear a great roar ahead, and approach it very cautiously. The sound grows louder and louder as we run, and at last we find ourselves above a long, broken fall, with ledges and pinnacles of rock obstructing the river. There is a descent of, perhaps, seventy-five or eighty feet in a third of a mile, and the rushing waters break into great waves on the rocks, and lash themselves into a mad, white foam. We can land just above, but there is no foot-hold on either side by which we can make a portage. It is nearly a thousand feet to the top of the granite, so it will be impossible to carry our boats around, though we can climb to the summit up a side gulch, and, passing along a mile or two, can descend to the river. This we find on examination; but such a portage would be impracticable for us, and we must run the rapid, or abandon the river. There is no hesitation. We step into our boats, push off and away we go, first on smooth but swift water, then we strike a glassy wave, and ride to its top, down again into the trough, up again on a higher wave, and down and up on waves higher and still higher, until we strike one just as it curls back, and a breaker rolls over our little boat. Still, on we speed, shooting past projecting rocks, till the little boat is caught in a whirlpool, and spun around several times. At last we pull out again into the stream, and now the other boats have passed us. The open compartment of the _Emma Dean_ is filled with water, and every breaker rolls over us. Hurled back from a rock, now on this side, now on that, we are carried into an eddy, in which we struggle for a few minutes, and are then out again, the breakers still rolling over us. Our boat is unmanageable, but she cannot sink, and we drift down another hundred yards, through breakers; how, we scarcely know. We find the other boats have turned into an eddy at the foot of the fall, and are waiting to catch us as we come, for the men have seen that our boat is swamped. They push out as we come near, and pull us in against the wall. We bail our boat, and on we go again.

崖壁现在超过一英里高——一个难以体会的垂直距离。站在华盛顿财政部大楼的南台阶上，沿宾夕法尼亚大道望向国会山公园，把这个距离量到头顶，想象崖壁延伸到那个高度，你就明白我的意思了；或者，站在纽约的运河街，沿百老汇望向格雷斯教堂，就是大约那个距离；或者，站在芝加哥的湖街桥，望向中央车站，你又有了一次。

The walls, now, are more than a mile in height--a vertical distance difficult to appreciate. Stand on the south steps of the Treasury building in Washington, and look down Pennsylvania Avenue to the Capitol Park, and measure this distance overhead, and imagine cliffs to extend to that altitude, and you will understand what I mean; or, stand at Canal Street, in New York, and look up Broadway to Grace Church, and you have about the distance; or, stand at Lake Street bridge, in Chicago, and look down to the Central Depot, and you have it again.

这高度有一千英尺是花岗岩岩峰，然后是陡坡和垂直崖壁，一层层升到峰顶。峡谷下方又黑又窄，上方红灰相间、烈焰般张开，崖壁上布满岩峰和棱角凸出——许多地方被侧谷切开——看起来像一片巨大的岩石荒野。我们滑行在这壮丽阴郁的深处，始终侧耳倾听——因为疯狂的水一直咆哮；始终注视、始终向前窥探——因为窄峡蜿蜒，河被夹得只能看见几百码，下方可能有什么我们不知道；但我们听着瀑布、盯着岩石，或不时在一个凹处的湾里停下，欣赏这巨大的景色。而一路前行，总有新的尖塔或塔岩，新的岩峰或山巅，上方高原的某个远景，某块形状奇特的岩石，或某条又深又窄的侧谷。然后我们又来到一处破碎的瀑布，看起来比今早闯过的那个更难。

A thousand feet of this is up through granite crags, then steep slopes and perpendicular cliffs rise, one above another, to the summit. The gorge is black and narrow below, red and gray and flaring above, with crags and angular projections on the walls, which, cut in many places by side cañons, seem to be a vast wilderness of rocks. Down in these grand, gloomy depths we glide, ever listening, for the mad waters keep up their roar; ever watching, ever peering ahead, for the narrow cañon is winding, and the river is closed in so that we can see but a few hundred yards, and what there may be below we know not; but we listen for falls, and watch for rocks, or stop now and then, in the bay of a recess, to admire the gigantic scenery. And ever, as we go, there is some new pinnacle or tower, some crag or peak, some distant view of the upper plateau, some strange shaped rock, or some deep, narrow side cañon. Then we come to another broken fall, which appears more difficult than the one we ran this morning.

一条小溪从右面流进来，水的第一段落差落在被这条侧流冲下来的巨砾上。我们在它口上靠岸，停了一两个小时察看这道瀑布。看来可以用绳从右壁一侧一点一点地往下放，至少放一部分。于是我们越过最初的岩石做搬运，在下方一些巨砾上找到立足点。然后我们把一条船放到它绳子的尽头，它到达一块突出岩石的角落，一个队员扒住它、稳住它，而我察看下方的一个回流。我想我们可以让其他船从我们身边放下去，在那个回流里接住它们。这很快办成了，回流里的队员把我们拉到他们那边。这小回流岸边水面上有两英尺的砾石滩。站在滩上，一些队员接过小船的绳子，让它漂下去顶住另一块突出的岩角。这里有段小岩架，我船上的一个人爬上去，一根更短的绳递给他，他把船拴在崖壁边。然后第二条放下去，带着第三条的绳。第二条拴好后，站在上方滩上的两个人跳进最后一条船，它正被……

A small creek comes in on the right, and the first fall of the water is over boulders, which have been carried down by this lateral stream. We land at its mouth, and stop for an hour or two to examine the fall. It seems possible to let down with lines, at least a part of the way, from point to point, along the right hand wall. So we make a portage over the first rocks, and find footing on some boulders below. Then we let down one of the boats to the end of her line, when she reaches a corner of the projecting rock, to which one of the men clings, and steadies her, while I examine an eddy below. I think we can pass the other boats down by us, and catch them in the eddy. This is soon done and the men in the boats in the eddy pull us to their side. On the shore of this little eddy there is about two feet of gravel beach above the water. Standing on this beach, some of the men take the line of the little boat and let it drift down against another projecting angle. Here is a little shelf, on which a man from my boat climbs, and a shorter line is passed to him, and he fastens the boat to the side of the cliff. Then the second one is let down, bringing the line of the third. When the second boat is tied up, the two men standing on the beach above spring into the last boat, which is pulled up alongside of ours. Then we let down the boats, for twenty-five or thirty yards, by walking along the shelf, landing them again in the mouth of a side cañon. Just below this there is another pile of boulders, over which we make another portage. From the foot of these rocks we can climb to another shelf, forty or fifty feet above the water.

夜晚在沙滩上。我们找到几根卡在岩石里的柴。雨下得很大，我们没有遮蔽，但还是生起火吃了晚饭。我们裹着斗篷坐在岩石上过夜，能睡多少算多少。

On this beach we camp for the night. We find a few sticks, which have lodged in the rocks. It is raining hard, and we have no shelter, but kindle a fire and have our supper. We sit on the rocks all night, wrapped in our ponchos, getting what sleep we can.

8 月 15 日。今早我们发现可以放下去三四百码，方法是这样的：我们沿崖壁走，从一个突出的点爬到另一个点，有时贴近水边，有时在五六十英尺高处，用绳子拉住船，船上留两个人，防止她撞上岩石，也别让绳子卡在壁上。两小时我们把它们都放到了能放的最远处。下游几码处，河猛烈地撞上一块突出的岩石，我们的船被拉进上方一个小湾。我们现在必须设法从这个湾里出去，绕过下方的那个角。小船被船头斜向上游拉住。我们跳进去，只划了几下就甩离了那块危险的岩石。其他船也照样跟着，这段激流就过了。

_August 15._--This morning we find we can let down for three or four hundred yards, and it is managed in this way: We pass along the wall, by climbing from projecting point to point, sometimes near the water’s edge, at other places fifty or sixty feet above, and hold the boat with a line, while two men remain aboard, and prevent her from being dashed against the rocks, and keep the line from getting caught on the wall. In two hours we have brought them all down, as far as it is possible, in this way. A few yards below, the river strikes with great violence against a projecting rock, and our boats are pulled up in a little bay above. We must now manage to pull out of this, and clear the point below. The little boat is held by the bow obliquely up the stream. We jump in, and pull out only a few strokes, and sweep clear of the dangerous rock. The other boats follow in the same manner, and the rapid is passed.

要描述这种航行的辛劳并不容易。我们必须防止波浪把船撞上悬崖。有时河很急，我们得把绳子绕在岩石上，防止她被一个浪从我们手里抢走；但当落差太大、或水槽太急时，我们必须让她跳下去，在下面接住她——否则回头浪会把她拖到落水之下，她就沉了。当我们想让她离岸稍远、穿过岩石间的一条水道时，先扔进几根小漂木，观察它们的路线，看我们该往哪儿驶，好让她安全通过水道。于是我们就这样握住、放掉、划、抬、挡——在岩石间、绕过岩石、越过岩石。

It is not easy to describe the labor of such navigation. We must prevent the waves from dashing the boats against the cliffs. Sometimes, where the river is swift, we must put a bight of rope about a rock, to prevent her being snatched from us by a wave; but where the plunge is too great, or the chute too swift, we must let her leap, and catch her below, or the undertow will drag her under the falling water, and she sinks. Where we wish to run her out a little way from shore, through a channel between rocks, we first throw in little sticks of drift wood, and watch their course, to see where we must steer, so that she will pass the channel in safety. And so we hold, and let go, and pull, and lift, and ward, among rocks, around rocks, and over rocks.

现在我们继续穿过这庄严、神秘的路。河很深，峡谷很窄，仍被阻塞，所以水流并不平稳；但水在旋转、翻滚、沸腾，我们几乎无法判断该往哪儿去。时而船被带向右，也许紧贴崖壁；时而又被射进主流，也许被拖到另一侧，在那里被漩涡抓住，打转。我们不能随心所欲地靠岸或行驶。船完全失控；行驶毫无秩序可言；时而这条在前，时而那条在前，每船的人都为自己求生存而拼命。在这样的地方我们又遇到一段激流。两条船被迫闯了过去。一条成功靠岸，但没有能搬运的落脚点，又被推进了主流。下一刻一道巨大的回流波灌满了敞开的船舱；她浸满了水，失控地漂着。一道又一道碎浪从她上面滚过，一道浪把她掀翻。人被甩了出来；但他们扒住船，她漂下来一段距离，与我们并行，我们抓住了她。她很快被舀干，人们重新上船；但桨丢了，所以从「爱玛·迪恩」号匀出一对。然后我们找到两英里平稳的水。

And now we go on through this solemn, mysterious way. The river is very deep, the cañon very narrow, and still obstructed, so that there is no steady flow of the stream; but the waters wheel, and roll, and boil, and we are scarcely able to determine where we can go. Now, the boat is carried to the right, perhaps close to the wall; again, she is shot into the stream, and perhaps is dragged over to the other side, where, caught in a whirlpool, she spins about. We can neither land nor run as we please. The boats are entirely unmanageable; no order in their running can be preserved; now one, now another, is ahead, each crew laboring for its own preservation. In such a place we come to another rapid. Two of the boats run it perforce. One succeeds in landing, but there is no foot-hold by which to make a portage, and she is pushed out again into the stream. The next minute a great reflex wave fills the open compartment; she is water-logged, and drifts unmanageable. Breaker after breaker rolls over her, and one capsizes her. The men are thrown out; but they cling to the boat, and she drifts down some distance, alongside of us, and we are able to catch her. She is soon bailed out, and the men are aboard once more; but the oars are lost, so a pair from the _Emma Dean_ is spared. Then for two miles we find smooth water.

今天云在峡谷里嬉戏。时而它们滚滚而下，大团大团地，让峡谷充满阴郁；时而它们悬在上方，从一壁到另一壁，给峡谷盖上一个即将来临的风暴之顶；而我们可以沿着这条峡谷走廊上下望出很远的距离——头顶是云做的屋顶，两侧是黑色花岗岩的壁，河里碎浪闪着光。然后一阵风沿侧沟扫下，在云中撕开一道缝，露出蓝天，一道阳光倾泻进来。然后云向远处飘去，绕在岩峰、山巅、尖塔、塔岩和崖壁周围，给它们披上一件斗篷，不时掀起，把一切都照得轮廓分明。然后幼云从侧谷里爬出来，绕过岩角滑行，又爬回去，钻进更远的峡谷。然后，云成层地横在峡谷上，中间留出一线线眺望的景色，望见远处的崖壁和岩石。云是天空的孩子，当它们在岩石间嬉戏时，就把它们举到上方的天空。

Clouds are playing in the cañon to-day. Sometimes they roll down in great masses, filling the gorge with gloom; sometimes they hang above, from wall to wall, and cover the cañon with a roof of impending storm; and we can peer long distances up and down this cañon corridor, with its cloud roof overhead, its walls of black granite, and its river bright with the sheen of broken waters. Then, a gust of wind sweeps down a side gulch, and, making a rift in the clouds, reveals the blue heavens, and a stream of sunlight pours in. Then, the clouds drift away into the distance, and hang around crags, and peaks, and pinnacles, and towers, and walls, and cover them with a mantle, that lifts from time to time, and sets them all in sharp relief. Then, baby clouds creep out of side cañons, glide around points, and creep back again, into more distant gorges. Then, clouds, set in strata, across the cañon, with intervening vista views, to cliffs and rocks beyond. The clouds are children of the heavens, and when they play among the rocks, they lift them to the region above.

下雨了！很快上方形成细流，细流很快长成溪流，溪流长成小河，化成无数瀑布翻过崖壁，给河的咆哮添上它们狂野的音乐。雨停时，细流、溪流和小河都干涸了。下雨时落在这些陡峭岩石上的水，立刻汇进河里；即使有一根巨大的水管从云里直通到河里，水也不可能注得更突然。当风暴在峡谷上空爆发时，一条侧沟是危险的，因为突然的洪水可能到来，灌进来的水会把河抬高，让岩石在你眼前消失。

It rains! Rapidly little rills are formed above, and these soon grow into brooks, and the brooks grow into creeks, and tumble over the walls in innumerable cascades, adding their wild music to the roar of the river. When the rain ceases, the rills, brooks, and creeks run dry. The waters that fall, during a rain, on these steep rocks, are gathered at once into the river; they could scarcely be poured in more suddenly, if some vast spout ran from the clouds to the stream itself. When a storm bursts over the cañon, a side gulch is dangerous, for a sudden flood may come, and the inpouring waters will raise the river, so as to hide the rocks before your eyes.

午饭后不久，我们发现一条从北面流来的溪——一条清澈美丽的小溪，穿过一条壮丽的红色峡谷流下。我们靠岸，在它河口上方的沙滩上、一棵巨大的张着阔叶（柳形叶）树下扎营。

Early in the afternoon, we discover a stream, entering from the north, a clear, beautiful creek, coming down through a gorgeous red cañon. We land, and camp on a sand beach, above its mouth, under a great, overspreading tree, with willow shaped leaves.

8 月 16 日。今天我们又得晾干口粮、做桨。

_August 16._--We must dry our rations again to-day, and make oars.

科罗拉多河从不是清流，但过去三四天大部分时间在下雨，从崖壁倾泻下来的洪水带下大量泥沙，使它现在极其浑浊。我们在这里发现的这条小支流，却是一条清澈美丽的溪——或按这西部地区的说法叫河，那里的溪流并不丰富。我们已经在很远的上游给一条河命了名，纪念「恶天使」的大首领；而这条与它形成美丽的对照，所以我们决定叫它「光明天使」（Bright Angel）。

The Colorado is never a clear stream, but for the past three or four days it has been raining much of the time, and the floods, which are poured over the walls, have brought down great quantities of mud, making it exceedingly turbid now. The little affluent, which we have discovered here, is a clear, beautiful creek, or river, as it would be termed in this western country, where streams are not abundant. We have named one stream, away above, in honor of the great chief of the “Bad Angels,” and, as this is in beautiful contrast to that, we conclude to name it “Bright Angel.”

清晨，全队出发去探查光明天使河，专门目的是寻找做桨的木材。上游两英里处，我们发现一根大松木，是从高原上漂下来的——很可能来自六千多英尺的高度，但离得不远。路上它一定越过了许多瀑布和瀑布，因为它带着饱受磨难的伤痕。队员们把它滚上垫木，开始锯桨。

Early in the morning, the whole party starts up to explore the Bright Angel River, with the special purpose of seeking timber, from which to make oars. A couple of miles above, we find a large pine log, which has been floated down from the plateau, probably from an altitude of more than six thousand feet, but not many miles back. On its way, it must have passed over many cataracts and falls, for it bears scars in evidence of the rough usage which it has received. The men roll it on skids, and the work of sawing oars is commenced.

这条溪在远处的一道陡崖线下方发源——那道崖线终止了高原——并在最初一两英里内跌落四千多英尺；然后穿过一条又深又窄的峡谷，直到汇入大河。

This stream heads away back, under a line of abrupt cliffs, that terminates the plateau, and tumbles down more than four thousand feet in the first mile or two of its course; then runs through a deep, narrow cañon, until it reaches the river.

傍晚我回来，走进营地上面约两百码、这条溪上方的一条小沟，发现两三座老房子的废墟，原是石头砌的、抹着灰泥。只剩地基，但建屋用的不规则石块散落一地。在一间房里我找到一块古老的磨石，磨得很深，仿佛用过很多。四周散落大量陶器，还能看到旧路——有些地方在岩石里磨得很深。

Late in the afternoon I return, and go up a little gulch, just above this creek, about two hundred yards from camp, and discover the ruins of two or three old houses, which were originally of stone, laid in mortar. Only the foundations are left, but irregular blocks, of which the houses were constructed, lie scattered about. In one room I find an old mealing stone, deeply worn, as if it had been much used. A great deal of pottery is strewn around, and old trails, which in some places are deeply worn into the rocks, are seen.

我们始终想不明白，为什么这些古代民族要寻找这样难以到达的地方做家园。他们无疑是一个农耕民族，但这里没有大面积可耕的土地。在亚利桑那北部「图萨扬省」的奥赖比镇以西，居民们真的沿着崖面筑起了小梯田——那里有泉水涌出——就这样造出了菜园地。也许这里古代的居民也是这样造他们的农田的。但他们为什么要找这样的地方呢？当然，这里的人口并没有多到需要利用这样贫瘠的地区。能想到的唯一的解释是：我们知道，墨西哥被殖民后的一两个世纪里，许多远征队被派进现在属于亚利桑那和新墨西哥的地区，目的是把这些建城民族置于西班牙政府统治之下。他们许多村庄被毁，居民逃到……

It is ever a source of wonder to us why these ancient people sought such inaccessible places for their homes. They were, doubtless, an agricultural race, but there are no lands here, of any considerable extent, that they could have cultivated. To the west of Oraiby, one of the towns in the “Province of Tusayan,” in Northern Arizona, the inhabitants have actually built little terraces along the face of the cliff, where a spring gushes out, and thus made their sites for gardens. It is possible that the ancient inhabitants of this place made their agricultural lands in the same way. But why should they seek such spots? Surely, the country was not so crowded with population as to demand the utilization of so barren a region. The only solution of the problem suggested is this: We know that, for a century or two after the settlement of Mexico, many expeditions were sent into the country now comprised in Arizona and New Mexico, for the purpose of bringing the town building people under the dominion of the Spanish government. Many of their villages were destroyed, and the inhabitants fled to regions at that time unknown; and there are traditions, among the people who inhabit the _pueblos_ that still remain, that the cañons were these unknown lands. Maybe these buildings were erected at that time; sure it is that they have a much more modern appearance than the ruins scattered over Nevada, Utah, Colorado, Arizona, and New Mexico. Those old Spanish conquerors had a monstrous greed for gold, and a wonderful lust for saving souls. Treasures they must have; if not on earth, why, then, in heaven; and when they failed to find heathen temples, bedecked with silver, they propitiated Heaven by seizing the heathen themselves. There is yet extant a copy of a record, made by a heathen artist, to express his conception of the demands of the conquerors. In one part of the picture we have a lake, and near by stands a priest pouring water on the head of a native. On the other side, a poor Indian has a cord about his throat. Lines run from these two groups, to a central figure, a man with beard, and full Spanish panoply. The interpretation of the picture writing is this: “Be baptized, as this saved heathen; or be hanged, as that damned heathen.” Doubtless, some of these people preferred a third alternative, and, rather than be baptized or hanged, they chose to be imprisoned within these cañon walls.

8 月 17 日。我们的口粮还在坏；腌肉坏得太厉害，我们不得不扔掉。今早还出了个意外，小苏打掉到船外了。我们现在只剩够十天吃的发霉面粉、一点干苹果，但咖啡充足。我们必须尽可能加快速度。如果我们像上面峡谷里那样遇到困难，就可能被迫放弃这次远征，设法向北去摩门教徒的定居点。我们但愿最糟的地方已经过去，但我们的气压计全都坏得没法用，所以我们失去了高度的推算，不知道河还要下降多少。

_August 17._--Our rations are still spoiling; the bacon is so badly injured that we are compelled to throw it away. By an accident, this morning, the saleratus is lost overboard. We have now only musty flour sufficient for ten days, a few dried apples, but plenty of coffee. We must make all haste possible. If we meet with difficulties, as we have done in the cañon above, we may be compelled to give up the expedition, and try to reach the Mormon settlements to the north. Our hopes are that the worst places are passed, but our barometers are all so much injured as to be useless, so we have lost our reckoning in altitude, and know not how much descent the river has yet to make.

溪流仍然狂野湍急，滚过一条窄水道。我们进展缓慢，常常靠上一面壁、绕到某个点，才能看到下方的河。虽然急于前进，我们决心极其谨慎地跑，免得再出一次事故就丢掉全部补给。那点面粉变得多么珍贵！我们把它分到各船，小心收藏，这样只有船本身丢了它才会丢。

The stream is still wild and rapid, and rolls through a narrow channel. We make but slow progress, often landing against a wall, and climbing around some point, where we can see the river below. Although very anxious to advance, we are determined to run with great caution, lest, by another accident, we lose all our supplies. How precious that little flour has become! We divide it among the boats, and carefully store it away, so that it can be lost only by the loss of the boat itself.

我们跑了十英里半，在右边的岩石间扎营。我们一整天不时淋雨，浑身湿透、冷得发抖；但阵雨之间太阳猛烈地照着，温度计水银柱停在华氏 115 度，所以我们在极大的冷热之间快速切换，非常难受。今晚在雨里格外冷。我们仅有的一点帆布烂得没用了；从绿河城出发时的橡胶雨披全丢了；一半以上的人没有帽子，没有一个人有一整套衣服，也没有人有一条毯子。于是我们捡来漂木生起火；但晚饭后倾盆大雨把火浇灭了，我们在岩石上坐了一整夜，瑟瑟发抖，这一夜的难受比一天的辛劳更让人精疲力竭。

We make ten miles and a half, and camp among the rocks, on the right. We have had rain, from time to time, all day, and have been thoroughly drenched and chilled; but between showers the sun shines with great power, and the mercury in our thermometers stands at 115°, so that we have rapid changes from great extremes, which are very disagreeable. It is especially cold in the rain to-night. The little canvas we have is rotten and useless; the rubber ponchos, with which we started from Green River City, have all been lost; more than half the party is without hats, and not one of us has an entire suit of clothes, and we have not a blanket apiece. So we gather drift wood, and build a fire; but after supper the rain, coming down in torrents, extinguishes it, and we sit up all night, on the rocks, shivering, and are more exhausted by the night’s discomfort than by the day’s toil.

8 月 18 日。这一天用来做搬运，我们只前进了两英里。雨还在下。

_August 18._--The day is employed in making portages, and we advance but two miles on our journey. Still it rains.

趁队员们做搬运，我爬上花岗岩到它的顶，越过铁锈色砂岩和绿黄色页岩，走到大理石壁脚下。我爬得那么高，下面的队员和船都消失在黑色深处，奔腾的河变成一条汩汩的小溪；而上方还有更多的峡谷。我周围全是有趣的地质记录。书摊开着，我可以一边跑一边读。我周围全是壮丽的景色，因为云又在这峡谷里嬉戏。但不知怎么，我想着那九天的口粮、糟糕的河、岩石的教训，这景色的辉煌只看到了一半。

While the men are at work making portages, I climb up the granite to its summit, and go away back over the rust colored sandstones and greenish yellow shales, to the foot of the marble wall. I climb so high that the men and boats are lost in the black depths below, and the dashing river is a rippling brook; and still there is more cañon above than below. All about me are interesting geological records. The book is open, and I can read as I run. All about me are grand views, for the clouds are playing again in the gorges. But somehow I think of the nine days’ rations, and the bad river, and the lesson of the rocks, and the glory of the scene is but half seen.

我继续走到一个角，希望看到峡谷之外的地方，看看我们能不能很快穿过这片高原——或者至少遇到某个能让我们离开花岗岩的地质变化；但到了那个点，我只看到下方一个深谷的迷宫。

I push on to an angle, where I hope to get a view of the country beyond, to see, if possible, what the prospect may be of our soon running through this plateau, or, at least, of meeting with some geological change that will let us out of the granite; but, arriving at the point, I can see below only a labyrinth of deep gorges.

8 月 19 日。今早又下雨。我们仍困在花岗岩牢笼里，中午前都在做一段又长又糟的搬运。

_August 19._--Rain again this morning. Still we are in our granite prison, and the time is occupied until noon in making a long, bad portage.

午饭后闯一段激流时，先驱船被一个浪掀翻了。我们比大船领先一些距离，河又急又快，我们无法靠岸，只能扒住船，被带着顺流冲过又一段激流。上方船里的人看到我们的困境，但他们被漩涡抓住，在涡流里打转，过了好一阵子才来救我们。最后他们终于来了；我们的船被翻正、舀干；那些幸运地一直跟着我们漂的桨被捞起来，我们继续前进，甚至没有靠岸。

After dinner, in running a rapid, the pioneer boat is upset by a wave. We are some distance in advance of the larger boats, the river is rough and swift, and we are unable to land, but cling to the boat, and are carried down stream, over another rapid. The men in the boats above see our trouble, but they are caught in whirlpools, and are spinning about in eddies, and it seems a long time before they come to our relief. At last they do come; our boat is turned right side up, bailed out; the oars, which fortunately have floated along in company with us, are gathered up, and on we go, without even landing.

事故后不久，云散开了，又见阳光。

Soon after the accident the clouds break away, and we have sunshine again.

很快我们找到一小片沙滩，刚好够靠岸。我们在这里扎营，但没有柴。河对岸稍上游处，我们看见一些漂木卡在岩石里。于是我们运了两船过来，生起大火，把一切摊开晾干。这是我们一周来第一个愉快的夜晚——营地中央一堆温暖烘烤的火，头顶一小片天空里几颗明亮的星星。

Soon we find a little beach, with just room enough to land. Here we camp, but there is no wood. Across the river, and a little way above, we see some drift wood lodged in the rocks. So we bring two boat loads over, build a huge fire, and spread everything to dry. It is the first cheerful night we have had for a week; a warm, drying fire in the midst of the camp, and a few bright stars in our patch of heavens overhead.

8 月 20 日。今早峡谷的特征变了。河更宽，崖壁更倾斜，由竖立的黑色板岩构成。这些近乎垂直的板岩有些地方被冲蚀——也就是说，较软的岩层在较硬的之间被冲走，较硬的留下来立着。这样形成了一些奇特的凹龛，里面有安静的水湾，但规模比大理石峡谷那些大湾和扶壁小得多。

_August 20._--The characteristics of the cañon change this morning. The river is broader, the walls more sloping, and composed of black slates, that stand on edge. These nearly vertical slates are washed out in places--that is, the softer beds are washed out between the harder, which are left standing. In this way, curious little alcoves are formed, in which are quiet bays of water, but on a much smaller scale than the great bays and buttresses of Marble Cañon.

河仍很急，我们几次停下用绳放船，但随着我们跑了十英里，进展更大了。我们在右岸扎营。在这里一块暗色火成岩台地上，我们又发现一组废墟。这块岩石上显然曾有相当一个村庄。我们又找到磨石和大量碎陶器，在废墟后面岩石上一个天然小洞里，我们找到一个球形篮子，大约能装三分之一蒲式耳。它破得很厉害，我想拿起它时，它碎成了一堆。还有许多漂亮的燧石片，仿佛这是一个老箭头匠人的家。

The river is still rapid, and we stop to let down with lines several times, but make greater progress as we run ten miles. We camp on the right bank. Here, on a terrace of trap, we discover another group of ruins. There was evidently quite a village on this rock. Again we find mealing stones, and much broken pottery, and up in a little natural shelf in the rock, back of the ruins, we find a globular basket, that would hold perhaps a third of a bushel. It is badly broken, and, as I attempt to take it up, it falls to pieces. There are many beautiful flint chips, as if this had been the home of an old arrow maker.

8 月 21 日。今早我们早早出发，被好天气的前景和昨天良好的行程鼓舞着。营地下游四分之一英里处，河猛地向左转，营地到那点之间水很急，沿一道长长的破碎水槽冲下去，在那左转处堆在崖脚。我们试着横渡过去，以便从另一侧下行，但水流湍急，看来我们躲不开下面的岩石；但在横渡时，船头转向对岸，所以我们被横着扫下去，靠回弹的水阻止我们撞上壁。我们在这些浪涛里颠簸了几秒，被带着越过了危险。下方河又向右转，峡谷很窄，我们只能看见前方很短的距离。水也非常急，没有靠岸处。从这个弯道后面传来一阵疯狂的咆哮，我们被带着以令人眩晕的速度冲向又一段激流的上端。两侧……

_August 21._--We start early this morning, cheered by the prospect of a fine day, and encouraged, also, by the good run made yesterday. A quarter of a mile below camp the river turns abruptly to the left, and between camp and that point is very swift, running down in a long, broken chute, and piling up against the foot of the cliff, where it turns to the left. We try to pull across, so as to go down on the other side, but the waters are swift, and it seems impossible for us to escape the rock below; but, in pulling across, the bow of the boat is turned to the farther shore, so that we are swept broadside down, and are prevented, by the rebounding waters, from striking against the wall. There we toss about for a few seconds in these billows, and are carried past the danger. Below, the river turns again to the right, the cañon is very narrow, and we see in advance but a short distance. The water, too, is very swift, and there is no landing place. From around this curve there comes a mad roar, and down we are carried, with a dizzying velocity, to the head of another rapid. On either side, high over our heads, there are overhanging granite walls, and the sharp bends cut off our view, so that a few minutes will carry us into unknown waters. Away we go, on one long, winding chute. I stand on deck, supporting myself with a strap, fastened on either side to the gunwale, and the boat glides rapidly, where the water is smooth, or, striking a wave, she leaps and bounds like a thing of life, and we have a wild, exhilarating ride for ten miles, which we make in less than an hour. The excitement is so great that we forget the danger, until we hear the roar of the great fall below; then we back on our oars, and are carried slowly toward its head, and succeed in landing just above, and find that we have to make another portage. At this we are engaged until some time after dinner.

我们就此冲出了花岗岩！

Just here we run out of the granite!

不到半天跑了十英里，下面是石灰岩壁。好心情回来了；我们忘掉风暴、阴郁、云遮的峡谷、黑色花岗岩和咆哮的河，兴高采烈地把船推出岸边。

Ten miles in less than half a day, and limestone walls below. Good cheer returns; we forget the storms, and the gloom, and cloud covered cañons, and the black granite, and the raging river, and push our boats from shore in great glee.

虽然出了花岗岩，河仍很急，我们又绕过一个角向右，掉头朝我们来的方向驶去，再次看见花岗岩——它的窄峡和黑色岩峰；但我们没再遇到大的瀑布或激流。我们仍谨慎地跑，不时停下察看一些看起来糟糕的地方。不过，下午我们又跑了十英里；今天一共二十英里。

Though we are out of the granite, the river is still swift, and we wheel about a point again to the right, and turn, so as to head back in the direction from which we came, and see the granite again, with its narrow gorge and black crags; but we meet with no more great falls, or rapids. Still, we run cautiously, and stop, from time to time, to examine some places which look bad. Yet, we make ten miles this afternoon; twenty miles, in all, to-day.

8 月 22 日。今早又遇到激流，花了好几个小时通过——用绳把船一段段地从岩石间放下去，近半英里，然后不得不做一次长搬运。趁队员们在忙，我爬上东北面的崖壁，到约两千五百英尺高处，那里能好好俯瞰下方很长一段峡谷。它向西南延伸。崖壁看起来升得很陡，有两千五百或三千英尺，然后两侧各有一条缓缓倾斜的台地，延伸两三英里，之后又看到一千五百或两千英尺高的崖壁。从这些崖壁边缘，高原向北和南延伸很长距离。沿着峡谷往下，在右壁上，我能看到一群山，有些似乎立在峡谷边缘。台地的效果是形成一种狭窄蜿蜒的山谷外观——两侧是高壁，中间一条又深又暗的蜿蜒深谷。从……

_August 22._--We come to rapids again, this morning, and are occupied several hours in passing them, letting the boats down, from rock to rock, with lines, for nearly half a mile, and then have to make a long portage. While the men are engaged in this, I climb the wall on the northeast, to a height of about two thousand five hundred feet, where I can obtain a good view of a long stretch of cañon below. Its course is to the southwest. The walls seem to rise very abruptly, for two thousand five hundred or three thousand feet, and then there is a gently sloping terrace, on each side, for two or three miles, and again we find cliffs, one thousand five hundred or two thousand feet high. From the brink of these the plateau stretches back to the north and south, for a long distance. Away down the cañon, on the right wall, I can see a group of mountains, some of which appear to stand on the brink of the cañon. The effect of the terrace is to give the appearance of a narrow winding valley, with high walls on either side, and a deep, dark, meandering gorge down its middle. It is impossible, from this point of view, to determine whether we have granite at the bottom, or not; but, from geological considerations, I conclude that we shall have marble walls below.

回到船上后，我们又跑了一英里，扎营过夜。

After my return to the boats, we run another mile, and camp for the night.

今天只跑了七英里多一点，又有部分面粉在河里泡了。

We have made but little over seven miles to-day, and a part of our flour has been soaked in the river again.

8 月 23 日。今天我们再次穿行大理石壁之间。不时有一小段路穿过花岗岩斑块，像山丘一样从石灰岩里顶出来。在这样一处，我们又得做一次搬运；趁这个空当，我沿一条小溪向北走，一路涉水，有时不得不没到脖子；别处被迫游过瀑布脚下冲出的水潭。沿途有许多瀑布和从两侧岩石里涌出的泉水。有时一棵三角叶杨长在水上。我来到一处美丽的瀑布，一百五十多英尺高，从右侧破碎的岩石绕上去。继续上行，我发现峡谷收得非常窄，只有十五到二十英尺宽；但两侧崖壁升起几百英尺、也许几千英尺；我很难说清。

_August 23._--Our way to-day is again through marble walls. Now and then we pass, for a short distance, through patches of granite, like hills thrust up into the limestone. At one of these places we have to make another portage, and, taking advantage of the delay, I go up a little stream, to the north, wading it all the way, sometimes having to plunge in to my neck; in other places being compelled to swim across little basins that have been excavated at the foot of the falls. Along its course are many cascades and springs gushing out from the rocks on either side. Sometimes a cottonwood tree grows over the water. I come to one beautiful fall, of more than a hundred and fifty feet, and climb around it to the right, on the broken rocks. Still going up, I find the cañon narrowing very much, being but fifteen or twenty feet wide; yet the walls rise on either side many hundreds of feet, perhaps thousands; I can hardly tell.

有些地方溪流不是垂直向下切穿岩石，而是斜着切，所以一壁悬在另一壁上方。另一些地方上方垂直、下方倾斜，或上方倾斜、下方垂直，所以根本看不见头顶的天空。但我走不了了。我估计搬运该花的时间几乎到了，便一路小跑往回赶，该涉水就涉水、该冲过水潭就冲过，发现队员们在等我，我们重新上了河。

In some places the stream has not excavated its channel down vertically through the rocks, but has cut obliquely, so that one wall overhangs the other. In other places it is cut vertically above and obliquely below, or obliquely above and vertically below, so that it is impossible to see out overhead. But I can go no farther. The time which I estimated it would take to make the portage has almost expired, and I start back on a round trot, wading in the creek where I must, and plunging through basins, and find the men waiting for me, and away we go on the river.

午饭后不久，我们经过右面一条溪，它用一百多英尺的直落跃入科罗拉多河，形成一道美丽的瀑布。上面是一层很硬的岩床，三四十英尺厚，下面是软得多的岩层。上方的硬岩层比被冲蚀的软岩层突出好几码，在瀑布后面形成一座深洞，溪水穿过上方一道窄缝，注入下方一个深潭。洞穴般的厅堂四周岩石上，长着美丽的蕨类——纤细的叶片、珐琅般的茎。小羽片尖端向下弯，形成孢子囊。它很像少女发蕨，但大得多。这纤细的叶丛覆盖了泉边所有的岩石，给厅堂增添了许多美。但我们没多少时间欣赏，继续前进。

Just after dinner we pass a stream on the right, which leaps into the Colorado by a direct fall of more than a hundred feet, forming a beautiful cascade. There is a bed of very hard rock above, thirty or forty feet in thickness, and much softer beds below. The hard beds above project many yards beyond the softer, which are washed out, forming a deep cave behind the fall, and the stream pours through a narrow crevice above into a deep pool below. Around on the rocks, in the cave like chamber, are set beautiful ferns, with delicate fronds and enameled stalks. The little frondlets have their points turned down, to form spore cases. It has very much the appearance of the Maiden’s hair fern, but is much larger. This delicate foliage covers the rocks all about the fountain, and gives the chamber great beauty. But we have little time to spend in admiration, so on we go.

今天下午我们进展很好，被湍急的河流带着，飞越激流，没有遇到严重障碍。

We make fine progress this afternoon, carried along by a swift river, and shoot over the rapids, finding no serious obstructions.

峡谷壁有两千五百或三千英尺，非常规整，几乎垂直升起，但这里那里有窄台阶，偶尔我们能望见远处上方的宽阔台地和更远的崖壁。

The cañon walls, for two thousand five hundred or three thousand feet, are very regular, rising almost perpendicularly, but here and there set with narrow steps, and occasionally we can see away above the broad terrace, to distant cliffs.

今晚我们宿在一个大理石洞穴里，算算行程，我们已跑了二十二英里。

We camp to-night in a marble cave, and find, on looking at our reckoning, we have run twenty-two miles.

8 月 24 日。今天峡谷更宽了。崖壁升到近三千英尺的垂直高度。许多地方河在大弯里从崖壁下流过，形成半穹顶形的圆形剧场。

_August 24._--The cañon is wider to-day. The walls rise to a vertical height of nearly three thousand feet. In many places the river runs under a cliff, in great curves, forming amphitheaters, half dome shaped.

虽然河很急，我们没遇到严重障碍，跑了二十英里。奇怪的是，现在我们每次停下都急着算行程——因为我们的伙食只剩大量的咖啡、很少的坏面粉和极少的干苹果。这已经变成一场冲向晚餐的赛跑。尽管如此，我们进展这么好，人人兴高采烈，但一刻日光也不浪费。

Though the river is rapid, we meet with no serious obstructions, and run twenty miles. It is curious how anxious we are to make up our reckoning every time we stop, now that our diet is confined to plenty of coffee, very little spoiled flour, and very few dried apples. It has come to be a race for a dinner. Still, we make such fine progress, all hands are in good cheer, but not a moment of daylight is lost.

8 月 25 日。今早我们跑了十二英里，然后来到立在河里的熔岩纪念碑——大多是低岩，但有些是超过一百英尺高的柱体。继续下行三四英里，发现它们越来越多。两侧看到大量冷却的熔岩和许多火山渣锥；然后我们来到一处陡峭的瀑布。就在瀑布上方右侧壁上，一座火山渣锥——或者说死火山——带一个轮廓分明的火山口，正立在峡谷边缘。这无疑是我们两三天前看到的那个。从这座火山，巨量熔岩倾泻进河里，一条熔岩流还沿峡谷向上游跑了三四英里，向下游跑了多远我们不知道。它正是从峡谷壁倾泻处形成那道瀑布。整个北侧，目力所及，都铺着黑色玄武岩，而对面壁上高处也有同样物质的斑块，歇在台地上，填满了旧凹龛和……

_August 25._--We make twelve miles this morning, when we come to monuments of lava, standing in the river; low rocks, mostly, but some of them shafts more than a hundred feet high. Going on down, three or four miles, we find them increasing in number. Great quantities of cooled lava and many cinder cones are seen on either side; and then we come to an abrupt cataract. Just over the fall, on the right wall, a cinder cone, or extinct volcano, with a well defined crater, stands on the very brink of the cañon. This, doubtless, is the one we saw two or three days ago. From this volcano vast floods of lava have been poured down into the river, and a stream of the molten rock has run up the cañon, three or four miles, and down, we know not how far. Just where it poured over the cañon wall is the fall. The whole north side, as far as we can see, is lined with the black basalt, and high up on the opposite wall are patches of the same material, resting on the benches, and filling old alcoves and caves, giving to the wall a spotted appearance.

岩石沿一条横穿此处的线断成两半，我们沿峡谷下来的这三十英里一直看到的岩层，在这条线较低的一侧下落了八百英尺——形成了地质学家所说的断层。火山锥正立在这条裂缝上方。在河的对岸，巨大的泉水从这条裂缝里涌出，在河面上方一两百英尺处，注入的水流几乎与小科罗拉多河一样大。

The rocks are broken in two, along a line which here crosses the river, and the beds, which we have seen coming down the cañon for the last thirty miles, have dropped 800 feet, on the lower side of the line, forming what geologists call a fault. The volcanic cone stands directly over the fissure thus formed. On the side of the river opposite, mammoth springs burst out of this crevice, one or two hundred feet above the river, pouring in a stream quite equal in volume to the Colorado Chiquito.

这条泉水似乎富含碳酸钙，水蒸发后在岩石上留下一层结壳；这个过程已持续很久，因为能看到大量沉积，里面有水盆，还有冒泡的泉。水是咸的。

This stream seems to be loaded with carbonate of lime, and the water, evaporating, leaves an incrustation on the rocks; and this process has been continued for a long time, for extensive deposits are noticed, in which are basins, with bubbling springs. The water is salty.

我们在这里做了一次搬运，约三小时完成，继续前进。

We have to make a portage here, which is completed in about three hours, and on we go.

我们顺流而下毫无困难，我能观察这次熔岩洪水相关的奇妙现象。峡谷无疑曾被填到一千二到一千五百英尺高——也许不止一次洪水。这会堵住河水；而在切穿这条巨大熔岩层时，形成了一条新河道，有时在一侧，有时在另一侧。冷却的熔岩比构成崖壁的岩石质地更坚硬，有些地方保留下来；另一些地方切出窄河道，两侧各留下一线玄武岩。可能熔岩靠壁处冷却更快，中心流走了；但这我们只能猜测。还有其他地方，几乎全部熔岩都不见了，只能看到粘在壁上的斑块。顺流而下时，我们看到它流进了侧谷。有些地方这玄武岩有漂亮的柱状结构，常呈同心棱柱状……

We have no difficulty as we float along, and I am able to observe the wonderful phenomena connected with this flood of lava. The cañon was doubtless filled to a height of twelve or fifteen hundred feet, perhaps by more than one flood. This would dam the water back; and in cutting through this great lava bed, a new channel has been formed, sometimes on one side, sometimes on the other. The cooled lava, being of firmer texture than the rocks of which the walls are composed, remains in some places; in others a narrow channel has been cut, leaving a line of basalt on either side. It is possible that the lava cooled faster on the sides against the walls, and that the center ran out; but of this we can only conjecture. There are other places, where almost the whole of the lava is gone, patches of it only being seen where it has caught on the walls. As we float down, we can see that it ran out into side cañons. In some places this basalt has a fine, columnar structure, often in concentric prisms, and masses of these concentric columns have coalesced. In some places, when the flow occurred, the cañon was probably at about the same depth as it is now, for we can see where the basalt has rolled out on the sands, and, what seems curious to me, the sands are not melted or metamorphosed to any appreciable extent. In places the bed of the river is of sandstone or limestone, in other places of lava, showing that it has all been cut out again where the sandstones and limestones appear; but there is a little yet left where the bed is of lava.

这里一定有过一场多么激烈的水火之争！想象一条熔岩河，流进一条融雪水河。水是怎样地翻腾沸腾；怎样的蒸汽云卷上天！

What a conflict of water and fire there must have been here! Just imagine a river of molten rock, running down into a river of melted snow. What a seething and boiling of the waters; what clouds of steam rolled into the heavens!

今天跑了三十五英里。万岁！

Thirty-five miles to-day. Hurrah!

8 月 26 日。峡谷壁随着我们前进稳步升高。它们依然雄壮，几乎垂直直到台地。我们仍能看到昨天发现的喷发的证据，但玄武岩的厚度沿河下行在减小；不过有些地方它被从上方台地上的火山流下来的溪流加强了——那些火山我们从河上看不见。

_August 26._--The cañon walls are steadily becoming higher as we advance. They are still bold, and nearly vertical up to the terrace. We still see evidence of the eruption discovered yesterday, but the thickness of the basalt is decreasing, as we go down the stream; yet it has been reinforced at points by streams that have come down from volcanoes standing on the terrace above, but which we cannot see from the river below.

自我们离开小科罗拉多河以来，还没看到任何证据表明两侧高原上的印第安部落下到河边来；但今天十一点左右，我们在右壁脚下发现一个印第安菜园——正是一条带窄洪泛平原的小溪穿过一条侧谷下来的地方。沿谷地，印第安人种了玉米，用崖脚泉水涌出的水灌溉。玉米长得相当好，但还不到能吃烤玉米穗的时候；不过有些不错的嫩西葫芦。我们带了十几个上船，匆匆离开——不愿被抓到偷盗，却用我们极度匮乏为自己辩解。我们往下游跑了一小段，到确定印第安人追不上的地方；我们做了一大锅西葫芦酱！是的，我们没有盐调味，但它给我们的死面饼和……

Since we left the Colorado Chiquito, we have seen no evidences that the tribe of Indians inhabiting the plateaus on either side ever come down to the river; but about eleven o’clock to-day we discover an Indian garden, at the foot of the wall on the right, just where a little stream, with a narrow flood plain, comes down through a side cañon. Along the valley, the Indians have planted corn, using the water which burst out in springs at the foot of the cliff, for irrigation. The corn is looking quite well, but is not sufficiently advanced to give us roasting ears; but there are some nice, green squashes. We carry ten or a dozen of these on board our boats, and hurriedly leave, not willing to be caught in the robbery, yet excusing ourselves by pleading our great want. We run down a short distance, to where we feel certain no Indians can follow; and what a kettle of squash sauce we make! True, we have no salt with which to season it, but it makes a fine addition to our unleavened bread and coffee. Never was fruit so sweet as these stolen squashes.

午饭后我们继续前进，速度很快，遇到许多激流，但没有一条危险到我们不能安全闯过；我们在黄昏停下，算算行程，发现又跑了三十五英里。

After dinner we push on again, making fine time, finding many rapids, but none so bad that we cannot run them with safety, and when we stop, just at dusk, and foot up our reckoning, we find we have run thirty-five miles again.

我们做了怎样一顿晚饭——死面饼、绿西葫芦酱和浓咖啡。我们几天来已经吃半份口粮，但烤西葫芦管够。

What a supper we make; unleavened bread, green squash sauce, and strong coffee. We have been for a few days on half rations, but we have no stint of roast squash.

再这样过几天，我们就能出牢笼了。

A few days like this, and we shall be out of prison.

8 月 27 日。今早河转向更偏南的方向。岩石向北倾斜，我们正快速跑进更低的岩层。除非我们改变方向，否则很快又要跑进花岗岩。这让我们有些焦虑。河不时转向西，唤起希望，又很快被一次向南的转向粉碎。大约九点我们来到那令人恐惧的岩石。看到河进入这些又黑又硬的壁，我们心里不是没有疑虑。刚进去就不得不做一次搬运；然后不得不用绳绕过一些丑陋的岩石放船。然后又跑一两英里，下面的激流就能看见了。

_August 27._--This morning the river takes a more southerly direction. The dip of the rocks is to the north, and we are rapidly running into lower formations. Unless our course changes, we shall very soon run again into the granite. This gives us some anxiety. Now and then the river turns to the west, and excites hopes that are soon destroyed by another turn to the south. About nine o’clock we come to the dreaded rock. It is with no little misgiving that we see the river enter these black, hard walls. At its very entrance we have to make a portage; then we have to let down with lines past some ugly rocks. Then we run a mile or two farther, and then the rapids below can be seen.

大约十一点我们来到一处河段，看起来比它全程遇到过的任何地方都糟得多。一条小溪从左边流下。我们先在右边靠岸，爬过花岗岩尖峰走了一两英里，但看不到能放船的路，闯过去则必死无疑。午饭后我们渡过去到左边察看。在河面高处，我们能沿花岗岩顶部走，那里边缘断裂、布满岩峰和尖塔，很难看到河。我急切地想找到一个能看到下方咆哮瀑布的点，在壁上走得太远了，进退两难。我一只脚踩在一块小凸出的岩石上，一只手抠住一条小裂缝。发觉自己被卡在这里——悬在河面四百英尺上方，脚一滑就会掉下去——我喊救命。队员们过来，递给我一根绳，但我松不了手……

About eleven o’clock we come to a place in the river where it seems much worse than any we have yet met in all its course. A little creek comes down from the left. We land first on the right, and clamber up over the granite pinnacles for a mile or two, but can see no way by which we can let down, and to run it would be sure destruction. After dinner we cross to examine it on the left. High above the river we can walk along on the top of the granite, which is broken off at the edge, and set with crags and pinnacles, so that it is very difficult to get a view of the river at all. In my eagerness to reach a point where I can see the roaring fall below, I go too far on the wall, and can neither advance nor retreat. I stand with one foot on a little projecting rock, and cling with my hand fixed in a little crevice. Finding I am caught here, suspended 400 feet above the river, into which I should fall if my footing fails, I call for help. The men come, and pass me a line, but I cannot let go of the rock long enough to take hold of it.[8] Then they bring two or three of the largest oars. All this takes time which seems very precious to me; but at last they arrive. The blade of one of the oars is pushed into a little crevice in the rock beyond me, in such a manner that they can hold me pressed against the wall. Then another is fixed in such a way that I can step on it, and thus I am extricated.

又花了一小时从这一侧察看这条河，但没有看清全貌，于是我们回到最初察看的那一侧，下午在岩峰和尖塔间攀爬、再次仔细审视那条河。我们发现侧流把巨砾冲进河里，形成一道坝，水从坝上形成十八到二十英尺的破碎瀑布；然后是一段布满岩石的激流，长两三百码；另一边，壁的角突进河里。再下面还有第二道瀑布；有多大，我们说不清。然后是一段满是巨砾的激流，长一两百码。在它底部，从右壁伸出一块巨大的岩石，几乎横过半个河面。它有一个向上游延伸的倾斜面，水带着上游瀑布和激流积蓄的全部动量，沿这个斜面卷上去许多英尺，然后翻落……

Still another hour is spent in examining the river from this side, but no good view of it is obtained, so now we return to the side that was first examined, and the afternoon is spent in clambering among the crags and pinnacles, and carefully scanning the river again. We find that the lateral streams have washed boulders into the river, so as to form a dam, over which the water makes a broken fall of eighteen or twenty feet; then there is a rapid, beset with rocks, for two or three hundred yards, while, on the other side, points of the wall project into the river. Then there is a second fall below; how great, we cannot tell. Then there is a rapid, filled with huge rocks, for one or two hundred yards. At the bottom of it, from the right wall, a great rock projects quite half way across the river. It has a sloping surface extending up stream, and the water, coming down with all the momentum gained in the falls and rapids above, rolls up this inclined plane many feet, and tumbles over to the left. I decide that it is possible to let down over the first fall, then run near the right cliff to a point just above the second, where we can pull out into a little chute, and, having run over that in safety, we must pull with all our power across the stream, to avoid the great rock below. On my return to the boat, I announce to the men that we are to run it in the morning. Then we cross the river, and go into camp for the night on some rocks, in the mouth of the little cañon.

晚饭后豪兰上尉要求和我谈谈。我们沿小溪走了一小段，我很快发现他的目的是抗议我继续前进的决定。他认为我们最好在这里放弃这条河。与他交谈后，我得知他弟弟、威廉·邓恩和他自己已决定不再坐船前进了。于是我们回到营地。没有对其他队员说什么。

After supper Captain Howland asks to have a talk with me. We walk up the little creek a short distance, and I soon find that his object is to remonstrate against my determination to proceed. He thinks that we had better abandon the river here. Talking with him, I learn that his brother, William Dunn, and himself have determined to go no farther in the boats. So we return to camp. Nothing is said to the other men.

过去两天我们的航线没有标绘。我现在坐下来标绘，目的是用航位推算法确定我们所在的位置。这是个晴朗的夜晚，我拿出六分仪观测纬度，发现天文测定与标绘结果非常接近——正如对一个行星做子午观测所能期望的那样精确。沿直线，我们距维尔京河口约四十五英里。如果我们能到达那里，就知道那条河上游约二十英里处有定居点。这四十五英里直线距离，沿蜿蜒的河道大概有八十或九十英里。但我们也知道，维尔京河口上游许多英里是比较开阔的乡野——那是我们的目的地。

For the last two days, our course has not been plotted. I sit down and do this now, for the purpose of finding where we are by dead reckoning. It is a clear night, and I take out the sextant to make observation for latitude, and find that the astronomic determination agrees very nearly with that of the plot--quite as closely as might be expected, from a meridian observation on a planet. In a direct line, we must be about forty-five miles from the mouth of the Rio Virgen. If we can reach that point, we know that there are settlements up that river about twenty miles. This forty-five miles, in a direct line, will probably be eighty or ninety in the meandering line of the river. But then we know that there is comparatively open country for many miles above the mouth of the Virgen, which is our point of destination.

我一确定这一切，就把标绘摊在沙地上，叫醒睡在河边下方的豪兰，给他看我认为我们在哪里，以及几处摩门定居点在哪里。

As soon as I determine all this, I spread my plot on the sand, and wake Howland, who is sleeping down by the river, and show him where I suppose we are, and where several Mormon settlements are situated.

关于明天我们又简短谈了一次，他躺下睡了；但对我来说没有睡意。整夜，我沿着河边几码沙滩上的一条小径来回踱步。继续走明智吗？我又走到船边看我们的口粮。我确信我们能闯过眼前的危险；下面可能有什么，我不知道。从昨天在崖壁上的眺望看，峡谷似乎又向南拐了一个大弯，而根据我们此前的经验，这意味着更多更高的花岗岩壁。我不确定我们能从这里爬出峡谷；而且，即使到了壁顶，我了解这片乡野，足以确定它与最近的摩门城镇之间是一片岩石和沙子的沙漠——按最直接的路线算也有七十五英里。诚然，最近的雨对我们有利——如果我们出去的话，因为很可能我们会发现水还积在坑里；而有一次，我几乎……

We have another short talk about the morrow, and he lies down again; but for me there is no sleep. All night long, I pace up and down a little path, on a few yards of sand beach, along by the river. Is it wise to go on? I go to the boats again, to look at our rations. I feel satisfied that we can get over the danger immediately before us; what there may be below I know not. From our outlook yesterday, on the cliffs, the cañon seemed to make another great bend to the south, and this, from our experience heretofore, means more and higher granite walls. I am not sure that we can climb out of the cañon here, and, when at the top of the wall, I know enough of the country to be certain that it is a desert of rock and sand, between this and the nearest Mormon town, which, on the most direct line, must be seventy-five miles away. True, the late rains have been favorable to us, should we go out, for the probabilities are that we shall find water still standing in holes, and, at one time, I almost conclude to leave the river. But for years I have been contemplating this trip. To leave the exploration unfinished, to say that there is a part of the cañon which I cannot explore, having already almost accomplished it, is more than I am willing to acknowledge, and I determine to go on.

我叫醒弟弟，告诉他豪兰的决定，他答应跟我走；然后我叫起厨子霍金斯，他也作了同样的承诺；然后是萨姆纳、布拉德利和霍尔，他们都同意继续前进。

I wake my brother, and tell him of Howland’s determination, and he promises to stay with me; then I call up Hawkins, the cook, and he makes a like promise; then Sumner, and Bradley, and Hall, and they all agree to go on.

8 月 28 日。天亮终于来临，我们吃了早饭，谁也没提未来。那顿饭像葬礼一样肃穆。早饭后，我问那三个人是否仍认为离开我们最好。豪兰兄长认为是的，邓恩同意他。豪兰弟弟试图劝他们随队前进，劝不动，便决定跟他哥哥走。

_August 28._--At last daylight comes, and we have breakfast, without a word being said about the future. The meal is as solemn as a funeral. After breakfast, I ask the three men if they still think it best to leave us. The elder Howland thinks it is, and Dunn agrees with him. The younger Howland tries to persuade them to go on with the party, failing in which, he decides to go with his brother.

然后我们渡过河。小船坏得很厉害，已不适航。由于三人离开减少了人手，我们没法跑所有的船，所以我决定放弃我的「爱玛·迪恩」号。

Then we cross the river. The small boat is very much disabled, and unseaworthy. With the loss of hands, consequent on the departure of the three men, we shall not be able to run all of the boats, so I decide to leave my _Emma Dean_.

两支步枪和一支猎枪给了要走的那些人。我请他们自己拿口粮，取他们认为公平的一份。他们拒绝了，说他们不怕找不到吃的；但厨子比利有一锅为晚饭准备的饼干，他把它们留在一块岩石上。

Two rifles and a shotgun are given to the men who are going out. I ask them to help themselves to the rations, and take what they think to be a fair share. This they refuse to do, saying they have no fear but that they can get something to eat; but Billy, the cook, has a pan of biscuits prepared for dinner, and these he leaves on a rock.

出发前，我们把气压计、化石、矿物和一些弹药从船里拿出来，留在岩石上。我们要尽可能轻地闯过这里。那三个人帮我们把船抬过一块二十五到三十英尺高的岩石，又从第一道瀑布放下，现在我们全都准备好了。离开前的最后一件事，我给妻子写了一封信，交给豪兰。萨姆纳把自己的表给他，交代如果再也听不到他的消息，就把它寄给他妹妹。远征的纪录一直有副本。其中一套交给了豪兰，现在我们准备好了。最后一次，他们恳求我们不要继续，告诉我们在这里出发是发疯；说我们永远无法安全通过；还说河又要向南拐进花岗岩，再走几英里这样的激流和瀑布就会耗尽我们全部口粮，那时再想爬出去就太晚了。有些人……

Before starting, we take our barometers, fossils, the minerals, and some ammunition from the boat, and leave them on the rocks. We are going over this place as light as possible. The three men help us lift our boats over a rock twenty-five or thirty feet high, and let them down again over the first fall, and now we are all ready to start. The last thing before leaving, I write a letter to my wife, and give it to Howland. Sumner gives him his watch, directing that it be sent to his sister, should he not be heard from again. The records of the expedition have been kept in duplicate. One set of these is given to Howland, and now we are ready. For the last time, they entreat us not to go on, and tell us that it is madness to set out in this place; that we can never get safely through it; and, further, that the river turns again to the south into the granite, and a few miles of such rapids and falls will exhaust our entire stock of rations, and then it will be too late to climb out. Some tears are shed; it is rather a solemn parting; each party thinks the other is taking the dangerous course.

我丢下我的旧船，登上「峡谷少女」号。那三个人爬上一块悬在河上的岩峰，目送我们出发。「峡谷少女」号离岸。我们沿壁脚迅速滑行，刚好擦过一块大岩石，然后稍许划进第二道瀑布的水槽，跃了过去。我们撞上下面第一道浪时，敞开的船舱灌满了水，但我们切浪而过，然后队员们用全力朝左壁划，顺利甩开了下面那块危险的岩石。我们闯过它用了不到一分钟，发现虽然从上面看它很糟糕，但我们经过了许多比这更糟的地方。

My old boat left, I go on board of the _Maid of the Cañon_. The three men climb a crag, that overhangs the river, to watch us off. The _Maid of the Cañon_ pushes out. We glide rapidly along the foot of the wall, just grazing one great rock, then pull out a little into the chute of the second fall, and plunge over it. The open compartment is filled when we strike the first wave below, but we cut through it, and then the men pull with all their power toward the left wall, and swing clear of the dangerous rock below all right. We are scarcely a minute in running it, and find that, although it looked bad from above, we have passed many places that were worse.

另一条船没有更多困难地跟了上来。我们在下方第一个可行点靠岸，开枪作为信号，告诉上方的人我们已安全过来。我们在这里待了两小时，希望他们会驾小船跟来。我们在一道弯后面，看不见我们离开他们的地方，所以我们等到他们看来不会来了，就继续前进。（脚注9）

The other boat follows without more difficulty. We land at the first practicable point below and fire our guns, as a signal to the men above that we have come over in safety. Here we remain a couple of hours, hoping that they will take the smaller boat and follow us. We are behind a curve in the cañon, and cannot see up to where we left them, and so we wait until their coming seems hopeless, and push on.[9]

现在直到中午我们闯过一连串激流和瀑布，全都安全通过。刚过午饭我们来到另一处糟糕的地方。一条小溪从左边流进来，下面有一道瀑布，再下面还有一道。上方，河在漩涡和大浪中翻过岩石、穿过岩石跌落，水被抽打成疯狂的白色泡沫。我们沿左岸在上方跑，很快发现这边下不去，但另一侧似乎可以放船。我们又逆流划了两三百码，渡了过去。现在峡谷这一北侧有一层玄武岩，带着一道陡崖，似乎有一百英尺高。我们能爬上去，沿它的顶部走到正好在瀑布上端的地方。这里玄武岩似乎又塌下去了，我指挥队员把一根绳带到崖顶，把船沿壁放下去。一个人留在船上，让她保持……

And now we have a succession of rapids and falls until noon, all of which we run in safety. Just after dinner we come to another bad place. A little stream comes in from the left, and below there is a fall, and still below another fall. Above, the river tumbles down, over and among the rocks, in whirlpools and great waves, and the waters are lashed into mad, white foam. We run along the left, above this, and soon see that we cannot get down on this side, but it seems possible to let down on the other. We pull up stream again, for two or three hundred yards, and cross. Now there is a bed of basalt on this northern side of the cañon, with a bold escarpment, that seems to be a hundred feet high. We can climb it, and walk along its summit to a point where we are just at the head of the fall. Here the basalt is broken down again, so it seems to us, and I direct the men to take a line to the top of the cliff, and let the boats down along the wall. One man remains in the boat, to keep her clear of the rocks, and prevent her line from being caught on the projecting angles. I climb the cliff, and pass along to a point just over the fall, and descend by broken rocks, and find that the break of the fall is above the break of the wall, so that we cannot land; and that still below the river is very bad, and that there is no possibility of a portage.

我甚至没有停下来再察看、再决定该怎么办，就赶紧回到崖顶，阻止船下来。我到时，发现他们已把一条船放到瀑布上端。她在急水里，他们没法把她拉回来；也没法继续放绳，因为绳不够长，够不到正前方那段更高的崖壁；所以他们把绳绕在一块岩峰上。我派两个人回去取另一根绳。船在极急的水里，布拉德利站在敞开的船舱里，伸出桨防止她撞上崖脚。现在她射进主流，顺着绳能到的限度往上冲，然后转过来，一头撞向岩石，又退出来再撞回去——一会儿绷紧绳子，一会儿撞上岩石。第二根绳一到，我们就递给他；但他的全部注意力都被自己的处境占满了，他……

Without waiting further to examine and determine what shall be done, I hasten back to the top of the cliff, to stop the boats from coming down. When I arrive, I find the men have let one of them down to the head of the fall. She is in swift water, and they are not able to pull her back; nor are they able to go on with the line, as it is not long enough to reach the higher part of the cliff, which is just before them; so they take a bight around a crag. I send two men back for the other line. The boat is in very swift water, and Bradley is standing in the open compartment, holding out his oar to prevent her from striking against the foot of the cliff. Now she shoots out into the stream, and up as far as the line will permit, and then, wheeling, drives headlong against the rock, then out and back again, now straining on the line, now striking against the rock. As soon as the second line is brought, we pass it down to him; but his attention is all taken up with his own situation, and he does not see that we are passing the line to him. I stand on a projecting rock, waving my hat to gain his attention, for my voice is drowned by the roaring of the falls.

就在这一刻，我看见他拔出鞘里的刀，上前去割绳子。他显然已经认定，与其等着船被撞碎，不如就这样随船翻下去。他俯身时，船又偏进主流，尾柱崩断，船脱了。布拉德利无比镇定地抓起那把大舵桨，插进船尾桨座，用全力划（他是个运动员）——要把船头转向下游，因为他想船头朝下走，而不是横着漂。他划了一桨、两桨，第三桨正好在她翻下时划出，船完全转正，她下去了，几乎出了我们的视线——尽管我们在河面一百多英尺上方。然后她又起来，骑上一道大浪，下去又上来，然后绕到一些大岩石后面，消失在下方疯狂的白色泡沫里。我们吓得僵住了，因为我们看不见船。布拉德利不见了，看起来是这样。但现在，在远远的下方……

Just at this moment, I see him take his knife from its sheath, and step forward to cut the line. He has evidently decided that it is better to go over with the boat as it is, than to wait for her to be broken to pieces. As he leans over, the boat sheers again into the stream, the stern-post breaks away, and she is loose. With perfect composure Bradley seizes the great scull oar, places it in the stern rowlock, and pulls with all his power (and he is an athlete) to turn the bow of the boat down stream, for he wishes to go bow down, rather than to drift broadside on. One, two strokes he makes, and a third just as she goes over, and the boat is fairly turned, and she goes down almost beyond our sight, though we are more than a hundred feet above the river. Then she comes up again, on a great wave, and down and up, then around behind some great rocks, and is lost in the mad, white foam below. We stand frozen with fear, for we see no boat. Bradley is gone, so it seems. But now, away below, we see something coming out of the waves. It is evidently a boat. A moment more, and we see Bradley standing on deck, swinging his hat to show that he is all right. But he is in a whirlpool. We have the stempost of his boat attached to the line. How badly she may be disabled we know not.

我指挥萨姆纳和鲍威尔沿崖壁下去，看他们能否从下方够到他。罗兹、霍尔和我跑到另一条船，跳上去，推出去，朝瀑布冲去。一个浪从我们头上滚过，我们的船失控了。又一个巨浪撞上我们，船翻了，翻腾颠簸，我不知道怎么过去的。我只知道是布拉德利在把我们捞起来。我们很快把一切都弄正，划到崖壁边，等萨姆纳和鲍威尔过来。经过艰难的攀爬，他们到了我们这里。我们又跑了两三英里，转向西北，一直跑到夜里，那时我们又一次跑出了花岗岩。

I direct Sumner and Powell to pass along the cliff, and see if they can reach him from below. Rhodes, Hall, and myself run to the other boat, jump aboard, push out, and away we go over the falls. A wave rolls over us, and our boat is unmanageable. Another great wave strikes us, the boat rolls over, and tumbles and tosses, I know not how. All I know is that Bradley is picking us up. We soon have all right again, and row to the cliff, and wait until Sumner and Powell can come. After a difficult climb they reach us. We run two or three miles farther, and turn again to the northwest, continuing until night, when we have run out of the granite once more.

8 月 29 日。今早我们很早就出发了。河仍然湍急，但我们没有严重困难，十二点我们从科罗拉多大峡谷里出来了。

_August 29._--We start very early this morning. The river still continues swift, but we have no serious difficulty, and at twelve o’clock emerge from the Grand Cañon of the Colorado.

我们现在处在一个山谷里，远处能看见低矮的山脉，向河下流延伸过来。我们认出这是「大洗地」（Grand Wash）。

We are in a valley now, and low mountains are seen in the distance, coming to the river below. We recognize this as the Grand Wash.

几年前，一队摩门教徒从犹他州圣乔治出发，带了一条船，来到大洗地口，在那里分成两路，一部分人渡过河去探查圣弗朗西斯科山脉。汉布林、米勒和克罗斯比三人带着船，继续沿河下行到卡尔维尔，在维尔京河口下游几英里处靠岸。我们带着他们的手稿日志，所以这条河相对来说是熟知的。

A few years ago, a party of Mormons set out from St. George, Utah, taking with them a boat, and came down to the mouth of the Grand Wash, where they divided, a portion of the party crossing the river to explore the San Francisco Mountains. Three men--Hamblin, Miller, and Crosby--taking the boat, went on down the river to Callville, landing a few miles below the mouth of the Rio Virgen. We have their manuscript journal with us, and so the stream is comparatively well known.

今晚我们在左岸的牧豆树丛里扎营。

To-night we camp on the left bank, in a _mesquite_ thicket.

摆脱危险后的解脱和成功的喜悦是巨大的。当一个人被伤痛锁在病院的床上，直到他的帆布帐篷像牢房一样，直到周围那些被探针和手术刀折磨的人的呻吟堆积成他甩不掉、忘不了的恐怖重压压在他耳上，直到溃烂伤口的恶臭和麻醉药物的气味让空气充满令人作呕的负担——他终于走进开阔的原野时，他看到了怎样一个世界！天空多么美，阳光多么明亮，怎样的「狂喜的音乐洪流」从鸟儿的喉咙里倾泻，泥土、树木和花朵的气息多么芬芳！康复自由的第一小时，似乎是对一切——痛苦、阴郁、恐惧——的丰厚补偿。

The relief from danger, and the joy of success, are great. When he who has been chained by wounds to a hospital cot, until his canvas tent seems like a dungeon cell, until the groans of those who lie about, tortured with probe and knife, are piled up, a weight of horror on his ears that he cannot throw off, cannot forget, and until the stench of festering wounds and anæsthetic drugs has filled the air with its loathsome burthen, at last goes out into the open field, what a world he sees! How beautiful the sky; how bright the sunshine; what “floods of delirious music” pour from the throats of birds; how sweet the fragrance of earth, and tree, and blossom! The first hour of convalescent freedom seems rich recompense for all--pain, gloom, terror.

类似这样的就是我们今晚的感觉。我们面前一直有一种未知的危险，比眼前的凶险更沉重。在大峡谷里度过的每一个清醒时辰都是辛劳。我们怀着深切的忧虑看着我们本已微薄的口粮不断消失，还不时看见河在我们饥饿时又夺走那剩下的一点里的一部分。而在这阴郁的深处，危险和辛劳被忍受着——那里常常白天云遮天、夜晚只能看见一窄条星星。只有在辛苦劳作后深沉的睡眠那几小时里，水的咆哮才停息。现在危险过去了；现在劳停止了；现在阴郁消失了；现在天穹只被地平线限定；而能看到多么辽阔的一片星座！

Something like this are the feelings we experience to-night. Ever before us has been an unknown danger, heavier than immediate peril. Every waking hour passed in the Grand Cañon has been one of toil. We have watched with deep solicitude the steady disappearance of our scant supply of rations, and from time to time have seen the river snatch a portion of the little left, while we were ahungered. And danger and toil were endured in those gloomy depths, where oft-times the clouds hid the sky by day, and but a narrow zone of stars could be seen at night. Only during the few hours of deep sleep, consequent on hard labor, has the roar of the waters been hushed. Now the danger is over; now the toil has ceased; now the gloom has disappeared; now the firmament is bounded only by the horizon; and what a vast expanse of constellations can be seen!

河在我们身边庄严地静静流过；营地的安静是甜蜜的；我们的喜悦几乎是狂喜。我们一直坐到午夜过后很久，谈着大峡谷，谈着家，但主要谈着离开我们的那三个人。他们是在那些深处游荡、找不到出路吗？他们是在上方沙漠里找水吗？还是正接近那些定居点？

The river rolls by us in silent majesty; the quiet of the camp is sweet; our joy is almost ecstasy. We sit till long after midnight, talking of the Grand Cañon, talking of home, but chiefly talking of the three men who left us. Are they wandering in those depths, unable to find a way out? are they searching over the desert lands above for water? or are they nearing the settlements?

8 月 30 日。今天我们穿过两三个又短又低的峡谷，从一个出来时，我们发现下方谷地里有一群印第安人。他们看见我们，用最急切的慌张跑开，躲进岩石间。虽然我们上岸叫他们回来，却一个印第安人也看不见。

_August 30._--We run through two or three short, low cañons to-day, and on emerging from one, we discover a band of Indians in the valley below. They see us, and scamper away in most eager haste, to hide among the rocks. Although we land, and call for them to return, not an Indian can be seen.

再往下两三英里，转过一个短弯，我们又碰上一个营地。我们离得那么近他们才看见我们，近得我能朝他们喊话——我会说一点他们的语言——我告诉他们我们是朋友；但他们全逃到岩石上，只有一个男人、一个女人和两个孩子留下。我们靠岸，与他们交谈。他们没有帐篷，但用树枝搭了小棚，在棚下沙地里打滚。那男人就穿一顶帽子；那女人只穿一串珠子。起初他们显然很害怕；但当我用他们的语言跟他们说话，告诉他们我们是朋友，还打听摩门城镇里的人时，他们很快就安心了，还讨要烟草。这种宝贵的东西我们一点也匀不出。萨姆纳在船里找了找有什么可以送他们，找到一小块彩色肥皂，他们把它当成一件贵重的礼物收下——不过与其说是实用物品，不如说是当作一件美丽的东西。他们不是……

Two or three miles farther down, in turning a short bend in the river, we come upon another camp. So near are we before they can see us that I can shout to them, and, being able to speak a little of their language, I tell them we are friends; but they all flee to the rocks, except a man, a woman, and two children. We land, and talk with them. They are without lodges, but have built little shelters of boughs, under which they wallow in the sand. The man is dressed in a hat; the woman in a string of beads only. At first they are evidently much terrified; but when I talk to them in their own language, and tell them we are friends, and inquire after people in the Mormon towns, they are soon reassured, and beg for tobacco. Of this precious article we have none to spare. Sumner looks around in the boat for something to give them, and finds a little piece of colored soap, which they receive as a valuable present, rather as a thing of beauty than as a useful commodity, however. They are either unwilling or unable to tell us anything about the Indians or white people, and so we push off, for we must lose no time.

中午我们在右岸下扎营。现在我们一出发就满怀期待，因为我们每分钟都盼着发现维尔京河口。

We camp at noon under the right bank. And now, as we push out, we are in great expectancy, for we hope every minute to discover the mouth of the Rio Virgen.

很快一个人喊道：「河里有印第安人！」看了一会儿，我们确实看见两三个人。队员们弯下腰用力划桨，朝他们划去。靠近后，我们看到是三个白人和一个印第安人在拉一张大围网，然后我们发现，这里正是我们长久寻找的那条河的河口。

Soon one of the men exclaims: “Yonder’s an Indian in the river.” Looking for a few minutes, we certainly do see two or three persons. The men bend to their oars, and pull toward them. Approaching, we see that there are three white men and an Indian hauling a seine, and then we discover that it is just at the mouth of the long sought river.

靠近时，那些人见到我们的惊讶远不如我们见到他们。他们显然知道我们是谁，交谈后，他们告诉我们，我们早已被报告失踪了，几周前有人从盐湖城派来一个信使，指示他们留意可能顺流漂下来的我们这支队伍的残片或遗物。

As we come near, the men seem far less surprised to see us than we do to see them. They evidently know who we are, and, on talking with them, they tell us that we have been reported lost long ago, and that some weeks before, a messenger had been sent from Salt Lake City, with instructions for them to watch for any fragments or relics of our party that might drift down the stream.

我们新结识的朋友，阿萨先生和他的两个儿子，告诉我们他们是将在河岸上建起的一座城镇的开荒者。

Our new found friends, Mr. Asa and his two sons, tell us that they are pioneers of a town that is to be built on the bank.

维尔京河河谷上游十八或二十英里处有两个摩门城镇——圣约瑟夫和圣托马斯。今晚我们派了一个印第安人去后一个地方，取回那里可能有的我们的信。

Eighteen or twenty miles up the valley of the Rio Virgen there are two Mormon towns, St. Joseph and St. Thomas. To-night we dispatch an Indian to the last mentioned place, to bring any letters that may be there for us.

我们到这里来得非常及时。清点补给时，我们发现约有十磅面粉、十五磅干苹果，但咖啡有七八十磅。

Our arrival here is very opportune. When we look over our store of supplies, we find about ten pounds of flour, fifteen pounds of dried apples, but seventy or eighty pounds of coffee.

8 月 31 日。下午那个印第安人带回一封信，告诉我们圣托马斯的莱思黑德主教和另外两三个摩门教徒正带一辆马车下来，给我们送补给。他们大约日落时到。阿萨先生尽其所能盛情款待我们；但莱思黑德主教用马车带来了两三打西瓜和许多其他小奢侈品，我们又一次舒服了。

_August 31._--This afternoon the Indian returns with a letter, informing us that Bishop Leithhead, of St. Thomas, and two or three other Mormons are coming down with a wagon, bringing us supplies. They arrive about sundown. Mr. Asa treats us with great kindness, to the extent of his ability; but Bishop Leithhead brings in his wagon two or three dozen melons, and many other little luxuries, and we are comfortable once more.

9 月 1 日。今早萨姆纳、布拉德利、霍金斯和霍尔带上少量口粮，驾船沿科罗拉多河出发。他们的打算是去莫哈维堡，也许从那里走陆路去洛杉矶。

_September 1._--This morning Sumner, Bradley, Hawkins, and Hall, taking on a small supply of rations, start down the Colorado with the boats. It is their intention to go to Fort Mojave, and perhaps from there overland to Los Angeles.

鲍威尔上尉和我随莱思黑德主教回圣托马斯。从圣托马斯我们去盐湖城。

Captain Powell and myself return with Bishop Leithhead to St. Thomas. From St. Thomas we go to Salt Lake City.

CHAPTER IX THE RIO VIRGEN AND THE U-IN-KA-RET MOUNTAINS[10]

我们已决定继续探查科罗拉多的峡谷。上次的行程因口粮损失而太仓促，科学仪器也损坏严重，我们对取得的结果并不满意，所以我们要再一次乘船穿行这些峡谷，并把这次考察花上两三年时间。

We have determined to continue the exploration of the cañons of the Colorado. Our last trip was so hurried, owing to the loss of rations, and the scientific instruments were so badly injured, that we are not satisfied with the results obtained, so we shall once more attempt to pass through the cañons in boats, devoting two or three years to the trip.

不可能在船上为全队带足这么长时间的食物，所以最好沿着河每隔一两百英里设立一个补给站。

It will not be possible to carry in the boats sufficient supplies for the party for that length of time, so it is thought best to establish dépôts of supplies, at intervals of one or two hundred miles along the river.

在甘尼森渡口与大峡谷底部之间，我们知道只有两个点能到达河边——一个是「神父渡口」，另一个是几英里下游、帕里亚河口，那条路是由摩门教传教士雅各布·汉布林探查过的。这两个点离得太近，只能选一个作上述用途，所以必须另找其他点。到目前为止，我们从印第安人或白人那里都得不到任何线索，找不到任何别的通到河边的路。

Between Gunnison’s Crossing and the foot of the Grand Cañon, we know of only two points where the river can be reached--one at the Crossing of the Fathers, and another a few miles below, at the mouth of the Paria, on a route which has been explored by Jacob Hamblin, a Mormon missionary. These two points are so near each other that only one of them can be selected for the purpose above mentioned, and others must be found. We have been unable, up to this time, to obtain, either from Indians or white men, any information which will give us a clue to any other trail to the river.

在塞维尔河源头，我们正处在一个巨大分水岭的顶上。塞维尔河本身向北、再向西流，注入同名的湖。维尔京河源头在近旁，向西南流入科罗拉多河，入河口在大峡谷下游六七十英里处。卡纳布河也发源于此，径直向南流入大峡谷的正中心。帕里亚河也发源于此，向南偏东流，在大理石峡谷的上端入河。从这个点向东北，其他注入科罗拉多河的溪流也在这一带发源，直到四五十英里外，我们到达「脏恶魔河」的南支——那条河的河口离格兰德与绿河汇合处不远。

At the head waters of the Sevier, we are on the summit of a great water-shed. The Sevier itself flows north, and then westward, into the lake of the same name. The Rio Virgen, heading near by, flows to the southwest, into the Colorado, sixty or seventy miles below the Grand Cañon. The Kanab, also heading near by, runs directly south, into the very heart of the Grand Cañon. The Paria, also heading near by, runs a little south of east, and enters the river at the head of Marble Cañon. To the northeast from this point, other streams, which run into the Colorado, have their sources, until, forty or fifty miles away, we reach the southern branches of the Dirty Devil River, the mouth of which stream is but a short distance below the junction of the Grand and Green.

庞萨甘特高原终止于一个角，被一线美丽的粉色崖壁围住。在这高原脚下西侧，维尔京河和塞维尔河的细小上游支流犬牙交错地交织在一起。高原的上表面向东北倾斜，所以它的水滚进塞维尔河；但从崖脚起、绕过高原的尖角、绵延十几英里，我们发现大量泉水，它们的水汇成了卡纳布河。再稍向东北，泉水汇成溪流，喂养了帕里亚河。

The Pouns-a′-gunt Plateau terminates in a point, which is bounded by a line of beautiful pink cliffs. At the foot of this plateau, on the west, the Rio Virgen and Sevier Rivers are dovetailed together, as their minute upper branches interlock. The upper surface of the plateau inclines to the northeast, so that its waters roll off into the Sevier; but from the foot of the cliffs, quite around the sharp angle of the plateau, for a dozen miles, we find numerous springs, whose waters unite to form the Kanab. But a little farther to the northeast the springs gather into streams that feed the Paria.

在卡纳布河的上游泉边，我们扎下营，从这里我们要向西南、向南和向东辐射出一系列考察行程。

Here, by the upper springs of the Kanab, we make a camp, and from this point we are to radiate on a series of trips, southwest, south, and east.

雅各布·汉布林在印第安人中做了二十多年传教士，他召集来一帮凯瓦维特人，他们的首领是丘阿鲁姆皮克，全都和我们扎营在一起。他们向我们保证我们到不了河边；我们无法下到峡谷深处，但答应给我们看泉水和蓄水坑——这整个地区都非常稀缺——并把他们能力所及的一切信息都告诉我们。

Jacob Hamblin, who has been a missionary among the Indians for more than twenty years, has collected a number of _Kai′-vav-its_, with _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_, their chief, and they are all camped with us. They assure us that we cannot reach the river; that we cannot make our way into the depths of the cañon, but promise to show us the springs and water pockets, which are very scarce in all this region, and to give us all the information in their power.

我们备好一支驮运队，用来驮被褥和仪器，补给则要送到下游的某个点。

Here we fit up a pack train, for our bedding and instruments, and supplies are to be carried on the backs of mules and ponies.

1870 年 9 月 5 日。队员们各自忙着做各项准备工作。

_September 5, 1870._--The several members of the party are engaged in general preparation for our trip down to the Grand Cañon.

我带了一个白人和一个印第安人，出发去爬我们东边耸起的庞萨甘特高原的峰顶。我们的路先有一英里多走在巨大的泥炭沼上，它在我们脚下颤动，不时有一头骡子陷进破碎的草皮，我们不得不用绳把它拉出来。

Taking with me a white man and an Indian, I start on a climb to the summit of the Pouns-a′-gunt Plateau, which rises above us on the east. Our way, for a mile or more, is over a great peat bog, that trembles under our feet, and now and then a mule sinks through the broken turf, and we are compelled to pull it out with ropes.

过了沼泽，我们的路沿一条沟壑上行，沟底就是粉色崖壁——这座巨大高原的断崖或者说墙。很快我们离开沟壑，爬上一道长山脊，它向右蜿蜒，通向这座大平顶的峰顶。

Passing the bog, our way is up a gulch, at the foot of the Pink Cliffs, which form the escarpment, or wall, of the great plateau. Soon we leave the gulch, and climb a long ridge, which winds around to the right toward the summit of the great table.

两个小时的骑行、攀爬和跋涉把我们带到了接近顶部的地方。我们往下看，见云从南方漂上来，在我们下方的崖脚前翻腾奔涌。很快，下方整个乡野都被一片蒸气之海覆盖——一片波浪起伏、汹涌却无声的海——而蒸气洪流仍从南方滚滚而来，巨浪拍打崖脚又滚回去；又一个潮头进来，被抛回去，一个又一个，鞭打着崖壁，直到雾升到峰顶，把我们全都裹住。

Two hours’ riding, climbing, and clambering brings us near the top. We look below, and see clouds drifting up from the south, and rolling tumultuously toward the foot of the cliffs, beneath us. Soon, all the country below is covered with a sea of vapor--a billowy, raging, noiseless sea--and as the vapory flood still rolls up from the south, great waves dash against the foot of the cliffs and roll back; another tide comes in, is hurled back, and another and another, lashing the cliffs until the fog rises to the summit, and covers us all.

峰顶之上是一片茂密的松树和冷杉林，布满枯死倒伏的木材。

There is a heavy pine and fir forest above, beset with dead and fallen timber, and we make our way through the undergrowth to the east.

下雨了！云把水分倾盆倒出，我们为自己搭了树枝的遮蔽所，但很快又放弃了，站在一堆我们点起的大松木和树枝火堆旁瑟瑟发抖——这火被狠砸的暴风雨扑灭了一半。

It rains! The clouds discharge their moisture in torrents, and we make for ourselves shelters of boughs, which are soon abandoned, and we stand shivering by a great fire of pine logs and boughs, which we have kindled, but which the pelting storm half extinguishes.

一小时、两小时、三小时、四小时的暴风雨，最后它才稍稍减弱。

One, two, three, four hours of the storm, and at last it partially abates.

在这段时间里，我们放开任其自便的牲口各自在树下找了遮蔽，其中两匹走得没了影。我出去追踪它们的足迹，来到一排岩石的边缘，在雾和雨里，我以为那只是一道小山脊，便找路下去。正站在这儿的当口，雾下被某股气流或一阵风撕开一道缝，露出一个几乎无底的深渊。我从一道两千多英尺的巨大悬崖边缘望下去；但透过雾，下方的形状半隐半现，一切距离的估量都失去了，它看起来像一万英尺、十英里——想象力想让它是多远就是多远。

During this time our animals, which we have turned loose, have sought for themselves shelter under the trees, and two of them have wandered away beyond our sight. I go out to follow their tracks, and come near to the brink of a ledge of rocks, which, in the fog and mist, I suppose to be a little ridge, and I look for a way by which I can go down. Standing just here, there is a rift made in the fog below, by some current or blast of wind, which reveals an almost bottomless abyss. I look from the brink of a great precipice of more than two thousand feet; but, through the mist, the forms below are half obscured, and all reckoning of distance is lost, and it seems ten thousand feet, ten miles--any distance the imagination desires to make it.

抓回牲口，我们回到营地。我们发现那些涨水的溪流——我们营地边那些几天前还干涸的溪流——现在……

Catching our animals, we return to the camp. We find that the little streams which come down from the plateau are greatly swollen, but at camp they have had no rain. The clouds which drifted up from the south, striking against the plateau, were lifted up into colder regions, and discharged their moisture on the summit, and against the sides of the plateau, but there was no rain in the valley below.

9 月 9 日。今早我们从卡纳布河源头那片美丽的草甸顺利出发，翻过维尔京河源头那线小山丘，向南经过一个秀丽的山谷，十点钟来到一条巨大的地理台地边缘——一线崖壁。我们身后是清凉的泉水、翠绿的草甸和森林覆盖的山坡；我们脚下，向南延伸直到世界消失在蓝色雾霭里，是一片彩绘的沙漠；不是沙漠平原，而是岩石的沙漠，被深谷切开，被高耸的崖壁和尖塔般的岩石点缀——光裸的岩石，在阳光下璀璨夺目。

_September 9._--We make a fair start this morning, from the beautiful meadow at the head of the Kanab, and cross the line of little hills at the headwaters of the Rio Virgen, and pass, to the south, a pretty valley, and at ten o’clock come to the brink of a great geographic bench--a line of cliffs. Behind us are cool springs, green meadows, and forest clad slopes; below us, stretching to the south, until the world is lost in blue haze, is a painted desert; not a desert plain, but a desert of rocks, cut by deep gorges, and relieved by towering cliffs and pinnacled rocks--naked rocks, brilliant in the sunlight.

我们沿一条艰难的小路下到玄武岩台地，无数溪流在这里汇成一条小河，奔流在一条深峡里。河紧贴右侧崖脚奔流，小路从右侧通过。中午我们休息，牲口吃着茂盛的青草。

By a difficult trail, we make our way down the basaltic ledge, through which innumerable streams here gather into a little river, running in a deep cañon. The river runs close to the foot of the cliffs, on the right hand side, and the trail passes along to the right. At noon we rest, and our animals feed on luxuriant grass.

我们又出发，沿一条石头路缓慢前进。夜里在一道悬垂的悬崖下扎营。

Again we start, and make slow progress along a stony way. At night we camp under an overarching cliff.

9 月 10 日。这里河转向西，而我们的路本应向南；但我们想尽可能探查维尔京河。印第安人告诉我们，峡谷往下几英里会逐渐变窄，我们不可能带着牲口沿河再走很远。清晨我下去察看这段窄处的上端。早饭后，我们决定步行探查峡谷几英里，安排主力队伍爬崖壁绕到下游十八到二十英里的一个点——印第安人说那里牲口可以沿河下去——我们三个人步行出发。

_September 10._--Here the river turns to the west, and our way, properly, is to the south; but we wish to explore the Rio Virgen as far as possible. The Indians tell us that the cañon narrows gradually, a few miles below, and that it will be impossible to take our animals much farther down the river. Early in the morning, I go down to examine the head of this narrow part. After breakfast, having concluded to explore the cañon for a few miles on foot, we arrange that the main party shall climb the cliff, and go around to a point eighteen or twenty miles below, where, the Indians say, the animals can be taken down by the river, and three of us set out on foot.

这条峡谷的印第安名字是 Pa-ru'-nu-weap，即「咆哮水峡谷」。在小河与壁脚之间，长着茂密的柳树、藤蔓和野蔷薇丛，我们极其费力地从这团纠缠中开路。它并不是一条宽河——多数地方只有二三十英尺宽；浅，但非常急。花了几小时在植被丛中开路、四处爬岩石之后，决定沿河涉水。有些地方这很容易，但这里那里会遇到深坑，我们得涉到腋窝。很快来到一些窄得河占满整个水道的地方，只好硬着头皮涉水。许多地方河底是流沙，我们陷进去，极其艰难地前进。有些地方坑太深，我们得游泳，我们的小包被褥和口粮绑在一只用漂木扎成的筏子上，推到前面。偶尔有一小片河漫滩可以走，我们来回横渡溪流，沿水道涉水——水急得几乎把我们冲得站不住脚……

The Indian name of the cañon is _Pa-ru′-nu-weap_, or Roaring Water Cañon. Between the little river and the foot of the walls, is a dense growth of willows, vines, and wild rose bushes, and, with great difficulty, we make our way through this tangled mass. It is not a wide stream--only twenty or thirty feet across in most places; shallow, but very swift. After spending some hours in breaking our way through the mass of vegetation, and climbing rocks here and there, it is determined to wade along the stream. In some places this is an easy task, but here and there we come to deep holes, where we have to wade to our arm pits. Soon we come to places so narrow that the river fills the entire channel, and we wade perforce. In many places the bottom is a quicksand, into which we sink, and it is with great difficulty that we make progress. In some places the holes are so deep that we have to swim, and our little bundles of blankets and rations are fixed to a raft made of driftwood, and pushed before us. Now and then there is a little flood-plain, on which we can walk, and we cross and recross the stream, and wade along the channel where the water is so swift as to almost carry us off our feet, and we are in danger every moment of being swept down, until night comes on. We estimate we have traveled eight miles to-day. We find a little patch of flood-plain, on which there is a huge pile of driftwood and a clump of box-elders, and near by a great stream, which bursts from the rocks--a mammoth spring.

我们很快生起一堆大火，把衣服摊开晾干，煮了一杯咖啡，围着火堆安顿下来。

We soon have a huge fire, our clothes are spread to dry, we make a cup of coffee, take out our bread and cheese and dried beef, and enjoy a hearty supper.

这里的峡谷约一千二百英尺深。它一直很窄，两壁……

The cañon here is about twelve hundred feet deep. It has been very narrow and winding all the way down to this point.

9 月 11 日。今早又涉水；陷进流沙，游过深水，在水急、河底多石的地方痛苦地缓慢前进。

_September 11._--Wading again this morning; sinking in the quicksand, swimming the deep waters, and making slow and painful progress where the waters are swift, and the bed of the stream rocky.

峡谷在稳步加深，许多地方非常窄——下面只有二三十英尺宽，有些地方几百英尺上方也不比这宽，甚至更窄。有些地方，河在弯道处远远掏进岩石下，但仍保持着狭窄的河道，所以一侧是悬垂的壁，另一侧是倾斜的壁。有些地方几百英尺上方又变成垂直的，于是天空的视野完全被关闭。处处这条深道都又黑又阴森，回响着急水的声音。中午我们处在一个两千五百英尺深的峡谷里，来到一处壁塌落的瀑布，巨大的岩石阻塞着水道，我们在上面找到立足点，下到两百英尺低的一个平台。这里峡谷又变宽，我们找到一片河漫滩，可以在上面走——一会儿在溪这边，一会儿在那边。峡谷逐渐变宽；沿河有陡峭的激流、瀑布和跌水，但我们只在必要渡河时才涉水。我们以极大的辛劳前进，要翻过一堆堆破碎的岩石。

The cañon is steadily becoming deeper, and, in many places, very narrow--only twenty or thirty feet wide below, and in some places no wider, and even narrower, for hundreds of feet overhead. There are places where the river, in sweeping by curves, has cut far under the rocks, but still preserving its narrow channel, so that there is an overhanging wall on one side and an inclined wall on the other. In places a few hundred feet above, it becomes vertical again, and thus the view of the sky is entirely closed. Everywhere this deep passage is dark and gloomy, and resounds with the noise of rapid waters. At noon we are in a cañon 2,500 feet deep, and we come to a fall where the walls are broken down, and huge rocks beset the channel, on which we obtain a foothold to reach a level two hundred feet below. Here the cañon is again wider, and we find a flood-plain, along which we can walk, now on this, and now on that side of the stream. Gradually the cañon widens; steep rapids, cascades, and cataracts are found along the river, but we wade only when it is necessary to cross. We make progress with very great labor, having to climb over piles of broken rocks.

傍晚我们来到谷地里一小片空地，看到其他人……

Late in the afternoon, we come to a little clearing in the valley, and see other signs of civilization, and by sundown arrive at the Mormon town of Schunesburg; and here we meet the train, and feast on melons and grapes.

9 月 12 日。过去两天我们穿过 Pa-ru'-nu-weap 峡谷的路线一直朝正西。另一条溪从北面流下，就在舒恩斯堡这里与维尔京河主干汇合。我们决定花一天探查这条溪。印第安人把这条溪流经的峡谷叫作 Mu-koon'-tu-weap，即「笔直峡谷」。进入它，我们必须逆流涉水；常常水占满整个水道，虽然我们走了许多英里，却找不到河漫滩、岩堆或崖脚破碎的岩石堆。壁面光滑平坦，处处都非常规整，一千英尺以上垂直，再往上似乎以倾斜的斜坡向更高处退去；而一路走去，我们处处发现泉水从壁脚喷涌，越过这些之后，上游的河就不断变小；下方奔流的大股水，是从这巨大的红砂岩层底下涌出来的；沿峡谷上行，它渐渐成了一条溪，然后是一条小溪。峡谷西壁上立着一些土台、塔岩和高耸的尖塔状岩石。沿峡谷上行，我们瞥见……

_September 12._--Our course, for the last two days, through _Pa-ru′-nu-weap_ Cañon, was directly to the west. Another stream comes down from the north, and unites just here at Schunesburg with the main branch of the Rio Virgen. We determine to spend a day in the exploration of this stream. The Indians call the cañon, through which it runs, _Mu-koon′-tu-weap_, or Straight Cañon. Entering this, we have to wade up stream; often the water fills the entire channel, and, although we travel many miles, we find no flood-plain, talus, or broken piles of rock at the foot of the cliff. The walls have smooth, plain faces, and are everywhere very regular and vertical for a thousand feet or more, where they seem to break back in shelving slopes to higher altitudes; and everywhere, as we go along, we find springs bursting out at the foot of the walls, and, passing these, the river above becomes steadily smaller; the great body of water, which runs below, bursts out from beneath this great bed of red sandstone; as we go up the cañon, it comes to be but a creek, and then a brook. On the western wall of the cañon stand some buttes, towers, and high pinnacled rocks. Going up the cañon, we gain glimpses of them, here and there. Last summer, after our trip through the cañons of the Colorado, on our way from the mouth of the Virgen to Salt Lake City, these were seen as conspicuous landmarks, from a distance, away to the southwest, of sixty or seventy miles. These tower rocks are known as the Temples of the Virgen.

我们把这条峡谷差不多探索到了源头，回到舒恩斯堡，……

Having explored this cañon nearly to its head, we return to Schunesburg, arriving quite late at night.

傍晚坐在营地里，我们队伍中的凯瓦维特人首领丘阿鲁姆皮克告诉我们，这一带部落里有一个传说：许多年前，住在西南方的帕鲁沙帕茨人曾在某处看到一道巨大的光，他们以为那是为警告他们——科罗拉多河以东的纳瓦霍人要来——而点起的信号。于是松树谷山、圣克拉拉山和尤因卡雷特山上又点起了别的信号火，北亚利桑那、南犹他、南内华达和南加州的全部部落都被警告了逼近的危险；但当帕鲁沙帕茨人走近时，他们发现那是某座巨大「神殿」上的一处火；于是他们知道这火不是人点的——没有人能攀上那些岩石。是「攀岩者」（Rock Rovers）点了一堆火来欺骗人们。印第安语里这地方叫 Tu'-mu-ur-ru-gwait'-si-gaip Tu-weap'，即「攀岩者之地」。

Sitting in camp this evening, _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_, the chief of the _Kai′-vav-its_, who is one of our party, tells us there is a tradition among the tribes of this country, that many years ago a great light was seen some-where in this region by the _Pa-ru′-sha-pats_, who lived to the southwest, and that they supposed it to be a signal, kindled to warn them of the approach of the _Navajos_, who live beyond the Colorado River to the east. Then other signal fires were kindled on the Pine Valley Mountain, Santa Clara Mountains, and U-in-ka-ret Mountains, so that all the tribes of Northern Arizona, Southern Utah, Southern Nevada, and Southern California were warned of the approaching danger; but when the _Pa-ru′-sha-pats_ came nearer, they discovered that it was a fire on one of the great Temples; and then they knew that the fire was not kindled by men, for no human being could scale the rocks. The _Tu′-mu-ur-ru-gwait′-si-gaip_, or Rock Rovers, had kindled a fire to deceive the people. In the Indian language this is called _Tu′-mu-ur-ru-gwait′-si-gaip Tu-weap′_, or Rock Rovers’ Land.

9 月 13 日。今早我们非常早出发，因为面前是一整天的行程。我们的路向南渡过维尔京河。来到河岸，我们发现一种奇异的变形。我们上游看到的那条在窄道里奔流、在岩石上跳跃猛冲、咆哮怒吼的溪流，在这里汇成一片几百码宽、只有几英寸深的薄薄的水面，却铺在流沙河床上。渡过溪流，我们的小路沿一条不很深的窄峡上行，然后在金黄、红、紫的页岩和泥灰岩丘陵间穿行。爬出维尔京河谷，我们穿过一片矮柏树林，来到朱红崖壁脚下。一整天我们沿这条印第安小路向东走，夜里在一个大泉边扎营——印第安人叫它黄岩泉，摩门教徒叫它烟管泉；附近有一间小屋，几个摩门牧人住在那里避风。烟管泉在亚利桑那州犹他州界线另一边，我们估计它离河约六十英里。摩门教徒打算明年在这里建一座堡垒，作为保护……

_September 13._--We start very early this morning, for we have a long day’s travel before us. Our way is across the Rio Virgen to the south. Coming to the bank of the stream here, we find a strange metamorphosis. The streams we have seen above, running in narrow channels, leaping and plunging over the rocks, raging and roaring in their course, are here united, and spread in a thin sheet several hundred yards wide, and only a few inches deep, but running over a bed of quicksand. Crossing the stream, our trail leads up a narrow cañon, not very deep, and then among the hills of golden, red, and purple shales and marls. Climbing out of the valley of the Rio Virgen, we pass through a forest of dwarf cedars, and come out at the foot of the Vermilion Cliffs. All day we follow this Indian trail toward the east, and at night camp at a great spring, known to the Indians as Yellow Rock Spring, but to the Mormons as Pipe Spring; and near by there is a cabin in which some Mormon herders find shelter. Pipe Spring is a point just across the Utah line in Arizona, and we suppose it to be about sixty miles from the river. Here the Mormons design to build a fort another year, as an outpost for protection against the Indians.

我们遣散了许多印第安人，但带了两个同行，让他们给我们指路、做翻译。

Here we discharge a number of the Indians, but take two with us for the purpose of showing us the springs, for they are very scarce, very small, and not easily found. Half a dozen are not known in a district of country large enough to make as many good sized counties in Illinois. There are no running streams, and these springs and water-pockets--that is, holes in the rocks, which hold water from shower to shower--are our only dependence for this element.

出发时，我们把一长线崖壁留在身后——几百英尺高，由橙色和朱红的砂岩构成。我给它们命名「朱红崖壁」。走出几英里后，我回头看，见朝阳灿烂地照在它们彩绘的脸上；突出的棱角在燃烧，凹进的棱角埋在阴影里，我凝视它们直到目光恍惚，那些崖壁看起来像一长岸紫色的云，从地平线一直堆到天上。中午我们沿一条巧克力色崖壁的台地走，掏出三明治，一边骑马一边吃了午饭。

Starting, we leave behind a long line of cliffs, many hundred feet high, composed of orange and vermilion sandstones. I have named them “Vermilion Cliffs.” When we are out a few miles, I look back, and see the morning sun shining in splendor on their painted faces; the salient angles are on fire, and the retreating angles are buried in shade, and I gaze on them until my vision dreams, and the cliffs appear a long bank of purple clouds, piled from the horizon high into the heavens. At noon we pass along a ledge of chocolate cliffs, and, taking out our sandwiches, we make a dinner as we ride along.

昨天，我们的印第安人花了几个小时讨论我们该走哪条路。有一条路，要远上十到十二英里，但水有把握；另一条更短，但水时有时无；他们的结论是现在会有水；我们走的就是这条路，但一整天我们都在为它焦虑。在外面待两天，只有两个小桶能带的水，会让我们的牲口吃大苦头。五点钟我们到了那个地方，那里有一个巨大的蓄水坑，装着好几桶水。多么解脱！我们在这里扎营过夜。

Yesterday, our Indians discussed for hours the route which we should take. There is one way, farther by ten or twelve miles, with sure water; another shorter, where water is found sometimes; their conclusion was that water would be found now; and this is the way we go, yet all day long we are anxious about it. To be out two days, with only the water that can be carried in two small kegs, is to have our animals suffer greatly. At five o’clock we come to the spot, and there is a huge water-pocket, containing several barrels. What a relief! Here we camp for the night.

9 月 15 日。破晓就起身，因为走到下一个水源是一整天的行军。

_September 15._--Up at day-break, for it is a long day’s march to the next water. They say we must “run very hard” to reach it by dark.

我们的路线向南。从烟管泉能看见一座山，我认出它就是去年夏天从一处俯瞰大峡谷的崖壁上看到的那座；我想到达那座山后面的河边。住在那群山中最高一座上的印第安人，我想顺路去拜访。这些山是火山成因的，我们很快来到铺满熔岩碎块的地面。路变得非常难走。我们得横穿深沟——那些注入大峡谷的峡谷的源头。现在观察我们印第安人的知识真是奇妙。没有一条小路他们不认得；每一条沟、每一块岩石都似乎熟悉。我素来自豪于能把握并在脑中记下一片乡野的地形；但这些印第安人让我羞愧。我的知识只是总体的，涵盖一个地区更重要的特征，像一张刻在我脑中的地图；而他们的是具体的。他们认识每一块岩石、每一道岩架、每一条沟和峡谷，以及该在这些之间如何蜿蜒才能找到一个山口；他们的知识从不出错。他们没法向你描述一片乡野，但能告诉你一条路的所有细节。

Our course is to the south. From Pipe Spring we can see a mountain, and I recognize it as the one seen last summer from a cliff overlooking the Grand Cañon; and I wish to reach the river just behind the mountain. There are Indians living in the group, of which it is the highest, whom I wish to visit on the way. These mountains are of volcanic origin, and we soon come to ground that is covered with fragments of lava. The way becomes very difficult. We have to cross deep ravines, the heads of cañons that run into the Grand Cañon. It is curious now to observe the knowledge of our Indians. There is not a trail but what they know; every gulch and every rock seems familiar. I have prided myself on being able to grasp and retain in my mind the topography of a country; but these Indians put me to shame. My knowledge is only general, embracing the more important features of a region that remains as a map engraved on my mind; but theirs is particular. They know every rock and every ledge, every gulch and cañon, and just where to wind among these to find a pass; and their knowledge is unerring. They cannot describe a country to you, but they can tell you all the particulars of a route.

我只有一匹小马给两个人骑，他们本打算「轮流骑」；但丘阿鲁姆皮克坚持走路，那匹小马让我和那个印第安人换着骑。

I have but one pony for the two, and they were to ride “turn about”; but _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_, the chief, rides, and _Shuts_, the one-eyed, bare-legged, merry-faced pigmy, walks, and points the way with a slender cane; then leaps and bounds by the shortest way, and sits down on a rock and waits demurely until we come, always meeting us with a jest, his face a rich mine of sunny smiles.

黄昏时我们到达蓄水坑。它在一条深沟里，在这座大山的侧翼。雨季里水从山坡滚下，越过悬崖跌落，在下方坚硬的岩石里挖出一个深盆。这个盆子照不到太阳，一年到头都蓄着水。

At dusk we reach the water-pocket. It is in a deep gorge, on the flank of this great mountain. During the rainy season the water rolls down the mountain side, plunging over precipices, and excavates a deep basin in the solid rock below. This basin, hidden from the sun, holds water the year round.

9 月 16 日。今早趁队员们收拾牲口，我爬上营地附近一座小山看地形。它是一大堆火山渣，松散轻盈得像铁匠炉的煤渣，在我脚下松动，我费了很大力气爬；但到了峰顶，向东南望去，我再次看到大峡谷两侧那些深谷的迷宫；数量太多，我无法确定它是否在视野之内。那些在它们深暗阴郁的孤寂中度过、壮丽又可怕的月份的记忆涌上来，一时间我又把那段生活重新活了一遍。

_September 16._--This morning, while the men are packing the animals, I climb a little mountain near camp, to obtain a view of the country. It is a huge pile of volcanic scoria, loose and light as cinders from a forge, which give way under my feet, and I climb with great labor; but reaching the summit, and looking to the southeast, I see once more the labyrinth of deep gorges that flank the Grand Cañon; in the multitude, I cannot determine whether it be in view or not. The memories of grand and awful months spent in their deep, gloomy solitudes come up, and I live that life over again for a time.

出发前，我以为能好好看到那座大山……

I supposed, before starting, that I could get a good view of the great mountain from this point; but it is like climbing a chair to look at a castle. I wish to discover some way by which it can be ascended, as it is my intention to go to the summit before I return to the settlements. There is a cliff near the summit, and I do not see the way yet. Now down I go, sliding on the cinders, making them rattle and clang.

印第安人说我们今天骑程很短，将到达一个印第安村落……

The Indians say we are to have a short ride to-day, and that we will reach an Indian village, situated by a good spring. Our way is across the spurs that put out from the great mountain, as we pass it to the left.

我们上上下下，横穿深沟，熔岩碎片在我们马蹄下叮当作响；时而在柏树间，时而在松林间，时而又穿过山坡草地。一点钟我们下到一个可爱的山谷，铺着一层起伏的青草；上端有时有点水，有些季节，我们想找的那些印第安人就扎营在这里。丘阿鲁姆皮克策马前去寻找他们，告诉他们我们是朋友——否则他们看见我们没打招呼地来，会跑掉或要跟我们打。很快我们看见丘阿鲁姆皮克全速驰来，听到他放声大喊，远处有两个印第安人正往山坡上逃窜。一个停住了；另一个继续跑，很快消失在视野里。我们骑马过去，见丘阿鲁姆皮克正和停下的那个说话。那是住在这些山坡草地上的一位女士；她显然在纳贡，戈黛娃式的。她告诉我们她的人在泉水边；只有两小时骑程；她好心的主人已经前去告诉他们我们要来，而她正在收种子。

Up and down we go, across deep ravines, and the fragments of lava clank under our horses’ feet; now among cedars, now among pines, and now across mountain side glades. At one o’clock we descend into a lovely valley, with a carpet of waving grass; sometimes there is a little water in the upper end of it, and, during some seasons, the Indians we wish to find are encamped here. _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ rides on to find them, and to say we are friends, otherwise they would run away, or propose to fight us, should we come without notice. Soon we see _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ riding at full speed, and hear him shouting at the top of his voice, and away in the distance are two Indians, scampering up the mountain side. One stops; the other still goes on, and is soon lost to view. We ride up, and find _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ talking with the one who had stopped. It is one of the ladies resident in these mountain glades; she is evidently paying taxes, Godiva like. She tells us that her people are at the spring; that it is only two hours’ ride; that her good master has gone on to tell them we are coming, and that she is harvesting seeds.

我们坐下吃午饭，把我们的饼干分给山里的那位妇女；然后继续走，翻过两座圆峰之间的一个垭口。我让队伍先往村子去，自己爬左边的山峰——骑马到树木的上限，然后徒步往上爬。从这个点我能看见大峡谷，知道自己身在何处。我也能看见那个印第安村庄，在一个草木葱茏的山谷里，被群山环抱，炊烟从他们的火堆上袅袅升起；我的人正在放出他们的马，一群土著人站在周围。我下山去，日落时到达营地。

We sit down and eat our luncheon, and share our biscuit with the woman of the mountains; then on we go, over a divide between two rounded peaks. I send the party on to the village, and climb the peak on the left, riding my horse to the upper limit of trees, and then tugging up afoot. From this point I can see the Grand Cañon, and know where I am. I can see the Indian village, too, in a grassy valley, embosomed in the mountains, the smoke curling up from their fires; my men are turning out their horses, and a group of natives stand around. Down the mountain I go, and reach camp at sunset.

晚饭后我们在火上添了些雪松枝，黑皮肤的村民围坐四周……

After supper we put some cedar boughs on the fire, the dusky villagers sit around, and we have a smoke and a talk. I explain the object of my visit, and assure them of my friendly intentions. Then I ask them about a way down into the cañon. They tell me that years ago, a way was discovered by which parties could go down, but that no one has attempted it for a long time; that it is a very difficult and very dangerous undertaking to reach the “Big Water.” Then I inquire about the _Shi′-vwits_, a tribe that lives about the springs on the mountain sides and cañon cliffs to the southwest. They say that their village is now about thirty miles away, and promise to send a messenger for them to-morrow morning.

办完当晚的事，我问营里有没有 tu-gwi'-na-gunt：也就是说，有没有擅长讲他们神话的人。丘阿鲁姆皮克说这些印第安人的首领托莫隆蒂凯是这方面非常有名的人；但他们俩都推辞，说现在还不是讲 tu-gwi'-nai 的季节。但我早有准备，很快队伍里一些人带着烟斗和烟草、一大壶咖啡和一盘饼干来了，经过一番点烟、吸烟的仪式后，我们全都饱餐一顿，被这——对他们来说——难得的丰盛伙食暖了身子后，决定这一夜就用来讲神话。我请托莫隆蒂凯给我们讲「苏库斯·瓦伊乌纳茨」（One Two Boys，一二男孩）的故事，他同意了。

Having finished our business for the evening, I ask if there is a _tu-gwi′-na-gunt_ in camp: that is, if there is any one present who is skilled in relating their mythology. _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ says _To-mor′-ro-un-ti-kai_, the chief of these Indians, is a very noted man for his skill in this matter; but they both object, by saying that the season for _tu-gwi′-nai_ has not yet arrived. But I had anticipated this, and soon some members of the party come with pipes and tobacco, a large kettle of coffee, and a tray of biscuits, and, after sundry ceremonies of pipe lighting and smoking, we all feast, and, warmed up by this, to them, unusual good living, it is decided that the night shall be spent in relating mythology. I ask _To-mor′-ro-un-ti-kai_ to tell us about the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_, or One Two Boys, and to this he agrees.

印第安营地漫长的冬夜通常用来讲述神话故事——据说是关于一古老种族动物神的传说。故事通常由一个老人讲述，队伍里的其他人协助扮演次要角色，而部落成员围拢过来，或作评论，或从讲述者——更确切地说，讲述者们——强调的道德中接受印象；因为这种表演多少带点戏剧的性质。

The long winter evenings of an Indian camp are usually devoted to the relation of mythological stories, which purport to give a history of an ancient race of animal gods. The stories are usually told by some old man, assisted by others of the party, who take secondary parts, while the members of the tribe gather about, and make comments, or receive impressions from the morals which are enforced by the story teller, or, more properly, story tellers; for the exercise partakes somewhat of the nature of a theatrical performance.

「一二男孩」的故事。

THE SO′-KUS WAI′-UN-ATS.

身披石衣的图姆普维奈罗圭努姆普杀死了鹤人西科尔，抢走了他的妻子，看到她带着一个孩子，觉得这孩子在旅途中会是个累赘，就命令她杀死孩子。但母亲爱这婴儿，把他藏在衣服下，带到了他祖母那里。而石衣人带着他抢来的新娘回了自己的地方。

_Tum-pwi-nai′-ro-gwi-nump_, he who had a stone shirt, killed _Si-kor′_, the Crane, and stole his wife, and seeing that she had a child, and thinking it would be an incumbrance to them on their travels, he ordered her to kill it. But the mother, loving the babe, hid it under her dress, and carried it away to its grandmother. And Stone Shirt carried his captured bride to his own land.

几年后，在祖母的照料下，孩子长成了一个好少年，无论她走到哪里都是她的伙伴。

In a few years the child grew to be a fine lad, under the care of his grandmother, and was her companion wherever she went.

有一天他们正在河边挖菖蒲根，把根堆在岸上。干了一会儿，男孩发觉根拔起来比往常省力，就问老妇人这是怎么回事，但她也不知道；他们继续干活，芦苇仍越来越容易拔出来，他们的惊异也越来越大，直到祖母说：「一定有什么奇怪的事要发生了。」于是男孩走到他们堆放根的地方，发现有人把根拿走了，他跑回来喊道：「祖母，是你把根拿走了吗？」她回答说：「不，孩子；也许是哪个鬼魂把它们拿走了；我们别挖了，走吧。」但男孩不满足，因为他非常想知道这一切意味着什么；于是他四处找了一会儿，然后……

One day they were digging flag roots, on the margin of the river, and putting them in a heap on the bank. When they had been at work a little while, the boy perceived that the roots came up with greater ease than was customary, and he asked the old woman the cause of this, but she did not know; and, as they continued their work, still the reeds came up with less effort, at which their wonder increased, until the grandmother said, “Surely, some strange thing is about to transpire.” Then the boy went to the heap, where they had been placing the roots, and found that some one had taken them away, and he ran back, exclaiming, “Grandmother, did you take the roots away?” And she answered, “No, my child; perhaps some ghost has taken them off; let us dig no more; come away.” But the boy was not satisfied, as he greatly desired to know what all this meant; so he searched about for a time, and at length found a man sitting under a tree, whom he taunted with being a thief, and threw mud and stones at him, until he broke the stranger’s leg, who answered not the boy, nor resented the injuries he received, but remained silent and sorrowful; and, when his leg was broken, he tied it up in sticks, and bathed it in the river, and sat down again under the tree, and beckoned the boy to approach. When the lad came near, the stranger told him he had something of great importance to reveal. “My son,” said he, “did that old woman ever tell you about your father and mother?” “No,” answered the boy; “I have never heard of them.” “My son, do you see these bones scattered on the ground? Whose bones are these?” “How should I know?” answered the boy. “It may be that some elk or deer has been killed here.” “No,” said the old man. “Perhaps they are the bones of a bear;” but the old man shook his head. So the boy mentioned many other animals, but the stranger still shook his head, and finally said, “These are the bones of your father; Stone Shirt killed him, and left him to rot here on the ground, like a wolf.” And the boy was filled with indignation against the slayer of his father. Then the stranger asked, “Is your mother in yonder lodge?” and the boy replied, “No.” “Does your mother live on the banks of this river?” and the boy answered, “I don’t know my mother; I have never seen her; she is dead.” “My son,” replied the stranger, “Stone Shirt, who killed your father, stole your mother, and took her away to the shore of a distant lake, and there she is his wife to-day.” And the boy wept bitterly, and while the tears filled his eyes so that he could not see, the stranger disappeared. Then the boy was filled with wonder at what he had seen and heard, and malice grew in his heart against his father’s enemy. He returned to the old woman, and said, “Grandmother, why have you lied to me about my father and mother?” and she answered not, for she knew that a ghost had told all to the boy. And the boy fell upon the ground weeping and sobbing, until he fell into a deep sleep, when strange things were told him.

他的沉睡持续了三天三夜，醒来后他对祖母说：「我要去召集所有部族参加我的战斗；」说罢他就出发了。

His slumber continued three days and three nights, and when he awoke, he said to his grandmother: “I am going away to enlist all nations in my fight;” and straightway he departed.

（这里男孩的游历被讲述得极其详细，包括各部族如何接待他，全用一系列很长的对话来叙述，此处从略。）

(Here the boy’s travels are related with many circumstances concerning the way he was received by the people, all given in a series of conversations, very lengthy, so they will be omitted.)

最后他先于他召集的部族回来，带回了狼人欣奥夫和响尾蛇托戈奥夫。三人吃了饭后，男孩对老妇人说：「祖母，把我切成两半！」但她迟疑了，说不想杀死她如此深爱的人。「把我切成两半！」男孩命令道；他给了她一把他从远方带来的石斧，用一种充满威严的口气再次命令她把他切成两半。于是她站在他面前，把他一分为二，然后惊恐地逃开。看哪！每一半都变成了一个完整的人，那一个美丽的男孩变成了两个，他们如此相像，没人能分辨。

Finally he returned in advance of the people whom he had enlisted, bringing with him _Shin-au′-av_, the wolf, and _To-go′-av_, the rattlesnake. When the three had eaten food, the boy said to the old woman: “Grandmother, cut me in two!” But she demurred, saying she did not wish to kill one whom she loved so dearly. “Cut me in two!” demanded the boy; and he gave her a stone ax, which he had brought from a distant country, and with a manner of great authority he again commanded her to cut him in two. So she stood before him, and severed him in twain, and fled in terror. And lo! each part took the form of an entire man, and the one beautiful boy appeared as two, and they were so much alike no one could tell them apart.

当男孩召集的那些部族涌入营地时，欣奥夫和托戈奥夫正在告诉他们男孩身上发生的奇事——现在有了两个；他们都认为这是去石衣人之地远征成功的预兆。于是他们踏上旅程。

When the people or natives, whom the boy had enlisted, came pouring into the camp, _Shin-au′-av_ and _To-go′-av_ were engaged in telling them of the wonderful thing that had happened to the boy, and that now there were two; and they all held it to be an augury of a successful expedition to the land of Stone Shirt. And they started on their journey.

原来，男孩在三天沉睡的梦里被告知有一个魔法杯，他从周游各部族的旅途中把它带回了家，「一二男孩」把它夹在两人之间捧着，里面装满水。欣奥夫走在他们右边，托戈奥夫在左边，部族们按被召集的先后顺序跟随。他们数量庞大，一字排开时，从前到后要走一整天。

Now the boy had been told in the dream of his three days’ slumber, of a magical cup, and he had brought it home with him from his journey among the nations, and the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_ carried it between them, filled with water. _Shin-au′-av_ walked on their right, and _To-go′-av_ on their left, and the nations followed in the order in which they had been enlisted. There was a vast number of them, so that when they were stretched out in line it was one day’s journey from the front to the rear of the column.

他们走了两天，来到沙漠深处，所有人都渴了，因为他们找不到水，人们倒在沙上呻吟，抱怨说他们被骗了，诅咒「一二」。

When they had journeyed two days, and were far out on the desert, all the people thirsted, for they found no water, and they fell down upon the sand, groaning, and murmuring that they had been deceived, and they cursed the One-Two.

但「一二男孩」在那神奇的梦里已被告诉将要忍受的苦难，以及杯中带的水只许在万不得已时使用；兄弟俩彼此说：「现在是我们喝水的时候了。」一个畅饮了那魔法碗，发现它仍是满的；他递给另一个喝，仍然满着；「一二」把它递给部族，一个接一个地喝，碗仍满到边缘。

But the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_ had been told in the wonderful dream of the suffering which would be endured, and that the water which they carried in the cup was only to be used in dire necessity; and the brothers said to each other: “Now the time has come for us to drink the water.” And when one had quaffed of the magical bowl, he found it still full; and he gave it to the other to drink, and still it was full; and the One-Two gave it to the people, and one after another did they all drink, and still the cup was full to the brim.

但欣奥夫死了，所有人都哀悼，因为他是个伟人。兄弟俩把碗举在他上方，向他洒水，他坐起来说：「你们为什么打扰我？我刚梦见山间溪流和草地，还有蜜露丰饶的藤丛。」他们把碗给他，他也喝了；但他喝完后，一滴不剩了。他们精神焕发、欢欣鼓舞地继续旅程。

But _Shin-au′-av_ was dead, and all the people mourned, for he was a great man. The brothers held the cup over him, and sprinkled him with water, when he arose and said: “Why do you disturb me? I did have a vision of mountain brooks and meadows, of cane where honey-dew was plenty.” They gave him the cup, and he drank also; but when he had finished there was none left. Refreshed and rejoicing they proceeded on their journey.

第二天，没有食物，他们饿了，大家都快要饿死；他们又向兄弟俩抱怨，诅咒他们。但「一二男孩」看见远处平原的一个高岗上站着一只羚羊，在天空的衬托下轮廓分明；欣奥夫知道那就是石衣人养的、有许多眼睛的神奇羚羊——他的哨兵；他提议去杀它，但托戈奥夫不同意，说：「最好我去，因为他会看见你，跑掉。」但「一二男孩」让欣奥夫去；他朝羚羊站立的左边方向出发，好绕过几座山做长迂回，从另一侧逼近它。托戈奥夫离开营地走了一小段，喊兄弟俩：「你们看得见我么？」他们回答看不见。「找我；」而趁他们……

The next day, being without food, they were hungry, and all were about to perish; and again they murmured at the brothers, and cursed them. But the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_ saw in the distance an antelope, standing on an eminence in the plain, in bold relief against the sky; and _Shin-au′-av_ knew it was the wonderful antelope with many eyes, which Stone Shirt kept for his watchman; and he proposed to go and kill it, but _To-go′-av_ demurred, and said: “It were better that I should go, for he will see you, and run away.” But the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_ told _Shin-au′-av_ to go; and he started in a direction away to the left of where the antelope was standing, that he might make a long detour about some hills, and come upon him from the other side. _To-go′-av_ went a little way from camp, and called to the brothers: “Do you see me?” and they answered they did not. “Hunt for me;” and while they were hunting for him, the rattlesnake said: “I can see you; you are doing”--so and so, telling them what they were doing; but they could not find him.

然后响尾蛇走出来，宣告：「现在你们知道我能看见别人，而我愿意时别人看不见我。欣奥夫杀不了那只羚羊，因为它有许多眼睛，是石衣人的神奇哨兵；但我能杀它，因为我能到它那里去，它看不见我。」于是兄弟俩信服了，允许他去；他去杀了羚羊。欣奥夫看见它倒下，非常生气，因为他极以自己的猎手名声为傲，急于得到杀死这只名羚羊的荣誉，他跑过去想杀托戈奥夫；但当他走近，看见羚羊很肥，能给部族一顿丰盛的宴席，他的怒气平息了。「谁杀猎物有什么关系，」他说，「反正我们都能吃。」

Then the rattlesnake came forth, declaring: “Now you know I can see others, and that I cannot be seen when I so desire. _Shin-au′-av_ cannot kill that antelope, for he has many eyes, and is the wonderful watchman of Stone Shirt; but I can kill him, for I can go where he is, and he cannot see me.” So the brothers were convinced, and permitted him to go; and he went and killed the antelope. When _Shin-au′-av_ saw it fall, he was very angry, for he was extremely proud of his fame as a hunter, and anxious to have the honor of killing this famous antelope, and he ran up with the intention of killing _To-go′-av_; but when he drew near, and saw the antelope was fat, and would make a rich feast for the people, his anger was appeased. “What matters it,” said he, “who kills the game, when we can all eat it?”

于是所有人都被喂得饱饱的，他们继续旅程。

So all the people were fed in abundance, and they proceeded on their journey.

第二天人们又渴了，魔法杯空了；但「一二男孩」既已在梦里被告知该怎么做，便变成鸽子，飞到一个湖边——石衣人的家就在湖岸边。

The next day the people again suffered for water, and the magical cup was empty; but the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_, having been told in their dream what to do, transformed themselves into doves, and flew away to a lake, on the margin of which was the home of Stone Shirt.

靠近湖岸，他们看见两个少女在水里沐浴；鸟儿们站在那里看，因为少女们非常美丽。然后他们飞进附近的灌木丛，想看个更近，却被少女们为捉多事的小鸟设下的陷阱逮住了。美丽的少女们走过来，把鸟从陷阱里取出来，非常喜欢它们，因为她们从没见过这样的鸟。她们把鸟带给父亲石衣人，他说：「女儿们，我非常担心这些是我敌人派来的探子，因为这样的鸟不住在我们的地方；」他要把它们扔进火里，少女们含泪求他不要毁掉她们美丽的鸟；但他满心疑虑地屈从了她们的恳求。然后她们把鸟带到湖边，放它们自由。

Coming near to the shore, they saw two maidens bathing in the water; and the birds stood and looked, for the maidens were very beautiful. Then they flew into some bushes, near by, to have a nearer view, and were caught in a snare which the girls had placed for intrusive birds. The beautiful maidens came up, and, taking the birds out of the snare, admired them very much, for they had never seen such birds before. They carried them to their father, Stone Shirt, who said: “My daughters, I very much fear these are spies from my enemies, for such birds do not live in our land;” and he was about to throw them into the fire, when the maidens besought him, with tears, that he would not destroy their beautiful birds; but he yielded to their entreaties with much misgiving. Then they took the birds to the shore of the lake, and set them free.

鸟儿们重新自由后，在灌木丛中飞了一圈，直到找到它们丢失的魔法杯，叼起它飞到湖中央，落在水面上——少女们以为它们淹死了。

When the birds were at liberty once more, they flew around among the bushes, until they found the magical cup which they had lost, and taking it up, they carried it out into the middle of the lake and settled down upon the water, and the maidens supposed they were drowned.

鸟儿们把杯装满后，又飞起来，回到沙漠里的人群中，正好及时赶到——用那杯水救了他们，每个人都喝了；直到最后一个人满足，杯仍是满的；然后一滴也不剩。

The birds, when they had filled their cup, rose again, and went back to the people in the desert, where they arrived just at the right time to save them with the cup of water, from which each drank; and yet it was full until the last was satisfied, and then not a drop remained.

兄弟俩报告说他们看见了石衣人和他的女儿们。

The brothers reported that they had seen Stone Shirt and his daughters.

第二天他们来到敌人住所附近，兄弟俩亲自出马去侦察。看见一个妇女在拾种子，他们走近，认出那是他们的母亲——被石衣人从鹤人西科尔那里抢走的。他们告诉她他们是她的儿子，但她否认，说她从来没有过一个以上的儿子；但男孩们向她讲述了自己的身世，讲到两个如何从一个而来，她信服了。她试图劝阻他们对石衣人开战，告诉他们任何箭都不可能穿透他的盔甲，他是个伟大的武士，唯一的乐趣就是杀敌，他的女儿们也配有魔法弓和箭，射得极快，箭会像云一样遮蔽天空，而且她们不需要瞄准，因为她们的箭射向它们愿意去的地方……

The next day they came near to the home of the enemy, and the brothers, in proper person, went out to reconnoitre. Seeing a woman gleaning seeds, they drew near, and knew it was their mother, whom Stone Shirt had stolen from _Si-kor′_, the crane. They told her they were her sons, but she denied it, and said she had never had but one son; but the boys related to her their history, with the origin of the two from one, and she was convinced. She tried to dissuade them from making war upon Stone Shirt, and told them that no arrow could possibly penetrate his armor, and that he was a great warrior, and had no other delight than in killing his enemies, and that his daughters also were furnished with magical bows and arrows, which they could shoot so fast that the arrows would fill the air like a cloud, and that it was not necessary for them to take aim, for their missiles went where they willed; they _thought_ the arrows to the hearts of their enemies; and thus the maidens could kill the whole of the people before a common arrow could be shot by a common person. But the boys told her what the spirit had said in the long dream, and had promised that Stone Shirt should be killed. They told her to go down to the lake at dawn, so as not to be endangered by the battle.

夜里，「一二男孩」变成老鼠，前往石衣人的住处，找到了属于少女们的魔法弓和箭，用他们锋利的牙齿咬断了弓背上的筋，啃断了弓弦，让它们都成了废品；而托戈奥夫藏在不远处一块岩石下。

During the night, the _So′-kus Wai′-un-ats_ transformed themselves into mice, and proceeded to the home of Stone Shirt, and found the magical bows and arrows that belonged to the maidens, and with their sharp teeth they cut the sinew on the backs of the bows, and nibbled the bow strings, so that they were worthless; while _To-go′-av_ hid himself under a rock near by.

当黎明降临天空，石衣人图姆普维奈罗圭努姆普起身走出帐篷，为自己的力量和安稳而洋洋得意，坐在托戈奥夫藏身的那块岩石上；他看到机会，把毒牙扎进这位英雄的肉里。石衣人高高跳起，喊女儿们说他们被出卖了，敌人就在近旁；她们抓起魔法弓和装满魔法箭的箭袋，急忙来为他防御。与此同时，所有围住营地的部族都冲下来投入战斗。但美丽的少女们发现武器已被毁坏，便向敌人挥手，仿佛要谈判；在她们被杀的父亲的尸体上方站了片刻，唱起死亡之歌，跳起死亡之舞，围着死去的英雄打着令人眩晕的转圈，哀号着……

When dawn came into the sky, _Tum-pwi-nai′-ro-gwi-nump_, the Stone Shirt man, arose and walked out of his tent, exulting in his strength and security, and sat down upon the rock under which _To-go′-av_ was hiding; and he, seeing his opportunity, sunk his fangs into the flesh of the hero. Stone Shirt sprang high into the air, and called to his daughters that they were betrayed, and that the enemy was near; and they seized their magical bows, and their quivers filled with magical arrows, and hurried to his defense. At the same time, all the nations who were surrounding the camp rushed down to battle. But the beautiful maidens, finding their weapons were destroyed, waved back their enemies, as if they would parley; and, standing for a few moments over the body of their slain father, sang the death song, and danced the death dance, whirling in giddy circles about the dead hero, and wailing with despair, until they sank down and expired.

征服者们把少女们葬在湖岸边；但图姆普维奈罗圭努姆普被留下腐烂，他的骨头在沙上漂白——正如他对待西科尔那样。

The conquerors buried the maidens by the shores of the lake; but _Tum-pwi-nai′-ro-gwi-nump_ was left to rot, and his bones to bleach on the sands, as he had left _Si-kor′_.

尤特人中有这样的谚语：「当你为执行神灵的命令而受苦时，不要抱怨，因为已备好一杯水。」另一条：「谁杀猎物有什么关系，反正我们都能吃。」

There is this proverb among the Utes: “Do not murmur when you suffer in doing what the spirits have commanded, for a cup of water is provided.” And another: “What matters it who kills the game, when we can all eat of it.”

表演结束时早已过了午夜。故事本身很有趣——虽然我以前听过许多遍；但也许从未在更有效的场合听过。伸展在高大阴郁的松树下；一堆巨大的营火，火边是年老、满脸皱纹、丑陋的男人；畸形、烂眼、歪脸的妇人；柔韧端庄的年轻男子；漂亮却傻笑的少女、光着身子的孩子，全都专注地听着，或不时大笑交谈，他们奇异的脸和黑黝黝的身影被松明火的强光照亮。一切环境都共同构成了一个奇异怪诞的场景。部落里一位巫师或巫医老人给我印象特别深。他不时会打断表演，为了纠正讲述者，或用一种奇特的威严和感染力强调故事的道德——那力量似乎到了……的边界

It is long after midnight when the performance is ended. The story itself was interesting, though I had heard it many times before; but never, perhaps, under circumstances more effective. Stretched beneath tall, sombre pines; a great camp fire, and by the fire, men, old, wrinkled, and ugly; deformed, blear eyed, wry faced women; lithe, stately young men; pretty but simpering maidens, naked children, all intently listening, or laughing and talking at times, their strange faces and dusky forms lit up with the glare of the pine-knot fire. All the circumstances conspired to make it a scene strange and weird. One old man, the sorcerer or medicine-man of the tribe, peculiarly impressed me. Now and then he would interrupt the play for the purpose of correcting the speakers, or impressing the moral of the story with a strange dignity and impressiveness that seemed to pass to the very border of the ludicrous; yet at no time did it make me smile.

故事讲完了，但还有一两个小时可以睡觉。我把丘阿鲁姆皮克拉到一边谈话。去年在峡谷里离开我们的那三个人，从那条侧向峡谷爬了出去，进入我们西边的希夫维茨山中，在那里遇到印第安人，和他们同住了一两夜，最后被杀。我很想知道详情，而干下这事的那一族的人就住在离这里不远、和这些人很熟的地方，所以我请丘阿鲁姆皮克替我打听。然后我们睡觉。

The story is finished, but there is yet time for an hour or two’s sleep. I take _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ to one side for a talk. The three men who left us in the cañon last year found their way up the lateral gorge, by which they went into the _Shi′-vwits_ Mountains, lying west of us, where they met with the Indians, and camped with them one or two nights, and were finally killed. I am anxious to learn the circumstances, and as the people of the tribe who committed the deed live but a little way from and are intimate with these people, I ask _Chu-ar′-ru-um-peak_ to make inquiry for me. Then we go to bed.

9 月 17 日。今早印第安人到我们营地来。他们决定派一个年轻人去找希夫维茨人。那信使系好他的鹿皮鞋，往袋里装了些食物、往小柳编壶里装了水，背在背上，迈着相当轻快的步子出发了。

_September 17._--Early this morning the Indians come up to our camp. They have concluded to send out a young man after the _Shi′-vwits_. The runner fixes his moccasins, puts some food in a sack and water in a little wicker work jug, straps them on his back, and starts at a good round pace.

我们决定沿峡谷下去，希望在返回时遇到希夫维茨人。很快我们准备出发，把营地和驮运牲口交给跟我们一起的两个印第安人照看。我们动身时，新向导走上来——一个烂眼、瘦脸、安静的老人，一手拿弓和箭，一手拿一根小手杖。这些印第安人都带一根弯把的手杖，他们说是用来打响尾蛇、从洞里掏兔子的。山谷高高在山中，我们从它下降，沿一条岩石嶙峋的陡峭小路，下、下、下，走了两个漫长疲倦的小时，牵着我们的小马，在岩石上绊绊磕磕。终于我们到了山脚，站在一个小土丘上，能望进下方一条峡谷。我们下到里面，然后沿着它走了好几英里，一路攀爬而下再而下。我们常常横穿被侧向水道灌进峡谷的熔岩床，这些带棱角的玄武岩碎块把路变得非常……

We have concluded to go down the cañon, hoping to meet the _Shi′-vwits_ on our return. Soon we are ready to start, leaving the camp and pack animals in charge of the two Indians who came with us. As we move out, our new guide comes up, a blear eyed, weazen faced, quiet old man, with his bow and arrows in one hand, and a small cane in the other. These Indians all carry canes with a crooked handle, they say to kill rattlesnakes, and to pull rabbits from their holes. The valley is high up in the mountain, and we descend from it, by a rocky, precipitous trail, down, down, down for two long, weary hours, leading our ponies and stumbling over the rocks. At last we are at the foot of the mountain, standing on a little knoll, from which we can look into a cañon below. Into this we descend, and then we follow it for miles, clambering down and still down. Often we cross beds of lava, that have been poured into the cañon by lateral channels, and these angular fragments of basalt make the way very rough for the animals.

大约两点钟，向导用手杖让我们停下，跳过岩石，消失在一道沟里。几分钟后他回来了，告诉我们下面一个坑里有点水。那水又脏又臭，我们的小马拒绝喝。我们继续走，仍在不断下降。离水坑一两英里，我们来到一道悬崖，到谷底有一千多英尺。有一条峡谷在更深处、与这里成直角奔流，这道悬崖就把它引入这里；而这第二条峡谷是那条更大峡谷的一条侧谷，我们将在它的底部找到河。四处寻找，我们找到一条能沿岩架、台阶和破碎的岩石堆下去的路径。

About two o’clock the guide halts us with his wand, and springing over the rocks he is lost in a gulch. In a few minutes he returns, and tells us there is a little water below in a pocket. It is vile and stinking, and our ponies refuse to drink it. We pass on, still ever descending. A mile or two from the water basin we come to a precipice, more than a thousand feet to the bottom. There is a cañon running at a greater depth, and at right angles to this, into which this enters by the precipice; and this second cañon is a lateral one to the greater one, in the bottom of which we are to find the river. Searching about, we find a way by which we can descend along the shelves, and steps, and piles of broken rocks.

我们牵着马出发；左边是一堵墙；右边是未知的深渊。有些地方我们走的路是在窄得、或斜得让我的心里都打颤的岩架上——生怕哪匹小马一失足，把人也一起带下悬崖。不时我们蹬落脚下的松石，它们滚下悬崖，轰隆隆地往下、往下，回声在远处的峡谷间回荡。终于我们沿一段平坦的岩架走了一段，然后转向右，沿一道陡坡之字形下到谷底。现在我们沿这条较低的峡谷走了两三英里，到它止于大峡谷的地方——正如另一条止于这条，只是河在我们脚下一千八百英尺，在这个距离上，它看起来不过是一条小溪。我们那干瘪的向导，这个「人体泡菜」，坐在一块岩石上，对我们的窘态似乎大为开心，因为看不出有任何办法能下到河边。几分钟后他安静地站起来，招手让我们跟上，指出一条窄斜的……

We start leading our ponies; a wall upon our left; unknown depths on our right. At places our way is along shelves so narrow, or so sloping, that I ache with fear lest a pony should make a misstep, and knock a man over the cliffs with him. Now and then we start the loose rocks under our feet, and over the cliffs they go, thundering down, down, as the echoes roll through distant cañons. At last we pass along a level shelf for some distance, then we turn to the right, and zigzag down a steep slope to the bottom. Now we pass along this lower cañon, for two or three miles, to where it terminates in the Grand Cañon, as the other ended in this, only the river is 1,800 feet below us, and it seems, at this distance, to be but a creek. Our withered guide, the human pickle, seats himself on a rock, and seems wonderfully amused at our discomfiture, for we can see no way by which to descend to the river. After some minutes, he quietly rises, and, beckoning us to follow, he points out a narrow sloping shelf on the right, and this is to be our way. It leads along the cliff, for half a mile, to a wider bench beyond, which, he says, is broken down on the other side in a great slide, and there we can get to the river. So we start out on the shelf; it is so steep we can hardly stand on it, and to fall, or slip, is to go--don’t look and see!

很快就明显看出，我们不能带小马沿那岩架走。自从向导多年前上次来过之后，暴风雨把它冲刷掉了。有一匹小马已经走出那么远，直到我们找到一处更宽的地方才能把它转回头，但最后我们还是把它弄了下来。我带着一部分人把马送回那个长着几丛灌木的地方，放开它们；与此同时，其他人正在寻找能下到河边的路。我回来时，有一个人——毕晓普上尉——已经找到一条路下去了。我们背着面包、咖啡、糖和两三床毯子出发。现在天几乎黑了，我们找不到上尉走的那条路，白找了一个小时。两个人绕过一个圆形剧场，走了四分之一英里多，从一道正对着我们的破碎裂谷开始下去。这些垂直或近乎垂直的壁常常被裂谷切开，暴雨顺它们流下，这些裂谷的顶部……

It is soon manifest that we cannot get the ponies along the ledge. The storms have washed it down, since our guide was here last, years ago. One of the ponies has gone so far that we cannot turn him back until we find a wider place, but at last we get him off. With part of the men, I take the horses back to the place where there are a few bushes growing, and turn them loose; in the meantime the other men are looking for some way by which we can get down to the river. When I return, one, Captain Bishop, has found a way, and gone down. We pack bread, coffee, sugar, and two or three blankets among us, and set out. It is now nearly dark, and we cannot find the way by which the captain went, and an hour is spent in fruitless search. Two of the men go away around an amphitheater, more than a fourth of a mile, and start down a broken chasm that faces us, who are behind. These walls, that are vertical, or nearly so, are often cut by chasms, where the showers run down, and the top of these chasms will be back a distance from the face of the wall, and the bed of the chasm will slope down, with here and there a fall. At other places, huge rocks have fallen, and block the way. Down such a one the two men start. There is a curious plant growing out from the crevices of the rock. A dozen stems will start from one root, and grow to the length of eight or ten feet, and not throw out a branch or twig, but these stems are thickly covered with leaves. Now and then the two men come to a bunch of dead stems, and make a fire to mark for us their way and progress.

与此同时我们找到这样一条沟，开始下去，但很快来到「跳崖处」，在这里我们可以扔一块石头，听见它在下方遥远的地方微弱地撞击。我们担心得在岩石上抓着撑到天亮。我们的小个子印第安人拾了几根干茎，捆成一束，点着了一头举起来。其他人也照做，借着这些火把我们找到了摆脱困境的路。我们互相帮助，互相举火把，一个人攥着另一个人的手直到站稳脚跟，然后再用肩膀托住另一个人，就这样我们下到了峡谷深处。现在毕晓普上尉已在河边点起一堆巨大的漂木火。这堆火、对面沟里的火堆、还有我们自己燃烧的火把，照亮了一小片一小片地方，反而更显出下方那可怕的黑暗。但我们仍然前进，走了一两个小时，终于看见毕晓普上尉举着一支巨大的火把从沟里上来。他……

In the meantime we find such a gulch, and start down, but soon come to the “jumping off place,” where we can throw a stone, and hear it faintly striking, away below. We fear that we shall have to stay here, clinging to the rocks until daylight. Our little Indian gathers a few dry stems, ties them into a bundle, lights one end, and holds it up. The others do the same, and with these torches we find a way out of trouble. Helping each other, holding torches for each other, one clinging to another’s hand until we can get footing, then supporting the other on his shoulders, so we make our passage into the depths of the cañon. And now Captain Bishop has kindled a huge fire of driftwood, on the bank of the river. This, and the fires in the gulch opposite, and our own flaming torches, light up little patches, that make more manifest the awful darkness below. Still, on we go, for an hour or two, and at last we see Captain Bishop coming up the gulch, with a huge torch-light on his shoulders. He looks like a fiend, waving brands and lighting the fires of hell, and the men in the opposite gulch are imps, lighting delusive fires in inaccessible crevices, over yawning chasms; our own little Indian is surely the king of wizards, so I think, as I stop for a few moments on a rock to rest. At last we meet Captain Bishop, with his flaming torch, and, as he has learned the way, he soon pilots us to the side of the great Colorado. We are hungry and athirst, almost to starvation. Here we lie down on the rocks and drink, just a mouthful or so, as we dare; then we make a cup of coffee, and, spreading our blankets on a sand beach, the roaring Colorado lulls us to sleep.

9 月 18 日。我们在大峡谷里，在科罗拉多河边，比我们在山侧的营地低六千多英尺——那营地离这里十八英里；但水平距离的英里数只占我们今天辛劳的一小部分。我们必须赢得的是那一英里的高度，这才让它成为一项艰巨的任务。我们起得很早；一点面包和咖啡，然后环顾四周。我们的结论是，如果必要，我们可以把这里建成一个补给站；可以把口粮驮到昨晚我们留下牲口的地方，还可以雇印第安人把东西运到水边。

_September 18._--We are in the Grand Cañon, by the side of the Colorado, more than six thousand feet below our camp on the mountain side, which is eighteen miles away; but the miles of horizontal distance represent but a small part of the day’s labor before us. It is the mile of altitude we must gain that makes it a herculean task. We are up early; a little bread and coffee, and we look about us. Our conclusion is, that we can make this a dépôt of supplies, should it be necessary; that we can pack our rations to the point where we left our animals last night, and that we can employ Indians to bring them down to the water’s edge.

在一个宽阔的岩架上，我们发现一座古老石屋的废墟，墙壁已经坍塌，我们能看到住在这里的古代人——一个比现在这些更文明的种族——曾开辟过一座园子，并用一股从岩石里涌出的大泉水灌溉。在附近一些岩石上，我们发现一些奇怪的刻画。继续搜寻，我们找到一条沿峡谷壁上行的隐秘小路，某些地方用建在松岩里的台阶作标记，别处是凿出的阶梯，我们找到一条比昨晚黑暗中下来的那条容易得多的上去的路。到了壁顶，我们抓住马，出发了。我们疲倦的小马在峡谷里艰难跋涉，我们到达第二道悬崖；我们沿它上去，走走停停地牵着牲口。现在我们到达那个臭水坑；我们的小马已经三十小时没喝水，连这污浊的液体也急切地想喝。我们小心地为自己滤出一壶，然后把剩下的分给……

On a broad shelf, we find the ruins of an old stone house, the walls of which are broken down, and we can see where the ancient people who lived here--a race more highly civilized than the present--had made a garden, and used a great spring, that comes out of the rocks, for irrigation. On some rocks near by we discover some curious etchings. Still, searching about, we find an obscure trail up the cañon wall, marked, here and there, by steps which have been built in the loose rock, elsewhere hewn stairways, and we find a much easier way to go up than that by which we came down in the darkness last night. Coming to the top of the wall, we catch our horses, and start. Up the cañon our jaded ponies toil, and we reach the second cliff; up this we go, by easy stages, leading the animals. Now we reach the stinking water-pocket; our ponies have had no water for thirty hours, and are eager even for this foul fluid. We carefully strain a kettleful for ourselves, then divide what is left between them--two or three gallons for each; but this does not satisfy them, and they rage around, refusing to eat the scanty grass. We boil our kettle of water, and skim it; straining, boiling, and skimming makes it a little better, for it was full of loathsome, wriggling larvæ, with huge black heads. But plenty of coffee takes away the bad smell, and so modifies the taste that most of us can drink, though our little Indian seems to prefer the original mixture. We reach camp about sunset, and are glad to rest.

9 月 19 日。我们疲惫酸痛，必须和我们的印第安邻居歇一天。在恶劣的季节里，他们住在用树枝或雪松皮搭的棚子里——把树皮撕成长条。在这个气候里，一年大部分时间又干又暖，这样的日子他们不需要遮蔽。清出一小块圆形地面，用灌木和沙子在周围垒起来，白天在里面打滚，夜里男男女女、大人小孩挤成一堆；鹿皮、破布和沙子。他们穿得很少，在这宜人的气候里也不需要穿多。

_September 19._--We are tired and sore, and must rest a day with our Indian neighbors. During the inclement season they live in shelters, made of boughs, or bark of the cedar, which they strip off in long shreds. In this climate, most of the year is dry and warm, and during such time they do not care for shelter. Clearing a small, circular space of ground, they bank it around with brush and sand, and wallow in it during the day, and huddle together in a heap at night, men, women, and children; buckskin, rags, and sand. They wear very little clothing, not needing much in this lovely climate.

总的来说，这些印第安人比我认识的大陆上任何其他人都更接近原始状态。他们从没从政府得到过任何东西，也太穷，引不起商人的兴趣，而他们的国土如此难以进入，白人从不去拜访他们。阳光灿烂的山坡上长满野果、坚果和本地谷物，他们就靠这些为生。乌斯果——丝兰、即西班牙刺刀果的果实——很丰腴，有点像俄亥俄河谷的泡泡果。他们生吃，也在灰烬里烤着吃。他们采集一种仙人掌的果实，又甜又美，像葡萄一样吃，或榨出汁，把干浆做成饼存起来过冬；那酒他们围着营火喝，直到午夜被欢宴声闹得喜气洋洋。

Altogether, these Indians are more nearly in their primitive condition than any others on the continent with whom I am acquainted. They have never received anything from the Government, and are too poor to tempt the trader, and their country is so nearly inaccessible that the white man never visits them. The sunny mountain side is covered with wild fruits, nuts, and native grains, upon which they subsist. The _oose_, the fruit of the yucca, or Spanish bayonet, is rich, and not unlike the paw-paw of the valley of the Ohio. They eat it raw, and also roast it in the ashes. They gather the fruits of a cactus plant, which is rich and luscious, and eat them as grapes, or from them express the juice, making the dry pulp into cakes, and saving them for winter; the wine they drink about their camp fires, until the midnight is merry with the revelries.

他们采集许多植物的种子，如向日葵、一枝黄花草和草籽。为此他们有大锥形篮子，能装两蒲式耳多。女人们背着它们，用宽皮带从额头吊着，左手拿一个小的，右手拿一把柳编扇子，在草丛间走，把种子扫进小篮子，不时倒进大篮子，直到装满种子和谷壳；然后扬去谷壳，烘烤种子。她们烘烤的方法很奇特；她们把种子和大量烧红的炭放进柳编托盘，靠迅速灵巧地抖动和抛掷，让炭保持通红，种子和托盘却不被烧着。仿佛变魔术似的——这些老妇人熟练得很——种子烤好时就滚到托盘一边，炭滚到另一边。然后把种子磨成细粉，做成饼和糊糊。

They gather the seeds of many plants, as sunflowers, goldenrods, and grasses. For this purpose, they have large conical baskets, which hold two or more bushels. The women carry them on their backs, suspended from their foreheads by broad straps, and with a smaller one in the left hand, and a willow woven fan in the right, they walk among the grasses, and sweep the seed into the smaller basket, which is emptied, now and then, into the larger, until it is full of seeds and chaff; then they winnow out the chaff and roast the seeds. They roast these curiously; they put the seeds, with a quantity of red hot coals, into a willow tray, and, by rapidly and dexterously shaking and tossing them, keep the coals aglow, and the seeds and tray from burning. As if by magic, so skilled are the crones in this work, they roll the seeds to one side of the tray, as they are roasted, and the coals to the other. Then they grind the seeds into a fine flour, and make it into cakes and mush.

有时看着女人们磨面是件开心事。她们用的磨是一块平放的大石头，手里再拿一块小圆柱石。她们趴坐在地上，用脚和腿夹住那块大平石，把膝盖围拢装满种子，用她们黝黑的腿给磨做一个料斗，把种子推过大石面磨碎，从那里落进托盘。我见过一群女人一起磨面，合着歌谣打拍子，或闲聊唠嗑，而年轻姑娘们则打趣说笑，让松林里回荡着她们的笑声。母亲们把婴儿奇妙地装在篮子里。她们编一块柳条板，两边各缝一块鹿皮布，中间装满收拢成口袋状，底部封口。顶部做一个柳条遮篷，像「我祖母的遮阳帽」，把小宝宝裹在一件野猫皮袍里放进篮子，然后……

It is a merry sight, sometimes, to see the women grinding at the mill. For a mill, they use a large flat rock, lying on the ground, and another small cylindrical one in their hands. They sit prone on the ground, hold the large flat rock between the feet and legs, then fill their laps with seeds, making a hopper to the mill with their dusky legs, and grind by pushing the seeds across the larger rock, where it drops into a tray. I have seen a group of women grinding together, keeping time to a chant, or gossiping and chatting, while the younger lassies would jest and chatter, and make the pine woods merry with their laughter. Mothers carry their babes curiously in baskets. They make a wicker board, by plaiting willows, and sew a buckskin cloth to either edge, and this is fulled in the middle, so as to form a sack, closed at the bottom. At the top, they make a wicker shade, like “my grandmother’s sun bonnet,” and, wrapping the little one in a wild cat robe, place it in the basket, and this they carry on their backs, strapped over the forehead, and the little brown midgets are ever peering over their mother’s shoulders. In camp, they stand the basket against the trunk of a tree, or hang it to a limb.

这地方猎物很少，但他们偶尔能用箭打到一只大角羊或鹿——因为他们还没有枪。他们抓很多兔子，有时用箭，有时用网。他们用本地亚麻的纤维搓成线织网。有时这网织成一百码长，摆成半圆形，用山艾树作翼。他们举行围猎，把大群兔子赶进网里，用箭射杀。他们的大多数弓是雪松做的，但最好的用大角羊角做。羊角被取来，泡在水里直到很软，切成长薄条，粘在一起，就相当有弹性了。秋天蝗虫非常多。天冷时这些昆虫冻僵，可以成蒲式耳地收集。这时他们在沙里挖个坑，在附近生火加热石头，往坑底放一些，铺一层蝗……

There is little game in the country, yet they get a mountain sheep now and then, or a deer, with their arrows, for they are not yet supplied with guns. They get many rabbits, sometimes with arrows, sometimes with nets. They make a net of twine, made of the fibers of a native flax. Sometimes this is made a hundred yards in length, and is placed in a half circular position, with wings of sage brush. They have a circle hunt, and drive great numbers of rabbits into the snare, where they are shot with arrows. Most of their bows are made of cedar, but the best are made of the horns of mountain sheep. These are taken, soaked in water, until quite soft, cut into long thin strips, and glued together, and are then quite elastic. During the autumn, grasshoppers are very abundant. When cold weather sets in, these insects are numbed, and can be gathered by the bushel. At such a time, they dig a hole in the sand, heat stones in a fire near by, put some in the bottom of the hole, put on a layer of grasshoppers, then a layer of hot stones, and continue this, until they put bushels on to roast. There they are left until cool, when they are taken out, thoroughly dried, and ground into meal. Grasshopper gruel, or grasshopper cake, is a great treat.

他们的学问由一大堆传说或神话构成。很难引他们讲给白人听；但当年征服新墨西哥时的老西班牙神父，在印第安人中传播了许多《圣经》故事，这些印第安人通常愿意讲。认出它们并不总是容易——印第安人的头脑是这类故事的奇怪容器，它们容易长出新的枝节。也许他们增添的许多古怪之处，要归因于「神父们」讲述的方式。但在私下里，当你独自一人时，或当你在冬夜被允许坐到他们的营火边时，你会听到他们神话的故事。我相信一个印第安人能给出的最大友谊或信任的表示，就是把他的宗教讲给你听。一个人这样和我谈过之后，我就永远信任他；而自从那天晚上的经历之后，我和这些印第安人相处得非常融洽。

Their lore consists in a mass of traditions, or mythology. It is very difficult to induce them to tell it to white men; but the old Spanish priests, in the days of the conquest of New Mexico, have spread among the Indians of this country many Bible stories, which the Indians are usually willing to tell. It is not always easy to recognize them, the Indian mind being a strange receptacle for such stories, and they are apt to sprout new limbs. Maybe much of their added quaintness is due to the way in which they were told by the “fathers.” But in a confidential way, while you are alone, or when you are admitted to their camp fire on a winter night, you will hear the stories of their mythology. I believe that the greatest mark of friendship, or confidence, that an Indian can give, is to tell you his religion. After one has so talked with me, I should ever trust him; and I feel on very good terms with these Indians, since our experience of the other night.

对水源地、小路和山口的了解被认为极其重要，是一个首领立身所必需的。

A knowledge of the watering places, and of the trails and passes, is considered of great importance, and is necessary, to give standing to a chief.

这天晚上，我们派去找的希夫维茨人来了，晚饭后我们举行了一次长议事会。点起一堆熊熊的火，围着它坐下——住在这里的印第安人、希夫维茨人、雅各布·汉布林和我。这位汉布林，说他们的语言说得很好，对这一带所有印第安人都有巨大影响。他是个沉默寡言、含蓄的人，一开口总是缓慢、平静的调子，令人肃然起敬。他说话声音那么低，他们必须全神贯注才听得见，他们围着他坐着，死一般寂静。他说完一句有分寸的话，首领就复述一遍，然后全体发出一声庄重的咕哝。但首先，我装满我的烟斗，点上，抽了几口，然后传给汉布林；他抽了，递给下一个人，就这样传下去。传到首领那里后，他掏出自己的烟斗，装满点上，在我的之后传一圈。轮到我自己时我能抽自己的烟斗，但当印第安人的烟斗传过来时，我不知所措……

This evening, the _Shi′-vwits_, for whom we have sent, come in, and, after supper, we hold a long council. A blazing fire is built, and around this we sit--the Indians living here, the _Shi′-vwits_, Jacob Hamblin, and myself. This man, Hamblin, speaks their language well, and has a great influence over all the Indians in the region round about. He is a silent, reserved man, and when he speaks, it is in a slow, quiet way, that inspires great awe. His talk is so low that they must listen attentively to hear, and they sit around him in deathlike silence. When he finishes a measured sentence, the chief repeats it, and they all give a solemn grunt. But, first, I fill my pipe, light it, and take a few whiffs, then pass it to Hamblin; he smokes, and gives it to the man next, and so it goes around. When it has passed the chief, he takes out his own pipe, fills, and lights it, and passes it around after mine. I can smoke my own pipe in turn, but, when the Indian pipe comes around, I am nonplussed. It has a large stem, which has, at some time, been broken, and now there is a buckskin rag wound around it, and tied with sinew, so that the end of the stem is a huge mouthful, and looks like the burying ground of old dead spittle, venerable for a century. To gain time, I refill it, then engage in very earnest conversation, and, all unawares, I pass it to my neighbor unlighted.

我告诉印第安人，我希望来年在他们国家住几个月，希望他们把我当朋友对待。我不想做买卖；不要他们的土地。以前我总觉得很难让土著人明白我的目的，但那位摩门传教士的庄重帮了我大忙。我告诉他们，所有伟大善良的白人都渴望知道许多许多事情；他们花大量时间学习，而最伟大的人是知道最多的人。他们想知道关于山和谷、河和峡、走兽、飞鸟和蛇的一切。然后我告诉他们许多印第安部落以及他们住的地方；讲欧洲各国；讲中国人、非洲人，以及我能想到的关于他们的种种奇异之事。我给他们讲海洋、大河和高山，讲奇异的走兽和飞鸟。最后我告诉他们，我希望了解他们的峡谷和山脉，了解他们自己，以便去讲给……

I tell the Indians that I wish to spend some months in their country during the coming year, and that I would like them to treat me as a friend. I do not wish to trade; do not want their lands. Heretofore I have found it very difficult to make the natives understand my object, but the gravity of the Mormon missionary helps me much. I tell them that all the great and good white men are anxious to know very many things; that they spend much time in learning, and that the greatest man is he who knows the most. They want to know all about the mountains and the valleys, the rivers and the cañons, the beasts, and birds, and snakes. Then I tell them of many Indian tribes, and where they live; of the European nations; of the Chinese, of Africans, and all the strange things about them that come to my mind. I tell them of the ocean, of great rivers and high mountains, of strange beasts and birds. At last I tell them I wish to learn about their cañons and mountains, and about themselves, to tell other men at home; and that I want to take pictures of everything, and show them to my friends. All this occupied much time, and the matter and manner made a deep impression.

然后他们的首领回答：「你说得好，我们相信你说的话。我们相信雅各布，把你当父亲看待。你饿了，可以有我们的猎物。你可以采我们甜蜜的果实。你到我们国家来，我们会给你食物。我们会带你看泉水，你可以喝；水是好的。我们愿意做朋友，你来时我们会高兴。我们会告诉大河那边住着的印第安人，我们见到了卡普拉茨，他是印第安人的朋友。我们会告诉他们，他是雅各布的朋友。我们很穷。看我们的女人和孩子；他们赤身露体。我们没有马；我们爬岩石，脚都磨破了。我们住在岩石间，那里出产的食物很少，荆棘却很多。寒冷的月份来临时，我们的孩子挨饿。我们没有多少东西可以给你；你不可认为我们小气。你很聪明；我们听你讲了许多奇怪的事。我们很无知。去年我们杀了三个白人。坏人告诉他们说我们是他们的敌人。他们说……」

Then their chief replies: “Your talk is good, and we believe what you say. We believe in Jacob, and look upon you as a father. When you are hungry, you may have our game. You may gather our sweet fruits. We will give you food when you come to our land. We will show you the springs, and you may drink; the water is good. We will be friends, and when you come we will be glad. We will tell the Indians who live on the other side of the great river that we have seen _Ka′-pu-rats_, and he is the Indians’ friend. We will tell them he is Jacob’s friend. We are very poor. Look at our women and children; they are naked. We have no horses; we climb the rocks, and our feet are sore. We live among rocks, and they yield little food and many thorns. When the cold moons come, our children are hungry. We have not much to give; you must not think us mean. You are wise; we have heard you tell strange things. We are ignorant. Last year we killed three white men. Bad men said they were our enemies. They told great lies. We thought them true. We were mad; it made us big fools. We are very sorry. Do not think of them, it is done; let us be friends. We are ignorant--like little children in understanding compared with you. When we do wrong, do not get mad, and be like children too.

「白人杀我们的人，我们就杀他们。然后他们杀我们更多的人。这不好。我们听说白人是很大的一群人。等他们杀完我们，就不会剩下一个印第安人去埋葬死者了。我们爱我们的国家；我们不认识别的土地。我们听说别的土地更好；我们不知道。松树唱歌，我们高兴。我们的孩子在暖沙里玩耍；我们听见他们唱歌，就高兴。种子成熟，我们有吃的，我们高兴。我们不想要他们的好土地；我们要我们的岩石，和我们祖先住过的大山。我们很穷；我们很无知；但我们很诚实。你们有马，有许多东西。你们很聪明；你们有一颗好心。我们愿意做朋友。我没有更多要说的了。」

“When white men kill our people, we kill them. Then they kill more of us. It is not good. We hear that the white men are a great number. When they stop killing us, there will be no Indian left to bury the dead. We love our country; we know not other lands. We hear that other lands are better; we do not know. The pines sing, and we are glad. Our children play in the warm sand; we hear them sing, and are glad. The seeds ripen, and we have to eat, and we are glad. We do not want their good lands; we want our rocks, and the great mountains where our fathers lived. We are very poor; we are very ignorant; but we are very honest. You have horses, and many things. You are very wise; you have a good heart. We will be friends. Nothing more have I to say.”

卡普拉茨是尤特人和肖肖尼人给我起的名字，意思是「断臂」。实际说的话比我记下来的重复多得多，语气也重得多。这之后送了一些礼物，我们握了手，议事会散了。

_Ka′-pu-rats_ is the name by which I am known among the Utes and Shoshones, meaning “arm off.” There was much more repetition than I have given, and much emphasis. After this a few presents were given, we shook hands, and the council broke up.

汉布林先生和其中一个人攀谈起来，一直谈到其他人都走了，然后了解到那三个人之死的更多细节。他们几乎饿死、筋疲力尽地来到那个印第安村庄。他们得到了食物，被送上回定居点的路。他们离开后不久，一个科罗拉多河以东的印第安人来到村里，告诉他们有一帮矿工在一场醉酒斗殴中杀死了一个印第安女人，毫无疑问就是这些人。从来没有人下过峡谷；那是不可能的；他们是想掩盖自己的罪。他这样把人们煽动得怒火中烧。他们追上去，把那些人围在埋伏圈里，把他们射成了刺猬。[11]

Mr. Hamblin fell into conversation with one of the men, and held him until the others had left, and then learned more of the particulars of the death of the three men. They came upon the Indian village almost starved and exhausted with fatigue. They were supplied with food, and put on their way to the settlements. Shortly after they had left, an Indian from the east of the Colorado arrived at their village, and told them about a number of miners having killed a squaw in drunken brawl, and no doubt these were the men. No person had ever come down the cañon; that was impossible; they were trying to hide their guilt. In this way he worked them into a great rage. They followed, surrounded the men in ambush, and filled them full of arrows.[11]

那一夜我睡得安宁，尽管这些杀我队员的人，以及他们的朋友尤因卡雷特人，就在不到五百码外睡着。我们下峡谷期间，驮运队和补给——多到足以让一个印第安人富得超乎他最狂野的梦想——全部交给他们照看，而一切都完好无损；连一块糖都没有被孩子偷走。

That night I slept in peace, although these murderers of my men, and their friends, the _U-in-ka-rets_, were sleeping not five hundred yards away. While we were gone to the cañon, the pack-train and supplies, enough to make an Indian rich beyond his wildest dreams, were all left in their charge, and were all safe; not even a lump of sugar was pilfered by the children.

9 月 20 日。几天来我们一直在讨论给这些山起几个名字的相对优劣。印第安人叫它们尤因卡雷特——松树之乡，我们采用了这个名字。那座大山我们叫它特伦布尔山，以纪念那位参议员。今天队伍出发返回峡谷蓄水坑，而毕晓普上尉和我去爬特伦布尔山。路上我们经过那个火山最后一次开口的地方。

_September 20._--For several days we have been discussing the relative merits of several names for these mountains. The Indians call them _U-in-ka-rets_, the region of pines, and we adopt the name. The great mountain we call Mount Trumbull, in honor of the Senator. To-day the train starts back to the cañon water-pocket, while Captain Bishop and I climb Mount Trumbull. On our way we pass the point that was the last opening to the volcano.

似乎没过几年，最后一次火的洪流就席卷了这山谷。它从两座粗糙的锥形小山之间涌出，沿山谷奔流到几乎在下端的一座山脚，然后分开，从山两侧流过。这最后一次溢流标记得非常清楚；在更古老的河床上，土壤、树木和青草一直长到它的边缘。这洪流在它的边缘处处有十到二十英尺高，骤然终止，从下面看像一堵墙。冷却时它碎成碎片，但这些碎片仍留在原处，你能看出溪流和波浪的轮廓。它流下来之后没过多久，风化物还没有风化成土壤，上面几乎没有什么植物，只在这里那里找到一点地衣。然而，它从深处喷出已经那么久远，以至于在少数积聚了灰和渣的地方，长出了巨大的雪松。在火山口附近，黑色玄武岩的冻结波浪……

It seems but a few years since the last flood of fire swept the valley. Between two rough, conical hills it poured, and ran down the valley to the foot of a mountain standing almost at the lower end, then parted, and ran on either side of the mountain. This last overflow is very plainly marked; there is soil, with trees and grass, to the very edge of it, on a more ancient bed. The flood was everywhere on its border from ten to twenty feet in height, terminating abruptly, and looking like a wall from below. On cooling, it shattered into fragments, but these are still in place, and you can see the outlines of streams and waves. So little time has elapsed since it ran down, that the elements have not weathered a soil, and there is scarcely any vegetation on it, but here and there a lichen is found. And yet, so long ago was it poured from the depths, that where ashes and cinders have collected in a few places, some huge cedars have grown. Near the crater the frozen waves of black basalt are rent with deep fissures, transverse to the direction of the flow. Then we ride through a cedar forest, up a long ascent, until we come to cliffs of columnar basalt. Here we tie our horses, and prepare for a climb among the columns. Through crevices we work, till at last we are on the mountain, a thousand acres of pine land spread out before us, gently rising to the other edge. There are two peaks on the mountain. We walked two miles to the foot of the one looking to be the highest, then a long, hard climb to its summit. And here, oh, what a view is before us! A vision of glory! Peaks of lava all around below us. The Vermilion Cliffs to the north, with their splendor of colors; the Pine Valley Mountain to the northwest, clothed in mellow, perspective haze; unnamed mountains to the southwest, towering over cañons, bottomless to my peering gaze, like chasms to the nadir hell; and away beyond, the San Francisco Mountains, lifting their black heads into the heavens. We find our way down the mountain, reaching the trail made by the pack-train just at dusk.

又走了两天，我们到了烟管泉；再一天，到了卡纳布。镇上游八英里有一条峡谷，两侧各有一群湖。我坐在其中一个旁边，清澈的水在我脚边，我可以随意痛饮。

Two days more, and we are at Pipe Spring; one day, and we are at Kanab. Eight miles above the town is a cañon, on either side of which is a group of lakes. By the side of one of these I sit, the crystal waters at my feet, at which I may drink at will.

户外冒险文库

OUTING ADVENTURE LIBRARY

霍勒斯·凯法特编

_Edited by Horace Kephart_

这里首次汇集了各个时代、各个国家伟大的冒险故事。这些是那些攀登高山、深入丛林；探索海洋、穿越沙漠；懂得机会并抓住它、活下来写下自己艰辛、坚忍与成就故事的人们的个人记录。这个系列将包含不定数量的卷册——因为故事多得数不清。全套由霍勒斯·凯法特编辑。每卷都经受这两个问题的检验：它真实吗？它有趣吗？

¶ Here are brought together for the first time the great stories of adventure of all ages and countries. 1.《老西部》，乔治·弗雷德里克·鲁克斯顿著。在墨西哥战争前，为白人从落基山脉开辟通往太平洋道路的，是那些独立的捕兽人和猎人。他们没有留下冒险记录，他们中的大多数人如今只剩下模糊的名字。但有一个年轻英国人曾和他们一起生活过一段时间，从他们的视角看待生活，和他们一起捕兽、一起对抗印第安人。那就是乔治·弗雷德里克·鲁克斯顿。他的故事是我们对真正拓荒者时代老西部的唯一完整图景——关于基特·卡森、吉姆·布里杰、比尔·威廉姆斯、萨布莱特兄弟，以及那支被遗忘的、打开了西部的光荣队伍的所有其他人。

1. IN THE OLD WEST, by George Frederick Ruxton. The men who blazed the trail across the Rockies to the Pacific were the independent trappers and hunters in the days before the Mexican war. They left no records of their adventures and most of them linger now only as shadowy names. But a young Englishman lived among them for a time, saw life from their point of view, trapped with them and fought with them against the Indians. That was George Frederick Ruxton. His story is our only complete picture of the Old West in the days of the real Pioneers, of Kit Carson, Jim Bridger, Bill Williams, the Sublettes, and all the rest of that glorious company of the forgotten who opened the West.

2.《遇难者与鲁滨逊》。自从人类开始航海，就面对着海难和饥饿的危险。散落在航海编年史里的是那些灾难降临者的故事，以及他们如何面对的个人记录。本卷收录了其中一些——那些在野蛮人中间、在荒凉海岸上靠双手为生，或在敞篷船里无助漂流的人们的叙事。它们从南太平洋到圣劳伦斯湾，从巴塔哥尼亚的铁海岸到古巴的海岸。它们是那个时代——出海的人所能指望的最好东西就是艰辛和男人干的活儿——的回声。

2. CASTAWAYS AND CRUSOES. Since the beginnings of navigation men have faced the dangers of shipwreck and starvation. Scattered through the annals of the sea are the stories of those to whom disaster came and the personal records of the way they met it. Some of them are given in this volume, narratives of men who lived by their hands among savages and on forlorn coasts, or drifted helpless in open boats. They range from the South Seas to the Gulf of St. Lawrence, from the iron coast of Patagonia to the shores of Cuba. They are echoes from the days when the best that could be hoped by the man who went to sea was hardship and man’s-sized work.

3.《印第安人中的俘虏》。首先是詹姆斯·史密斯上尉的故事，他在布拉多克战败时被特拉华印第安人俘获，被部落收养，四年里作为印第安人生活，和他们一起打猎，研究他们的习性，学习他们的视角。然后是布雷萨尼神父的故事，他尝过易洛魁人的酷刑；玛丽·罗兰森，她是菲利普王战争中的人类战利品之一；以及默西·哈比森，她在圣克莱尔战败后那片红色洪流中受苦。这些都是演员们自己在印第安人还常驻我们祖先家门口的那些年代留下的个人记录。

3. CAPTIVES AMONG THE INDIANS. First of all is the story of Captain James Smith, who was captured by the Delawares at the time of Braddock’s defeat, was adopted into the tribe, and for four years lived as an Indian, hunting with them, studying their habits, and learning their point of view. Then there is the story of Father Bressani who felt the tortures of the Iroquois, of Mary Rowlandson who was among the human spoils of King Philip’s war, and of Mercy Harbison who suffered in the red flood that followed St. Clair’s defeat. All are personal records made by the actors themselves in those days when the Indian was constantly at our forefathers’s doors.

4.《首次穿越科罗拉多大峡谷》，约翰·韦斯利·鲍威尔少校著。鲍威尔少校是联邦军军官，在夏伊洛之战失去一条手臂。尽管如此，战后四年他组织了一支探险队，乘船探查了科罗拉多大峡谷——这是第一次有人完成这段旅程。他的故事在科学报告的湮没中埋没了多年。在这里它被抢救出来，作为一次伟大的个人探险壮举的记录呈现，可与派克、刘易斯与克拉克、麦肯齐的功绩相提并论。

4. FIRST THROUGH THE GRAND CANYON, by Major John Wesley Powell. Major Powell was an officer in the Union Army who lost an arm at Shiloh. In spite of this four years after the war he organized an expedition which explored the Grand Canyon of the Colorado in boats--the first to make this journey. His story has been lost for years in the oblivion of a scientific report. It is here rescued and presented as a record of one of the great personal exploring feats, fitted to rank with the exploits of Pike, Lewis and Clark, and Mackenzie.

5.《在北极冰群中漂流》，伊莱沙·肯特·凯恩医学博士著。在五十多年前为寻找约翰·富兰克林爵士而北上的许多远征队中，有一支由一位纽约商人资助的，注定要在兰开斯特海峡被极地冰群裹挟着漫无目的地漂流过一个北极冬天。远征队的医生坚持记日记，依据那记录讲出了关于一个极北冬天的最完整故事。这里呈现的正是那个故事，去掉了纯粹的科学数据，剥到作者和船员其他成员的个人功绩与冒险。

5. ADRIFT IN THE ARCTIC ICE-PACK, By Elisha Kent Kane, M. D. Out of the many expeditions that went north in search of Sir John Franklin over fifty years ago, it fell to the lot of one, financed by a New York merchant, to spend an Arctic winter drifting aimlessly in the grip of the Polar ice in Lancaster Sound. The surgeon of the expedition kept a careful diary and out of that record told the first complete story of a Far Northern winter. That story is here presented, shorn of the purely scientific data and stripped to the personal exploits and adventures of the author and the other members of the crew.
